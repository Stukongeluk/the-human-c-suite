# AI in the product: customer value over hype

Read this for AI features, an "AI story" for the board or investors, competitor AI launches, and AI tools the product and technology organisation pays for.

## 1. Start from the customer problem

- Which customer job does the AI make easier, faster or cheaper? Evidence from usage, support tickets, churn reasons and customer requests comes first.
- Would a simpler non-AI change solve it? Often better defaults, search or an integration do.
- A competitor's launch is a signal to understand, not a deadline. Ask customers what they actually want from it.

## 2. Make AI features safe and worth it

- **Evaluate before launch:** a test set of real cases, measured accuracy, failure modes, and what happens when the AI is wrong. Keep evaluating after launch.
- **Cost per use:** model and infrastructure cost per conversation or task, against the value to the customer and the price you can charge.
- **Stage the rollout:** design partners, then a beta, then general availability, with gates on quality, customer satisfaction and cost.
- **Keep a human route** for anything that matters to the customer, and be clear when they are dealing with an AI.
- **Compliance:** check data protection and the EU AI Act's transparency duties for AI systems that interact with people, with counsel. Not legal advice.
- The company remains liable for what its AI tells customers (Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024).

## 3. An honest AI story for the board

A good AI story is specific and measured, not a list of buzzwords:

- the two or three customer problems AI will address, with the evidence
- what has been measured so far, and what will be measured next, with dates and gates
- cost, including AI spend already running and what it delivers
- risks and how they are controlled: quality, security, data, regulation
- what it means for people: which work changes, how teams are trained and where freed time goes

Base rates help set expectations: most enterprise AI pilots haven't shown measurable P&L impact yet, and many agentic AI projects are forecast to be cancelled. Check `evidence.md` for the current figures.

## 4. AI that replaces people's work

When an AI idea would take over work people do today (support, onboarding, operations, QA):

- Size the real share of work it can take, with measured quality, before any staffing decision.
- Follow the IKEA pattern: when AI takes routine work, move people to the valuable work customers want and nobody had capacity for. Ingka retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff as remote interior design advisers.
- Don't plan headcount reductions into an AI roadmap. If capacity is freed over time, use attrition, redeployment and growth first.

## 5. AI tools the organisation pays for

- Inventory every AI tool and API line with an owner, seats paid against weekly active users, monthly cost and how value is measured.
- Low use is a signal to right-size, change or stop, including tools you chose yourself.
- Dashboards and analytics tools are only worth what decisions they change. Ask who uses them, for what, and what it would cost to get the same answer another way.
