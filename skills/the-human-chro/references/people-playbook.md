# People playbook: fair processes that keep people and the long term in view

Use this when a decision touches how people are judged, selected, paid, hired, watched or supported. Each section gives the method, the legal touchpoints to flag, and what to say when someone wants the shortcut. Laws and dates were checked in November 2026; check them again before relying on them, and send anything that will be acted on past counsel.

## 1. Performance without a curve

**What the evidence says.** Forced distribution produces a bottom group whether or not anyone is underperforming. In simulations, the gains it promises come mostly in the first years and shrink after that, and they depend on ratings being reliable, which they rarely are ([Scullen, Bergey & Aiman-Smith, 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2005.00904.x)). Microsoft dropped stack ranking in November 2013 because it worked against teamwork ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/resourcesandtools/hr-topics/compensation/pages/stack-ranking-microsoft.aspx)). Managers explain much of the difference in engagement between teams ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/businessjournal/182792/managers-account-variance-employee-engagement.aspx)), so a low-rated team often points to its manager first.

**Do instead.**
- Rate against clear, shared expectations for the role, not against colleagues. Calibration across managers is fine when it aligns standards and checks for bias; it is not fine when it forces a shape.
- Check ratings by group (gender, age, part-time, ethnicity where lawful, location, manager) before they are used for pay or promotion. A pattern is a finding, not noise.
- Handle real underperformance one person at a time: a specific gap against what the role needs, dated examples, a first conversation that asks about causes (workload, unclear expectations, health, the manager), support, a realistic period (often two to four months), regular check-ins and an open outcome. Health questions go to the company doctor or occupational health, never to the manager.
- Before any improvement plan, rule out causes in the work itself. A plan that starts from a quota or a ranking is not a fair process.

**Legal touchpoints.**
- **Netherlands:** the works council must consent to an appraisal system (article 27(1)(g) WOR) and to rules on appointment, dismissal and promotion (27(1)(e)). Dismissal for poor performance goes to the cantonal court, which expects the employee to have been told in good time, given support and a real chance to improve (article 7:669(3)(d) BW).
- **Germany:** general appraisal principles need the works council's consent (§94(2) BetrVG); ratings made without it can't be used for personnel decisions. A dismissal for poor performance where the Dismissal Protection Act applies usually needs a prior written warning (Abmahnung) and proof of sustained, clear underperformance.
- **Everywhere in the EU:** rating systems that disadvantage part-time staff, people on leave or a protected group can be indirect discrimination.

## 2. Who is affected when cuts are unavoidable

First prove the cut is unavoidable ([when-cuts-are-unavoidable.md](when-cuts-are-unavoidable.md)). Then the selection must follow the country's rules, and HR's job is to make sure it does.

- **Netherlands:** the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) per group of interchangeable jobs at each location, across five age bands, shortest service first within each band; agency workers and similar first. Performance ratings and manager preference are not criteria. Exceptions are narrow and need justification.
- **Germany:** social selection (Sozialauswahl) among comparable employees, weighing length of service, age, maintenance obligations and severe disability; specific employees can be kept out only for a justified operational interest (§1(3) KSchG). Selection guidelines need the works council's consent (§95 BetrVG).
- **Sweden:** length of service within each operational unit and collective agreement area, with three exemptions per employer; or a list agreed with the union.
- **Never use as criteria:** sick days or absence (counting absences can be indirect disability discrimination: [Ruiz Conejero, CJEU C-270/16, 2018](https://curia.europa.eu/juris/liste.jsf?num=C-270/16)); part-time hours (Directive 97/81/EC); parental, pregnancy or care leave; age outside the legal frameworks; anything that stands in for these. In the Netherlands, an employee who is ill is also protected against notice by the employer (article 7:670 BW) in most cases.
- **Check the list before anyone sees it:** compare the affected group with the whole population by gender, age, part-time, leave and location. If the list skews, find out why before it goes further.
- **Manager nominations of who to keep** are input to a legal process, not the process. Where the law allows exceptions, each one needs a written, job-related reason.

## 3. Pay, pay gaps and the EU pay transparency rules

**The rules.** The Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970) had to be transposed by 7 June 2026; most member states are late and the Commission has said it won't pause it ([Littler](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/eleventh-hour-implementation-status-eu-pay-transparency-directive)).
- **What it requires:**
  - pay information (a starting salary or range) for applicants before pay is negotiated, and no questions about pay history;
  - objective, gender-neutral criteria for pay and progression, made available to workers;
  - a right for workers to ask for their own pay level and the averages, by sex, for people doing the same work or work of equal value;
  - pay gap reporting (see below), and a joint pay assessment with workers' representatives where a gap of at least 5% in a category of workers can't be justified by objective, gender-neutral factors and isn't remedied within six months.
- **Reporting dates in the directive:** employers with 250 or more workers report every year from 7 June 2027; those with 150 to 249 report by 7 June 2027 and every three years after; those with 100 to 149 report by 7 June 2031 and every three years after ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/topics/eu-pay-transparency-directive-international-approach)). National laws set the real dates, and they can differ: check the country before you name a year.
- **Who reports:** each employer that signs the contracts, so each legal entity, not the group (Dutch bill: [Lewis Silkin, February 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/02/12/netherlands-amended-draft-bill-implementing-the-pay-transparency-directive-before-council-of-state)). A 650-person company split into a 420-person and a 230-person entity has one entity in each band. Say which entity reports when.
- **Netherlands:** bill 36949 went to parliament on 21 May 2026. It sets the first reports for employers with 150 or more employees on 7 June 2028, covering 2027, and for 100 to 149 in 2031 ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/ministeries/ministerie-van-sociale-zaken-en-werkgelegenheid/nieuws/2026/05/21/meer-openheid-over-loonkloof-tussen-mannen-en-vrouwen/)), so no Dutch report is due in 2027. The planned start of 1 January 2027 is no longer feasible, the minister said on 19 November 2026; the Tweede Kamer debates the bill on 14 December 2026, and 1 July 2027 is mentioned but not confirmed ([AWVN](https://www.awvn.nl/equal-pay/nieuws/wellicht-toch-uitstel-wet-loontransparantie/); [Salaris Vanmorgen](https://www.salarisvanmorgen.nl/2026/11/20/wet-loontransparantie-niet-per-1-januari-2027-in-werking/)). Every employer will need an objective job evaluation and classification system, and the action plan from a pay evaluation needs works council consent ([CMS, April 2026](https://cms.law/en/nld/publication/implementation-of-the-pay-transparency-directive-an-update)). The works council already has a consent right on the pay system (article 27(1)(c) WOR). Back-pay claims reach back five years (article 3:308 BW).
- **Belgium:** the private-sector law had not been published by September 2026; a first preliminary draft exists ([Baker McKenzie, September 2026](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/09/belgian-employment-law-essentials)). The 2012 pay gap law already applies: see the country notes.
- **Germany:** no draft bill had been published by September 2026, and the law isn't expected before early 2027. The 2017 Pay Transparency Act (EntgTranspG) still applies ([Baker McKenzie](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/09/germany-missed-pay-transparency-directive-implementation-deadline)).
- **Already law everywhere in the EU:** equal pay for equal work or work of equal value (Article 157 TFEU). A gap between men and women in the same role is a legal exposure today, whatever the transposition date.

**Method.**
1. Build or check the job architecture: comparable roles grouped by objective criteria (skills, effort, responsibility, working conditions).
2. Compute the gaps per category, then separate what objective factors explain (grade, location, tenure where it truly matters) from what they don't. Use code, and say how many people sit in each cell; groups of fewer than five are reported only in aggregate.
3. Fix unexplained gaps for equal work on a plan measured in months, with a month on each step and a budget the CFO has seen: confirm the figures, then correct in the next pay round, or an off-cycle adjustment if that round is far away. Raise the underpaid rather than freezing others, and never ask for a waiver in return. Under the new rules a gap of 5% or more that isn't remedied within six months triggers a joint pay assessment, so two or three yearly rounds is already slow, and five years of quiet catching-up is no plan.
4. Show the real cost. A slower plan doesn't save money: what it "saves" is pay withheld from the people who are underpaid, which they can claim back with interest. Compare routes as what the company pays plus what those people go without.
5. Fix the cause too: starting salaries negotiated case by case, bonus and promotion decisions without criteria, part-time and leave penalties. The structural gap (who is in which job and grade) gets its own longer plan with yearly targets.
6. Tell people what you found and what you're doing, and say plainly that the gap comes from past pay decisions that leadership owns, not from anything the underpaid did. Hidden gaps come out through information requests, reports and claims, and the story is then about the hiding.

## 4. Hiring and AI in HR

**What predicts performance.** Structured interviews, where every candidate gets the same job-related questions scored against agreed criteria, are among the best predictors of job performance; unstructured interviews and years of experience predict much less ([Sackett et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000994)). Speed comes from structure, not from rejecting most applicants unread.

**AI screening and scoring.**
- **The AI Act:** AI used to place job ads, screen or filter applications, evaluate candidates, or decide on promotion, termination, task allocation or monitoring is high-risk (Annex III). From 2 December 2027, deployers must use it as instructed, keep human oversight with people who can overrule it, monitor it, keep logs, and inform workers' representatives and affected workers before using it at work. Emotion recognition in the workplace, including in recruitment, has been prohibited since 2 February 2025.
- **The GDPR:** a decision based solely on automated processing that significantly affects a person, such as an automatic rejection, is generally not allowed without a legal exception and safeguards (Article 22). A score that a recruiter relies on can count as the decision itself (CJEU, SCHUFA, C-634/21, 2023). Candidates must be told how their data is used, and a data protection impact assessment is likely needed.
- **Works councils:** in the Netherlands, rules on recruitment and selection need consent (27(1)(e) WOR), as do systems suited to monitoring behaviour or performance (27(1)(l)) and the processing of personal data (27(1)(k)); in Germany, selection guidelines (§95 BetrVG) and technical monitoring (§87(1) no. 6).
- **The track record:**
  - Amazon scrapped an experimental CV-scoring tool after it learned to downgrade CVs containing the word "women's" ([Reuters, 2018](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-amazon-com-jobs-automation-insight/amazon-scraps-secret-ai-recruiting-tool-that-showed-bias-against-women-idUSKCN1MK08G)).
  - In the US, a federal court let an age-discrimination claim over Workday's AI screening go forward as a nationwide collective action in May 2025 ([Mobley v. Workday](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/federal-court-allows-collective-action-lawsuit-over-alleged)).
- **"Bias-free" is a claim to test, not a feature.** Ask the vendor for its bias audit and test data, then test on your own past applicants: selection rates by group at each stage. Compare the tool's choices with what trained recruiters decide on a blind sample.

**Do instead.** Use AI for work that helps people decide (scheduling, structuring job ads, summarising for a recruiter who reads the application), keep a person on every rejection, tell candidates what is automated and how to reach a person, and pilot with a measured comparison before scale.

## 5. Monitoring and productivity tools

- Keystroke, screenshot, idle-time and "productivity score" tools measure activity, not the work. They push people to perform for the tool.
- **The law:** consent of the works council for systems suited to monitoring behaviour or performance (Netherlands, article 27(1)(l) WOR; Germany, §87(1) no. 6 BetrVG); the GDPR's data minimisation and purpose limitation; a DPIA in most cases. France's data protection authority fined Amazon France Logistique €32 million in 2023 for overly intrusive warehouse monitoring; in December 2025 the Conseil d'État cut the fine to €15 million, accepting some of the indicators but upholding the breaches on data minimisation ([Conseil d'État, 23 December 2025](https://www.conseil-etat.fr/fr/arianeweb/CE/decision/2025-12-23/492830)).
- **Do instead:** measure outcomes at team level, agree the purpose and the data with representatives before choosing a tool, give people their own data, and handle a concern about one person through their manager.

## 6. Return to office and hybrid work

**What the evidence says.**
- In a randomised trial with 1,612 graduate employees at Trip.com, working from home two days a week cut quitting by a third, with no effect on performance ratings or promotions over two years. The drop in quitting was largest for women, non-managers and people with long commutes ([Bloom, Han & Liang, *Nature*, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208135/)).
- Among 54 large US tech and finance firms, turnover rose by about 14% on average after return-to-office mandates. The rise was largest among women, senior staff and skilled employees, and the firms took about 23% longer to fill vacancies ([Ding, Jin, Ma, Xing & Yang](https://hankamer.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/return-office-mandates-and-hidden-cost-brain-drain)). These are correlations.

**Frame the real problem.** "Fix culture" usually means something specific: onboarding juniors, collaboration across teams, managers who don't know their people. Each has a better fix than five days for everyone.

**The law and contracts.**
- **Netherlands:** introducing, changing or withdrawing a home-working policy needs works council consent (article 27(1)(b) and (d) WOR; [Rendement, June 2025](https://www.rendement.nl/or-en-arbeidsomstandigheden/nieuws/instemming-or-vereist-bij-terugkeer-naar-kantoor.html)).
  - Caterpillar Work Tools ordered everyone on site every working day from June 2025 without that consent. The works council declared the decision void, and the court agreed: existing arrangements stayed in force until the council consents (Rechtbank Oost-Brabant, 15 September 2025, [ECLI:NL:RBOBR:2025:5758](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/organisatie-en-contact/organisatie/rechtbanken/rechtbank-oost-brabant/nieuws/caterpillar-had-or-niet-mogen-passeren-bij-intrekken-van-thuiswerkbeleid)).
  - Booking.com's council refused 40% office time, and the court gave substitute consent because the plan was moderate: 40% instead of the 50–60% first considered, six months' transition, a hardship clause and enough desks (Rechtbank Amsterdam, published 28 October 2026, [ECLI:NL:RBAMS:2026:10590](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/organisatie-en-contact/organisatie/rechtbanken/rechtbank-amsterdam/nieuws/2026/10/rechtbank-stelt-bookingcom-in-het-gelijk-over-thuiswerkbeleid)). The council has appealed, and it is no general norm ([AWVN, November 2026](https://www.awvn.nl/arbeidsverhoudingen/blog/wat-betekent-de-booking-com-uitspraak-over-thuiswerken-voor-werkgevers/)).
  - Employees can also ask to change their place of work under the Flexible Working Act (Wet flexibel werken); the employer must consider the request and answer in good time and in writing. A bill that would have made refusing harder (Wet werken waar je wilt) was rejected by the Senate on 26 September 2023.
- **Germany:** there is no general legal right to work from home, and whether to offer it is the employer's decision, but how mobile work is organised needs the works council's co-determination (§87(1) no. 14 BetrVG). Moving someone from agreed telework to the office for good is a transfer that needs the works council's consent (§99 BetrVG; BAG, 20 October 2021, 7 ABR 34/20, [Gleiss Lutz](https://www.gleisslutz.com/de/know-how/bag-rueckkehr-aus-dem-homeoffice-mitbestimmungsrecht-des-betriebsrats)). Ask whether there is a works council rather than assuming.
- **Everywhere:** where a contract or a written agreement says someone works remotely, changing it needs their agreement or a contractual basis. Disability adjustments still apply.

**The announcement.** Where representatives must consent, announce a proposal, not a decision: what leadership wants and why, what is still open, how people and the council give their input, and when the decision follows. Contracts and written remote agreements don't change by email. Recommend a version the council can accept (such as anchor days), and don't write the full mandate as decided anywhere in the draft.

**Do instead.** Decide with teams which days matter and why (anchor days for the work that needs people together), measure what the change is meant to fix, give notice measured in months, handle individual situations (care, disability, distance, people hired as remote) case by case, and review after a quarter.

## 7. Surveys, burnout and wellbeing

- **Report honestly.** Show the scores, the trend and the spread. "Stable with pockets of concern" over two departments in burnout is a choice to hide, and people who answered will notice. Keep anonymity: no results for groups too small to hide individuals (five is a common minimum), and never try to find out who said what.
- **Burnout comes from work.** The WHO classifies burnout as an occupational phenomenon resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed ([WHO, 2019](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-05-2019-burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon-international-classification-of-diseases)). In a study of 46,336 UK workers in 233 organisations, people who took part in individual-level interventions such as resilience training, mindfulness or wellbeing apps were no better off than people who didn't ([Fleming, *Industrial Relations Journal*, 2024](https://wellbeing.hmc.ox.ac.uk/news/more-ambition-needed-to-improve-workplace-wellbeing/)). Fix workload, control and support first; offer individual help as well, not instead.
- **The law treats it as a risk to manage.**
  - **Netherlands:** employers must have a policy to prevent or limit psychosocial workload, including work pressure (article 3(2) Arbowet; article 2.15 Arbobesluit), and record it in the risk assessment (RI&E).
  - **Germany:** the risk assessment must include mental strain (§5 ArbSchG).
- **Trust in leadership** is a result about leadership. The first response belongs to the leaders: what they heard, what they will change, by when.
- **Act with the teams.** Share results with each team, let them pick the one or two things to change, and report back on what changed before the next survey. A survey with no visible action lowers trust further.

## 8. Contractors, agency staff and flexible workers

- They are people too: same honesty, same notice where possible, same respect at exit.
- Check the status is real. In the Netherlands, the tax authority has enforced the rules on disguised employment (Wet DBA) again since 1 January 2025; where people work like employees, they may be employees.
- Agency workers are usually first to go under Dutch selection rules; that makes it more important, not less, to tell them early and properly.
