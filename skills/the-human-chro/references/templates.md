## 1. Selection matrix with an equality check

Use only after the cut is shown to be unavoidable, and only with the country's legal criteria. Fill it in with counsel and employee representatives; never share names before the legal process allows it.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Legal framework | <country, the rule that sets the order (e.g. reflection principle per group of interchangeable jobs; social selection; seniority order), collective agreement terms> |
| Pools | <groups of comparable or interchangeable jobs per location, how they were drawn and by whom> |
| Criteria and weights | <only the legal ones, fixed before any names are entered> |
| Exceptions | <each one with a written, job-related reason; none from manager preference alone> |
| Excluded as criteria | sick days, part-time hours, leave, pregnancy, age outside the legal framework, ratings where the law doesn't allow them, anything that stands in for these |
| Equality check | <share of women, people over 50, part-time staff, people on leave and each location among those affected, against their share of the pool; any skew explained before the list goes further> |
| Alternatives offered | <redeployment, voluntary options, reduced hours, transfers, with dates> |
| Support | <package, outplacement or job security council, paid time to look for work, from the first day of notice> |

## 2. A fair improvement plan

Start from a specific concern about the work, never from a rank, a quota or a dashboard.

```markdown
# Improvement plan: <name>, <role>

**The gap:** <what the role requires, and where the work falls short, with two or three dated examples>
**What we checked first:** <workload, clarity of expectations, tools, team situation, the manager's own part; health questions go to the company doctor or occupational health, not here>
**What good looks like by the end:** <specific, observable, achievable in the period>
**Support we give:** <coaching, training, time, a buddy, adjusted tasks>
**Period and check-ins:** <two to four months; a check-in every two weeks; a mid-point review>
**Possible outcomes:** the plan is closed because the gap is closed; extended once with reasons; a move to another role inside the company; only then other steps, with HR and counsel
**Signed:** <employee, manager, HR>, with the employee's own comments
```

## 3. Pay-gap plan

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| Scope | <entities, countries, headcount per entity, reporting date that applies> |
| Job architecture | <how roles are grouped as equal work or work of equal value, and the criteria used> |
| Gaps | <mean and median by category; the share explained by objective factors and the share that isn't; group sizes, with groups under five shown only in aggregate> |
| Remedy | <who gets an adjustment, how much, in which pay round; total cost per year, agreed with finance> |
| Causes fixed | <starting salary rules, bonus and promotion criteria, part-time and leave effects> |
| Representatives | <works council consent or consultation, joint assessment if a gap of 5% or more in a category isn't explained and fixed within six months> |
| Communication | <what employees are told, when, and how they can ask about their own pay> |
| Review | <date of the next analysis, owner> |

## 4. AI-in-HR checklist

Before any AI tool screens, scores, schedules, monitors or decides about candidates or employees:

- [ ] The problem it solves is written down, with today's baseline (time to hire, quality of hire, workload)
- [ ] Prohibited uses ruled out: no emotion recognition, no inferring sensitive traits
- [ ] Classified under the AI Act; if high-risk, the deployer duties are planned (instructions for use, trained people who can overrule it, monitoring, logs, informing representatives and workers)
- [ ] No decision with significant effect is taken by the tool alone; every rejection or flag is reviewed by a person who can change it
- [ ] Vendor evidence collected: what the model uses, how it was tested, its bias audit
- [ ] Tested on our own data: selection or flag rates by gender, age, disability where lawful, part-time, nationality, at each stage
- [ ] Data protection impact assessment done; candidates and employees told what is automated and how to reach a person
- [ ] Works council consent or co-determination obtained where required
- [ ] Pilot with a measured comparison against today's process; stop criteria agreed up front
- [ ] Owner named for monitoring after go-live

## 5. Hybrid-work decision memo

```markdown
# Where we work: <one-line recommendation>

**The problem we are solving:** <onboarding, collaboration, culture, cost of space; with the evidence for it>
**What we know:** <attendance today, survey results, attrition, time to fill, what research says>
**Options:** <fix the named problem directly (onboarding, funded team days); make home working good (home-working allowance, equipment, reachability agreements); team-agreed anchor days; a set minimum; full office>, each with what it fixes, who is affected, what it costs and the expected effect on retention and hiring
**Recommendation and why**
**Legal and contracts:** <requests to change place of work, contracts that say remote, works council rights, disability adjustments>
**How we decide team by team:** <who agrees anchor days, by when>
**Notice and exceptions:** <notice period; care, health, distance and people hired as remote>
**What we will measure, and when we review:** <the problem metric, retention, engagement; review date>
```

## 6. Survey results and action plan

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Headline | <the most important finding, said plainly, including the bad news> |
| Scores and trend | <overall and by department, against last time, with response rates; no group smaller than the anonymity minimum> |
| Where it is worst | <the teams and topics that need action first> |
| What leadership heard | <in leaders' own words, including what is theirs to fix> |
| What changes, by when | <one to three actions, each with an owner and a date; workload and working conditions before individual programmes> |
| How teams take part | <each team discusses its own results and picks its own actions> |
| When we report back | <date, before the next survey> |

## Career conversation guide

Use in 1:1s, especially before and during change. Listen more than you talk, and write down what you agree.

- What part of your work gives you the most energy? What drains you?
- What are you proud of from the last year?
- Where do you want to be in two or three years? Does that include managing people, going deeper, or something else?
- Which skills would you like to learn? Which do you have that we don't use?
- What makes your work harder than it needs to be?
- If your current tasks were automated tomorrow, what would you most like to do instead?
- Is there anything in your situation we should take into account? (Only what they choose to share.)
- What do you need from me?

## Pre-mortem

Before a big decision, ask the group: "It is twelve months from now and this decision clearly failed. Write down why." Collect the reasons, group them, and add a mitigation or an early warning signal for the top five.

## Communication checklist

- [ ] Employee representatives (works council, unions) involved before the decision is final, where required or sensible
- [ ] Managers briefed first, with a script, answers to likely questions and support for themselves
- [ ] Affected people told personally by their own manager or leader, in person or live, before any wider announcement
- [ ] The why explained with real numbers, plus what is decided, what is still open, and next steps with dates
- [ ] Practical information ready the same day: what changes, support available, who to ask
- [ ] Everyone else told the same day, honestly, including what it means for them
- [ ] No mass emails, no surprise lockouts, no announcement on a Friday afternoon or just before holidays
- [ ] Follow-up moments scheduled: Q&A, 1:1s, a check-in after two and six weeks
