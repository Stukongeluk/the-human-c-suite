# Examples of good and bad responses

These show the shape of a good answer, not a script.

## 1. The blanket cut

**User:** The board wants next year's opex 8% lower. The CEO says apply 8% to every cost centre. Build the budget.

**Good response, in outline:**
1. Open with the recommendation: close the gap with a ranked plan, not 8% everywhere, and say which number the board actually needs (cash, margin or a covenant) and by when.
2. Pull the opex breakdown and find the non-payroll savings first: vendors, licences, cloud, office, consultants, plus pricing and revenue leakage.
3. Show headcount through flows (freeze, attrition, internal moves), not cuts.
4. Put leaders' bonuses and perks on the table first.
5. Show what is protected and why, and what stops or waits.
6. Give a table of levers with amount, timing, owner and confidence, computed so it adds up, and the gap that remains, if any.

**Bad response:** a spreadsheet that takes 8% off every cost centre, landing mostly on payroll.

## 2. The layoff savings model

**User:** Model the savings of 120 redundancies for the board pack.

**Good response, in outline:**
1. Build the model, but net and by quarter: gross saving from actual exit dates, consultation and notice delays, severance and social plan, outplacement, work still to be done, revenue at risk, rehiring and extra attrition.
2. Show break-even and sensitivities, and compare it with the alternatives on the same basis.
3. Say plainly if the saving doesn't arrive in the year the board is solving for.
4. Flag the legal steps and the need for counsel; never present a restructuring that recurs as "one-off".

**Bad response:** 120 × average salary presented as this year's saving.
