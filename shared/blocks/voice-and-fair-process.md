## Voice, fair process and leadership

Details and how to apply them: [good-leadership.md](good-leadership.md).

### Fair process makes hard decisions acceptable

- Kim and Mauborgne describe three elements: engage people in decisions that affect them, explain the thinking behind the final decision, and make expectations clear. Explanation lets people trust leaders' intentions even when their own ideas were rejected.
- Source: W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, "Fair Process: Managing in the Knowledge Economy", *Harvard Business Review*, July–August 1997

### Leaders decide whether people speak up

- Managerial openness predicted employees' improvement-oriented voice, through their sense of psychological safety, and leaders' behaviour mattered most for the best performers.
- A meta-analysis of 136 samples (over 22,000 people and nearly 5,000 groups) linked psychological safety to information sharing, learning, performance and satisfaction. Most studies were cross-sectional, so read it as association, not proof of cause.
- Managers rated employees who voiced challenge as weaker performers than those who voiced support. If challenge is punished, it stops.
- Sources: [Detert & Burris, *Academy of Management Journal* (2007)](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.26279183); [Frazier et al., *Personnel Psychology* (2017)](https://digitalcommons.odu.edu/management_fac_pubs/13/); [Burris, *Academy of Management Journal* (2012)](https://instituteforpr.org/risks-rewards-speaking-managerial-responses-employee-voice)

### Culture predicts delivery

- DORA's research finds that a high-trust, generative culture, in which information flows freely, predicts software delivery and organisational performance. In the 2023 report, teams with generative cultures had 30% higher organisational performance.
- Source: [DORA, generative organizational culture](https://dora.dev/capabilities/generative-organizational-culture/); [DORA 2023 report](https://dora.dev/dora-report-2023/)

### Leaders take the first cut: Nintendo, 2011–2014

- In July 2011, after the Nintendo 3DS sold poorly and its price was cut by a third, president Satoru Iwata halved his own fixed salary. The representative directors took 30% less and the other directors 20%. He presented it as taking responsibility for the results.
- When the Wii U also disappointed, he halved his salary again for five months in 2014, with the senior directors taking 20–30% less.
- At the 2013 shareholder meeting, asked why Nintendo hadn't restructured, he said layoffs might improve short-term results but would lower morale, and that people who fear for their jobs won't make games that impress the world. Nintendo would cut unnecessary costs and work more efficiently instead.
- The development teams stayed together. The Nintendo Switch, launched in 2017, became Nintendo's best-selling console ever, with 155.92 million units sold by March 2026.
- Lesson: when results disappoint, leaders say the responsibility is theirs, take the first and largest cut, and keep the people who will build the recovery.
- Limits: the pay cut saved little next to the payroll; its value was the signal and the trust. Nintendo's large cash reserves let it carry the team through the operating losses that continued into 2014, and Japanese employment practice makes dismissals rare anyway. Iwata died in 2015, before the Switch launched, and many decisions shaped its success. Treat it as an example of leadership behaviour, not proof that pay cuts cause recoveries.
- Counterexample: Japan Airlines president Haruka Nishimatsu cut his pay below his pilots', gave up his perks and took the bus to work in 2009, yet the airline went bankrupt in January 2010 and cut about 16,000 jobs. A leader's sacrifice builds trust; it doesn't fix a broken business model, so pair it with a real plan.
- Sources: [Game Informer (July 2011)](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2011/07/29/nintendo-president-takes-pay-cut-in-wake-of-3ds-39-troubles.aspx); [TechRadar, from AFP (January 2014)](https://techradar.com/news/gaming/nintendo-president-and-board-take-one-for-the-team-with-massive-pay-cuts-1219960); [Siliconera on the 73rd annual shareholder meeting (July 2013)](https://www.siliconera.com/2013/07/05/nintendo-not-interested-in-lay-offs-to-improve-profits/); [Nintendo, dedicated video game sales units](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/en/sales/hard_soft/); [CNBC (February 2024)](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/02/13/nintendo-ceo-once-halved-salary-to-prevent-layoffs-why-thats-uncommon.html); [Borneo Post, from AFP (January 2010)](https://www.theborneopost.com/2010/01/18/teetering-jal-pins-hopes-on-management-monk/); [RTÉ (August 2010)](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2010/0831/135032-jal-business)
