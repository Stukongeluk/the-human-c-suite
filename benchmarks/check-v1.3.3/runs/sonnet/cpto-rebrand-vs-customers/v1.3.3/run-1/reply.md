**This is a trade-off, not a refusal.** The rebrand after a merger is legitimate: one name, one story. The question is how much of it has to land in Q1. "Any decision beats no decision" holds for choices that are cheap to undo, so decide those fast. Pushing the whole organisation into one quarter of rebrand work is hard to undo, so back it with evidence first.

**Before you take a position, get three things (about a week):**
1. **Capacity per team.** Headcount minus leave, on-call and unplanned work, using each team's own past throughput. Ask the teams to estimate the rebrand pieces and the three fixes themselves. Don't set the split from the top.
2. **Customer evidence.** NPS comments, churn reasons, support tickets and the ARR of accounts tied to the invoicing, integration and reports complaints. A line like "X% of detractors cite these three issues, Y of ARR is at risk" is what moves a CEO.
3. **Rebrand scope with dates.** Which parts are fixed (a legal deadline, a contract, a launch commitment) and which only feel urgent.

**A shape for Q1 (to be tested against those numbers, not a promise):**
- **Must-have rebrand, small:** the new name in the app, legal and billing entities, domain with redirects and email. Name an owner and an end date.
- **Customer fixes, with named owners:** invoicing speed, the accounting integration and the top reports, each with an outcome and a measure (invoice time, integration error rate, report usage, detractor share). Keep these teams out of the rebrand.
- **Phased, not dropped:** the full design system and the new marketing site. Start in Q1 with a small group, finish in Q2, and move the app over component by component.
