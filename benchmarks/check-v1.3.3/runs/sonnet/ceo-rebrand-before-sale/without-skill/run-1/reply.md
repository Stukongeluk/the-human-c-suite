# Board plan: stabilise, then grow profit ahead of the sale

I wrote this as the plan you take to the board. It does not endorse the three moves exactly as the leadership team agreed them. Two of them, as framed, work against the goal the owners care about. I would rather you hear that from me now than from the board in month six.

## 1. The core message for the board

Your owners want a higher exit value in about 18 months. A buyer will not pay for this year's profit alone. They will pay for **recurring revenue that stays**, and they will check that in diligence: churn, net revenue retention, NPS, and whether the people who deliver the product are still there.

Right now the evidence points the wrong way. NPS fell from +31 to +6 and churn is rising. That is what a customer base looks like while the group is still integrating four companies. A buyer's diligence team will read it as "integration not finished, revenue at risk". A profit lift bought by cutting delivery capacity and then followed by more churn would be discounted, or reversed, in the price.

So the recommendation is: **fix retention first, take cost out where it does not touch customers, and sequence the three moves instead of running them together.**

## 2. What I would change in the three agreed moves

| Move as agreed | Problem | Recommended version |
|---|---|---|
| Rebrand all four companies in Q1 | Customers are already unhappy and confused by two years of acquisitions. A rebrand in Q1 puts a visible disruption (new name, new contracts and invoices, new domains and email) on top of that, and it does nothing for NPS or churn. It also costs money and management attention at the moment both are scarcest. | Move it later. Do the legal and brand groundwork now. Launch it only after service stabilises, most likely in the second half of the year, so it is done well before the sale process and is not a change in progress during diligence. Keep the four brands visible as "part of [group]" in between. |
| A fourth restructure to remove management layers | Four reorganisations in two years is likely a cause of the NPS drop. Customers feel it as changing contacts, slower answers and dropped hand-offs. A fourth round invites the best people to leave first. In Germany it also means works council (Betriebsrat) negotiation, which takes months and is not a Q1 action. | Do not announce a fourth open-ended restructure. Do one final, designed structure change: define the target organisation once, with clear customer-facing ownership, and say publicly that it is the last one. Remove layers where the spans of control show duplication, and protect customer-facing roles. |
| End all contractor and junior roles | This is the riskiest of the three. Contractors are probably carrying delivery and specific systems knowledge. Junior roles are the cheapest capacity and your future senior pipeline. Cutting both at once reduces delivery capacity while service is already deteriorating. A buyer will see key-person risk and a thin bench. | Replace the blanket ban with a **case-by-case review**. End contractor roles where the work is stable and can be absorbed or insourced. Keep or convert contractors who hold critical knowledge. Keep a smaller, deliberate junior intake. There is a separate legal point: if some contractors work like employees, that is a misclassification risk (Scheinselbständigkeit) worth fixing on its own, which may argue for converting some of them. |

If the board insists on all three as agreed, the honest framing is that the plan carries a material risk of further churn, and the board should say so in the minutes together with the retention targets in section 4.

## 3. The plan

### First 30 days: find out what is actually happening
- **Churn and NPS diagnosis.** Break both down by company, product, segment, contract size and cause. Interview the 20 to 30 largest accounts and a sample of churned ones. Find out whether the drop comes from integration issues (billing, support hand-offs, product changes), service quality, price, or competitors. Without this, every cost decision is a guess.
- **Revenue at risk.** List contracts renewing in the next 18 months, with a risk rating. This is the number the board and the buyer will care about most.
- **Cost and capacity baseline.** Where the money actually goes, per company: people, contractors, tools, overlapping systems and duplicate functions. Where contractors and juniors sit and what they deliver.
- **Key people and key knowledge.** Who holds critical systems, customer relationships and know-how. Put retention arrangements in place for them now.
- **Legal and works council.** Get German employment counsel and (if one exists) the works council in the room before anything is announced. Check the works council structure across the four entities, and whether any entity merges or changes employer, because that has its own rules on transfers of employees.

### Months 2 to 6: stop the bleeding and take out the easy cost
- **Customer recovery programme.** A named executive owner for every top account. Fix the top three causes found in the diagnosis. Report NPS and churn monthly to the board.
- **One front door for customers.** A single support entry point, one escalation path, and one invoicing experience across the four companies. This is the integration work customers actually notice, and it is worth more than the logo.
- **Cost out that does not touch customers.** Consolidate tools, licences, offices, duplicated back-office functions and supplier contracts. Procurement and systems consolidation usually give the profit lift with the lowest retention risk.
- **Pricing and contract hygiene.** Review renewals, indexation clauses and discounting inherited from the four companies. Pricing is often the fastest profit lever in a post-merger group.
- **The one final structure change.** Design it carefully, run it through the works council properly, and announce it once with a clear rationale. Avoid layered or repeated announcements.
- **Contractor and junior review.** Decide per role, using the baseline. Plan knowledge transfer before any contractor leaves.

### Months 6 to 12: build the story a buyer pays for
- **Rebrand launch**, when service is stable, with a customer communication plan, a transition period for old names, and a checklist for contracts, invoices, domains, email, certifications and legal entities.
- **Show a retention trend.** Two or three consecutive quarters of improving NPS and falling churn matter more to a buyer than any single result.
- **Product and platform roadmap.** One coherent roadmap across the four product lines, so the buyer sees one business and not four.
- **Margin improvement visible in the numbers**, with a bridge that shows what was a real, repeatable saving and what was one-off.

### Months 12 to 18: sale readiness
- Clean reporting: ARR or recurring revenue, retention cohorts, NPS, margin by line, all on one definition across the group.
- Vendor due diligence pack, management presentations, and a stable leadership team.
- No major reorganisation in the final 6 to 9 months.

## 4. Targets and how the board can hold me to them

These are proposed targets. I would set the actual numbers after the 30-day diagnosis, and I will bring them back to the board then.

- NPS: stop the decline in the first two quarters and show recovery after that. The aim is to recover a large part of the drop before the sale process starts.
- Gross revenue churn and net revenue retention: set a baseline in month 1, a ceiling for churn, and a quarterly trend.
- Revenue at risk in the next-18-month renewal book, tracked monthly.
- Voluntary attrition among key people and customer-facing staff.
- Cost savings delivered, split into one-off and recurring.
- Delivery health: on-time delivery and support response times, so we see capacity problems before customers do.

## 5. Risks and how I would handle them

- **Works council and German labour law.** Restructuring that affects jobs normally means negotiating with the works council, and may involve a reconciliation of interests and a social plan. Dismissal protection rules and, for larger reductions, notification duties apply. This takes months, so the timeline above assumes it. Please have counsel confirm the specifics for each of the four entities before the board commits to dates.
- **Attrition of the best people.** The people with the most options leave first when a group announces another reorganisation. Mitigation: retention arrangements, clear communication, and one final structure only.
- **Knowledge loss from contractors.** Mitigation: knowledge transfer, handover periods, and keeping critical roles.
- **Owners expect near-term profit.** The cost programme above delivers savings from tools, vendors, pricing and duplicate functions, which carry less risk than headcount cuts. I would show the board a savings bridge with the timing of each item.
- **The diagnosis may show something different.** If the data shows churn is caused by something I have not anticipated, the plan changes. The board should expect that and agree to it.

## 6. Decisions I am asking the board to make

1. Agree that the rebrand moves to a later date, with a go/no-go gate based on service stability.
2. Agree to one final restructure, designed once, instead of an open-ended fourth round.
3. Replace the blanket contractor and junior ban with a case-by-case review and a criteria list.
4. Approve a 30-day diagnosis and a retention-first programme, with a board checkpoint at day 30 and monthly after that.
5. Approve funding for key-person retention and for specialist German employment counsel.

## 7. What I could not check and what I assumed

I have not seen your numbers. I do not know how much of the profit gap each move was meant to close, the cause of the NPS fall, how much revenue is up for renewal, or how the works councils are set up. Before you present this, please fill in:

- The profit target and the savings each of the three moves was expected to deliver. If the contractor and junior cuts are carrying a large share of the target, the board will push back, and you will need the alternative savings (tools, procurement, pricing, duplicate functions) quantified in advance.
- Headcount and cost of contractors and juniors, by company and function.
- Renewal calendar and top-account concentration.
- Whether the leadership team already announced any of these moves internally. If so, the first step is a clear message about what is being paused and why.

One suggestion on tone with the board and your leadership team: you are not reversing the team's decisions, you are sequencing them and adding a gate based on customer data. That keeps the team with you and gives the owners a plan that protects the sale price.
