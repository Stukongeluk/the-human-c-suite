# Board paper: Protecting value through the sale window

**For decision:** board meeting, week of 23 November 2026 · **Prepared by:** CEO · **Status:** proposal; figures marked [confirm] are to be filled from finance and CRM before circulation

## 1. Recommendation

Make recovering customers the first priority for the next two quarters, and re-sequence the three agreed moves so that none of them competes with it:

1. **Rebrand:** decide the single name in Q1, but do only the must-have part then (name, legal and invoice details, domains and redirects, a personal message to customers). Phase the rest after churn has turned.
2. **Restructure:** do not start a fourth restructure until we can name what the first three failed to solve. Design one final layer-removal with employee representatives, aiming to be settled by about month 9, so the buyer sees at least two stable quarters.
3. **Contractors and juniors:** replace "end all" with a role-by-role review. End contractor roles that are commodity work, with proper notice. Keep a deliberate entry-level intake.
4. **Profit:** start with duplicated spend across the four companies, pricing and churn reduction. These raise profit without weakening what a buyer pays for.

## 4. Options to reach the profit goal

Ranked from least to most harm to people. Effect figures to be sized by finance [confirm].

| Option | Effect on profit | Time to effect | People affected | Hidden costs | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Duplicated spend across four companies: ERP, cloud, licences, vendors, offices | Likely the largest early lever | 3-9 months | None directly | Migration effort | Mostly |
| Pricing and revenue: price discipline on services, cross-sell to retained customers, fewer discounts to hold at-risk accounts | Sized per segment | 3-12 months | None | Needs customer trust first | Yes |
| Stop low-value work: overlapping products, unprofitable contracts | Sized per product | 3-6 months | Some redeployment | Customer notice | Partly |
| Automation of manual service work | Sized per process | 6-12 months | Roles change | Pilot first; AI gains must be measured, not assumed | Yes |
| Hiring freeze and natural attrition, with a protected entry-level intake | Moderate | Gradual | Workload | Gaps in critical roles | Yes |
| Redeploy and reskill into customer support and onboarding | Protects revenue | 3-6 months | Positive | Paid learning time | Yes |
| Leaders first: executive bonuses tied to retention and NPS, not profit alone; perks and executive pay reduced before any frontline change | Small, a signal of trust | Immediate | Executive team | None | Yes |
| Voluntary exits | Moderate | 6-9 months | Those who choose | Wrong people may leave | Not easily |
| Involuntary reductions | Larger on paper | 9-15 months | Many | See below | No |

Involuntary reductions: consultation, notice periods and social-plan payments mean the first-year saving is well below the annual figure, and costs come first. Research by Trevor and Nyberg (Academy of Management Journal, 2008) found that a 1% downsizing was associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the following year: people we want to keep leave too. In a sale window that is the opposite of what diligence rewards. We do not propose this option now.

## 5. Human impact and legal steps

Not legal advice; counsel to confirm before any step.

- **Works councils.** Confirm which of the four companies have an elected works council (Betriebsrat) and whether there is a group or central one. Where there is one, a significant restructuring needs negotiation of a reconciliation of interests and a social plan (sections 111-112 BetrVG), and monitoring-capable tools need its agreement (section 87(1) no. 6). Bring representatives in at the problem stage, with these numbers, while they can still change the plan.
- **Dismissals.** Where the Dismissal Protection Act applies, operational dismissals need a social selection among comparable staff, not a performance ranking (section 1(3) KSchG). Larger reductions require advance notification (section 17 KSchG); errors can void the dismissals.
- **Contractors.** Review each engagement for genuine self-employment and agency-worker rules. Counsel to check before notice is given. Contractors who lose work get the same honest notice as employees.
- **Legal entities.** A name change alone is simple. Merging entities or operations can itself be a restructuring that triggers consultation, and customer contracts may need notice.
- **Who is hit hardest.** Juniors and contractors by roles ended; long-serving middle managers by layer removal; customer-facing staff by a rebrand during a satisfaction crisis. Offer coaching, paid training, internal roles and job-search support from the day of notice.

## 7. How we will know, and when we change course

| Customers and money | People |
|---|---|
| NPS by company and segment (monthly) | Regretted attrition (monthly) |
| Gross revenue retention and logo churn | Engagement pulse: "decisions that affect me are explained" |
| Time to resolve top complaints | Time to productivity for new joiners |
| EBITDA vs. plan, with savings separated into recurring and one-off | Absence and overtime |

The board reviews these monthly. If NPS has not stopped falling by the end of Q2 2027, or regretted attrition rises materially, we pause structural changes and review.

## 8. Accountability

The fall in NPS and the rise in churn happened under this leadership team, and responsibility sits with us. Executive bonuses will be tied to customer retention and NPS, and any reduction in perks or pay starts with the executive team before any frontline role is touched. We ask the owners to review whether fees or targets that favour one-year profit work against the sale value they want.

## 9. Assumptions, and what would change this recommendation

- Assumption: the NPS fall and rising churn are driven by service and integration problems, not price or product. If the interviews show otherwise, the priorities change.
- Assumption: the group is profitable and cash-positive. If cash is short, we will say so and bring forward the faster levers, still ahead of any dismissals.
- Assumption: a buyer values retention and team stability over one year's margin. If the likely buyers are purely financial and cost-led, we will test this with the owners' advisers.
- What would change this: evidence that the four brands themselves are the main cause of lost customers, which would move the rebrand earlier.
