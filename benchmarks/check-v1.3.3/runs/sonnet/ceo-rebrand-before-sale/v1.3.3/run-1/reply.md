Don't take the three moves to the board as agreed. Take a sequenced plan that fixes customer loss first, because retained customers are what a buyer pays for in 2028, and NPS going from +31 to +6 with rising churn is the thing a buyer will find in due diligence. I've drafted the full board paper: `board-plan.md`, in the same folder as this reply.

**What I recommend for each move**

- **Rebrand in Q1:** do the minimum version. That means one name, the legal entities, redirects and an "x, formerly y" notice to every customer. Phase the rest (product names, visual system, marketing) through 2027, behind the customer fixes. A new name does not fix an unhappy customer, and it competes for the same people.
- **Fourth restructure:** don't announce it in Q1. Run a six-week diagnosis first and decide at a gate in Q2 2027. Before another structure change, the board should see what the first three failed to solve. If a change is needed, do it once, with the works council from the start. Under German rules (§§111-112 BetrVG, §17 KSchG, social selection) and notice periods, much of the saving would land in 2028 anyway.
- **Ending all contractor and junior roles:** don't make it a blanket rule. Keep juniors and apprentices: they are your cheapest people and your 2030 seniors. Review contractors role by role, convert the core ones where it fits, and end only the genuinely surplus roles, with proper notice. Ending contractors still ends someone's income.

**How the profit goal still gets met:** consolidating vendors, cloud, licences and property across four companies; renewals, pricing and cross-sell; automating support toil; and hiring discipline through attrition. The plan puts leadership first: you and the leadership team say plainly that this year's result is yours, and you defer part of 2027 bonuses until churn and NPS targets are met.

**What the paper does not have, because I don't have it:** the P&L, the churn and NPS breakdown by company and segment, the contractor register, and whether each company has a works council. I did not invent euro figures. The paper lists each missing fact with an owner and a date (mid-January), and every savings number needs Finance to size it before the board decides. The legal points are flagged for your counsel to confirm; they are not legal advice.

**The risk to be honest about:** the owners want profit up, and this sequence shows less cost reduction in the first quarters than the original three moves. My view is that a buyer will discount a profit lift built on falling retention and a depleted team, but that is a judgement your owners' M&A advisers should test, not a fact.

Two questions would change the plan most:
1. Is the NPS and churn drop concentrated in one company or segment, or spread across all four? If it is concentrated, the fix and the restructure can both be much smaller.
2. Is there a cash or covenant pressure I haven't seen? If so, the cost measures move earlier.
