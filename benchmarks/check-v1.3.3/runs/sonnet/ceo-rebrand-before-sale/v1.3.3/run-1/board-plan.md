# Board paper: protecting customers and profit through the sale process

**For:** Supervisory board and shareholder representatives · **From:** CEO · **Status:** Draft for discussion; decisions requested in section 12 · **Prepared:** 20 November 2026

## 1. Recommendation

Build the plan around keeping customers, because that is what a buyer pays for in 2028 and what is leaving now. Net promoter score (NPS) fell from +31 to +6 this year and churn is rising. Of the three moves the leadership team agreed, we recommend keeping one in a smaller form, delaying one until we know what it must fix, and not doing the third.

| Agreed move | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Rebrand four companies in Q1 | Do the minimum in Q1 (one name, legal entities, redirects, an "x, formerly y" bridge for customers). Phase the rest through 2027, behind the customer fixes. |
| A fourth restructure to remove management layers | Do not announce in Q1. Run a six-week diagnosis, then decide in Q2 2027 whether a single, final structure change is needed. Works council involved from the start. |
| End all contractor and junior roles | Do not end them as a blanket rule. Review role by role. Keep juniors and apprentices; end only contractor roles that are surplus, with proper notice. |

The leadership team is responsible for this year's result. We merged four companies and customers feel it. This paper asks the board to hold us to fixing that first.

## 2. The problem

The profit goal is clear: a higher-earning company, sold in about 18 months (around mid-2028). The three moves were chosen to deliver it. But none of them addresses why customers are leaving, and all three draw on the same people who serve customers.

- NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year (source: company NPS reporting, 2026; breakdown by company, segment and customer size not yet reviewed).
- Churn is rising (rate, revenue at risk and reasons: not yet in front of us).
- This will be the fourth restructure in about two years. We have not yet written down what the first three failed to solve.

In due diligence a buyer examines churn, recurring revenue, satisfaction and dependence on key people. A year of cost savings that lifts profit while retention keeps falling is likely to be priced down, not up. That is an expectation, not a certainty; our advisers should test it with the owners' own M&A team.

## 5. The plan

### Phase 0: Diagnose (December 2026 to mid-January 2027)
- Customer review of the churn and NPS data above. Name the top three customer problems, each with an owner, a measure and a date.
- Interview 30 to 40 customers who left or are unhappy, across all four companies. Leadership team members make some of the calls themselves.
- Start the works council conversations at problem stage, with the real numbers (see section 7).

### Phase 1: Fix and consolidate (Q1 to Q2 2027)
1. **Customer fixes first.** Dedicated, protected capacity for the top three problems. Where contractors or juniors work on them, they stay.
2. **Rebrand, minimum version, Q1.** Name, legal entities, domains and redirects, contracts and invoices, an "x, formerly y" notice to every customer. Nothing that needs the customer-facing teams for more than a small share of their time. Product naming, new visual systems and the full marketing refresh follow in H2 2027, once the churn trend turns.
3. **Spend consolidation.** One set of vendors, cloud accounts and licences across four companies, and property. Post-acquisition groups often hold duplicates; Finance to quantify.
4. **Revenue.** Review renewal terms and pricing, and cross-sell opportunities between the four customer bases. Do not harmonise prices in a way that triggers churn.
5. **Remove toil.** Automate repetitive support and back-office tasks. Time freed goes to customer fixes before any headcount decision.
6. **Hiring discipline.** Replace roles on a case-by-case basis; no blanket freeze on customer-facing roles. Attrition does part of the work.

### Gate: Management layers decision (Q2 2027)
The diagnosis answers three questions: where do decisions wait, which layers add no value, and what did the previous three changes fail to fix? If the answer is a structure change, we do it once and for all, with the works council. Draft principles: remove approval steps nobody uses first, merge duplicate functions, offer redeployment and voluntary routes first, no individual performance rankings.

### Contractors and juniors
- **Juniors and apprentices:** keep. They are among the cheapest people in the group, and today's juniors are our 2030 seniors. If intake must slow, do it through smaller cohorts, not by ending existing roles.
- **Contractors:** classify each role: core work or commodity, customer-facing or back-office, holds unique knowledge or not. Convert core roles to employment where that is right for both sides (Legal to confirm status issues). End only roles that are genuinely surplus, with the notice the contract requires and honest, early conversations.

## 7. People, law and consultation

This is not legal advice; Legal and external employment counsel must confirm everything below before it is relied on.

- **Works council (Betriebsrat):** it exists only where employees have elected one. Where there is one, it has co-determination over systems that can monitor behaviour or performance (section 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG). For major operational changes in companies with more than 20 employees, we negotiate a reconciliation of interests and a social plan with it (sections 111-112 BetrVG). We will bring it in at the problem stage, while it can still change the plan.
- **Collective dismissals:** above set thresholds the employment agency must be notified before the dismissals (section 17 KSchG). Mistakes can void the dismissals. Dismissals for operational reasons need a social selection among comparable employees (section 1(3) KSchG); performance rankings are not a basis for that.
- **Supervisory board:** with more than 500 employees, a third of the supervisory board seats are for employee representatives. They should hear the plan from us before the announcement.
- **Sale process:** economic committee and information duties linked to a change of control to be checked by counsel.
- **Notice:** statutory notice periods run up to seven months after 20 years of service (section 622 BGB), so any saving from dismissals arrives later than the annual figure suggests.
- **Support for people:** coaching, paid training and job-search help from the first day of notice. Affected people hear from their own manager first, never in a mass announcement.

## 8. Leadership first

Leadership owns the result. Before anyone's job is considered, I propose that:
- the CEO and leadership team agree to defer part of 2027 bonuses, released only when churn and NPS targets are met;
- leadership perks and non-essential spend are reviewed;
- the leadership bonus scheme is linked to retention and customer outcomes, not profit alone.
This is a signal of accountability and does not replace fixing the strategy.

## 10. Risks and early warnings

A pre-mortem for the plan: "twelve months from now it failed." Likely reasons and warnings:
- Churn is bigger than we think: more than one company's NPS keeps falling after Q1. Early warning: monthly cohort churn.
- Rebrand eats the capacity: customer fix dates slip. Early warning: delivery of the top three fixes.
- Owners want faster profit: cost savings lag. Early warning: savings vs forecast; we bring forward vendor and revenue measures first.
- Key people leave after rumours: Early warning: regretted attrition. We communicate dates and what is open versus decided.

## 11. Assumptions, and what would change my mind

- Assumption: churn has causes we can fix in 2027 (service, integration, product gaps). If the data shows customers leaving for price or because of the market, the cost side moves forward.
- Assumption: a buyer will weigh retention and team strength more than one year of cost-cutting. If the owners' advisers say otherwise, we should hear that now.
- Assumption: the cost base has duplicates worth removing before touching jobs. If Finance shows otherwise, the restructure gate may come earlier.
- If the group is in a cash or covenant crisis I have not seen, this paper changes: say so and we re-plan.
