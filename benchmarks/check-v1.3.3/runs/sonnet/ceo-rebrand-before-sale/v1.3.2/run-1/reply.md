I would not take the three moves to the board as one package starting in Q1. I would take a sequenced 18-month plan that fixes the customer experience first, takes out duplicate cost customers don't feel, and treats the rebrand and the structural changes as dated board gates. The reason is the number you gave me: NPS fell from +31 to +6 and churn is rising, and a buyer pays for retained revenue. All three moves land on the customers and people who are already leaving.

The draft board paper is in `board-plan.md`, in the same folder as this reply. It has the options table, human impact assessment, phases and gates, communication plan and the measures. I had no company systems connected, so EBITDA, churn, contractor and junior numbers are marked [FILL]. Please don't present it until Finance, Customer Success and HR have filled them in.

**Why not the package as agreed**
- **Rebrand in Q1:** it spends money and attention in the quarter customers trust you least, and the old brands may be what is holding churn back. Decide it at a month-6 gate after customer research. An endorsed-brand step ("Brand, a [Group] company") is the fallback if the evidence is mixed.
- **A fourth restructure:** stacked reorganisations drive out the people customers rate. One study found that cutting 1% of a workforce was followed by a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor and Nyberg, 2008). Removing layers is a good idea if it is designed once, on spans and decision rights, with the works councils involved early. In Germany, the reconciliation of interests and social plan (§§111-112 BetrVG) and collective dismissal notification (§17 KSchG) also push first-year savings well below the annual figure, so the saving may arrive late in your 18 months. Please have counsel confirm this for each legal entity.
- **No contractors or juniors:** contractors probably hold the knowledge of the systems you are merging, and ending them still ends someone's income. Juniors are your cheapest people and your pipeline for 2029. Convert permanent roles to employment or plan handovers; keep a junior intake.

**What the plan does instead**
1. **Months 0-3:** you and the leadership team call the top 50 accounts and a sample of detractors and lost customers, and break NPS and churn down by company and segment. Start the vendor, tool, office and duplicate-system review, with a hiring freeze that exempts customer-facing and critical roles. Tell the works councils the problem and the options now, with the real numbers.
2. **Months 3-9:** one support desk, one invoice, one renewal process (pilot, then waves), aligned pricing, and the savings from duplicated spend and overlapping products. Design the organisation once, with the works councils as co-designers.
3. **Months 9-18:** roll out in waves, each stable before the next, launch the brand if the gate said go, and build the diligence pack: cohort retention, NPS trend, delivered savings, regretted exits.

If NPS and churn haven't turned by month 6 and the cost bridge still falls short, the board decides the next step with the data, including any reduction in roles. We would do that once, with consultation and paid support from the first day of notice.

**Leadership owns this.** The NPS fall happened on our watch, after four integrations we ran, and the paper says so. I would link the leadership team's variable pay to NPS and churn now, and cut it before any frontline role is touched if the gates are missed. That is a signal of trust, not a savings plan, so it doesn't replace fixing the causes.

**The owners' side, fairly put:** profit has to rise, buyers like one brand and a lean structure, and delay costs time. Those points are fair. The plan answers them with savings that come sooner and hold up better in diligence, and it keeps the layers and the single brand on the table. I cannot yet say whether it closes the euro gap, because I don't know the target.

**Two questions that would change the plan most**
- What euro profit uplift do the owners need, and by what date? Without that, the board can't judge any option.
- What do detractors and lost customers say? If they point to price or a competitor, not to integration pain, the plan shifts toward pricing and product.

**Assumptions:** the NPS fall comes from integration, the four brands carry customer trust, and buyers weigh retention over a headline cost cut. Each is testable within six weeks. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
