I'd take the board a different plan from the one your leadership team agreed: protect customers and renewals first, find savings in the duplication four companies create, and do one restructure, designed with employee representatives, instead of a fourth. The board paper is in `board-paper.md` in this folder.

**Why not the three moves as agreed**

All three reduce cost or change appearance, and none starts from why customers are leaving. NPS fell from +31 to +6 and churn is rising. Revenue that leaves now is worth more to a buyer than savings that arrive late.

- **Rebrand in Q1.** It changes what four unhappy customer bases see, is hard to reverse and does nothing about the causes. A new owner may also rename you again after the sale (my judgement; ask the owners). Use an endorsed name now, fix legal, invoicing and support behind the scenes, and decide the full rebrand around Q3 2027 once NPS has stopped falling.
- **A fourth restructure.** Three in two years is a plausible cause of the NPS fall. That is a hypothesis to test, not a finding. Pause, analyse spans and layers, and design it once. Move managers into specialist or customer roles first, then use attrition, then voluntary exits. Involuntary exits only if the numbers still don't close.
- **Ending all contractor and junior roles.** "All" is the problem. Review contractor roles one by one, because some hold knowledge you can't replace. Keep a small, protected junior intake: they are cheap and they are your 2030 seniors. Contractors still lose income, so give proper notice.

**The paper I'd bring**

- An 8-week diagnosis: NPS and churn by company, segment and driver; calls to the top 30 accounts from the leadership team.
- A savings list from systems, vendors, licences, offices and pricing, with owners and dates.
- A staged timeline to a mid-2028 sale: diagnose, pilot, expand, then show the buyer two to three quarters of stable retention.
- Leaders go first: say plainly that the NPS fall is leadership's responsibility. Tie leadership bonuses to NPS, churn and retention, and take the first reduction if targets are missed.
- Germany: ask now whether each entity has a Betriebsrat. If so, bring them in at the problem stage. Reconciliation of interests and social plan, collective dismissal notifications and social selection (not performance rankings) may apply. This is not legal advice; confirm with employment counsel.

**What I could not fill in.** I have no figures from your systems. The paper has marked gaps for revenue churn, renewals due, cost overlap, contractor and junior numbers and attrition. Finance should fill them before it goes out; the board will want a profit bridge with savings set against rebrand and severance costs.

**Before the board.** Talk privately with the leadership team first. They agreed these moves, and they need the reasons and a chance to challenge them, not a surprise.

Two questions would change my advice most:
1. Do you know why NPS fell, by company? If it is mostly one product or one acquired company, the plan narrows.
2. What exactly is the owners' profit target, and is it measured at sale or as a run-rate?

My key assumptions: the NPS fall is partly integration disruption, savings exist in duplication, and buyers discount cost-out that coincides with churn. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
