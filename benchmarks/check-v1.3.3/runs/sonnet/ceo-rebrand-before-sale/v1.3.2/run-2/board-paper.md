# Board paper: Protecting customers and profit ahead of the planned sale

**For decision by:** [board date] · **Decision owner:** CEO · **Prepared:** 20 November 2026
**Status:** Proposal for discussion. Figures marked [Finance] or [CX] are to be filled from our systems before circulation. Nothing here is legal advice; employment-law points need confirmation from German employment counsel.

## 1. The problem in one sentence

We want a higher-profit, more valuable group in about 18 months, but our NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year and churn is rising, so the first risk to profit and to the sale price is revenue leaving through the front door while we reorganise the back.

Leadership is responsible for this result. Integrating four companies in two years, with three restructures already, is a leadership decision, and the customer experience is where it showed up. This paper is about what we do next, not about who to blame.

That is my judgement, not a measured fact; we should ask our advisers how recent buyers in this sector priced it.

## 3. What was proposed, and what we found when we tested it

| Proposal | What it is meant to do | Our concern |
|---|---|---|
| Rebrand four companies under one name in Q1 | Look like one group; reduce duplication | It changes what all four customer bases see while they are already unhappy. It is hard to reverse. A new owner may rebrand again after the sale. It does not fix the reasons NPS fell. |
| A fourth restructure to remove management layers | Lower cost, faster decisions | Three restructures in two years is a plausible cause of the NPS fall (hypothesis to test, not a finding). Stacking another one on top of the last is how knowledge and customer contacts leave. |
| End all contractor and junior roles | Lower cost | "All" is the problem. Contractors may hold knowledge nobody else has; juniors are among the cheapest people we have and are the 2030 seniors. Ending contracts still ends someone's income and needs proper notice. |

All three are cost and appearance moves. None starts from why customers are leaving.

## 5. Recommendation: Option C, in four parts

**1. Find out why customers are leaving before changing anything customer-facing (next 8 weeks).**
Split NPS and churn by company, segment, product and driver. Call the top 30 accounts by revenue personally; leadership team members take accounts. Compare with the timing of each acquisition, restructure and system migration. Output: a ranked list of causes and a save plan for the accounts renewing in the next 12 months.

**2. Profit from waste and pricing first.**
Four companies means four of many things: ERP and finance systems, CRM, cloud accounts, licences, offices, insurers and vendors, and overlapping products. Target these first. Review price harmonisation and renewal terms account by account, never as a blanket increase while NPS is falling. Stop low-value projects. Automate the repeated work nobody enjoys. Output: a costed list with owners, size and date, so the board sees the profit bridge by [date].

**3. Rebrand: decouple it, gate it, and make it cheap.**
- Q1 2027: no customer-facing rebrand. Use an endorsed approach ("[Company], part of [Group]") and one shared support and service standard.
- Do the invisible work now: one legal and contract structure, one invoice format, one support entry point.
- Decide on the full rebrand in [Q3 2027], gated on NPS and churn having stopped falling, and after asking the owners whether a buyer would keep the name.

**4. Structure and people: one design, done once, with representatives.**
- Pause the fourth restructure. Run a spans-and-layers analysis (fact base, no names), then design the structure once, tied to how we serve customers.
- Bring any works councils in at the problem stage, with the real numbers, while they can still change the plan. In Germany, a works council has co-determination rights over major operational changes (reconciliation of interests and social plan, BetrVG sections 111 to 112), and collective dismissal notification rules apply (KSchG section 17). Where roles are cut, social selection applies; performance rankings are not a lawful method. Counsel to confirm for each entity, and whether entity mergers trigger transfer-of-undertaking rules.
- Delayer by moving managers into senior specialist, customer-facing or integration roles first. Use attrition and a hiring freeze on non-critical roles. Voluntary exits second. Involuntary exits only if the numbers still do not close, once, and decently, with paid transition support from the first day of notice.
- Contractors: review each role. Where work is commodity or finishing, end it with proper notice and a handover. Where the person holds knowledge we depend on, offer a permanent role or an extension. Counsel to check misclassification risk before any conversion or extension.
- Juniors: no blanket end. Keep a smaller, protected intake and pair them with seniors; the pipeline is cheap and we cannot buy it back quickly.

**Leaders go first.** Leadership team and CEO bonuses for 2026 and 2027 are tied to NPS, churn and employee retention, and leadership takes the first and largest pay and bonus reduction if targets are missed. This is a signal of trust, not a savings plan.

## 6. Human impact

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who is affected? | Managers (layers), contractors, junior roles, and customer-facing teams in all four companies. Numbers to be set after analysis. |
| What changes for them? | Role, manager, income and job security; for contractors, their contract. |
| Disproportionate impact? | Long-tenured staff in acquired companies, part-timers, people on leave, one site or one acquired company. To check before any design. |

| Customers and service quality? | The main risk. Service standards and renewal saves are protected first. |
| Legal and consultation steps | Works councils, collective dismissal notifications, social selection, contractor notice and classification. Counsel to confirm. |
| Early warning and response | See section 8. If a trigger trips, we stop the next phase and review. |

## 7. Timeline (to a sale in about 18 months, around mid-2028)

| Phase | When | What happens | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Dec 2026 to Feb 2027 | Customer diagnosis, cost baseline, works council briefed, account saves started, board agrees principles | Fact base complete; representatives consulted |
| Pilot | Mar to Jun 2027 | Savings on systems and vendors; one customer-facing fix per company; delayering design in one business | NPS decline stopped; quality and wellbeing indicators hold |
| Expand | Jul 2027 to Jan 2028 | Structure rolled out in waves; rebrand decision taken on evidence | Each wave stable before the next one |
| Settle | Feb to Jun 2028 | Show the buyer two to three quarters of stable or improving retention and NPS, and a repeatable profit bridge | Engagement and attrition back to baseline |

## 8. Measures and early warnings

Money: profit bridge against plan, revenue churn, net revenue retention, renewals saved, cost savings delivered against costs incurred (including severance and rebrand).
People: regretted attrition, engagement pulse ("I can raise concerns without fear"; "decisions that affect me are explained"), absence, junior pipeline.
Customers: NPS by company and driver, support resolution times, incidents.
Rhythm: monthly to the board. Triggers to stop and review: regretted attrition above [x]%, NPS falling two quarters running, renewal losses in the top 30 accounts.
Measures apply to teams and the business, never to ranking individuals.

## 9. Assumptions, and what would change my mind

- Assumption: the NPS fall is at least partly caused by integration churn and service disruption. Would change my mind: a driver analysis showing it is mostly price, a single product defect or one acquired company.
- Assumption: waste and pricing can close a meaningful part of the profit gap. Would change my mind: the cost baseline showing little overlap.
- Assumption: buyers discount cost-out that coincides with falling retention. Would change my mind: adviser evidence from comparable recent sales that they do not.
- If the profit target cannot be met without involuntary reductions, we return to the board with the numbers, the options already used and a plan for doing it once, with consultation.

## 10. Pre-mortem: it is November 2027 and this plan failed. Why?

The diagnosis found nothing we could act on. Savings from waste were smaller than expected. The owners lost patience. The works council process ran longer than planned. Key people left during the pause. Mitigation: first savings list within 8 weeks; monthly profit bridge; clear communication that the pause is a plan, not drift; retention conversations with critical people.
