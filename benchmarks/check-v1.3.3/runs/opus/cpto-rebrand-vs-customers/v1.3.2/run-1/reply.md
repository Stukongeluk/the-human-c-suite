Plan two protected tracks: ship the parts of the rebrand that really need Q1, and ring-fence a few teams for slow invoicing, the accounting integration and the missing reports. The trap is arguing against the rebrand. Argue about sequencing, with numbers, and give the CEO a decision to make. That takes "any decision beats no decision" at its word.

**This week, with your engineering lead and head of customer success**

1. **Put money on the complaints:** ARR renewing by June among accounts that raised one of the three issues, ticket volume per issue, churn reasons since the merger.
2. **Split the rebrand.** The in-app name, domain and marketing site are usually a few teams' work. Rolling a new design system through every screen is the expensive part: build the foundation in Q1 and apply it as teams touch screens in Q2 and Q3.
3. **Flag the overlap.** A domain change touches OAuth redirect URLs, webhooks and email sending, the same plumbing as the broken accounting integration. Fix the integration before or with the cutover.
4. **Have the teams estimate both tracks** against real capacity, with slack for incidents. If it only fits with overtime, it doesn't fit.

**Next week: the CEO, privately, before any planning forum**

One page, two options, a date for the decision:

- **A:** everyone on the rebrand, as decided. The complaints stay open until at least Q2, with €X of renewals exposed.
- **B:** core rebrand in Q1 with N teams; M teams fix the three complaints; the design-system rollout runs through Q3. The launch story becomes "new name, and invoicing that works".

A line you can use: *"I'm not asking to reopen the rebrand; I want it to land well. €X of revenue renewing by June sits with customers waiting on invoicing and the accounting integration. Can I bring you two costed options by Friday, and you choose?"*

The CEO's case is real: after a merger, one name matters for sales, staff and cost. Option B still delivers it.

**Then**

Whichever option wins, commit, tell the teams why, and review mid-quarter on the same numbers. Give the complaint teams problems, not a feature list, and agree how you'll know it worked: invoice run time, integration errors, report tickets, NPS among affected accounts.
