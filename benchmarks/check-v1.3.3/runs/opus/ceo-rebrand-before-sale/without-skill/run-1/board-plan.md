## 1. Summary

Our owners plan to sell the group in about 18 months, around [Q2 2028], and want profit up before then. I agree with that goal. The question for this board is how to raise **exit value**, which is sustainable EBITDA multiplied by the multiple a buyer will pay. The multiple depends on how safe our revenue looks.

The most important fact in the business today is that **NPS has fallen from +31 to +6 in a year and customer churn is rising.** The next three quarters will make up the trailing numbers a buyer underwrites. A buyer's due diligence will look at our churn by cohort and will talk to our customers. If that trend continues, it will cost us more in multiple and forecast than any cost programme can add in EBITDA.

The leadership team has agreed three moves: rebrand all four companies under one name in Q1, a fourth restructure to remove management layers, and ending all contractor and junior roles. **I support what they aim at: one company, a leaner management structure and a lower cost base. I recommend we change how and when we do each one.** As specified, all three add disruption for customers in the very period buyers will scrutinise. Under German labour law, two of them are unlikely to deliver their full savings in time.

The plan has three parts:

1. **Customers first, starting now.** A recovery programme for top accounts, renewals and the root causes of the NPS fall, with retention measured at board level next to EBITDA.
2. **Profit from levers that don't touch customers first:** stopping churn, pricing discipline at renewal, the non-people cost of running four of everything, services margin, hiring discipline and an individual review of contractors.
3. **The three moves, revised:**
   - **Brand:** decide the name in Q1 and use it at group level at once. Move customer-facing brands across in stages, company by company, once each company passes a customer gate.
   - **Organisation:** one final, targeted delayering where the acquisitions left duplicate layers, negotiated once with the works councils. After that, a public commitment to no further restructuring before the exit.
   - **Contractors and juniors:** a contract-by-contract review instead of a blanket exit. Junior roles stay, and junior intake is matched to supervision capacity.

## 3. How a buyer will price us

A buyer pays sustainable EBITDA times a multiple, and churn affects both. Lost recurring revenue comes straight out of EBITDA, because software gross margins are high. A falling NPS and rising churn also make the buyer doubt the forecast, which shows up as a lower multiple or a lower revenue case.

The evidence on this is strong in software. McKinsey looked at more than 100 B2B SaaS companies from 2019 to 2024. The top quartile by valuation had a median EV/revenue multiple of 24×, against 5× for the bottom quartile, with net revenue retention of 113% against 98%. Our business mixes software and services, so the numbers will differ, but the direction holds.

Two more things will happen in diligence:

- **Quality of earnings.** The buyer's advisers will separate one-off cost cuts from sustainable EBITDA. They will also test whether the savings removed capacity we need to keep our revenue.
- **Commercial due diligence.** This typically includes customer interviews or a survey. A 25-point NPS fall in the year before a sale will be one of the first questions in the management presentation.

**Illustration (not our numbers):** EBITDA of €20m at 10× is worth €200m.

| | EBITDA | Multiple | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today | €20.0m | 10.0× | €200m |
| **Path A: cost first.** Cut €3m of cost, but churn keeps removing €1.5m of EBITDA and the buyer discounts the trend | €21.5m | 8.5× | **€183m** |
| **Path B: customers first.** €2m from pricing and non-people cost, churn stabilised, trend improving | €22.0m | 10.0× | **€220m** |

Path A saves more cost and produces less value. The size of the multiple effect is a judgement the buyer will make, but the direction is not in doubt. One turn of multiple is worth a full year of EBITDA. For us, that is [€ ].

## 4. The three agreed moves: keep the intent, change the method and timing

| Agreed move | Intent we keep | Risk as specified | Recommended version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rebrand all four companies under one name in Q1 | One company and one story for buyers and customers | A new name arrives on top of a bad customer experience, at the moment churn needs to fall | Decide the name in Q1 and use it for the group at once. Migrate customer brands in stages behind a customer gate |
| Fourth restructure to remove layers | A lean, clear management structure | The fourth disruption in two years. Savings arrive late under German process, while disruption arrives at once | One final, targeted delayering, negotiated once, then no more restructures before the exit |
| End all contractor and junior roles | A lower cost base | Loses delivery knowledge and the cheapest capacity, and raises legal risk | Review contractors one by one. Keep juniors and match intake to supervision capacity |

### 4.1 Brand

**Why not all four in Q1.** Customers who are already unhappy get a natural moment to reconsider. In logistics software a rename reaches well beyond the logo: contracts, invoices, tenders, support portals, domains, API endpoints, certificates, EDI identifiers and search rankings. Doing it for four companies in one quarter lands in the window where we need churn to turn. A strategic buyer may also retire our brand after the deal, which would make most of the customer-facing migration spend wasted.

**Recommended:**

- **Q1 2027:** decide the name and brand architecture, with trademark and domain checks. Use the name at once for the group, the employer brand, internal communication and the equity story for the sale.
- **From Q2 2027:** use an endorsed brand per company ("[Legacy brand], a [NewName] company"). After acquisitions, keeping or endorsing the acquired brands is far more common than creating a new identity for all of them (Appendix D).
- **Full migration per company only when it passes the gate:** company NPS at or above +20 in two consecutive measurements, GRR at or above target, and no top-20 account in active escalation.
- A company that has not passed the gate by [Q4 2027] stays endorsed through the sale. Buyers routinely buy endorsed-brand groups.
- **No product, domain or API renames before the exit.**
- Brand budget capped at [€ ].

### 4.2 Organisation

**Why not a fourth broad restructure:**

- **Turnover.** Research on downsizing is consistent: it raises later voluntary turnover among the people who stay (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). Survivors stay when they see the process as fair and trust management (Spreitzer & Mishra, 2002). After three rounds, that trust is thin.
- **Customer relationships.** Removing layers mid-integration often removes the people who hold customer relationships and integration knowledge.
- **German process and timing.** A restructure of this kind is likely to count as a *Betriebsänderung*. That means informing and consulting the works councils and negotiating an *Interessenausgleich* and a *Sozialplan*, which often takes several months, and up to six months more if it goes to the *Einigungsstelle*. Notice periods for long-serving staff can run to seven months on top. A restructure announced in Q1 brings disruption at once and full run-rate savings late in the sale window.

**Recommended:**

- **Diagnose by end of January 2027.** Map layers and spans by company. Look in particular for duplicate leadership roles left by the acquisitions (for example several heads of finance, HR or marketing) and managers with very small teams.
- **Design once by end of Q1 2027,** only where the data shows real duplication. Ring-fence customer-facing roles: account managers, support leads, and delivery leads on top accounts.
- **Implement through, in this order:** open vacancies, natural attrition, redeployment into customer-facing work, and voluntary agreements (mutual termination, early retirement, part-time). Dismissals come last, within one negotiated *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan*.
- **Then commit publicly: no further restructuring before the exit.** Stability becomes part of the equity story.

### 4.3 Contractors and juniors

**Contractors: a review, not a blanket exit.** We complete a contract-by-contract review within six weeks and sort every contractor into one of three groups:

- **(a) Critical knowledge or customer delivery.** Retain, or convert to employment, with a knowledge-transfer plan.
- **(b) Not critical.** End at contractual notice, on a plan per contract rather than one date.
- **(c) Status risk.** Long-running, integrated, single-client freelancers who could be treated as employees in disguise. Resolve now. This is a standard diligence finding, and the exposure includes back-payment of social security contributions.

The target reduction in contractor spend, [ ]%, is set from the review, not before it. Contractors today: [n], at [€ ] a year.

**Juniors: keep the roles.** Juniors today: [n], including [n] apprentices and dual students. The reasons:

- **Cost.** Juniors are our cheapest capacity per hour. If the work stays and moves up to seniors, the cost of each delivered hour rises.
- **Services margin.** In the services business, margin comes from leverage: seniors directing juniors. Remove the juniors and services margin falls, or prices have to rise.
- **Capacity where we need it.** The likely NPS problems (support backlog, migrations, documentation) are exactly where junior capacity helps. We redeploy juniors there.
- **The law.** In Germany a whole category of "juniors" cannot simply be ended. An operational dismissal needs the work to have gone, a social selection among comparable employees, and a works council hearing before each dismissal. Apprentices past probation can be dismissed only for good cause. A measure aimed at "junior" roles could also raise an age-discrimination question. See Appendix C.
- **The pipeline.** Juniors are tomorrow's seniors. A buyer will look at our skills pyramid and key-person risk.

Instead, we match graduate and apprentice intake to the supervision capacity we actually have.

## Appendix A: If the board prefers the original three moves, the minimum safeguards

- **Plan them as one change programme, not three.** Measures that come from a single planning decision may be treated as one *Betriebsänderung* in any case.
- **Rebrand:**
  - No product, domain or API changes.
  - Personal executive contact with each top-50 account before any announcement.
  - Contract, invoicing and support changes tested before launch.
  - NPS and churn tracked monthly through the switch.
- **Restructure:**
  - Customer-facing roles ring-fenced.
  - One announcement, not a series.
  - Counsel and works councils involved from day one.
  - The bridge shows savings only after negotiation and notice periods.
- **Contractors:** an exit plan per contract with knowledge transfer, not a single end date. A delivery-risk check on every live customer project first.
- **Juniors:**
  - A legal review before any announcement.
  - Apprentices excluded.
  - Each role shown to have no remaining work.
- **Forecast:** the board includes an explicit churn impact in the forecast and the exit model.

## Appendix C: German labour-law points for counsel to confirm (not legal advice)

1. **Works council participation.** Planned operational changes that may cause substantial disadvantages for staff (*Betriebsänderung*, §111 BetrVG) in companies with more than 20 employees require informing and consulting the works council. An *Interessenausgleich* is negotiated, and a *Sozialplan* can be enforced through the *Einigungsstelle*. Negotiations often take several months, and in the worst case the *Einigungsstelle* stage for the *Interessenausgleich* can take up to six months. Staged measures based on one planning decision can be treated as one measure.
2. **Mass dismissal notification** (§17 KSchG), assessed per establishment for dismissals within 30 days:
   - 20–59 employees: more than 5 dismissals.
   - 60–499 employees: 10%, or more than 25.
   - 500 or more employees: at least 30.

   The works council must be consulted, with written information on reasons, numbers, selection criteria and severance.
3. **Works council hearing before each dismissal** (§102 BetrVG). Without it, the dismissal is invalid.
4. **Unfair-dismissal protection** (KSchG) applies after six months' service in establishments with more than 10 employees. An operational dismissal requires that the work has gone and a social selection among comparable employees (tenure, age, maintenance obligations, severe disability).
5. **Notice periods** (§622 BGB) of up to seven months for long-serving employees; collective agreements may differ.
6. **Apprentices** (§22 BBiG): after probation, the employer can terminate only for good cause.
7. **Severely disabled employees:** dismissal requires prior consent of the *Integrationsamt* (§168 SGB IX).
8. **Age discrimination (AGG):** whether a measure aimed at "junior" roles could be challenged as indirect age discrimination.
9. **Contractors:** status risk (false self-employment; status procedure under §7a SGB IV) and agency-worker rules (AÜG). Exposure includes back-payment of social security contributions.
