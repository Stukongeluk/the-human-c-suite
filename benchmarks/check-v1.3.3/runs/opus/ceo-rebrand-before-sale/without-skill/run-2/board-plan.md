## 2. Summary

Our owners want profit up before a sale in about 18 months. That is the right goal, and this plan delivers it. But buyers do not pay for profit alone. They will price our FY2027 EBITDA, then raise or lower the multiple depending on how well we keep and grow customers. Their advisers will also interview our customers. On that measure we are heading the wrong way. NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year and churn is rising, in a period when we restructured three times in two years.

The leadership team agreed three moves. I keep the intent of each one and change the how and the when:

| Agreed move | What we keep | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Rebrand all four companies under one name in Q1 | One company, one name, in front of buyers before the sale | We decide the name in Q1 and launch it at group level. Each company's customers move over when its service metrics pass a gate. All of this finishes before the data room opens. |
| Fourth restructure to remove management layers | Fewer layers and no duplicate leadership roles | One final, targeted redesign, done properly with the works councils and done once. No broad fourth round. |
| End all contractor and junior roles | Lower workforce cost and tighter hiring control | Each contractor engagement reviewed one by one. Apprenticeships honoured. Junior roles kept where they are the cheapest way to serve customers. |

I am adding two things the agreed moves leave out:

- **A customer win-back programme.** Retention is the biggest single driver of our multiple.
- **A cost programme that starts with non-people spend.** Duplicate systems, cloud, procurement and commercial leakage are profit sources with less risk to revenue than headcount cuts.

**What we know**
- About 900 people in four acquired companies, put together over roughly two years, with three restructures so far.
- NPS fell from +31 to +6 in twelve months, a drop of 25 points. Churn is rising: [gross revenue retention, logo churn and the trend by quarter and by legacy company].

**What we do not yet know, and must know before committing numbers**

- How much revenue comes up for renewal before the sale, and when the notice dates fall.
- Our cost base by category, our contractor count and spend and what those contractors actually do, our junior headcount and where it sits, and our spans and layers.

**The hypothesis I will test first: our own changes are a major cause of the NPS fall.** In logistics software and services, customers buy relationships and reliability. Every restructure changes their contacts, their support routines and the people who know their setup. The six-week diagnosis (Section 5.1) will confirm or reject this. If it shows a different main cause, such as a price change or a forced product migration, the retention actions change. The case for doing any reorganisation once, and carefully, stays the same.

## 4. What decides our sale price

**FY2027 is the year a buyer will price.** Vendor due diligence will run around [Q4 2027 to Q1 2028]. Anything we do has to show up in FY2027 numbers and in an improving trend going into 2028. Buyers will look at four things:

1. **Quality of earnings.** Savings must be sustainable, not one-off. Run-rate savings are only credited with evidence. Restructuring costs are adjusted out but get close scrutiny.
2. **Quality of revenue.** The recurring share, gross and net revenue retention, cohort trends and customer concentration. Commercial due diligence normally includes customer interviews. At NPS +6, those interviews will hurt.
3. **Whether the integration is real.** Are we one company, or four with a shared logo? Buyers check for one leadership team, one roadmap, shared systems and evidence of cross-selling.
4. **Organisational stability.** Management depth, key-person risk, attrition, works council relations and open legal claims.

The evidence that retention drives value is strong:
- McKinsey analysed B2B SaaS companies from 2019 to 2024. The top-valued quartile had a median EV/revenue multiple of 24x, against 5x for the bottom quartile. Net revenue retention was 113% in the top quartile and 98% in the bottom.
- Bain finds that, on average, the NPS leader in an industry grows more than twice as fast as its competitors.

**The value equation.** This example is illustrative; our own figures replace it in January. Assume a buyer pays 12x EBITDA and our recurring revenue has a 75% gross margin.

| Change | EBITDA effect | Value effect at 12x |
|---|---|---|
| €1m of sustainable cost saved | +€1.0m | **+€12m** |
| €1m of recurring revenue lost to churn | −€0.75m | **−€9m**, before any change to the multiple |
| Multiple falls by one turn on €[15]m EBITDA | none | **−€[15]m** |

The implication: a saving that causes more than about €1.3m of lost recurring revenue for every €1m saved destroys value even if the multiple holds. Once falling retention also pulls the multiple down, the break-even point is far lower. If buyers price the software business on a revenue multiple, as is common for recurring software, the balance tips further towards retention.

As specified, the agreed moves are a bet that savings will outrun the churn and multiple damage they cause. With NPS at +6 and churn rising, I do not recommend that the board take that bet blind.

## 5. The plan: six workstreams

### 5.1 Win customers back (first priority, starts now)

**Weeks 1 to 6: find out why customers are leaving**
- Break down churn, downsell and NPS by legacy company, segment, product and customer tenure.
- Code every detractor comment by theme.
- Overlay the results with the dates of our restructures, contact changes, support-model changes, migrations and price changes.

- Build a renewal map: every contract renewing before [mid-2028], with its value, notice date and health score.

**Immediately**
- **Executive sponsors.** Each of the top [50] accounts, or the accounts that together make up about [70]% of recurring revenue, gets a named executive sponsor.
- **Weekly at-risk review.** I chair it for the first quarter.
- **Customer change freeze.** We do not change a customer's named contact or support model, or force a migration on them, without the account owner's sign-off. This holds until that company's NPS passes its gate.
- **Close the loop.** Every detractor gets a personal follow-up within [7] days.
- **Fix the top three causes.** Each of the top three detractor themes gets an owner, a date and a measure.
- **Protect customer-facing capacity.** Support, customer success and implementation are ring-fenced from cuts.

### 5.2 Profit programme: non-people spend first

These levers carry little risk to customers, and most can show in the P&L in H1 2027:

- **Duplicate systems and licences.** Four companies mean four of most things: CRM, ERP, HR, ticketing, monitoring. We consolidate as each contract term allows.
- **Cloud and hosting.** Rightsizing, commitments and decommissioning old environments. In Flexera's 2026 State of the Cloud survey, organisations estimated 29% of their cloud spend was wasted.
- **Procurement.** Agencies, advisers, travel, telecoms and insurance, bought once for the group instead of four times.
- **Real estate.** Office consolidation where leases allow. Moving a site can count as a Betriebsänderung (a major operational change under the Works Constitution Act), so it belongs in the works council plan.
- **Commercial hygiene.**
  - Apply the indexation clauses already in our contracts.
  - Bill out-of-scope services work.
  - Enforce discount discipline on new deals.
  - Collect overdue invoices.
  - We do *not* push broad price rises on accounts at risk.
- **Services margin.** Utilisation, rate cards and control of overruns on fixed-price projects.
- **Cross-selling across the four customer bases.** This is a growth lever, and it is also the best proof for a buyer that the integration is real.

### 5.3 One organisation, done once (replaces the "fourth restructure")

- **Baseline first.** Map spans and layers in every company, and list the duplicate roles across the four former management teams and their support functions.
- **Design rules** (draft):
  - One head per function at group level.
  - No more than [N] layers between the CEO and the front line.
  - Manager spans of [6 to 10] in most teams.
  - Customer-facing roles and named account contacts are out of scope unless the account owner agrees.
- **Order of measures.** First, redeploy people into open roles. Second, offer a fair voluntary leaver programme. Dismissals come last.
- **Process.**
  - Inform the Wirtschaftsausschuss (the economic committee of the works council).
  - Negotiate an Interessenausgleich (the agreement on how the change is carried out) and a Sozialplan (compensation for affected employees) with the relevant works councils.
  - File mass-dismissal notifications under §17 KSchG wherever the thresholds are hit.
  - Hold a §102 BetrVG works council hearing for every dismissal, and apply social selection.
  - Details are in Appendix A.
- **Realistic timing.**
  - Design finished by end of February 2027.
  - Consultation from March to [June].
  - Effective in [Q3 2027], subject to statutory notice periods of up to seven months (§622 BGB).
  - The original plan would not have delivered savings in Q1 either. German process and notice periods mean run-rate savings arrive in H2 2027 at the earliest.
- **One commitment.** We announce that this is the last structural change before the sale, and the board backs it. Without that promise, the people we most need to keep, the ones with other options, will leave first.

### 5.4 Workforce cost policy (replaces "end all contractor and junior roles")

**Contractors.** In Q1 we review every engagement and put it in one of three groups:
- **End:** not tied to critical work, or more expensive than an employee would be, with no good reason.
- **Convert:** long-running work that holds core knowledge, especially where the arrangement carries a risk of being reclassified as disguised employment (Scheinselbstständigkeit).
- **Keep:** specialist skills or temporary peaks.

No contractor leaves before their knowledge has been handed over. Planning target: cut contractor spend by [30 to 50]%, to be confirmed after the review.

**Junior roles.** No blanket end. The rules:
- **Apprentices.** Apprenticeships are honoured. After the probation period, German law allows an apprenticeship to be ended only for an important reason (§22 BBiG).
- **Pause where work can be absorbed.** Junior hiring pauses in functions where seniors and tooling can absorb entry-level work. Some entry-level tasks are genuinely shrinking with AI, so fewer junior hires in some teams is a legitimate choice, made team by team.
- **Keep where juniors are cheapest.** Junior capacity stays in support, implementation and QA, where juniors are the lowest-cost unit of customer-facing work.
- **Set the mix.** Each team gets a target junior-to-senior ratio as a design parameter.

**Hiring control.** The CEO and CFO approve every new hire outside customer-facing roles. Duplicate roles are not backfilled.

**Why not end every contractor and junior role**
- **The work stays.** It moves to more expensive seniors or stops being done, often in exactly the support and delivery functions behind our NPS fall.
- **The legal basis is weak.** "Junior" is not a reason for dismissal in itself. Apprentices are protected, and a category defined by experience can raise age-discrimination questions under the AGG (the General Equal Treatment Act).
- **It cuts our talent pipeline.** The IT skills gap has narrowed: Bitkom counts 79,000 unfilled IT roles in 2026, down from 109,000 in 2025. But in 2025 an IT vacancy still took 7.7 months on average to fill, and 37% of recent IT hires came through a dual apprenticeship. Buyers look at our bench and our succession.
- **The savings per head are the smallest in the company.** Juniors are our lowest-paid staff.

### 5.5 One brand, phased (replaces "rebrand all four in Q1")

- **Q1 2027: choose the name and the brand architecture.**
  - Test the shortlist with [15 to 20] key customers and with staff.
  - Clear the trademark (DPMA/EUIPO) and secure the domains.
- **End of Q1 / Q2: launch at group level.**
  - The new name goes on the corporate site and on investor, recruiting and group communications.
  - The legacy brands carry an endorsement line: "[Legacy], a [NewCo] company".
  - Customers hear about it from their account owner first: same people, same contract, same support, and more on offer.
- **Gate before any company's full customer-facing migration:**
  - its NPS is at least +[15];
  - its gross revenue retention has been stable or improving for two quarters;
  - no major migration or renewal peak falls in the launch window.
- **Finish line.** Every company that passes the gate migrates by [Q1 2028], before the data room opens. A company that has not passed stays endorsed through the sale. Buyers accept endorsed brands; they do not accept churn.
- **Budget.** A hard cap of €[X], spent in phases.
- **Keep the legal entity question separate.** A rebrand is not a merger of legal entities. Whether to merge entities is a tax and exit-structure decision for our advisers, with its own works council steps.
- **Make the integration real.** Buyers check substance, not logos: one leadership team, one roadmap, one CRM and ERP, one finance function, and evidence of cross-selling.

### 5.6 People, works councils, retention

- **Works councils.** We open the relationship before the plan is final. In [December], we inform the economic committee and the works councils (company-level and group-level, where they exist) of the economic position and the direction of the plan. Late or partial information is a common way for German restructurings to lose months.
- **Staff.** After the board decision, staff get one honest message covering:
  - what changes;
  - what does not;
  - the last-structural-change commitment;
  - how decisions will be made.
- **Key-people retention.**
  - We identify about [60 to 80] critical people: account owners for top customers, platform engineers and the people who hold integration knowledge.
  - Their retention awards are tied to the sale and paid in tranches, at closing and at closing plus [6] months.
- **Measure what matters.** Regretted attrition is reported monthly. Layoffs leave a cost behind:
  - In a survey of 13,683 employees, people who survived layoffs reported lower attachment to their employer and higher intention to quit than people in firms without downsizing (Maertz et al., 2010).
  - Gartner found employees' willingness to support organisational change fell from 74% in 2016 to 38%.
  - We are asking for a fourth change in two years.
- **Incentives.** Proposal: the leadership bonus is weighted [50]% on EBITDA and [50]% on gross or net revenue retention plus NPS. Then no one is paid to cut into revenue.

If the board decides to go ahead as originally agreed, it should do so knowingly, with these minimum guardrails and the tripwires in Section 8:

- **Rebrand in Q1.**
  - Contact the top accounts personally before any public announcement.
  - Make no product, interface or contract changes in the same quarter.
  - Do not launch into the notice window of major renewals.
  - Report churn weekly.
- **Fourth restructure.**
  - Run the full works council process with no shortcuts. Skipping it risks compensation claims under §113 BetrVG and dismissals that do not stand under §17 KSchG.
  - Protect customer-facing roles and named contacts.
  - Have retention awards in place before the announcement.
  - Make one announcement, not a drip of them.
- **End all contractors and juniors.**
  - Transfer knowledge before any contractor leaves.
  - Check employment status before ending long-running freelance engagements.
  - Honour apprenticeships, which cannot lawfully be ended after probation for this reason.
  - Schedule exits away from customer go-lives.
  - Quantify the work that moves to seniors.
- **Minute the risk.** The minutes should record that the board accepts the revenue and multiple risk described in Section 4.

## Appendix A: German labour-law checklist (for employment counsel to confirm; not legal advice)

- **Major operational changes (§§111–113 BetrVG).**
  - In companies with more than 20 employees eligible to vote, a Betriebsänderung requires informing and consulting the works council. It also requires an attempted Interessenausgleich and a Sozialplan.
  - A Betriebsänderung includes cutting back or relocating significant parts of an operation, and fundamental changes to the organisation.
  - The Sozialplan can be imposed by the Einigungsstelle.
  - Carrying out the change without first attempting an Interessenausgleich exposes us to compensation claims (Nachteilsausgleich, §113(3)).
- **Economic committee (§106 BetrVG).** Must be informed early and fully about economic matters, including restructuring plans.
- **Mass dismissals (§17 KSchG).** The thresholds apply per establishment, within 30 days:
  - more than 20 and fewer than 60 employees: more than 5 dismissals;
  - 60 to 499 employees: 10% or more than 25;
  - 500 or more employees: at least 30.

  Notifiable dismissals require written consultation with the works council first and notification to the Agentur für Arbeit before any dismissal. On 30 October 2025 the ECJ confirmed, in case C-134/24, that a dismissal issued before notification cannot be cured by notifying afterwards. Errors in the process remain a real risk to the validity of dismissals.
- **Individual dismissals.**
  - A works council hearing is required before every dismissal (§102 BetrVG).
  - After six months of service, the Dismissal Protection Act applies in establishments with more than 10 employees.
  - Social selection weighs length of service, age, maintenance obligations and severe disability (§1(3) KSchG).
  - Severely disabled employees need prior consent from the Integrationsamt (§168 SGB IX).
  - Special protection applies during pregnancy (MuSchG) and parental leave (BEEG).
  - Statutory notice periods run up to seven months, depending on tenure (§622 BGB).
- **Apprentices.** After probation, an apprenticeship can be ended only for an important reason (§22 BBiG).
- **Contractors.**
  - Long-running freelance engagements need a status check for disguised employment, with the option of a formal status determination (§7a SGB IV).
  - Agency workers are subject to an 18-month maximum assignment (AÜG).
- **Voluntary leaver agreements.** These need to be structured so that employees do not lose unemployment benefit for a waiting period (Sperrzeit, §159 SGB III).
- **Discrimination.** Selecting people by "junior" status or years of experience needs an AGG review for indirect age discrimination.
- **Supervisory board.** If a group company has more than 500 employees, employee representatives may sit on its supervisory board (DrittelbG). Write and share board papers with that in mind.
