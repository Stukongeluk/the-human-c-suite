The board paper is ready: **board-plan.md**. It's structured as decisions first, then the case, the six workstreams, a profit bridge, the roadmap to exit, a monthly dashboard, risks, and appendices on German labour law and the data you'll need.

Before you read it, one thing you should know: it doesn't present the three moves as the leadership team agreed them. It keeps what each move is for: one name, fewer layers, lower workforce cost. But it changes the scope and the timing, because as specified I think they put the sale price at risk.

**Why**

- **Buyers price retention, not only profit.** They'll price FY2027 EBITDA, then move the multiple based on revenue retention, and their advisers will interview your customers. NPS falling from +31 to +6 while churn rises is the number most likely to cost you value. On illustrative figures (12x EBITDA, 75% gross margin), €1m saved adds about €12m of value and €1m of recurring revenue lost removes about €9m, before the multiple moves at all. The paper shows the full calculation.
- **The timing works against the agreed moves.** A Q1 rebrand would reach unhappy customers just as they decide whether to renew. A fourth restructure in two years lands on a workforce that has had three. And in Germany it can't deliver savings in Q1 anyway: works council negotiations (Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan), mass-dismissal notification under §17 KSchG and notice periods of up to seven months push run-rate savings into H2 2027.
- **Ending all junior and contractor roles saves less than it looks.** Juniors are your cheapest staff, and their work moves up to seniors or doesn't get done, often in the support and delivery teams behind the NPS drop. Apprenticeships can't lawfully be ended after probation for this reason (§22 BBiG). Contractors may hold integration knowledge you can't afford to lose before the sale.

**What the plan does instead**

1. **Wins customers back first.** A six-week diagnosis of why customers are leaving, executive sponsors for the top accounts, a freeze on changes that customers would feel, and NPS and retention targets.
2. **Takes profit from non-people spend first.** Duplicate systems, cloud, procurement, commercial leakage and services margin, all with low revenue risk and mostly visible in H1 2027.
3. **One final organisation redesign, done once.** It removes duplicate management, protects customer-facing roles, and comes with a public promise that it's the last structural change before the sale.
4. **Contractors and juniors handled one engagement or team at a time.** Contractors are ended, converted or kept, hiring is tightly controlled, and apprenticeships are honoured.
5. **One name decided in Q1, rolled out in stages.** The group-level launch comes first. Each company's customers move over once that company passes a service gate, and everything finishes before the data room opens.
6. **Management incentives tied to retention and NPS as well as EBITDA.**
