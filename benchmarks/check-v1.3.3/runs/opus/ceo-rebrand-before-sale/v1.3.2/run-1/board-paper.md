# Ready to sell in 18 months: profit from integration and from winning customers back

**Board paper, draft for discussion** · Prepared by: [Name], CEO · Board meeting: [date], November 2026 · Decision owner: the Board

**Status:** proposal. Every measure in this paper that affects employees is a proposed measure, subject to information and consultation with the works councils, and will change where that consultation finds a better route.

---

## 1. Summary and recommendation

We want what our owners want: the highest possible exit value in about 18 months, around Q2 2028. Exit value is EBITDA multiplied by what a buyer will pay per euro of EBITDA, and the multiple is what is most at risk right now. **Our NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year, and customer churn is rising.** A buyer's commercial due diligence will phone our customers and analyse our retention by cohort. A plan that adds EBITDA while customers keep leaving can still lower the price.

The numbers behind this:

- **A cost cut that raises EBITDA by 20% is cancelled out if the buyer's multiple falls by 17%** (table 2, section 6).
- **Public software companies whose net revenue retention was below 100% traded at a median of 3.1x revenue, against 5.7x for the index as a whole: a 46% discount** (Software Equity Group, Q2 2024 data).

**I recommend that we keep the goals behind the three moves the leadership team agreed, and change how and when we pursue them:**

1. **Raise profit first from the integration of our four companies** (duplicate systems, vendors, cloud, offices and advisers), from pricing discipline and from winning customers back.
2. **Keep the single brand.** Choose the name and the brand architecture in Q1 and make us one company internally first. Launch externally once service has recovered, with a gate at the end of Q2 2027.
3. **No fourth company-wide restructure now.** Remove duplicated management through vacancies, moves into customer-facing leadership roles and voluntary options. If a gap remains at the Q2 2027 gate, we make one properly consulted structural change, once.
4. **Review contractors one by one instead of ending all contracts at once. Keep junior roles** and move junior capacity to where customers are hurting and where the integration needs hands.
5. **Leadership owns this result and goes first** (section 8).

## 3. The problem

**We need higher, credible EBITDA by the time buyers look at our last-twelve-months figures (from around Q4 2027 onwards) without lowering the multiple. Falling customer satisfaction and rising churn threaten both.**

Part of this really is a cost problem. Four companies bought in two years almost certainly still run four of many things. But the trend that moves value most is revenue quality: whether customers stay, buy more and recommend us. If we do nothing, churn compounds into lower recurring revenue, lower EBITDA and a lower multiple.

## 5. The three agreed moves: what to keep and what to change

### 5.1 Rebrand all four companies in Q1

- **The goal is right.** One company is easier to sell to, cross-sell within, hire into and present to buyers.
- **The risk as planned.** A new name lands on customers whose NPS is +6, before we have fixed what made them unhappy. It spends money and management attention on the outside before the inside works.
  - Research on mergers found that customer satisfaction at acquiring firms was 1.14% lower a year after the deal and firm value 2.43% lower. Losing familiar brands and products was among the causes.
  - Firms that kept their attention on customers rather than on financial matters lost 45% less value (Umashankar, Bahadir and Bharadwaj, *Journal of Marketing*; press release August 2021).
  - A strategic buyer may also rebrand us anyway.
- **Proposal:**
  - Decide the name and architecture in Q1.
  - Become one company internally first: one leadership team, one set of service standards, one intranet.
  - From Q3 2027, if the gate is met, move to an endorsed transition ("[Brand A], a [NewName] company").
  - Switch fully before the data room opens if that strengthens the equity story.

### 5.2 A fourth restructure to remove management layers

- **The goal is right.** After four acquisitions we will have duplicated management, and fewer layers mean faster decisions.
- **The risk as planned.** Each restructure changes customers' contacts again, and people stop putting in discretionary effort while those with options leave.
  - Gartner found that employees' willingness to support enterprise change fell from 74% in 2016 to 38% in 2022. Only 43% of employees with above-average change fatigue intend to stay, against 74% of those with low fatigue (Gartner, November 2022).
  - Downsizing a workforce by 1% was associated with a 31% increase in voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor and Nyberg, *Academy of Management Journal*, 2008).
  - In a study of business customers surveyed before and after a service provider's downsizing, satisfaction and expected retention fell, and the lost future revenue substantially offset the labour savings (Williams, Khan and Naumann, *Industrial Marketing Management*, 2011).
- **Timing in Germany.**
  - A company-wide removal of a management layer is likely to count as a major operational change (Betriebsänderung, §111 BetrVG). Where works councils exist, that means negotiating a reconciliation of interests and a social plan.
  - Notice periods run up to seven months (§622 BGB).
  - When a hierarchy level is removed, the employer must show with a concrete, workable forecast how the remaining staff can absorb the tasks within their normal hours. Otherwise the dismissals are unjustified (Federal Labour Court, 24 May 2012, 2 AZR 124/11).
  - Our estimate is that savings would arrive in the second half of 2027 at the earliest, with the one-off costs earlier.
- **Proposal:**
  - No new company-wide restructure now.
  - Map layers and spans across the group.
  - Where duplication from the acquisitions is clear, leave management vacancies unfilled and offer managers moves into customer-facing leadership roles (key accounts, customer success, implementation). Offer voluntary options such as part-time work.
  - Decide at the Q2 2027 gate whether one consulted structural change is still needed.

### 5.3 End all contractor and junior roles

- **The goal is partly right.** We should depend less on external staff for core work, and we should pay only for work that matters.
- **Risks as planned:**
  - **Delivery.** In a group built from acquisitions, contractors often hold knowledge of older systems, integrations and customer implementations. Ending every contract at once, while churn rises, removes capacity exactly where customers feel it. The CTO will check which systems have a contractor as one of their few maintainers.
  - **Due diligence.** Long-term freelancers who work like employees are a false self-employment risk (Scheinselbstständigkeit) that buyers check. Social security contributions can be claimed back for four years, or thirty if they were withheld intentionally (§25 SGB IV), and managing directors can face personal criminal liability (§266a StGB). Ending the contracts doesn't remove past exposure; reviewing their status does.
  - **Juniors.** Juniors are among our least expensive staff, so the saving is small, and they are the seniors we would otherwise have to buy in at a premium by 2029.
  - **Legal.** After the probation period, apprentices can only be dismissed for good cause (§22 BBiG). Dismissals for operational reasons need a social selection among comparable employees (§1(3) KSchG), and "junior" is not a selection criterion. Choosing people by seniority level also risks claims of indirect age discrimination (AGG).
- **Proposal:**
  - **Contractors.** A review of every engagement within six weeks:
    - core work done long-term: offer employment
    - duplicated or low-value work: end with fair notice and a proper handover
    - specialist, short-term work: keep, with an end date
  - **Juniors.** Keep the roles. Move junior capacity into support, implementation, data migration and testing for the systems consolidation, each person with a named mentor.

**The view from each seat:**

- **Customers.** Option A changes their name, their contacts and their delivery team in the same year their satisfaction collapsed. Option B gives them a named executive and fixes first.
- **Finance.** Option A's savings look larger but arrive late, and the one-off costs come out of cash before the exit. Option B starts sooner, and buyers accept it more readily as run-rate.
- **Operations.** Option A removes the capacity that handles escalations. Option B protects it until retention turns.
- **Technology.** Systems consolidation is the largest integration saving, and it needs the people who know the old systems, often contractors, until the migration is done.
- **People.** Option A is a fourth reorganisation and a signal that early-career people have no future here. Option B asks for change once, with reasons.

**Table 2. How far the multiple can fall before a cost cut stops adding value.** The break-even fall is c ÷ (1 + c), where c is the cut as a share of EBITDA. It is the same at any multiple.

| Cost cut as % of EBITDA | Break-even fall in multiple | At 8x | At 12x | At 16x |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | 9.1% | 0.7 turns | 1.1 turns | 1.5 turns |
| 20% | 16.7% | 1.3 turns | 2.0 turns | 2.7 turns |
| 30% | 23.1% | 1.8 turns | 2.8 turns | 3.7 turns |

This is arithmetic, not a forecast. For comparison, the gap between software companies with net revenue retention below 100% and the index median was 46% (Software Equity Group, October 2024). That figure covers public software companies valued on revenue; we are a software and services group, so read it as a direction, not a precise number for us. Buyers are also selective: Bain reports that software assets still sell at high prices largely because owners have been selling their best companies, while buyouts made from 2020 to 2022 are tracking below earlier returns (Bain, September 2026). A weaker asset will be priced as one.

## 8. Leadership goes first

The fall in customer satisfaction and the rise in churn are leadership's result. I am new, but I now lead that team, so the result is mine to own and fix.

**Proposed:**

- I reduce my own base salary by 20% for 2027.
- Leadership team base salaries are frozen for 2027.
- Leadership team bonuses for 2026 are cut by [half] to reflect customer outcomes.
- From 2027, at least half of leadership team variable pay depends on gross revenue retention and NPS, with EBITDA alongside them.
- I ask the board to consider a retention condition in the management incentive plan, so that management is rewarded for the exit value we create, not only for the EBITDA we report.

This is a signal of accountability, not a savings plan, and it doesn't replace any part of the plan above.

**Human impact, in brief:**

| Question | Answer |

| Who is affected? | Managers in duplicated roles (moves and changed roles); contractors in duplicated or low-value work (contracts ended with fair notice); junior staff (moved to other teams, with mentors); leaders (pay) |

| Customers and service | Customer-facing capacity protected until retention turns |
| How we'll know it's going badly | Regretted attrition or absence rising, a falling engagement pulse, or service metrics worsening. Then we slow down and ask the teams why |

**Legal and consultation steps** (to be confirmed by employment counsel; this paper is not legal advice):

- Confirm which of our entities have works councils, and whether a company or group works council is the competent body for group-wide measures.
- Involve them before decisions are final.
- Any structural change after the Q2 gate is likely to need a reconciliation of interests and a social plan (§§111–112 BetrVG), and possibly a collective dismissal notification to the employment agency (§17 KSchG). Mistakes in that notification can make dismissals void.
- Have the status of long-term freelancers assessed. Where appropriate, use the status determination procedure (§7a SGB IV).
- If the rebrand is combined with merging legal entities, that brings its own duties to inform employees. We keep the brand decision separate from any entity restructuring unless counsel advises otherwise.

## 12. Communication

- **Order:** board, then the leadership team, then the works councils (problem stage, real numbers, time to respond), then managers briefed with answers to likely questions, then staff, each told by their own manager. The top accounts hear from their executive sponsor.
- **What we say:** what is decided, what is still open and when it will be decided.
- **Nothing early:** no announcement of a rebrand or a restructure before the works councils have been involved, and no mass emails.

## 13. Assumptions, and what would change my mind

- **Assumption:** churn comes mainly from service and integration disruption, not from price or product gaps. **Would change my mind:** if the customer calls show customers leaving for a competitor's product, the priority shifts to product investment and a sharper portfolio choice.
- **Assumption:** duplicated non-payroll cost across four companies is material. **Would change my mind:** if the spend cube shows little duplication, the Q2 decision on structure comes forward.
- **Assumption:** likely buyers will price retention and service quality. **Would change my mind:** if the owners expect a buyer who values only cost synergies, the rebrand matters less and the cost case weighs more, though customers still decide what revenue the buyer gets.
- **Assumption:** contractors and juniors do work that customers and the integration need. **Would change my mind:** where the review shows a role isn't needed, we end it with fair notice.
- **Assumption:** this plan can reach the owners' EBITDA ambition within the last twelve months buyers will see. **Would change my mind:** if Finance's sizing shows a gap this plan can't close by the Q2 gate, I will bring Option C, done once and done properly.
