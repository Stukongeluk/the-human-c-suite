My recommendation is to take the board a plan that raises profit through integration savings, pricing and winning customers back. I would not bring them a Q1 rebrand, a fourth restructure and the end of every contractor and junior role. With NPS at +6 and churn rising, a buyer will price your customers before your cost base.

Two numbers carry the argument:

- **A cost cut that lifts EBITDA by 20% is wiped out if the buyer's multiple falls by 17%.** This is plain arithmetic, and it holds at any multiple.
- **Software companies whose existing customers spend less each year (net revenue retention below 100%) traded at a 46% discount to the median**: 3.1x revenue against 5.7x ([Software Equity Group](https://softwareequity.com/?p=10531), Q2 2024 public SaaS data; read it as a direction for a software-and-services group like yours).

The board paper is in `board-paper.md`. It keeps the goal behind each of the three moves and changes the method or the timing:

- **Rebrand:** choose the name in Q1 and become one company internally. Launch externally once service has recovered, with a gate at the end of Q2 2027. A study of acquisitions found customer satisfaction 1.14% lower a year after the deal and firm value 2.43% lower. Losing familiar brands was one of the causes ([Journal of Marketing](https://www.ama.org/press-releases/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-before-undertaking-an-ma-read-this-study-on-how-to-preserve-firm-value/)).
- **Layers:** no fourth restructure now. Remove duplicated management through vacancies, moves into customer-facing roles and voluntary options. If a gap remains at the Q2 gate, consult on one change and do it once. In Germany, removing a hierarchy level only holds up if you can show how the remaining people absorb the work within normal hours ([BAG, 2 AZR 124/11](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/?p=1147962)). Consultation and notice periods of up to seven months mean the savings would land around H2 2027 anyway.
- **Contractors and juniors:** review each contractor instead of ending them all. That also deals with false self-employment exposure, which buyers check and which can reach back four years, or thirty if intentional (§25 SGB IV). Keep the juniors and move their capacity to support, implementation and the systems migration. Apprentices can only be dismissed for good cause after probation (§22 BBiG), and selecting people by "junior" risks age discrimination claims.

It also says plainly that the customer decline is leadership's result and that you now own it. You take the first cut and the leadership team's pay follows. I've put in a 20% cut to your own 2027 base salary as a placeholder. Set the number you'll stand behind, but keep it in.

**The trap:** these three moves were picked before anyone diagnosed why customers are leaving, and each one lands on those customers in the 18 months a buyer will study most closely.

**Before next week:**

1. **Take it to the leadership team first, privately.** They agreed the three moves, so walk them through how this keeps their goals. Don't let them first see it in the board pack.
2. **Brief the chair or the lead PE partner one-to-one** before the meeting. Ask them directly what EBITDA they need and for which twelve months.
3. **Ask the CFO for five numbers:** revenue split into recurring and services; gross and net revenue retention by company; non-payroll spend by vendor across all four companies; contractor count, cost and tenure; and how much of the NPS drop happens within each company rather than coming from the mix.
4. **Ask HR which entities have works councils.** Say nothing to staff about a rebrand or a restructure before they're involved.

**The steelman for your owners:** cost cuts are certain and quick, and buyers pay on EBITDA. The plan answers this with the Q2 2027 gate. If integration and pricing can't close the gap by then, it brings the structural option back, consulted properly. The brackets in the paper are what Finance and HR need to fill, and section 4 lists them with owners.

**Two questions that would change my answer:**

- What do the churned customers say? If they're leaving for a competitor's product rather than because of service and integration disruption, the money should go to product, not to service recovery.
- How much of the cost base is duplicated across the four companies? If it's small, the structural decision has to come earlier.

None of this is legal advice. Have employment counsel check the German steps before anything goes to staff.
