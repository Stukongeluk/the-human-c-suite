# Value plan to exit: win customers back, raise profit, change once

**Board paper** · Draft for the board meeting of [date] · Prepared by the CEO, 20 November 2026

**Decision requested:** the direction of the 18-month plan to exit, including changes to the three moves the leadership team agreed.

---

## 1. Summary

Our customers are telling us something our cost plan doesn't yet answer. NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year and churn is rising, about 18 months before a sale in which buyers will price us on how good and how durable our revenue is, not only on EBITDA. Every €1M of recurring revenue we lose costs about €0.7M of EBITDA and, at an illustrative 12x multiple, around €8M of exit value.

The NPS fall and the rising churn are leadership's result, not our teams'. From today they are mine to fix.

I recommend we raise profit in an order that protects that revenue:

1. **Win customers back first.** A 90-day recovery programme run by the executive team, with NPS and churn targets the board reviews every quarter.
2. **Take profit from revenue quality, spend and contractors before jobs.** That means pricing discipline, the duplicated systems and vendors of four companies, a contractor-by-contractor review, and a hiring freeze with exceptions for customer-facing roles. Most of this lands in 2027, inside the period a buyer will study, and none of it needs a social plan.
3. **Change the three agreed moves:**
   - **Rebrand:** in two steps. Adopt and legally clear the group name in Q1 2027. Decide on the customer-facing switch at the Q3 2027 board, and only once customer recovery is visible.
   - **Fourth restructure:** not in 2027. Reduce management layers as managers leave or move into customer-facing roles, not through another company-wide reorganisation.
   - **Contractors and juniors:** review contractors one by one (end, convert or keep). Keep our junior employees and apprentices and put them on the customer backlog.
4. **Leaders go first.** The executive team, starting with me, gives up its 2026 bonus. 2027 bonuses are weighted on retention and customer outcomes as well as EBITDA.
5. **Two checkpoints.** At the Q2 2027 board (Gate 1), if the EBITDA run-rate is behind the bridge, I will bring one targeted structural proposal: voluntary measures first, designed with the works councils. At the Q3 2027 board (Gate 2), we decide on the brand.

I will bring a sized EBITDA bridge, built from our systems of record, to the January board.

**Decisions requested today**
- (a) Endorse the direction above.
- (b) Approve the two-step brand plan.
- (c) Approve the executive pay changes in section 7.
- (d) Agree the measures, review rhythm and gates in section 10.

## 3. The three agreed moves, tested

Each of the three moves has a sound aim. I tested them against our customer data, German process and timelines, and what buyers look at. On that basis I recommend changing two of them and narrowing the third.

### 3.1 Rebrand all four companies in Q1 2027

**The aim is right:** one group, one story for buyers, and cross-selling across the four portfolios.

**The timing is wrong.**
- **Customers live through a rebrand.** Ours are already unhappy, and a new name on invoices, portals and contracts would reach them when they are least patient. In mergers, losing customers is a major reason deals disappoint. Bain cites a bank that lost 20% of its customers in the first year after an acquisition, and recommends putting retention targets into the deal model and checking every integration decision for its effect on customers (Bain, 2011).
- **Our software runs inside our customers' operations.** If domains, certificates, API endpoints, EDI identifiers or email-sender domains change along with the brand, customer integrations can break. Those identifiers stay fixed whatever the name.
- **A legal merger would be a bigger project.** If the rebrand comes with merging legal entities or operations, the works councils have a say (§111 BetrVG covers mergers of operations), and every customer contract may need to be transferred.
- **Q1 starts in six weeks.** Choosing a name, clearing the trademark and waiting out the three-month opposition period after an EU trade mark application is published leave little room for a clean launch in Q1.
- **Cost:** a full customer-facing rebrand of four companies is an estimated €1–2.5M one-off, including internal time (estimate; agency and legal quotes needed). Almost all of it would hit 2027 EBITDA.

**Recommendation: do it in two steps.**
- **Q1 2027:** choose and clear the group name. Use it for the group, meaning owners, buyers, recruiting, the group website and internally, in the form "[Company A], part of [Group]". Product names, domains and technical identifiers stay as they are.
- **Q3 2027 board:** decide on the full customer-facing switch. It goes ahead only if relationship NPS has been +15 or better in two consecutive quarterly surveys and gross churn is falling. If not, we stay with the endorsed brands through the sale and the buyer decides. A switch during the sale process would add risk at the worst possible moment.

### 3.2 A fourth restructure to remove management layers

**The aim is right:** fewer layers, faster decisions and lower overhead.

**The method is wrong.**
- **People are tired of change.** Our people have been through three restructures in about two years. Gartner found that employees' willingness to support enterprise change fell from 74% in 2016 to 38% in 2022, and that only 43% of employees with high change fatigue intend to stay, against 74% of those with low fatigue (Gartner, 2022). Cutting a workforce by 1% has been associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). The people most likely to leave are the ones our customers know.
- **Customers rely on managers.** In a services business, managers are often the escalation route customers use. Removing them in the middle of a recovery puts the recovery at risk.
- **German process is slow.** Removing a layer is a fundamental change to the organisation under §111 BetrVG. It needs a reconciliation of interests (Interessenausgleich) with the works councils and usually a social plan, followed by notice periods of up to seven months. If dismissals follow, the Federal Labour Court requires the employer to show concretely how the work is redistributed and that the remaining staff can do it within normal working hours (BAG, 2 AZR 770/09, 16 December 2010).
- **The saving costs money first.** For every €1M of annual saving from dismissing long-serving managers, 2027 comes out about €0.2M worse after social-plan costs (section 5).
- **Buyers may not accept it as one-off.** After four restructures in about two years, a buyer's advisers may stop treating restructuring costs as one-off.

**Recommendation: no company-wide restructure in 2027.**
- In Q1, review spans and layers with the works councils, using aggregated data.
- Where spans are narrow, merge teams when managers leave or move.
- Offer managers moves into senior customer-facing roles (account leadership, delivery, solution architecture), with pay protected for 12 months.
- Don't backfill manager roles the review shows we don't need.
- Remove now the approval steps, reports and meetings nobody uses. This is quick, free and reversible.

### 3.3 End all contractor and junior roles

**Contractors: narrow the move, don't drop it.** Contractors are usually our most expensive capacity per hour, and reducing them is a legitimate saving. The risk lies in ending all of them at once, because some hold integration, migration or customer-critical knowledge. We will review every engagement by 15 January 2027 and sort it into one of three groups:
- **End** where the work is low-value, duplicated or can stop. Each contractor gets full contractual notice, a personal conversation and a reference. Ending a contract still ends someone's income.
- **Convert** long-running contractors doing core work into employees where that is cheaper. Illustratively, replacing a €140k contractor with a €90k employee saves €50k a year.
- **Keep** time-bound specialist work, with an end date and a plan for handing over knowledge.

Separately, counsel should review contractors' employment status now. Long-running, embedded freelancers can be found to be employees (false self-employment), which brings back-payments of social-security contributions. Due diligence will ask about this, and ending the contracts doesn't remove a past liability. Agency workers fall under the Temporary Agency Work Act (AÜG), including its 18-month limit.

**Juniors: keep them.**
- **They are our cheapest capacity.** Ending all junior roles saves little. Their work either moves to more expensive people or goes undone in the very queues our customers complain about.
- **The law limits this move.** After the probation period, an apprenticeship can be ended only for good cause (§22(2) BBiG). Junior employees with more than six months' service are covered by the Dismissal Protection Act. Being "junior" is not a lawful basis for selecting people for dismissal, and moving their work to others has to pass the same court test as above.
- **Seniors are expensive to buy later.** Germany still had about 109,000 unfilled IT positions in 2025, and 97% of companies with openings struggle to fill them (Bitkom, reported September 2026). A buyer will also look at our seniority mix and at key-person risk.
- **Instead:**
  - keep our current juniors and apprentices
  - limit 2027 intake to our apprenticeship and dual-study commitments
  - pair juniors with seniors on the support and integration backlog, where they add capacity at the lowest cost

Retention also moves the multiple, not just EBITDA. Among listed software companies in Q2 2024, those whose net revenue retention was below 100% traded at a median 3.1x revenue, against 5.7x for the index as a whole, a 46% discount (Software Equity Group, 2024). That is a different market from ours, but the direction holds.

Assumptions behind the table:

## 6. The plan

### A. Win customers back (owner: CEO, with the heads of customer operations)
- **By mid-December 2026:** break down the NPS fall by company, segment and touchpoint, and read the detractors' comments. Pull churn and downgrade reasons from the CRM, and support data (backlog, first response, reopen rate, P1 incidents) since each acquisition.
- **By end of January 2027:** give each of our top 50 accounts by revenue at risk an executive sponsor, who contacts them personally. Map renewals due in the next 18 months by value.
- **Close the loop:** someone who can act contacts every detractor within five working days.
- **Publish a customer promise for 2027:**
  - named contacts stay
  - one escalation route across the four companies
  - published service targets
  - no forced product migration in 2027 unless the customer wants it
- **Fix the three biggest causes of detractors**, each with a named owner and a budget, and review progress weekly in the executive team.
- **Protect customer-facing capacity:** support, delivery and customer success are exempt from the freeze. Juniors and redeployed managers add capacity here.

### B. Revenue quality (CFO and commercial lead)
- Apply price indexation where contracts allow it and service is good. Apply no increases to accounts still in recovery.
- Bring price lists and discount approval for the four companies under one set of rules.
- Find and bill work we deliver but don't invoice, and licences used beyond contract.
- Reprice or exit loss-making service contracts at renewal, with notice and help for the customer to move.

### C. Spend and integration (CFO and CTO)
- **Now:** cut unused licences and cloud waste, and review vendors and external consultants across the four companies.
- **In 2027:** consolidate duplicated internal systems such as finance, HR and collaboration tools. Systems that touch customers, such as ticketing and billing, move only after support has stabilised.
- **Before any contractor leaves,** the CTO confirms who maintains each critical system and how many people can.

### D. People and organisation (CHRO, with the works councils)
- **Hiring freeze from 1 December 2026, with exceptions.** Customer-facing roles and critical skills are backfilled with executive approval, and internal candidates come first.
- **Contractor review** completed by 15 January 2027, with individual conversations in January. No one is told by email or by a login that stops working.
- **Spans and layers review** in Q1 2027, using aggregated data and run with the works councils. Teams merge as managers leave or move, managers get role moves with pay protected for 12 months, and approval steps and reports nobody uses are removed.
- **Juniors and apprentices** stay, paired with seniors on customer work, with protected learning time.
- **Workload review** every quarter for teams where leavers are not replaced. If overtime builds up, the freeze exception applies.

### E. Brand (marketing lead)
- **Q1 2027:** select and clear the group name, and launch it at group level with the companies endorsed. No change to product names, domains or technical identifiers.
- **Q3 2027 board:** decide on the customer-facing switch against the Gate 2 criteria.

## 7. Leadership responsibility and pay

The NPS fall and the rising churn happened under this leadership team. They are our result, not the result of the people who serve our customers. I am new in the role, and from today the result is mine.

- **2026 bonus:** the executive team, starting with me, gives up its 2026 annual bonus. As CEO I take the first and largest cut.
- **2027 bonuses** are weighted equally across EBITDA, revenue retention and customer and employee indicators, so that no leader is paid for savings that cost us customers.
- **Executive perks** (cars, travel, events) are frozen and reviewed.

This is a signal that leaders carry the cost first, not a savings plan. It does not replace fixing the business.

## 8. People, works councils and the law

### Human impact in brief

| Group | What changes | What we offer |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Named contacts, one escalation route, no forced migrations in 2027, stable brands and integrations | Executive sponsors for top accounts and a closed loop on every detractor |
| Contractors whose engagements end (number known after the review) | Their income from us ends | Full contractual notice, a personal conversation, a reference, and first call for future work |
| Contractors converted to employment | They become employees | A standard contract and onboarding |
| Managers in narrow-span teams | They move to senior customer-facing or specialist roles, or their team merges when a colleague leaves | A choice of roles, pay protected for 12 months, paid training |
| Juniors and apprentices | They stay, with more customer work alongside a senior | Mentoring and protected learning time |
| Teams where leavers aren't replaced | More work for some | A quarterly workload review, with freeze exceptions where overtime builds |
| Everyone | No company-wide restructure in 2027; clarity on what is decided and what is open | A quarterly pulse survey, Q&A sessions and skip-level conversations |

### Legal and consultation steps (to be confirmed by employment counsel; this paper is not legal advice)

- **Works councils.** Confirm which group companies have works councils and whether there is a group works council. Bring them in at the problem stage, with the real numbers, while they can still change the plan.
- **§111–112 BetrVG.** Any fundamental change to the organisation or merger of operations needs a reconciliation of interests and, where people are disadvantaged, a social plan.
- **§87 BetrVG.** Changes to working hours, and any tool suited to monitoring performance, need the works council's agreement.
- **If Gate 1 leads to reductions:**
  - Mass-dismissal notifications (§17 KSchG) count termination agreements the employer initiates, and errors can make dismissals void.
  - Selection must follow social criteria among comparable employees (§1(3) KSchG), and the employer must show how the remaining staff absorb the work (BAG, 2 AZR 770/09).
- **Apprenticeships** can be ended after probation only for good cause (§22(2) BBiG).
- **Contractors and agency workers.** Honour contractual notice, check employment status, and respect the AÜG limits.
- **Supervisory board.** Check whether the One-Third Participation Act (DrittelbG) applies to any group company with more than 500 employees.

**Communication principles**
- People hear news that affects them from their own manager, live, before any wider announcement.
- Every message says what is decided, what is still open, and the next dates.
- No mass emails, no surprise lockouts, and nothing announced on a Friday afternoon or just before the holidays.
- Q&A sessions and check-ins follow two and six weeks after each announcement, with a quarterly progress update to all staff.

## 11. Assumptions, risks and what would change my mind

| Assumption | What would change my mind |
|---|---|
| Churn comes mostly from damage done during integration (lost contacts, changed support, slipping service), which we can repair in two to three quarters | The diagnosis shows a product gap, or a competitor winning on product. Then we invest in the product and have an honest conversation with the owners about the exit timetable. |
| Revenue quality, spend, contractors and attrition can deliver most of the EBITDA uplift the owners need | The January bridge or Gate 1 shows a gap these levers can't close |
| Buyers pay more for retention and a working integration than for a single brand | The owners' advisers show that buyers will pay for a single brand. Then we bring Gate 2 forward once customers are recovering. |
| Attrition continues at about its 2026 rate | Attrition falls sharply, leaving less room, or regretted attrition rises, adding risk |
| The NPS fall is real and comparable | It turns out +31 and +6 were measured differently |

**The case for the agreed moves.** The owners' and the CFO's case is real. A fast, visible cost programme gives a buyer a full year of savings in the numbers, and contractors, layers and duplication are genuine costs. Where that case holds, this plan keeps it: contractor spend comes down, layers shrink through attrition and role moves, and duplication goes. Where it doesn't hold is in dismissing juniors, a fourth restructure and a rebrand during a customer slump. In those cases the saving arrives late, the costs come first, and the moves put at risk the very revenue a buyer pays the multiple on.

**Pre-mortem: it is mid-2028 and this plan failed. Likely reasons and early warnings:**
1. **Recovery stayed a side project.** Early warning: detractor follow-up below 90% and top-account contacts behind schedule.
2. **Spend savings slipped because system consolidation ran late.** Early warning: monthly spend run-rate behind its range by March.
3. **Freeze exceptions became the norm.** Early warning: the share of leavers backfilled stays above 60%.
4. **Works councils first heard of decisions once they were finished.** Early warning: no joint sessions held by February.
5. **Key people left anyway.** Early warning: regretted attrition rising in customer-facing teams.
