I wouldn't take the three moves to the board as they were agreed. Take a plan that wins customers back first, finds the profit in spend, contractors and natural attrition, keeps your juniors, and only rebrands for customers once they are recovering.

The numbers behind that:
- **NPS fell 25 points in one year.** It dropped from +31 to +6, and churn is rising 18 months before a sale.
- **Lost revenue costs more than any cut saves.** Every €1M of recurring revenue that leaves costs about €0.7M of EBITDA. That equals roughly 13 junior salaries, and about €8M of exit value at 12x. These are illustrative figures: a 70% contribution margin and a 12x multiple.

The board paper is in `board-paper.md`.

**The trap:** the leadership team agreed three solutions before anyone diagnosed the problem. That problem, customers leaving, is the one that sets the sale price.

**What I'd do with each move**
- **Rebrand in Q1: do it in two steps.**
  - In Q1, clear the group name and use it at group level ("Company A, part of Group").
  - Don't change product names, domains, API endpoints or EDI identifiers. In logistics software, changing those can break customer integrations.
  - At the Q3 2027 board, decide on the customer-facing switch. Go ahead only if NPS has been +15 or better for two quarters and churn is falling. Otherwise let the buyer decide.
  - A full rebrand is an estimated €1–2.5M, all of it in 2027 EBITDA.
- **Fourth restructure: don't do it in 2027.**
  - Your people have been through three in two years. Gartner found that only 43% of employees with high change fatigue intend to stay, against 74% of those with low fatigue.
  - Reduce layers as managers leave, or move them into customer-facing roles with their pay protected.
  - Removing a layer by dismissal in Germany means a reconciliation of interests and a social plan, notice periods of up to seven months, and proof that the remaining staff can absorb the work within normal hours (BAG 2 AZR 770/09).
  - Each €1M saved by dismissing long-serving managers leaves 2027 about €0.2M worse off once social-plan costs are paid.
- **Contractors: review them one by one rather than ending them all.**
  - End low-value engagements with full notice and a personal conversation.
  - Convert long-running core contractors to employees where that is cheaper.
  - Keep time-bound specialists, with an end date.
  - Have counsel check their employment status now, because due diligence will.
- **Juniors: keep them.**
  - They are your cheapest capacity.
  - Apprentices can't be let go after probation without good cause (§22 BBiG).
  - Germany still had about 109,000 unfilled IT jobs in 2025.
  - Put them on the support and integration backlog that is producing your detractors.

**Where the profit comes from**
- **The levers:** pricing discipline (indexation, discount control, unbilled work), four companies' duplicated systems and vendors, the contractor review, and a hiring freeze that exempts customer-facing roles.
- **Most of it lands in 2027,** inside the year a buyer will study, and none of it needs a social plan.
- **The paper includes a fallback.** If the Q2 2027 board shows a gap, you bring one targeted proposal: voluntary first, designed with the works councils, done once.

**Human impact, in brief**
- No company-wide restructure in 2027.
- Contractors are told personally, with full notice.
- Managers are offered role moves.
- Workload is reviewed in teams where leavers aren't replaced.

**Leaders first:** the paper says plainly that the NPS fall is leadership's result, not the teams'. The executive team gives up its 2026 bonus, with you taking the first and largest cut. 2027 bonuses are weighted on retention and customer outcomes as well as EBITDA. Decide the size you'll stand behind before the paper goes out.

**Before it goes out**
- **Talk to the leadership team first, in private.** They agreed the three moves and shouldn't hear the change for the first time in the boardroom.
- **Have finance fill the bracketed figures:** recurring revenue, churn by company, third-party and contractor spend, and attrition. Also have them confirm that +31 and +6 were measured the same way across all four companies.
- **Ask employment counsel to confirm the legal steps.** That includes whether any company over 500 people needs a one-third employee supervisory board, whose members would then read this paper. This isn't legal advice.
- **Brief any works councils** in the week after the board, before staff hear rumours.

**Two questions that would change the plan**
1. **What is driving detractors and churn, by company and touchpoint?** If it's a forced platform migration or a product gap, the fix and the timeline change.
2. **Is there a cash or covenant problem, or is this purely about exit value?** A covenant squeeze would justify faster, deeper measures. Exit value alone doesn't justify cutting the people who keep your customers.

**My key assumptions**
- Churn is mostly integration damage that can be repaired in two to three quarters.
- Buyers pay more for retention than for a single name.
- Attrition gives you room to reshape without dismissals.

What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
