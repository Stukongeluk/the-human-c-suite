I recommend you don't take the three moves to the board as agreed. Take a plan that raises profit by winning customers back first. With a sale 18 months away, customer retention is worth more to your owners than the cuts. NPS fell 25 points in a year. At a 70% contribution margin, losing €1.43m of recurring revenue wipes out €1m of cost savings, and that's before a buyer lowers the multiple for weaker retention.

The board paper is in **board-paper.md**. I wrote it for the board itself, including any employee representatives on your supervisory board, so it has no notes meant only for you.

**The trap:** all three moves add disruption at exactly the moment customers are deciding whether to stay, and a buyer's due diligence will see it in the churn cohorts and customer interviews.

**What the plan does instead**
- **Customers first.**
  - Run a six-week diagnosis of the NPS drop and the churn, by business.
  - You and the leadership team call the top 50 accounts and every renewal at risk.
  - Give each of the top three causes a named owner.
- **Profit from what four companies still have four of.** Systems, licences, cloud, vendors and offices. Add discount discipline and unbilled work. This is the fastest money, and it costs no jobs.
- **Vacancies before exits.**
  - At 8–12% attrition (HR to confirm), 72–108 people leave each year.
  - Not refilling one in three of those roles outside customer-facing work removes 24–36 roles a year without a single dismissal.
- **One organisation change, done once.**
  - Start by writing down what the last three restructures did and didn't fix.
  - Design it with the works councils and decide by the end of June.
  - Finish it in one wave by October 2027, then hold the structure steady until the sale.
- **Review contractors one by one and keep the juniors.**
  - Juniors are your cheapest roles, so cutting them saves the least and empties your pipeline of future seniors.
  - Apprentices can only be dismissed for good cause once their probation ends.
  - Long-running freelancers are worth reviewing anyway: there's a status risk (Scheinselbstständigkeit), and a buyer will check it.
- **Rebrand: the name now, the migration later.**
  - Choose and clear the name in Q1, then use it for the group and the equity story.
  - In 2027, add "part of [name]" to product sites and new contracts.
  - Migrate fully once NPS has recovered.
  - Don't touch the API endpoints, EDI identifiers or invoice details customers depend on.

**Where your leadership team is right:** one name does help the story, four acquisitions very likely left duplicate management, and contractor cost is real. The paper says so. It changes the timing, scope and order of the moves, not their direction.

**Leaders go first.** The paper says plainly that the fall in customer satisfaction is a leadership result, and that it is now yours to fix. It proposes:
- 2027 leadership bonuses weighted at least half on retention and NPS
- a 12-month base-pay cut for the leadership team before any compulsory notice: 20% for you, 10% for the others

Adjust the numbers if you need to, but keep the order.

**Before the meeting**

  - EBITDA, cash and covenants
  - revenue retention by business
  - contractor numbers and cost
  - spans of control and management layers

**Two questions that would change the plan**
1. Is this a survival problem or a value goal? If cash or covenants get tight in the next 12 months, the cost measures need to come sooner and go further.
2. What do the NPS comments and churn reasons say, business by business? And did the drop start around one of the earlier restructures?
