My recommendation: don't take the three moves to the board as they stand. Take a plan that puts customers first and raises profit from third-party spend, pricing, contractor costs and natural attrition. Cut the rebrand down to the group name, hold off the fourth restructure, and swap the blanket end of contractor and junior roles for a review. The numbers behind this: NPS fell from +31 to +6 and churn is rising. Every €1m of recurring revenue you lose costs about €0.7m of EBITDA, or roughly €7m of exit value at an illustrative 10x multiple (my estimate, assuming a 70% margin). So a €2m cut that triggers €2.9m of extra churn adds nothing to the price.

The draft board paper is in `board-plan.md`. Here's what it changes:

- **Rebrand:** in Q1, launch the group name, corporate site, employer brand and one marketing budget. Products stay "[Product], a [NewName] company" until NPS and retention pass an agreed gate. Your customers are at +6, so don't make them re-learn who you are while they decide whether to renew.
- **Fourth restructure:** not before the sale. Remove unused approval steps and reports now. Let manager roles with narrow spans go as people leave. Bring one targeted proposal later, and only if the data calls for it. In Germany, removing a management layer is likely an operational change under §111 BetrVG. That means negotiating a reconciliation of interests and a social plan, then notice periods of up to seven months. The savings would arrive in late 2027, right inside the buyer's diligence window.
- **Contractors:** this is a real lever. A full-time IT freelancer costs about €155k a year at the 2026 DACH average rate, against about €90k for an employee (estimate). Review each engagement: convert it, end it with fair notice, or keep it. Long-running freelancers also carry a false self-employment risk, and a buyer will check for it.
- **Juniors:** keep them. They're your cheapest capacity and often billable. German law doesn't let you pick them out by label: dismissals need a social selection (§1 KSchG), and apprentices can only be dismissed for an important reason after probation (§22 BBiG). With 79,000 IT roles unfilled in Germany, they're also your pipeline. Freeze external hiring outside customer-critical roles instead, and move some juniors into the customer recovery.
- **Leadership goes first:** the paper says plainly that the NPS fall happened on leadership's watch. It proposes that the executive team, you included, give up the 2026 cash bonus, and that at least half of each 2027 bonus depends on retention and NPS. Change that line to what you can actually commit to, but keep it in.

The trap is that all three moves were chosen before anyone worked out why customers are leaving. Churn is the first thing a buyer will examine.
