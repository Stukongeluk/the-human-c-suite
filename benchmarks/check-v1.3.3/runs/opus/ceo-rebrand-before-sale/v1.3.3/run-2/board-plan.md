## 1. Recommendation

**Make winning back customers the first line of the exit plan, and raise profit from third-party spend, pricing, contractor costs and natural attrition before any dismissals.** Our NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year and churn is rising. A buyer will see both in due diligence and price them in. Every €1m of recurring revenue we lose removes about €0.7m of EBITDA (*estimate*, at a 70% contribution margin), which is about €7m of exit value at an illustrative 10x multiple.

This changes the three moves the leadership team proposed:

| Proposed | Recommended instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rebrand all four companies under one new name in Q1 | Launch the group name in Q1 as our corporate, investor and employer brand. Customer-facing products keep their names as "[Product], a [NewName] company". Rename products only after NPS and retention pass an agreed gate | Keeps the one-company story and the marketing saving, without asking unhappy customers to re-learn who we are while they decide whether to renew |
| A fourth restructure to remove management layers | No group-wide restructure before the sale. Remove approval steps and reports nobody uses now; reduce layers through vacancies; bring one targeted proposal only if the data shows duplication that attrition can't fix | Three restructures since the first acquisition haven't fixed the customer problem. A fourth puts its costs into 2027 and its disruption into the buyer's diligence window |
| End all contractor and junior roles | Review every contractor engagement by [mid-January]: convert, end with fair notice, or keep. Freeze external hiring outside customer-critical roles. Keep juniors and move some to the customer recovery for six months | Contractors are a real cost lever and a legal risk worth cleaning up. Juniors are our cheapest capacity, often billable, protected by German law and the senior engineers of 2030 |

Leadership takes the first cut (section 3). I will bring a fully sized profit bridge to the [January] board.

## 2. The problem

We need EBITDA of €[target]m in the twelve months before signing, on a run-rate a buyer accepts in its quality-of-earnings review, against €[current]m today. Signing is expected around [Q2 2028]. At the same time, customers are leaving faster.

If we raise profit by cutting the capacity that serves customers, we raise one year's EBITDA and lower the price. A buyer pays a multiple of the earnings it believes will last. To judge what will last, it reads churn, retention by customer cohort and key-person risk.

**How cuts and churn show up in the price** (illustrative: 70% contribution margin on recurring revenue, 10x EBITDA):

| | EBITDA | Exit value |
|---|---|---|
| A €2.0m cost cut | +€2.0m | +€20m |
| ...which causes €2.9m of extra recurring revenue to churn | −€2.0m | −€20m |
| Net | ≈ €0m | ≈ €0m, before any discount on the multiple for falling retention |

On our €[current]m EBITDA, every turn of multiple a buyer takes off for weak retention costs €[current]m of value. Among more than 100 B2B software companies analysed by McKinsey (2019–2024), the top quartile by valuation traded at a median 24x revenue with net revenue retention of 113%; the bottom quartile traded at 5x with 98%. Those are public software companies, so the direction applies to us more than the size of the gap.

**External context**

- More than a third of companies fail to achieve their revenue goals after a merger (McKinsey, 2017).

## 5. Why the three moves should change

### 5.1 Rebrand

**The case for it:** one name tells buyers, staff and the market that we are one company. It supports cross-selling and hiring, and four marketing budgets and websites become one.

**The case against doing all of it in Q1:** our customers are at +6. A new name on invoices, contracts, product screens and support channels, arriving while they decide whether to renew, gives an unhappy customer one more reason to go to tender. It also takes marketing, product and engineering time that the customer fixes need. And a strategic buyer may rebrand us again.

**Proposal**

- **Q1:** group name, corporate website, employer brand, investor story, and one marketing team and budget.
- **Customer-facing:** "[Product], a [NewName] company". Account managers and support contacts stay the same, and no customer has to sign anything new because of the name.
- **Product renaming:** only after the customer gate in section 8 is met.
- **Legal entity mergers:** only where they save real cost. Under German law the merger agreement must go to each works council at least one month before the shareholders' meeting (§5(3) UmwG), and combining establishments counts as an operational change that requires informing and consulting the works council (§111 BetrVG).

### 5.2 Fourth restructure

**The case for it:** four acquired companies often carry duplicate management and slow approvals. Fewer layers can mean faster decisions and lower cost.

**The case against it now:**

- We can't yet say what the first three restructures failed to solve. Until we can, a fourth risks the same result.
- Removing a management layer is likely a fundamental change to the organisation under §111 BetrVG. That means negotiating a reconciliation of interests and a social plan with the works councils, then notice periods of up to seven months for long-serving staff (§622 BGB) and a social selection among comparable employees (§1(3) KSchG). Realistically, savings start in late 2027, the one-off costs land in 2027, and the disruption sits in the buyer's diligence window.
- In acquired companies, many customer relationships and much of the system knowledge sit with managers. In a study of the video game industry, middle managers explained 22.3% of the variation in project revenue, against just over 7% for the creative leads (Mollick, 2012). It is a different industry, but the point carries over: managers are not overhead by default.

**Proposal**

- **Q1:** a spans-and-layers review from aggregated HR data, plus a written answer to "what did the last three restructures fail to solve?"
- **Now:** remove approval steps, reports and meetings nobody uses, and give teams clear decision rights.
- **Through 2027:** management vacancies are not automatically refilled where spans are narrow. Managers who want to move back to expert or customer roles can, with no pay cut for [12] months.
- **If the review shows duplication that attrition can't remove by Q3 2027:** I will bring one targeted proposal, shared with the works councils at the problem stage, and carried out once.

### 5.3 Contractors and juniors

**The case for it:** contractors are expensive. At the 2026 average rate a full-time freelancer costs about €155,000 a year, against about €90,000 fully loaded for a comparable employee (*estimate*: €75,000 salary plus about 20% employer costs). Some engagements have outlived their purpose.

**The case against ending them all:**

- Some contractors carry customer-critical work such as migrations and integrations. Ending them in one go slows the fixes that protect revenue.
- Long-running freelancers who work like employees can be reclassified as employees (Scheinselbstständigkeit). The client then owes social security contributions for up to four years. Agency workers may not stay with us for more than 18 consecutive months unless a collective agreement allows it (§1(1b) AÜG). Buyers check both in diligence. Both are reasons to review engagements and convert some of them, not to end them all at once.
- Juniors are our cheapest capacity: about €60,000 fully loaded on a €50,000 salary (*estimate*). Where they are billable in our services business, cutting them cuts revenue, and their work moves to seniors who cost more.
- German law doesn't let us select a group by a seniority label. An operational dismissal requires that the work actually goes away, and the choice among comparable employees follows social criteria such as length of service, age, maintenance obligations and disability (§1 KSchG). Collective dismissals above set thresholds must be notified to the employment agency in advance (§17 KSchG). After their probation period, apprentices can only be dismissed for an important reason (§22 BBiG).
- Juniors are the seniors of 2030. With 79,000 IT roles unfilled, building that capacity again later costs more, and a buyer assessing key-person risk wants to see a pipeline.

**Proposal**

- **Contractor review by [mid-January]:** every engagement is marked convert, end or keep, with an owner and a date. Where an engagement ends, it ends at the natural end of the work, with at least the contractual notice and more where we can give it. The person who manages the work tells the contractor directly. Where the work is core and ongoing, we offer employment.
- **Hiring freeze from [1 December]:** no external hires except customer-critical roles signed off by the CFO and me. Internal candidates come first.
- **Juniors stay.** [n] move to the customer recovery for six months (support backlog, onboarding, migration help), with paid training time and a mentor. Apprentice and graduate intake continues at [a reduced] level, and every signed contract is honoured.

## 11. Scorecard

Reviewed monthly by the executive team and reported to every board meeting. People data is shown for teams and companies only, never for individuals.

- **Money:** EBITDA run-rate against plan; savings realised against savings booked; cash conversion.
- **Customers:** gross and net revenue retention by company; customers lost; NPS each quarter, with response counts; support backlog and time to resolve; recurring revenue renewing before signing, and its risk status.
- **People:** regretted leavers; quarterly pulse ("decisions that affect me are explained", "I can raise concerns without fear"); sickness absence; internal moves; open customer-critical roles.
