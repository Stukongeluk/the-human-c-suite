## 3. Why the Three Proposed Moves Carry High Exit Risk

A buyer in 18 months will underwrite **Net Revenue Retention (NRR), gross margin, and key-person stability** during due diligence.

1. **Q1 Full Rebrand:** A new name does not resolve product or service failures. Launching a full rebrand in Q1 diverts engineering, marketing, and account management capacity away from the top customer issues driving NPS from +31 to +6.
2. **Fourth Restructure in Two Years:** Each restructuring creates a 3–6 month productivity dip, breaks informal customer escalation paths, and triggers regretted departures. Changing reporting lines again before diagnosing why the previous three restructurings failed to stabilise delivery will compound customer frustration.
3. **Blanket Termination of Contractors and Juniors:**
   - **Juniors** are among the lowest-cost roles in the group; cutting them yields minimal near-term EBITDA while emptying the delivery and support pipeline and signalling instability to the market.
   - **Contractors** often hold critical integration and legacy-system knowledge across the four acquired entities. Terminating all contracts simultaneously risks immediate service outages and SLA breaches.

## 5. Recommendation

**Pause the fourth restructuring and the blanket Q1 rebrand; redirect the next 90 days to stopping customer churn and capturing non-payroll EBITDA.**

Specifically, ask the Board to approve four actions starting next week:

1. **Stabilise the organisation and fix the top customer problems first.** Freeze structural changes for 6 months. Within 14 days, publish the top 5 drivers of the NPS drop (+31 to +6) and churn from support and CRM data, assign single-threaded owners, and halt low-value internal projects that compete for engineering and service capacity.
2. **Capture non-payroll profit levers in 90 days.** Consolidate duplicate SaaS licences, cloud infrastructure, external vendors, and overlapping facilities across the four acquired companies. Review pricing hygiene and cross-sell opportunities across the combined customer base.
3. **Replace blanket contractor/junior cuts with disciplined workforce management.**
   - **Keep and structure junior roles:** Pair juniors with senior engineers/consultants on customer-facing delivery and tooling rather than cutting low-cost future capacity.
   - **Audit contractors by dependency:** Map every contractor to critical systems and customer commitments; wind down only non-critical contracts with proper notice and documented handover.
   - **Hiring freeze & natural attrition:** Pause non-critical external hiring and redeploy internal capacity to customer retention.
   - **Leaders first:** Pause executive bonuses or discretionary leadership perks until NPS and NRR trend back toward target.
4. **Right-size the rebrand.** Keep only the minimum legal/commercial alignment needed for sales (e.g., "Group" endorsement on existing trusted product names) and defer a full identity overhaul until churn is under control.

## 6. Human Impact & German Legal Considerations

*Note: This section outlines operational and governance requirements for planning purposes; it is not legal advice. Employment counsel must review all specifics before any action.*

- **Works Council (*Betriebsrat*) & Co-determination:** Across four acquired German entities totalling ~900 employees, works councils may exist at one or more sites (and at >500 employees in Germany, supervisory board co-determination rules under the *Drittelbeteiligungsgesetz* should be verified). Any major operational change (*Betriebsänderung*, §§111–112 BetrVG) requires early, good-faith negotiation of a reconciliation of interests (*Interessenausgleich*) and a social plan (*Sozialplan*) *before* final decisions are implemented.
- **Dismissal Protection & Timing:** Under German law, collective redundancies above statutory thresholds (§17 KSchG) require advance notification to the employment agency (*Agentur für Arbeit*), and operational dismissals require a formal social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, §1(3) KSchG) based on tenure, age, maintenance obligations, and severe disability—not management-layer preferences. Combined with statutory notice periods (§622 BGB), involuntary reductions rarely deliver net cash savings within a 12-to-18-month exit window.
- **Communication:** If any roles or contractor engagements do change, managers are briefed first, affected people are told personally by their own manager before any wider announcement, and knowledge transfer is funded and scheduled.
