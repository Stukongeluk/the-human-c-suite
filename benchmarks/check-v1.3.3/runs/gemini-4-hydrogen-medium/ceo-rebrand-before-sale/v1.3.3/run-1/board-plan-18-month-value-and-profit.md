# Board Plan: Protecting Exit Value and Lifting Profit Over 18 Months

**Prepared for:** Board of Directors & Shareholders  
**Prepared by:** Chief Executive Officer  
**Date:** 20 November 2026  
**Decision needed by:** Next week's Board Meeting  

---

## Executive Summary

We should **not** execute the three currently proposed moves—a full Q1 rebrand of all four acquired companies, a fourth management-layer restructuring, and a blanket termination of all contractors and junior roles—in their current form. With Net Promoter Score (NPS) down from **+31 to +6** this year and customer churn rising, those three moves will reduce delivery capacity, deepen operational disruption, and erode the recurring revenue and customer retention that private equity buyers price in an 18-month exit.

Instead, I recommend a **sequenced 18-month plan** that lifts EBITDA without damaging the asset we are preparing to sell:
1. **Months 1–3 (Stabilise & Cut Non-People Waste):** Pause the fourth restructure and descope the Q1 rebrand to minimal legal/group endorsement; redirect capacity to stopping customer churn; capture immediate savings from duplicate post-merger software, cloud, vendor, and facility spend, plus pricing/scope discipline; freeze executive bonuses until NPS and churn stabilise.
2. **Months 3–9 (Focus & Selective Efficiency):** Institute a targeted hiring freeze with internal redeployment; wind down *only* non-critical contractors after knowledge transfer; retain junior staff as our lowest-cost delivery and succession pipeline; and work transparently with our works council (*Betriebsrat*, where established) on process simplification.
3. **Months 9–18 (Exit-Ready Growth):** Revisit unified branding only after NPS recovers above +20 and churn flattens, presenting buyers with four integrated businesses, clean margins, and a stable 900-person organisation.

## 1. The Problem We Actually Need to Solve

Our shareholders want higher profitability ahead of a planned sale in roughly 18 months. The leadership team's instinct—rebrand, delayer, and cut contractors and juniors—treats this purely as a short-term cost problem.

The data points to a **customer and integration problem** that is already threatening both profit and exit valuation:
- **Customer health is deteriorating fast:** NPS has dropped 25 points (from +31 to +6) in 12 months, and churn is rising. Lost recurring revenue hits EBITDA directly and compounds over 18 months.
- **Change fatigue is visible:** Three restructures in two years across four acquired companies have not resolved overlapping systems or unclear customer ownership. A fourth restructure will trigger another productivity dip and regretted departures of people who hold customer and product knowledge.
- **Exit due diligence looks beyond one-year EBITDA:** Prospective buyers scrutinise Net Revenue Retention (NRR), gross churn, NPS trends, and key-person risk. An EBITDA lift achieved by hollowing out frontline delivery and entry-level talent will result in a valuation discount at exit.
- **German labour law delays cash savings from headcount cuts:** Under German law (subject to verification with employment counsel), operational dismissals across a 900-person group involve works council reconciliation of interests and social plans (*§§ 111–112 BetrVG* where a works council exists), advance agency notifications (*§ 17 KSchG*), mandatory social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, *§ 1(3) KSchG*—meaning we cannot selectively target "juniors" by age or tenure), and statutory notice periods (*§ 622 BGB*). Severance and legal costs hit cash upfront, while net savings arrive months later.

*Note on leadership accountability:* Leadership owns the current NPS decline and the failure of the first three restructures to resolve post-merger friction. Before asking teams for further sacrifice, executive variable pay and perks should be the first items tied to—or withheld against—churn and NPS recovery.

## 4. The Recommended 18-Month Plan

### Phase 1: Stabilise Customers & Capture Non-People Savings (Months 1–3)
- **Leadership accountability first:** Freeze executive perks/discretionary travel and tie executive bonuses to three shared metrics: NPS recovery, churn reduction, and EBITDA.
- **Stop the churn bleed:** Put a 90-day hold on internal structural reorganisations. Assign clear single-point account ownership for our top 50 accounts and dedicate cross-entity engineering/support capacity to the top three customer pain points.
- **Descope the Q1 rebrand:** Replace the full Q1 rebrand with a minimal, low-cost group endorsement where legally or commercially required (e.g., unified contracting/invoicing). Defer the full brand overhaul until NPS is back above +20.
- **Non-people cost discipline:** Audit and consolidate duplicate software licences, cloud environments, external marketing/consulting agencies, and underused office leases across the four acquired companies.
- **Commercial & pricing hygiene:** Eliminate unbilled custom services work in our logistics deployments and enforce inflation/indexation clauses at contract renewal.

### Phase 2: Focus, Redeployment & Selective Efficiency (Months 3–9)
- **Hiring freeze & internal redeployment:** Freeze external hiring except for revenue-critical roles. Reskill and redeploy internal staff from low-value internal projects into customer onboarding and retention.
- **Selective contractor transition (not a blanket cut):** Review every contractor engagement for system/customer criticality. Extend or convert critical integration specialists until knowledge is documented; wind down non-critical contractors with fair notice and clean handovers.
- **Protect and leverage junior talent:** Keep junior roles. They carry lower payroll cost, bring modern tooling fluency, and form the succession bench buyers look for. Pair them with senior staff on customer support, QA, and structured delivery.
- **Works council & team transparency:** Engage any existing *Betriebsrat* early with the real financial and customer numbers—before decisions are finalised—and consult early on any productivity, analytics, or AI tooling (*§ 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG*).

### Phase 3: Scale & Exit Readiness (Months 9–18)
- **Unified brand & cross-sell (conditional):** Once NPS exceeds +20 and NRR stabilises, execute the unified brand rollout on a stable operational base to support cross-selling across the four customer bases.
- **Last-resort structural adjustments (only if needed):** If non-people savings, pricing, attrition, and redeployment do not close the EBITDA gap by Month 9, design a single, well-funded operational adjustment with the works council (*§§ 111–112 BetrVG*), honouring *Sozialauswahl*, statutory notice, and day-one outplacement/coaching support.

## 5. Success Metrics (Reviewed Monthly by the Board)

We will track financial and people/customer health side by side so we do not trade exit value for a single quarter's margin:
- **Customer & Revenue:** NPS (target: +6 to +20+ within 9 months), Gross Churn, Net Revenue Retention (NRR).
- **Financial:** Monthly EBITDA margin, non-payroll opex reduction (€), unbilled services hours reduced.
- **Organisation:** Regretted voluntary attrition (especially among senior product/account leads), critical-role vacancy rate, and employee pulse score on clarity of direction.

## 6. Key Assumptions & Questions for the Board

**Key assumptions:**
1. Customer churn is primarily driven by post-merger delivery/support friction rather than permanent market contraction. *(Would change my mind: if win/loss data shows a structural product gap that requires major R&D capex.)*
2. Duplicate vendor, cloud, and facility spend across the four acquired companies can yield meaningful Q1–Q2 savings without cutting customer-facing staff. *(Would change my mind: if the procurement audit shows these costs were already consolidated.)*
3. No debt covenant requires immediate headcount reduction within the next 90 days. *(Would change my mind: if a covenant breach is imminent and requires emergency cash preservation.)*

**Questions for discussion:**
- Are there any debt-covenant or lender deadlines in the next 6 months that constrain how we phase these savings?
- What am I missing in the buyer thesis—is unified branding a hard commercial requirement for a specific enterprise segment, or can it wait until customer health recovers?
