## 3. Why the Three Proposed Moves Put the Exit at Risk

Leadership bears responsibility for the integration friction behind our +6 NPS. Attempting to force EBITDA up through three simultaneous shocks creates severe commercial, operational, and legal risk:

1. **A fourth restructure in two years:** Teams have not had time to settle from the first three reorganisations. Removing management layers now increases span-of-control chaos, slows customer escalations, and triggers regretted attrition among the mid-level leaders who hold customer and legacy-system knowledge. In Germany, major operational changes in entities with a works council require formal negotiation of a reconciliation of interests (*Interessenausgleich*) and social plan (*Sozialplan*) under §§111–112 BetrVG, meaning disruption starts immediately while net savings are delayed by notice periods and severance.
2. **Ending all contractor and junior roles:**
   - *Contractors:* A blanket cut risks severing niche integration or logistics-domain specialists mid-implementation, worsening churn. Where contracts end, abrupt exits without handover leave senior staff absorbing undocumented work.
   - *Juniors:* Juniors typically handle frontline support, data hygiene, and routine delivery. Cutting them forces expensive senior engineers and consultants onto L1/L2 tickets, increasing burnout and slowing product fixes—while hollowing out the talent bench buyers inspect during due diligence.
3. **Rebranding all four companies under one new name in Q1:** Rebranding while NPS is +6 puts a new label on an unresolved service problem. It risks confusing loyal customers of the four acquired brands, creating vendor-portal and contract friction, and diverting commercial and marketing capacity away from retention.

## 5. Recommended 18-Month Plan

### Phase 1: Stop Churn & Capture Non-Headcount EBITDA (Months 1–3)
1. **Retention War Room (Own the +6 NPS):** Pause the 4th restructure. Assign clear single-threaded account owners for top at-risk accounts across all four companies. Fix the top three root causes of support and onboarding delays before changing org charts.
2. **Non-Headcount Cost Capture:**
   - Audit and consolidate duplicate SaaS licences, cloud/hosting environments, and overlapping third-party vendors across the four entities.
   - Review real estate footprint across legacy offices.
   - Stop low-value internal reporting and duplicate post-merger workstreams.
3. **Commercial & Pricing Discipline:** Audit unbilled custom services/scope creep in logistics implementations, enforce contract indexation, and tighten discounting.
4. **Hiring Freeze, Natural Attrition & Redeployment:** Freeze external hiring in overlapping G&A and non-critical roles. Rather than cutting juniors, redeploy and train willing junior and mid-level staff into customer onboarding, QA, and support bottlenecks.
5. **Targeted Contractor Review (Not a Blanket Ban):** Classify contractors into *commodity/non-core* vs. *critical delivery/architecture*. Wind down non-core contracts with fair notice and structured handover; retain critical specialists until internal staff are trained to take over.
6. **Leadership Accountability First:** Executive bonuses will be tied jointly to **NRR/Churn + EBITDA** (never EBITDA alone). Discretionary leadership perks and bonuses are paused until NPS and churn trends reverse.

### Phase 2: Operational Consolidation & Brand Sequencing (Months 4–9)
1. **Selective Integration:** Consolidate shared back-office processes and common tooling only after customer SLAs stabilise, running old and new workflows in parallel briefly to prevent service dips.
2. **Brand Strategy:** Replace the Q1 full rebrand with a low-cost **"Part of [Group Name]"** endorsement where helpful for cross-selling, deferring full brand retirement until NPS recovers above +20 and NRR is stable.

### Phase 3: Exit Readiness (Months 10–18)
1. **Clean Equity Story:** Present buyers with four quarters of recovering NPS/NRR, consolidated gross margins, documented platform architecture, and a stable leadership and delivery bench.

## 6. German Legal, Governance & People Safeguards

*(Note: To be verified with German employment counsel and HR for each of the four legal entities before any execution.)*

- **Works Councils (*Betriebsrat*):** Confirm which entities have a works council. Involve them early at the problem stage—with real numbers—on any operational changes (§§111–112 BetrVG) and before introducing any analytics, productivity, or AI tools that can monitor employee behaviour or performance (§87(1) no. 6 BetrVG).
- **Dismissal Protection (*KSchG*) & Notice Periods (*§622 BGB*):** Should any structural role reductions later prove unavoidable after non-headcount levers are exhausted, German operational dismissals require formal social selection (*Sozialauswahl*—weighing tenure, age, maintenance obligations, and severe disability, not performance rankings), advance employment agency notification above statutory thresholds (*§17 KSchG*), and statutory notice periods of up to seven months.
- **Supervisory Board Co-determination:** At ~900 employees in Germany, verify group-level compliance with one-third worker representation on the supervisory board (*Drittelbeteiligungsgesetz*, applicable from 500 employees).
- **Communication:** Managers are briefed first; affected teams hear changes directly from their own leaders with clear rationale and dates—never by rumour or mass email.
