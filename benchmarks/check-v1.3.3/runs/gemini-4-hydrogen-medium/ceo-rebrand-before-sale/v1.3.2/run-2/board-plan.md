# Board Memo: 18-Month Value Creation & Retention Plan

**Decision needed by:** Next week's Board Meeting · **Decision owner:** CEO · **Prepared:** November 2026

---

## 1. The problem in one sentence

Our private equity owners target an exit in ~18 months requiring higher EBITDA, but NPS has fallen 25 points (+31 to +6) and churn is rising after four acquisitions and three restructures—meaning the leadership team's proposed Q1 rebrand, fourth restructure, and blanket cuts to contractors and juniors will accelerate customer attrition and compress our exit multiple rather than build sustainable profit.

## 3. Why the current three-move proposal fails the 18-month exit test

In logistics software and services, exit valuation is driven by **EBITDA × Multiple**. Both depend on **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** and delivery reliability.

1. **Q1 Rebrand of all four companies:** Consumes cash, marketing bandwidth, and leadership attention in Q1 while delivering zero near-term EBITDA or customer relief. Changing names while NPS is +6 signals cosmetic integration while service issues remain unresolved.
2. **A fourth restructure to remove management layers:** Three restructures in two years have already disrupted customer handoffs and accountability. A fourth triggers German co-determination obligations (*Betriebsrat* reconciliation of interests and social plan under **§§111–112 BetrVG**), statutory notice periods (**§622 BGB**, up to 7 months), and social selection (**§1(3) KSchG**). Upfront severance, consultation timelines, and productivity loss mean net year-one savings are heavily delayed, while operational disruption will push churn higher.
3. **Ending all contractor and junior roles:**
   - **Contractors:** A blanket stop risks stalling active customer implementations, integrations, and support—directly worsening churn.
   - **Juniors:** Entry-level staff represent a small share of payroll. Cutting them saves little cash in an 18-month window while emptying the delivery pipeline and pushing routine work onto expensive senior engineers and consultants.

### View from each seat (Option C vs. Option B)
- **Customer:** Option C fixes onboarding, support, and account continuity first. Option B disrupts account teams during a +6 NPS crisis.
- **Finance:** Option C captures fast, clean EBITDA from duplicate IT/vendor/office spend and pricing hygiene without upfront severance cash burn.
- **Operations:** Option C gives teams 6 months of structural stability to finish integrating workflows across the four entities.
- **Technology:** Option C retains junior engineers/analysts and critical contractors to pay down integration debt rather than freezing delivery.
- **People & Governance:** Option C respects German co-determination, avoids unnecessary *Sozialauswahl* disputes, and rebuilds trust with the supervisory board (which at ~900 employees in Germany includes one-third worker representation under the *Drittelbeteiligungsgesetz*).

## 5. Recommendation

**Reject the blanket three-move package and adopt a two-stage 18-month plan: Stabilise & Capture Non-Payroll Margin (Months 1–6), then Scale & Position for Exit (Months 7–18).**

Specifically, ask the Board to approve:
1. **Defer the full Q1 rebrand** until NPS recovers above +20 and core product/service workflows are unified (optional: low-cost "Part of [Group]" endorsement only where needed for cross-selling).
2. **Freeze org-chart restructures for 6 months.** Replace layer-cutting with removing duplicate reporting, unnecessary approval steps, and conflicting post-M&A processes.
3. **Replace blanket contractor/junior cuts with a targeted spend & delivery reset:**
   - Audit contractors within 30 days: retain those on critical customer delivery or knowledge transfer; wind down only non-essential/commodity roles with fair notice.
   - Retain juniors and pair them with senior leads on customer delivery, testing, and AI-assisted tooling.
   - Consolidate duplicate cloud infrastructure, SaaS licences, external agencies, and overlapping office space across the four entities.
   - Audit underpriced legacy services contracts and stop low-margin customisations.
   - Apply a **hiring freeze** on non-customer-facing roles and use **natural attrition + internal redeployment** to shift capacity toward retention and delivery.
4. **Leaders go first:** Acknowledge plainly that leadership owns the drop from +31 to +6 NPS. Freeze executive bonuses/raises until NRR and NPS stabilise, and tie leadership incentives to **Net Revenue Retention + EBITDA**, not headcount reduction.

## 9. Assumptions, and what would change my mind

- **Assumption:** The +25-point NPS drop and rising churn are driven primarily by post-M&A disruption and delivery/support friction across the four companies.
  - *Would change my mind:* If churn data shows customers are leaving purely on price or structural market contraction rather than service/integration issues.
- **Assumption:** Meaningful duplicate spend (cloud, licences, vendors, facilities) and pricing leakage exist across four recently acquired companies.
  - *Would change my mind:* If a 30-day Finance audit shows all four entities are already fully consolidated on lean contracts and cash runway requires immediate payroll reduction.
- **Assumption:** Exiting in 18 months at an attractive multiple requires stabilising NRR as much as raising EBITDA.
  - *Would change my mind:* If the PE owners explicitly prefer a short-term cash-harvest exit over a growth/SaaS multiple.
