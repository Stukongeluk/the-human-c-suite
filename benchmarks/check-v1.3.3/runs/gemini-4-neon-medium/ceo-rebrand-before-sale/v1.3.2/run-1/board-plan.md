# Board Paper: 18-Month Value-Creation and Stabilisation Plan

**Decision needed by:** Next week's Board Meeting · **Decision owner:** CEO · **Prepared:** November 2026

---

## Executive Summary

**Recommendation:** Pause the Q1 rebrand, cancel the fourth restructure, and reject blanket cuts to contractors and junior roles. Instead, execute an 18-month **Stabilise & Compound** plan that lifts EBITDA through post-merger vendor/cloud consolidation, disciplined pricing and cross-sell, and selective wind-down of non-critical external spend—while stopping customer churn and protecting the exit multiple.

**The problem in one sentence:** After four acquisitions and three restructures in two years, our NPS has dropped from +31 to +6 and customer churn is rising; pushing a Q1 rebrand, a fourth restructure, and blanket junior/contractor cuts now will compress our 18-month exit multiple and front-load German restructuring costs faster than it improves profit.

Leadership owns this result. The NPS decline and rising churn are the direct consequence of stacking three reorganisations on top of four unintegrated businesses without giving teams or customers time to settle. Fixing profit before exit starts with stopping self-inflicted disruption, starting with executive accountability.

## 2. Why the Three Proposed Moves Threaten Exit Value

For a private equity exit in 18 months, enterprise value is **EBITDA × Exit Multiple**. A buyer running commercial, technical, and HR due diligence in 12 months will price the business on net revenue retention (NRR), churn trajectory, and organisational stability—not on one year of squeezed costs.

1. **Q1 Rebrand of all four companies:**
   - **Financial & customer impact:** Consumes cash upfront (agency, legal, UI/product changes, sales collateral) with zero EBITDA upside inside 18 months. Changing trusted regional logistics brands while NPS sits at +6 signals further instability and invites competitors into renewing accounts.
   - **Verdict:** Defer. Keep existing customer-facing brands (or light endorsement) until NPS recovers above +20.

2. **Fourth restructure in two years (removing management layers):**
   - **Delivery & retention impact:** Stacking a fourth reorganisation before the first three have settled freezes delivery for 6–9 months. Research by Trevor and Nyberg (*Academy of Management Journal*, 2008) found that a 1% workforce reduction was associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the following year—meaning we risk losing the senior engineers and account managers keeping customers from churning.
   - **German legal & timing reality** *(not legal advice; verify with German employment counsel)*: Across 900 employees, major operational changes trigger *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan* negotiations with any existing works council (*Betriebsrat* / *Gesamtbetriebsrat*) under §§ 111–112 BetrVG, advance notification to the *Agentur für Arbeit* if collective dismissal thresholds are crossed (§ 17 KSchG, where procedural errors can void dismissals), and statutory notice periods up to 7 months depending on tenure (§ 622 BGB). With ~500+ employees, one-third employee representation on the supervisory board (*Drittelbeteiligungsgesetz*) may also apply. Severance, legal fees, and productivity loss hit immediately, while run-rate savings barely land before the sell-side process opens.
   - **Verdict:** Cancel the fourth restructure. Remove unnecessary reporting and approval bureaucracy directly without redrawing reporting lines.

3. **Ending all contractor and junior roles:**
   - **Juniors:** Under Germany's Dismissal Protection Act (*KSchG*, applicable above 10 employees and 6 months' tenure), operational dismissals require social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, § 1(3) KSchG) across comparable roles based on tenure, age, maintenance obligations, and severe disability. Targeting "all juniors" selectively carries high legal and works council risk, saves comparatively little payroll, and hands buyers a hollowed-out talent pipeline in HR and technical due diligence.
   - **Contractors:** Blanket termination across four unintegrated software stacks risks breaking live customer implementations and legacy integrations, accelerating churn. External spend should be cut selectively where non-critical, with proper notice and structured knowledge handover.
   - **Verdict:** Retain junior roles and redesign their onboarding/pairing to accelerate delivery; wind down only non-customer-critical contractors cleanly.

### View from Each Executive Seat (Option C)

- **Customer / Product:** Stops brand confusion and account hand-offs immediately; focuses engineering and services on the top five pain points driving the NPS drop from +31 to +6.
- **Finance:** Captures faster, higher-certainty EBITDA from duplicate SaaS/cloud/vendor consolidation and pricing alignment without upfront severance cash burn or rebrand spend.
- **Technology:** Protects live integrations across the four logistics platforms by auditing contractor knowledge before any exit, while retiring duplicate infrastructure and tooling.
- **Operations:** Replaces a fourth org-chart reshuffle with direct removal of redundant status meetings and approval gates, freeing frontline capacity.
- **People:** Restores predictability after two years of M&A upheaval, brings works councils in early as problem-solvers rather than adversaries, and preserves the junior-to-senior pipeline.

## 4. Recommended 18-Month Plan

### Phase 1: Stabilise & Capture Clean Savings (Months 1–3)
1. **Pause the Q1 rebrand and cancel the fourth restructure.** Redirect the rebrand budget directly to EBITDA and customer stabilisation.
2. **Leadership accountability first.** Freeze executive discretionary bonuses until NRR stabilises and NPS recovers above +20; tie leadership exit incentives to NRR, NPS, and EBITDA quality (not cost-cutting alone).
3. **Post-merger non-people spend consolidation.** Audit and eliminate duplicate cloud environments, overlapping SaaS/ERP/CRM licences, redundant external agencies, and unused office space across the four acquired entities.
4. **Top-account retention sprint.** Assign stable executive and delivery owners to at-risk logistics accounts; fix the top five recurring service and software defects driving the +31 to +6 NPS drop.
5. **Selective contractor wind-down.** Map every contractor by customer and system criticality. Keep those protecting live revenue; wind down non-critical engagements with honest communication, fair notice, and documented handover.
6. **Early works council engagement.** Share the real financial and NPS numbers with any works council(s) and employee supervisory board representatives while operational choices are still open.

### Phase 2: Grow Margin & Simplify Work (Months 4–9)
1. **Pricing, packaging, and cross-sell.** Harmonise discounting across the four legacy customer bases, recover inflation on underpriced service contracts, and cross-sell complementary software modules to existing logistics clients.
2. **Remove bureaucracy without a restructure.** Cut unused reports, duplicate steering committees, and low-value internal projects; let cross-entity teams own clear customer outcomes.
3. **Targeted hiring freeze, natural attrition, and internal redeployment.** Pause non-critical external hiring. Where workloads shrink in one entity, fund paid reskilling and internal moves into growth or customer-facing roles before considering any exits. Retain junior roles with structured mentoring.

### Phase 3: Prove Momentum for Buyers (Months 10–15)
1. **Demonstrate three consecutive quarters of improving NRR, recovering NPS, and expanding EBITDA margin.**
2. **Re-evaluate brand architecture** only once NPS is back above +20—favouring a low-cost endorsed brand model over a disruptive full rename before exit.
3. **Prepare commercial, technical, and HR due-diligence packs** showing low regretted attrition, clean vendor architecture, and a healthy talent pyramid.

### Phase 4: Exit Execution (Months 16–18)
- Enter buyer discussions with expanding margins, stabilising retention, intact institutional knowledge, and no unresolved works council or labour-court disputes.

## 6. Communication Plan

- **Before any announcement:** Consult works council(s) and employee representatives with the real numbers; brief all people managers first with a clear Q&A and support.
- **Same-day transparency:** Live all-hands led by the CEO—plainly acknowledging that three restructures in two years hurt customers and teams, explaining the 18-month goal with real numbers, stating clearly what is paused (rebrand, fourth restructure, junior cuts), what is changing (spend discipline, customer stabilisation, selective contractor wind-down), and setting dates for the next update.
- **Individual dignity:** Any contractor whose engagement is ending is told personally by their lead with full notice and a handover plan—no surprise lockouts, no Friday-afternoon announcements.
- **Follow-up rhythm:** Manager 1:1s within two weeks, anonymous pulse check at Week 6, and quarterly open Q&As.

## 8. Key Assumptions & What Would Change My Mind

- **Assumption 1:** The NPS drop (+31 to +6) and rising churn are driven primarily by post-merger disruption and delivery friction, not by a permanently broken core product.
  - *Would change my mind:* Customer exit interviews showing structural product obsolescence that cannot be fixed within 12 months.
- **Assumption 2:** Duplicate post-merger non-people spend (cloud, SaaS, vendors, rebrand budget) plus pricing/cross-sell can close the majority of the EBITDA gap without involuntary headcount reductions.
  - *Would change my mind:* The Weeks 1–4 CFO audit showing less than 30% of the required EBITDA uplift is available from non-people spend, pricing, and churn reduction, or an imminent debt-covenant breach.
- **Assumption 3:** Buyers in 18 months will value recurring revenue quality (NRR/churn) and an intact organisation at least as highly as peak short-term EBITDA.
  - *Would change my mind:* Sponsor confirmation that a specific strategic acquirer has already priced a pure cost-synergy carve-out regardless of standalone NRR.
