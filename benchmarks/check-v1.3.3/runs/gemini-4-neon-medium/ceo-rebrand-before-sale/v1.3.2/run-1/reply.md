### The real problem (and how to frame it for PE)

Your owners want maximum exit value in ~18 months, which means **EBITDA × Exit Multiple**. Right now, the leadership team is treating a **retention and post-merger integration problem** as a headcount problem. Sell-side due diligence starts in roughly 12 months. Buyers will look straight at net revenue retention (NRR), churn trajectory, and organisational stability—and discount the multiple hard if customers and key engineers are walking out the door.

Why each of the three agreed moves backfires inside an 18-month German window:

1. **Q1 rebrand:** Burns cash upfront (agency, product UI, legal, collateral) with zero EBITDA upside before exit, and unsettles logistics customers who already feel whiplash from three restructures. Defer it until NPS is back above +20.
2. **Fourth restructure in two years:** Freezes delivery for 6–9 months and triggers regretted exits among the seniors holding accounts together (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008, found a 1% workforce reduction correlated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year). In Germany, major operational changes also mean *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan* negotiations with any works council (*Betriebsrat* / *Gesamtbetriebsrat*, §§ 111–112 BetrVG), advance collective dismissal notification to the *Agentur für Arbeit* if thresholds are crossed (§ 17 KSchG—where errors can void dismissals), and statutory notice up to 7 months (§ 622 BGB). Severance and disruption hit now; net savings barely arrive before you go to market. *(Not legal advice—verify your exact entity setup, works council status, and one-third supervisory board co-determination under the Drittelbeteiligungsgesetz with German employment counsel.)*
3. **Ending all contractors and junior roles:** Under the German Dismissal Protection Act (*KSchG*, >10 employees, >6 months' tenure), operational dismissals require *Sozialauswahl* (§ 1(3) KSchG) across comparable roles—you cannot simply clear out "all juniors" without serious legal and works council exposure. Juniors are also your lowest-cost staff and your future pipeline. Cutting *all* contractors indiscriminately across four unintegrated software stacks risks breaking live customer implementations and accelerating churn.
