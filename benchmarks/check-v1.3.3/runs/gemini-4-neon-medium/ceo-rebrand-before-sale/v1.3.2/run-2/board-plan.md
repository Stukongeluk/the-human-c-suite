# Board Paper: 18-Month Value Creation & Stabilization Plan

**Decision needed by:** Next week's Board Meeting (late November 2026) · **Decision owner:** Chief Executive Officer · **Prepared:** 20 November 2026

---

## The problem in one sentence

To maximize enterprise value for an exit in roughly 18 months, we must stop our customer decline (NPS down from +31 to +6, churn rising) and expand EBITDA through durable operational savings—because executing a Q1 rebrand, a fourth restructure in two years, and a blanket cut of contractors and juniors right now will accelerate churn, stall in German labour consultation, and compress our exit multiple.

---

## Executive summary

Our private equity shareholders rightly expect higher EBITDA and a clean, integrated story before we enter the sale process. Over the last two years we combined four logistics software and services businesses into a 900-person group, and we have not yet captured the cost synergies or commercial scale of the combination.

However, the leadership team's current three-move proposal—a Q1 full rebrand, a fourth restructure to remove management layers, and ending all contractor and junior roles—solves the wrong problem at the wrong time. Leadership owns the fact that three restructures in 24 months have pulled attention inward while customer satisfaction dropped 25 NPS points and churn began to rise. In logistics software and services, enterprise value is **EBITDA × Multiple**. A buyer conducting commercial due diligence in 12–15 months will penalize rising churn, delivery instability, and an empty talent bench far more heavily than a few percentage points of short-term payroll savings can offset.

**Recommendation:** Pause the fourth restructure and defer the Q1 rebrand. Instead, approve a **90-day Stabilization and Margin Capture Plan** that (1) stops customer churn, (2) captures non-headcount synergies across the four acquired entities (overlapping cloud, licences, vendors, and office spend) plus pricing and discounting discipline, (3) keeps our junior talent and transitions only non-critical contractors with fair notice and knowledge handover, and (4) sets a clear Day-90 financial gate before any structural headcount reduction is considered.

## Why the current three-move proposal risks exit value

1. **Q1 full rebrand:** Rebranding four established logistics companies takes cash, agency fees, legal/contract updates, and leadership bandwidth right when customers are frustrated. Retiring four trusted names while NPS is +6 creates renewal friction and gives dissatisfied customers a trigger to re-tender. A brand change does not fix product or service issues.
2. **Fourth restructure in two years:** Stacking a fourth reorganisation onto three recent ones deepens change fatigue and drives out high performers. Research by Trevor and Nyberg (*Academy of Management Journal*, 2008) found that a 1% workforce reduction is associated with a 31% increase in voluntary turnover the following year.
   - **German legal & timing reality *(not legal advice; verify with German employment counsel)*:** At 900 employees across Germany, operational restructures affecting more than 20 employees require negotiating a reconciliation of interests (*Interessenausgleich*) and a social plan (*Sozialplan*) with any works council (*Betriebsrat*, §§111–112 BetrVG). Collective dismissals above statutory thresholds require advance notification to the employment agency (§17 KSchG), where procedural errors can void terminations. Under the Dismissal Protection Act (*KSchG*, >10 employees, >6 months' service), operational dismissals require social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, §1(3) KSchG) based on tenure, age, dependants, and severe disability—not simply removing a layer by title. Statutory notice periods run up to seven months (§622 BGB). With ~900 staff, a one-third employee-represented supervisory board (*Drittelbeteiligungsgesetz*, threshold 500 employees) may also apply. Between consultation, notice, and severance, cash costs arrive immediately while net savings often do not materialize until close to—or after—the due-diligence window.
3. **Ending all contractors and junior roles:**
   - *Contractors:* Blanket termination risks pulling out people who hold undocumented knowledge across our four legacy codebases and active customer implementations, accelerating churn. Even where contracts are non-core, ending someone's income abruptly damages our employer reputation; any wind-down needs honest notice and structured handover.
   - *Juniors:* Entry-level staff are among the lowest-cost employees on the payroll, so cutting them yields minimal EBITDA relief. Worse, their work shifts onto senior engineers and consultants—increasing burnout and attrition—and empties our delivery bench. As AWS CEO Matt Garman noted in August 2025, cutting juniors removes the most affordable, tool-fluent part of the future talent pipeline. Buyers in diligence will spot a hollowed-out delivery organization immediately.

### View from each seat

- **Customer:** Option 3 immediately stops disruption, keeps familiar account contacts and brand names, and focuses teams on fixing the issues driving the +31 to +6 NPS drop.
- **Finance / PE Shareholders:** Option 3 protects Gross and Net Revenue Retention (the primary driver of software/services multiples), avoids unbudgeted severance and rebrand cash burn in Year 1, and delivers verifiable EBITDA expansion from duplicate-spend consolidation and pricing.
- **Operations:** Pausing a fourth restructure lets managers run the business instead of spending Q1 in internal reorganisations and legal consultations.
- **Technology & Product:** Retaining critical contractors during documented handover and keeping juniors protects system reliability across our four legacy platforms and frees seniors to pay down the highest-churn product defects.
- **People:** Signals that leadership owns past integration friction, protects jobs while cheaper savings are captured first, and rebuilds trust with staff and employee representatives.

## Recommended plan: three phases over 18 months

### Phase 1: Stabilize & Capture Non-People Savings (Days 1–90)
- **Own the baseline and lead from the front:** Acknowledge plainly to the organization that leadership is responsible for the pace of past restructures and the resulting customer strain. Freeze executive discretionary bonuses and perks until NPS recovers and churn stabilizes, and align leadership incentives to **both NRR and EBITDA**.
- **Pause the fourth restructure and defer the Q1 rebrand:** Keep the four operating brand names through stabilization (add a low-cost endorsement line such as *"A [Group] Company"* only where helpful for cross-selling). Revisit full rebranding only once NPS is back above +25 and churn is flat or declining—or leave it as an upside lever for the next owner.
- **90-day Churn & NPS Turnaround:** Identify the top 20 at-risk accounts and the top 5 operational/product causes of the NPS drop. Assign a named executive sponsor to each major at-risk account and dedicate engineering/service capacity to fixing root causes rather than internal integration projects.
- **Non-headcount cost & revenue capture:**
  - *Duplicate spend:* Audit and consolidate overlapping cloud environments, SaaS licences, external agencies, and office leases across the four acquired entities.
  - *Pricing & discounting:* Standardize renewal price increases, tighten ad-hoc discounting, and eliminate unprofitable custom work.
  - *Focus:* Stop low-value internal projects and strip out redundant reporting and approval steps across the four entities.
- **Selective contractor audit (no blanket cut):** Map every contractor to customer delivery and legacy system risk. Retain delivery-critical contractors while building a documented knowledge handover to internal staff; wind down non-critical contracts with fair notice and clear offboarding.
- **Protect juniors and freeze non-customer hiring:** Keep entry-level roles, pair juniors with seniors on support and product delivery, and institute a hiring freeze on non-customer-facing roles—filling essential gaps through internal mobility across the four companies first.
- **Early works council engagement:** Bring any existing *Betriebsrat* / *Konzernbetriebsrat* in during Month 1 with the real numbers to co-design operational improvements and internal mobility, rather than waiting until a crisis forces formal restructuring negotiations.

### Phase 2: Operational Integration & Margin Expansion (Months 4–9)
- **Day-90 Financial Gate:** Review audited savings from vendor/cloud consolidation, pricing, contractor wind-downs, and churn reduction against the 18-month EBITDA plan.
  - *If on track:* Continue scaling cross-sell across the four customer bases and selective back-office process integration (e.g., billing, support tooling) that removes manual toil.
  - *If a verified structural cost gap remains:* Trigger Option 4—one properly consulted, legally compliant programme starting with voluntary exits and internal redeployment, backed by early transition support (coaching and training from day one of notice).
- **Simplify management organically:** Where management spans are genuinely too narrow, adjust through natural attrition and redeployment into customer-facing or technical-lead roles rather than a disruptive fourth group-wide restructure.

### Phase 3: Exit Readiness (Months 10–18)
- Enter commercial and financial due diligence with:
  - **12 months of improving trailing EBITDA** driven by verifiable cost synergies and pricing rather than deferred maintenance.
  - **Stabilized/recovering NPS and improving NRR/GRR**, proving the four acquisitions are a sticky platform, not a melting ice cube.
  - **Low regretted attrition and an intact talent pipeline**, giving buyers confidence that the team will stay post-close.

## Pre-mortem, assumptions, and what would change my mind

**12-month pre-mortem ("It is November 2027 and this plan failed. Why?"):**
1. *Non-people savings were smaller or slower than expected*, leaving EBITDA short of the exit target. → **Mitigation:** Hard Day-90 audit with named owners for every euro of vendor/cloud/pricing savings.
2. *Churn was driven by a structural product gap, not integration fatigue*, so pausing restructures didn't stop losses. → **Mitigation:** First 30 days of customer interviews and churn root-cause analysis by Product/CTO.
3. *The four acquired leadership teams resisted consolidating tools and pricing*, preserving silos. → **Mitigation:** Clear single-owner accountability per synergy stream and aligned executive incentives.

**Key assumptions—and what would change my mind:**
- **Assumption:** At least part of the EBITDA gap can be closed via 4-entity spend consolidation, pricing discipline, churn reduction, and selective contractor wind-downs. **What would change my mind:** A CFO audit showing cash/covenant distress within 6 months or non-people savings under 25% of the required EBITDA bridge—which would require moving directly to a single, legally compliant structural programme with counsel and the works council.
- **Assumption:** The NPS drop (+31 → +6) is reversible and closely linked to integration disruption and service quality. **What would change my mind:** Customer diligence showing churn is primarily driven by price uncompetitiveness or an unfixable legacy product, which would change our product and pricing strategy.
