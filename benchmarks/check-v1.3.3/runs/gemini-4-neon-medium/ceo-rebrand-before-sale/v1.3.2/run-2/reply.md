### The trap—and the board's real goal

The pressure from your PE owners is real: they bought four companies expecting synergies and a higher valuation in 18 months. The trap in the leadership team's current plan is treating **profit as a payroll problem when you have a customer and integration-fatigue problem**.

In logistics software and services, exit value is **EBITDA × Multiple**. Due diligence typically starts 6–9 months before close—meaning you have roughly 9–12 months to show a buyer a healthy trajectory. Three things will break the current plan on that timeline:

1. **Q1 rebrand:** Retiring four trusted names while NPS is +6 burns cash and gives unhappy customers a contract-renewal trigger to shop around.
2. **Fourth restructure in two years:** Stacking another reorg drives out your best people (research by Trevor & Nyberg, 2008, links a 1% layoff to a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year). Worse, in Germany *(not legal advice—verify with employment counsel)*, operational changes above 20 people require negotiating an *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan* with any *Betriebsrat* (§§111–112 BetrVG), collective dismissals need advance agency notification (§17 KSchG), and operational terminations require *Sozialauswahl* (§1(3) KSchG)—you cannot simply remove a management layer or cut all juniors by title. Between consultation, notice periods (up to 7 months under §622 BGB), and severance, cash leaves now while net savings arrive too late to help the exit story.
3. **Cutting all contractors and juniors:** Juniors are among your lowest-cost staff; cutting them saves little, dumps routine work onto seniors who will burn out, and signals a hollowed-out delivery bench to buyers. Cutting contractors blindly risks pulling out the only people who know your four legacy systems—and still ends people's livelihoods without a proper handover.
