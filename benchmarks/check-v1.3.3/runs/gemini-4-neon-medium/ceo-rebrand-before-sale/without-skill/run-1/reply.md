### The Core Message to the Board

Your PE owners care about one equation over the next 18 months: **Exit Value = EBITDA × Multiple**.

Right now, your leadership team’s three agreed moves optimize for short-term paper cost cuts while **destroying your exit multiple**. An NPS collapse from **+31 to +6** alongside rising churn is a five-alarm fire for a software and services business heading into a sale. In commercial due diligence 12–18 months from now, buyers will heavily discount—or walk away from—a business with deteriorating Gross Revenue Retention (GRR), no matter how lean the P&L looks.

Even worse, executing those three moves in a **900-person German group** will almost certainly stall the business for the next 6–9 months.

---

### Why You Must Push Back on All Three Moves

1. **Q1 Full Rebrand → Defer it.**
   * **The trap:** Rebranding four acquired logistics companies while customers are already unhappy (+6 NPS) burns cash and executive focus, confuses conservative German Mittelstand buyers who trust their legacy vendor, and creates an active trigger for accounts to re-evaluate contracts. Sophisticated PE buyers also see right through a cosmetic rebrand if the underlying products and support remain fragmented.
   * **The replacement:** Use a low-cost umbrella endorsement (*"[Legacy Brand] — a [Group] Company"*) for enterprise credibility and the investor deck. Leave full brand unification as post-exit upside for the next owner.

2. **Fourth Restructure in 24 Months → Halt it.**
   * **The trap:** Three restructures in two years are almost certainly a primary cause of your NPS crash—customers have lost consistent contacts and internal handoffs are broken. In Germany, a fourth broad delayering at 900 heads triggers Works Council (*Betriebsrat*) negotiations (*Interessenausgleich / Sozialplan*), 6–9 months of organizational paralysis, severance cash outflows, contested "one-time" EBITDA add-backs in QoE, and regretted attrition of the exact people keeping key accounts alive.
   * **The replacement:** Announce an immediate moratorium on broad org-chart reshuffles. Fix accountability within the existing structure and handle isolated redundancies quietly (natural attrition, selective *Aufhebungsverträge*) without triggering a mass restructuring.

3. **Ending All Contractor & Junior Roles → Replace with a Surgical Audit.**
   * **The trap:** Cutting all contractors indiscriminately will halt live customer implementations and critical support/R&D work, directly accelerating churn. Cutting junior roles in Germany is legally slow/expensive (*Kündigungsschutz*, *Ausbildung* protections), saves relatively little per head, and forces expensive senior engineers and consultants to absorb routine delivery and support—**crushing your services gross margin** and driving senior burnout.
   * **The replacement:** Run a 14-day audit to separate **billable/customer-critical contractors** (keep or selectively convert) from **non-critical overhead contractors** (exit cleanly in 30–60 days). Keep junior delivery staff to protect your margin pyramid, and institute a strict hiring freeze on non-billable roles.
