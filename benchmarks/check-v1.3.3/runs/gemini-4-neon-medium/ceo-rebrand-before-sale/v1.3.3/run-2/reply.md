### The trap—and the owners' real view

The trap is treating an 18-month profit target as a headcount and branding exercise when the actual leak is customer retention.

To make your case land with PE owners, start from their own math: **exit price = EBITDA × Multiple**. Their instinct makes sense—they have 18 months to show margin expansion and a coherent "one group" story to buyers. The problem is that a buyer in vendor due diligence (which typically starts 6 months before close—so roughly 12 months from now) will scrutinise gross/net retention, NPS, customer references, and key-person risk. Three restructures in two years already broke hand-offs and drove NPS down to +6. A fourth restructure, a Q1 rebrand that pulls teams off customer fixes, and a blanket exit of contractors and juniors will depress recurring revenue and show buyers an unstable roll-up.

### What to do instead (the sequence)

1. **Own the baseline first.** Tell the board—and your teams—plainly that the integration friction and NPS drop are leadership's responsibility, not a frontline failure. Re-weight executive bonuses to net revenue retention, NPS recovery, and cash EBITDA, and freeze discretionary executive perks before touching frontline roles.
2. **Capture faster, reversible EBITDA first (Days 1–90).** Four recently acquired entities almost always carry duplicate cloud spend, overlapping SaaS licences, redundant vendors/agencies, overlapping office leases, and pricing/billing leakage. Those savings hit EBITDA in 30–90 days with zero severance burn and no customer harm.
3. **Put Q1 capacity on the top churn drivers, not a full rebrand.** If buyers or sales need a single commercial umbrella, use a lightweight endorsement ("Company X, part of [Group]") and defer the full rebrand until retention is back on track.
4. **Replace blanket cuts with a hiring freeze and case-by-case contractor review.**
   - **Juniors** are your lowest-cost tier—cutting them barely moves EBITDA, hollows out succession, and signals fragility to buyers.
   - **Contractors** often hold undocumented knowledge of legacy logistics systems. Keep the ones critical to delivery long enough for a paid handover, and wind down non-core contracts with honest notice.
5. **Hold a Month 6 decision gate.** Only if non-people synergies, pricing, stopping low-value work, and natural attrition cannot close the margin gap—or if one of the four units is structurally unviable—design one well-scoped adjustment, once.

### German legal and timeline reality (confirm with employment counsel)

*This is not legal advice; have German employment counsel and HR verify each of the four legal entities before next week.*

- **Works councils (*BetrVG*):** Confirm which entities have a *Betriebsrat* (and whether a *Gesamtbetriebsrat* applies). A fourth restructure or broad reduction triggers *Betriebsänderung* (§§ 111–112 BetrVG: *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan*). Negotiating that, plus statutory notice periods (§ 622 BGB) and severance cash outflows, typically takes 6–12 months before net EBITDA turns positive—burning cash and focus right into due diligence. Bring works councils in early at the problem stage, while options are still open.
- **No blanket targeting of juniors (*KSchG*):** Under § 1(3) KSchG, operational dismissals require *Sozialauswahl* (weighing tenure, age, maintenance obligations, and severe disability), and mass-dismissal thresholds trigger mandatory advance notification to the *Agentur für Arbeit* (§ 17 KSchG), where procedural errors can void dismissals.
- **Supervisory board & contractors:** At ~900 employees in Germany, verify whether one-third employee board representation (*DrittelbG*, from 500 employees) applies to your corporate form, and audit contractors for *Scheinselbstständigkeit* / *AÜG* exposure before ending engagements.
