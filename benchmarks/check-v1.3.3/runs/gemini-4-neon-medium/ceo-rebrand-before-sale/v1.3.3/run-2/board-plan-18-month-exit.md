## Executive Summary

**The problem in one sentence:** Our exit valuation in 18 months is threatened by accelerating customer churn and a 25-point drop in NPS (from +31 to +6) following four acquisitions and three restructures in two years; executing a Q1 full rebrand, a fourth restructure, and a blanket cut of juniors and contractors now will depress recurring revenue and compress our exit multiple more than it lifts near-term EBITDA.

**Recommendation:** Pause the full Q1 rebrand, halt the fourth restructure, and replace the blanket elimination of contractor and junior roles with a sequenced 18-month value-creation plan:
1. **Months 1–3 (Stabilise & Capture Non-People Synergies):** Fix the top customer churn drivers, consolidate duplicate post-M&A vendor, cloud, licence, and office spend across the four entities, review pricing and low-margin services, and conduct a case-by-case review of non-core contractors with structured handover.
2. **Months 4–12 (Targeted Operational Integration):** Remove duplicate manual workflows through targeted tooling, redeploy staff from low-value internal projects to customer delivery, and keep the organisational structure stable.
3. **Months 13–18 (Exit Readiness):** Enter vendor due diligence with four quarters of recovering NPS, stabilised net revenue retention (NRR), durable EBITDA expansion, and low key-person risk.

**Leadership accountability:** The integration friction, organisational churn, and drop in customer satisfaction over the last 24 months are leadership's responsibility. Before asking frontline teams to absorb further disruption, executive leadership will align its own variable compensation to net revenue retention, NPS recovery, and cash EBITDA, and freeze discretionary executive perks.

## 2. Why the Three Proposed Moves Threaten Exit Valuation

In an 18-month private equity exit, enterprise value is driven by **EBITDA × Multiple**. Financial and strategic buyers in German logistics software and services will scrutinise gross and net retention, customer satisfaction, engineering stability, and key-person risk during due diligence.

1. **Full Q1 Rebrand across four companies:** A rebrand does not fix product defects, broken hand-offs, or slow support. Running a full brand migration in Q1 diverts product, engineering, marketing, and account-management capacity away from at-risk customers and risks discarding trusted legacy names in a relationship-driven German logistics market.
2. **Fourth restructure to remove management layers:** Three restructures in 24 months have already created hand-off friction and organisational fatigue without resolving post-M&A integration. A fourth restructure in Germany triggers formal co-determination (*Betriebsänderung*, §§ 111–112 BetrVG) wherever a works council exists, requiring a reconciliation of interests (*Interessenausgleich*) and social plan (*Sozialplan*). Combined with statutory notice periods (§ 622 BGB) and severance cash outflows, net EBITDA savings often take 9–12 months to materialise—while productivity dips and top performers leave right before due diligence begins.
3. **Blanket elimination of all contractor and junior roles:**
   - **Juniors** are typically the lowest-cost salary tier, yielding minimal EBITDA impact while creating acute legal risk under German dismissal protection (*Sozialauswahl*, § 1(3) KSchG, which prohibits targeting roles by seniority/age group rather than applying statutory social criteria) and signalling to buyers that the talent pipeline is hollowed out.
   - **Contractors** often hold undocumented institutional knowledge across recently acquired legacy systems. Cutting all contractors abruptly risks delivery failures and further churn. Non-core contractor spend should be reduced selectively, with proper handover and notice.

## 4. Recommended 18-Month Transition Plan

### Phase 1: Stabilise and Capture Non-People Savings (Months 1–3)
- **Customer retention war room (top priority):** Analyse the 25-point NPS drop and rising churn by entity. Assign executive owners to the top 20 at-risk accounts and resolve the top 5 recurring product/service defects.
- **Post-M&A spend consolidation:** Audit and consolidate duplicate cloud infrastructure, overlapping SaaS licences, external agencies, and redundant office leases across the four entities.
- **Pricing, packaging, and scope discipline:** Review underpriced legacy contracts, unbilled services, and low-value internal projects; stop work that does not support retention or margin.
- **Hiring freeze and selective contractor review:** Freeze non-critical external hiring; rely on natural attrition first. Review contractors case by case: extend only those holding critical delivery/system knowledge (with mandatory handover plans), and wind down non-core contracts with respectful notice.
- **Minimal brand wrapper:** Defer the full Q1 rebrand. If commercial clarity is needed for cross-selling or buyers, use a lightweight endorsement ("Company X, part of [Group]") at minimal cost and zero product-team distraction.
- **Leadership accountability:** Freeze discretionary executive perks and re-weight executive bonuses to 50% NRR/NPS recovery and 50% cash EBITDA.

### Phase 2: Targeted Integration and Efficiency (Months 4–12)
- **Remove toil, not layers:** Work with frontline leads across the four entities to eliminate duplicate reporting, manual hand-offs, and unnecessary approval steps.
- **Redeploy and reskill:** Move people from sunset internal projects or overlapping administrative functions into customer onboarding, support, and high-margin service delivery, supported by paid training time.
- **Decision gate at Month 6:** Review EBITDA trajectory, churn, and unit economics by entity. Only if non-people synergies, pricing, and attrition cannot close the gap—or if a specific unit is structurally unviable—design a single, well-scoped operational adjustment in early consultation with works councils.

### Phase 3: Exit Readiness and Vendor Due Diligence (Months 13–18)
- **Demonstrable track record:** Present buyers with four consecutive quarters of recovering NPS, improving GRR/NRR, clean EBITDA margin expansion, and documented post-M&A synergies.
- **Low key-person risk:** Show stable leadership, low regretted attrition, documented systems, and an intact junior-to-senior talent pipeline.

| Area | Plan & Guardrails |
|---|---|
| **Works councils (*BetrVG*)** | Map existing *Betriebsräte* across all four entities (and whether a *Gesamtbetriebsrat* or *Konzernbetriebsrat* applies). Involve them at the problem stage—with real numbers, while options are still open—before any operational change (§§ 111–112 BetrVG) or new monitoring/analytics/AI tooling (§ 87(1) No. 6 BetrVG). |
| **Supervisory board (*DrittelbG*)** | At ~900 employees in Germany, verify whether one-third employee representation on the supervisory board applies to our corporate structure. |
| **Dismissal protection (*KSchG*)** | Do not target juniors or any demographic/seniority slice as a blanket category. Operational dismissals require statutory social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, § 1(3) KSchG) and, above statutory thresholds, advance notification to the *Agentur für Arbeit* (§ 17 KSchG), where procedural errors can void dismissals. |
| **Contractors** | Audit contractor engagements for *Scheinselbstständigkeit* (misclassification) and *AÜG* compliance before changing terms. End non-core contracts with clear notice, honest reasons, and paid handover. |
| **Communication** | End "decision by ambush." Publish a short written summary of the 3–5 group priorities and what is paused (no Q1 full rebrand, no fourth restructure). Brief managers first; communicate any role changes personally through a person's own manager before wider announcements; run a brief anonymous quarterly pulse on clarity and ability to raise risks. |

## 8. Key Assumptions and What Would Change This Plan

- **Assumption 1:** The group is cash-positive or has sufficient runway/covenant headroom for the next 6–12 months. *What would change this:* An imminent liquidity crisis or debt covenant breach within 90 days would require immediate emergency cash measures.
- **Assumption 2:** Customer churn is driven primarily by post-M&A product, service, and integration friction rather than a terminal collapse in demand for our logistics software. *What would change this:* Evidence that one of the four acquired products is structurally obsolete and cannot retain customers even with dedicated fixes.
- **Assumption 3:** Material duplicate spend (cloud, SaaS, vendors, facilities, pricing leakage) remains uncaptured across the four entities. *What would change this:* The 30-day audit showing that non-people synergies are already exhausted and a large structural cost gap remains.
