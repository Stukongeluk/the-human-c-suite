### Why the three moves fail from Product, Tech, and German law

- **Product / Customer:** A new name in Q1 does not fix unhappy customers; it consumes the engineering and commercial capacity needed to stop churn. Keep brand unification minimal now (legal/endorsement line) and phase the rest once NPS recovers.
- **Technology:** Three restructures haven't fixed delivery. Before ending contractor roles, your CTO must map which contractors hold undocumented knowledge of the four legacy logistics platforms—cutting them blindly risks outages and SLA breaches.
- **Juniors & German employment law (flag, not legal advice — confirm with German counsel):** Juniors are typically among your lowest-cost staff, so the EBITDA gain is small while the pipeline damage is permanent. Under Germany's Dismissal Protection Act (*KSchG*, >10 staff, >6 months' service), operational dismissals require social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, §1(3) KSchG), not blanket targeting of juniors. With 900 employees, also check works council (*Betriebsrat* / *Gesamtbetriebsrat*) co-determination (§§111–112 BetrVG), collective dismissal notifications (§17 KSchG), and one-third employee representation on the Supervisory Board (*DrittelbG*, from 500 staff). Bring employee representatives in at the problem stage, before any structural decision is final.
