# Board Paper: 18-Month Value Creation & Stabilization Plan

**Decision needed by:** Next week's Board Meeting · **Decision owner:** Chief Executive Officer · **Prepared:** November 2026

---

## 1. The problem in one sentence

We have 18 months to maximize exit value for our 900-person logistics software and services group, but a 25-point drop in NPS (from +31 to +6) and rising customer churn are actively eroding both recurring revenue and the multiple a buyer will pay—and the currently proposed Q1 rebrand, fourth restructure, and blanket cuts to juniors and contractors risk accelerating that erosion.

## 2. Leadership accountability

Before proposing any changes to the organisation, I want to state plainly where responsibility sits: leadership owns the current position. Over the last two years, we integrated four acquisitions through three consecutive restructures while customer satisfaction fell from +31 to +6. That is a leadership and integration failure, not a failure of frontline teams. Accordingly, this plan starts by stabilizing customer delivery, fixing management decision-making, and aligning executive incentives before asking employees to carry the cost of past decisions.

## 4. Why the current three-move plan threatens exit value

Private equity owners rightly want stronger EBITDA and a coherent "one group" story before a sale in 18 months. However, buyers in logistics software and services due diligence scrutinize net revenue retention (NRR), gross churn, NPS trajectory, technical debt, and key-person risk just as closely as current-year EBITDA.

*Illustrative multiple math:* If short-term cuts lift EBITDA by 10% (from 1.00 to 1.10) while rising churn and hollowed-out delivery compress the exit multiple from 10x to 7x, enterprise value falls from 10.0 to 7.7—a **23% loss in exit value**.

1. **Q1 full rebrand:** A new name does not fix unhappy customers. A rushed Q1 rebrand across four entities diverts product, engineering, and commercial capacity away from the reliability and service issues driving churn, and confuses existing customers while trust is already low.
2. **Fourth restructure in two years:** Three restructures have not prevented a 25-point NPS drop. Each reorganisation creates a productivity trough, new hand-offs, and regretted attrition of people who hold institutional knowledge. Changing the org chart again without diagnosing why the first three failed treats a symptom, not the cause.
3. **Ending all contractor and junior roles:**
   - **Juniors** are typically among the lowest-cost employees, yielding small EBITDA savings while emptying the future talent pipeline and signalling distress in buyer due diligence. In Germany, under the Dismissal Protection Act (*Kündigungsschutzgesetz*, KSchG—applicable to establishments with >10 employees and staff with >6 months' service), operational dismissals require a social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, §1(3) KSchG) weighing tenure, age, maintenance obligations, and severe disability. A blanket cut targeting juniors carries high legal and financial risk.
   - **Contractors** often hold undocumented knowledge of legacy logistics systems across acquired entities. Ending all contracts at once risks outages, missed SLAs, and further churn. Moreover, ending a contract still ends a person's livelihood and requires fair notice and orderly handover.

*(Note: Legal points above are governance flags, not legal advice. German employment counsel must review any workforce measure.)*

### View from each seat (Option C vs. Option B)
- **Customer / Product:** Option C puts NPS (+6) and churn at the top of the agenda before any rebrand. Option B spends Q1 on brand assets while customers leave.
- **Finance:** Option C captures fast, clean EBITDA from duplicate cloud, SaaS, vendor, and office spend across four entities without upfront severance/social-plan cash outflows, and protects the valuation multiple.
- **Technology:** Option C audits which contractors and engineers own critical legacy logistics code before changing staffing, preventing SLA breaches and technical-debt blowups.
- **Operations:** Option C removes unused reporting, duplicate approvals, and manual toil rather than adding a fourth round of org-chart disruption.
- **People:** Option C stabilizes teams, preserves the junior pipeline, involves employee representatives early, and keeps leadership accountable first.

## 6. Recommendation

**Adopt Option C: pause the Q1 full rebrand, halt the fourth restructure, and drop blanket cuts to juniors and contractors.** Instead, execute a disciplined 18-month plan sequenced to protect recurring revenue, capture real post-merger cost synergies, and present a healthy, growing business to buyers:

1. **Days 1–90: Stop the bleeding (Customers & Cash Hygiene)**
   - **Customer rescue:** Product, Engineering, and Customer Success diagnose the top 5 drivers of churn and the NPS drop within 14 days, and dedicate Q1 capacity to fixing them.
   - **Non-headcount synergy capture:** CFO and CTO audit and consolidate overlapping cloud infrastructure, SaaS licences, third-party agencies, and underused office space across the four entities.
   - **Selective contractor review:** Map every contractor by system criticality. Wind down non-core/duplicate agency spend with honest notice and structured handover; retain critical system owners until knowledge is transferred internally.
   - **Hiring freeze & focus:** Freeze non-critical external hiring. Pause the full Q1 rebrand; execute only minimal legal/commercial unification (e.g., master group endorsement line) now, and defer full rebranding until Q3/Q4 once NPS is recovering.
   - **Executive accountability:** Tie executive bonuses to NRR, NPS recovery, and sustainable EBITDA. Hold executive pay flat and cut executive perks/discretionary spend before any frontline reductions.
2. **Months 4–12: Operational leverage & commercial growth**
   - **Fix how work flows without a 4th reorg:** Clarify decision rights across the four entities and strip out duplicate status reporting and approval layers, rather than redrawing boxes.
   - **Automate operational toil:** Target manual logistics support and onboarding bottlenecks; measure savings in real hours freed before changing any roles.
   - **Redeploy & reskill:** Use natural attrition and internal mobility (including structured junior development) to staff high-margin services and customer-retention teams.
   - **Pricing & cross-sell:** Once service quality stabilizes, harmonize legacy pricing and cross-sell across the four customer bases.
3. **Months 13–18: Exit readiness (and last-resort contingency)**
   - **Only if a structural cost gap remains at Month 6** after non-headcount savings and churn stabilization: evaluate voluntary exits first, and only as a last resort a single, well-designed, lawful restructuring with full works council partnership and paid transition/coaching support from day one of notice.
   - **Exit narrative:** Present buyers with recovering NPS, stabilized NRR, clean post-merger unit economics, and low key-person risk.

## 9. Communication & governance plan

- **Before any wider announcement:** Confirm works council (*Betriebsrat* / *Gesamtbetriebsrat*) and supervisory board representation, and brief employee representatives at the problem stage—sharing the real NPS, churn, and financial picture while the operational details are still open to their input.
- **Managers first:** Brief people managers across all four entities with written decision records, clear Q&As, and what is decided vs. still open.
- **All-hands & written decision record:** Share the 3–5 priorities for the next 6 months, what is pausing (Q1 rebrand, 4th restructure), why, and when we will review progress. Acknowledge leadership's responsibility for the past two years of churn and disruption.
- **Contractors:** Anyone whose contract will wind down is told directly by their manager with maximum possible notice and an orderly handover plan—never by surprise or mass email.

## 10. Assumptions, and what would change my mind

- **Assumption 1:** The NPS drop (+31 to +6) and rising churn are primarily driven by post-merger disruption, reliability/support gaps, and lack of focus—not by a permanent collapse in market demand for our logistics software. *Would change my mind:* Customer interviews and win/loss data showing our core product is structurally obsolete.
- **Assumption 2:** Four recently acquired entities hold meaningful overlapping cloud, SaaS, vendor, agency, and office costs that can deliver near-term EBITDA gains without cutting permanent headcount. *Would change my mind:* A 30-day CFO/CTO audit showing non-headcount savings cover less than 30% of the board's required EBITDA bridge.
- **Assumption 3:** Liquidity and debt covenants allow 90–180 days to capture operational savings and stabilize churn before any structural headcount decision. *Would change my mind:* An imminent cash or covenant breach within 6 months requiring immediate payroll reduction.
