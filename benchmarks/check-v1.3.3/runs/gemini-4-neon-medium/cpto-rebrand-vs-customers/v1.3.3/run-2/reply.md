Split Q1: ship the must-have rebrand slice (new name in the app, domain with redirects, legal/billing entities, and a Marketing-led site) while putting the majority of product and engineering capacity on the three open customer problems, and phase the new design system to Q2. **See the CEO's side first.** After a merger, presenting one name and one story to prospects, customers and the board matters. And "any decision beats no decision" holds for reversible choices. The trap is treating a full-quarter engineering freeze as reversible when three months of unresolved core bugs turn into lost renewals you cannot undo.

**What to do next:**

1. **Pull the numbers from your systems of record.** Before you talk, pair the NPS drop with support ticket volume and ARR at renewal risk tied to slow invoicing, the broken accounting integration and missing reports (from CRM and support). Ask your engineering counterpart for real Q1 net capacity—headcount minus leave, on-call and unplanned work—plus rough sizing for the minimal rebrand vs. the full design system.
2. **Align quietly with key allies.** Check with Engineering, CS/Sales (who feel the churn risk) and Marketing (whether an agency can own the marketing site so product engineering stays out of it).
3. **Go to the CEO privately first.** Never challenge "leadership decides, teams execute" in a wider forum. Ask for the reasons and show the trade-off on one page—Option A (full Q1 rebrand, with the explicit retention cost) vs. Option B (must-have rebrand + top three fixes, design system in Q2).
4. **Once decided, publish the trade-off.** If the CEO still insists on the full rebrand, put what is out of Q1 in writing so teams are not expected to absorb both through overtime.

**A line you can use in that 1:1:**
> "I'm behind getting us to one brand in Q1. Before we lock the full quarter, I want to show you what three more months without fixing invoicing, accounting and reporting costs us—NPS is already down from +28 to +4. If we ship the name, domain and marketing site in Q1 and phase the design system to Q2, we hit the launch and stop the bleeding. What would have to be true for you to back that split?"

**Key assumptions:** There is no legal or contractual deadline requiring the full design system in Q1, and Marketing can run the new site mostly independently. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
