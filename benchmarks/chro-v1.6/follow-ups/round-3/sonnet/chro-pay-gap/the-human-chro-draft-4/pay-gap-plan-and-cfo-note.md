# Pay gap: plan and note for the CFO

Draft for the CHRO. Not legal advice: have employment counsel in the Netherlands and Belgium review it before anything goes to staff or representatives.

## 1. Note for the CFO (can be sent as is)

**Recommendation:** close the unexplained same-role gap within 12 months, in one or two pay rounds, and tell the works councils now. Do not spread it over five years and do not keep it quiet.

**Why the five-year plan does not save money**
- Equal pay for equal work is already law (Article 157 TFEU). A 6% gap between men and women in the same role and grade is a legal exposure today, not only once the new pay transparency rules arrive.
- A gradual plan does not remove the cost. It delays paying people who are already underpaid. In the Netherlands, back-pay claims can reach back five years (article 3:308 BW), and in Belgium a discrimination claim can carry a lump sum of six months' pay. The delay is a liability we keep growing.
- Under the Pay Transparency Directive, an unexplained gap of 5% or more in a category of workers that is not fixed within six months triggers a joint pay assessment with employee representatives. A 6% gap is above that line. Five years of quiet catching-up would not meet it.
- Staying silent is not a safe option. In the Netherlands the works council has a consent right on the pay system, and the action plan from a pay evaluation will need its consent. In Belgium, with 50 or more workers, the pay-structure analysis by sex goes to the works council every two years. Staff will also be able to ask for pay averages by sex for their role.
- Reporting: each legal entity reports for itself. The Dutch bill sets first reports for 150 or more employees on 7 June 2028, covering 2027. The Belgian law is not yet published. Check which entities we have and what applies to each.

**Cost** (to be confirmed with Finance): cost per year = sum, over every person in a comparable group who is paid below the objective benchmark, of the unexplained difference, plus social charges. Illustration only, with made-up numbers: 120 women at an average of EUR 70,000 with a 6% unexplained gap is about EUR 500,000 per year before charges. Real numbers come from step 1 below. The unexplained share may be smaller than 6% once the analysis is checked, which lowers the cost.

**Ways to fund it without a cliff**
- One adjustment in the next pay round, or an off-cycle correction if that round is more than six months away.
- Raise the people who are underpaid. Do not freeze anyone else's pay, and do not ask anyone to sign away a claim in return.
- Fix the causes, which cost little: starting-salary rules, promotion criteria, and how part-time work and leave affect progression.
- Phase in two steps within 12 months if the cash profile needs it. Leadership bonuses can be adjusted before anyone else's pay.

**What I need from you:** agreement to confirm the figures within 30 days and to hold a budget line for the correction. This gap comes from our own pay decisions, so it is ours to fix.

## 2. Plan

| Step | Content | When |
|---|---|---|
| Confirm the figures | Re-run the analysis in code. Group roles as equal work or work of equal value using objective criteria. Report group sizes; groups under five are shown only in aggregate. Split the 6% into what grade, location, tenure and skills explain and what they do not. Test the 14% overall gap for causes: representation by grade, part-time, and pay at hiring. | Weeks 1-4 |
| Counsel | Employment counsel on both countries: exposure, back pay, how to set the benchmark, privilege for the work. | Weeks 1-3 |
| Representatives | Brief the Dutch works council and the Belgian works council (or union delegation) with the real numbers, before the plan is fixed. Dutch works council consent applies to the pay system and the action plan. | Weeks 3-6 |
| Remedy | List who is adjusted, by how much, and in which round. Budget agreed with the CFO. | Weeks 4-8 |
| Causes fixed | Salary ranges per grade, published. Written rules for starting pay, promotion and bonus criteria. Calibration of pay decisions checked for gender effects. | Months 2-6 |
| Tell staff | One message to everyone on the same day: what we found, what it means, what we are doing, by when, and how to ask about own pay. It should say plainly that the gap comes from the company's pay decisions and that leadership will correct it. | After the works councils have been heard |
| Correction paid | Adjustments in the pay round or off-cycle. | By month 6-12 |
| Review | Re-run the analysis. Report gaps by group to the leadership team and works councils twice a year. | Month 12, then yearly |

## 3. Questions that would change the advice
- How large is the unexplained part of the 6%, and in how many comparable groups?
- Which entities do we have in each country, and which works councils exist?
- When is the next pay round?
- Who has seen the analysis so far?
