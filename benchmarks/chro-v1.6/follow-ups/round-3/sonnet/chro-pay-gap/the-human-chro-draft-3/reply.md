Don't take the CFO's plan to the board. Close the 6% in the next pay round (or one off-cycle adjustment), tell your people and their representatives, and treat the 14% as a separate, longer job. Quiet, five-year catch-up is the one option that gets more expensive and riskier every year.

**Why the CFO's plan fails**
- Equal pay for equal work is already law (Article 157 TFEU). A 6% gap between men and women in the same role and grade is a legal exposure today. Under the pay transparency rules, an unexplained gap of 5% or more that isn't fixed within six months triggers a joint pay assessment with worker representatives. Five years is far outside that.
- "Saving" money means withholding pay from underpaid women. In the Netherlands they can claim it back for five years, with interest.
- Belgium: with 50+ workers, the 2012 pay gap law already requires a pay analysis by sex for the works council every two years. Staying silent isn't an option there.
- Netherlands: the works council has consent rights over the pay system, and the action plan will need it too. Both entities will have to report. The Dutch bill is still in parliament (first report expected June 2028, covering 2027), so confirm dates with counsel.
- Women who find out later leave, and replacing an engineer costs half to two times salary.

**What to do**
1. Before anything else, check that the 6% is real: how many people per cell, and whether grade or location explains any of it. 
2. Fix the unexplained part for equal work in months: raise the underpaid, don't freeze anyone else, and ask for no waiver in return.
3. Fix the causes: starting salaries, bonus and promotion criteria.
4. Brief both works councils early, while the plan can still change. Then tell all staff plainly that this is leadership's pay system to put right, not the employees' fault.
5. Treat the 14% separately. It mostly reflects who sits in senior, higher-paid roles, so look at hiring, promotion and progression, and set a longer plan with dated targets.

**What to tell the CFO**
- Cost: 6% of the women's pay in the affected grades. Ask finance to price it. It is one budgeted line, not a surprise.
- Slow isn't cheap: it means back pay, interest, legal fees and disclosure on someone else's timetable.
- Offer a concession that keeps the rules: phase the cost within the year, with the biggest adjustments first. Don't phase over five years.
- His worry about budget and a leak is fair. You control the timing by telling people first.

Assumptions: the 6% survives checking, and you have a works council in both countries. This isn't legal advice, so involve counsel before telling staff.
