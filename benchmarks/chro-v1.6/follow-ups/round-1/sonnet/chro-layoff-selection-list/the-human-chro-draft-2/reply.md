I can't finalise this list, and I'd advise against announcing on the 15th. The spreadsheet ranks people on sick days and part-time status, which are not allowed as criteria. In both countries the law, not the ranking or the managers' keep-lists, decides who can be selected. A list built this way is likely to be challenged and lost, and the cost lands on the company and on the people named.

**Why the spreadsheet can't be used**
- **Sick days.** Counting absences can be indirect disability discrimination (CJEU, Ruiz Conejero, C-270/16). In the Netherlands, someone who is ill is also generally protected against notice from the employer (art. 7:670 BW).
- **Part-time.** Penalising part-time work breaches EU rules on equal treatment (Directive 97/81/EC). It also usually hits women harder, which adds a second discrimination risk.
- **Performance rating.** It is not a legal selection criterion in either country. A single year's rating is also weak evidence of anything.
- **Manager keep-lists.** These are input to a legal process, not the process. Where the law allows an exception, each one needs a written, job-related reason.

**What decides who is affected**
- **Rotterdam (Netherlands).**
  - Selection follows the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel). Within each group of interchangeable jobs at the location, people are sorted into five age bands, exits are spread across the bands in proportion, and within each band the shortest-serving go first.
  - Agency workers, freelancers and secondees go before it applies.
  - Dismissal for business reasons needs a UWV permit or a settlement agreement.
  - If 20 or more people in one UWV work area are affected within three months, UWV and the unions must be notified and consulted first. That includes settlement agreements you initiate.
  - The works council has an advisory right on a reduction like this (art. 25 WOR). Its advice has to be asked while the decision can still change.
- **Munich (Germany).**
  - Dismissals need a social selection (Sozialauswahl) among comparable employees, weighing length of service, age, maintenance obligations and severe disability (§1(3) KSchG).
  - If there is a works council, it must be consulted, and selection guidelines need its consent (§95 BetrVG). A reconciliation of interests and social plan are negotiated with it (§§111-112 BetrVG).
  - Mass-dismissal notification to the employment agency may apply (§17 KSchG; the thresholds depend on the size of the establishment). A missing or premature notification makes the dismissals void.
- **Both:** the board's approval doesn't replace the consultation. These steps take weeks, so the 15th almost certainly isn't a date for announcing named individuals. It could be a date for announcing the proposal and opening consultation.

I'm not your lawyer, and an employment lawyer in each country needs to confirm all of this before anything goes further.

**What I'd do instead**
1. **Prove it is the last resort, in writing.**
   - Document the size of the savings gap and the deadline.
   - Document what was modelled and why it isn't enough: hiring freeze, natural attrition, leadership pay, reduced hours, voluntary exits, and cutting the work itself.
   - Document that internal vacancies were offered to affected roles first. Works councils and UWV will ask for this. If any item is empty, go back to it.
2. **Define the pools.** Decide with counsel which jobs are interchangeable at each location, and who draws the pools and how. Cut work along with roles, so the people who stay aren't left with the same work and fewer colleagues.
3. **Set criteria before names.** Use only the legal ones for each country, fixed and shared with the works councils before anyone is entered.
4. **Run the manager keep-lists as exception requests.** Each one gets a written, job-related reason (a unique skill, a critical project) and is reviewed by HR and counsel. Where the law gives no room for exceptions, the list can't override it.
5. **Check the result before anyone sees it.** Compare those affected with the whole pool by gender, age, part-time, people on leave and location. If it skews, find out why before it goes further.
6. **Offer voluntary exits first,** with a fair package. Often this reduces the number that has to be forced.
7. **Plan the people side now.** Paid outplacement, coaching and training budget and time to job-hunt from the first day of notice. Extra care for people on leave, near retirement or on a work visa.
8. **Tell people properly.** Each person hears it from their own manager, in person, with a written summary that day. Nobody finds out from a locked login or a mass email. Everyone else is told the same day, plainly, that this comes from leadership's decisions and is leadership's to put right, not the employees'.

**A realistic timeline.** Consult the works councils and counsel this week. Use the 15th to announce the proposal, the reasons and the consultation timetable, not names. Personal conversations follow once the legal steps are done.
