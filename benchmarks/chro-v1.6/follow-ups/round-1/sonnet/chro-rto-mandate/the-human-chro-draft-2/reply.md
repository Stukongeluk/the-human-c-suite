I'd advise against sending the "everyone back five days from 1 March" announcement as a decision: the email I've drafted presents it as a proposal with a date for input, because it probably can't be announced as settled.

- **Works council consent.** In the Netherlands, changing a home-working policy needs works council consent (article 27(1)(b) and (d) WOR). Caterpillar ordered everyone in every day without it in 2025, and the court let the council void the order. Booking.com got substitute consent from a court for 40% office time in 2026. It got it only because the plan was moderate, with six months' transition and a hardship clause, and the council has appealed. Five days with about 14 weeks' notice looks much more like Caterpillar. In Germany, moving people from agreed telework to the office for good needs the Betriebsrat's consent (§99 BetrVG), and how mobile work is organised is co-determined (§87(1) no. 14). Please check whether Berlin has a council.
- **Contracts.** For the third hired as remote or hybrid, a written agreement doesn't change by email. Please have counsel check those contracts.
- **Retention.** A study of 54 large US tech and finance firms found turnover up about 14% after mandates, most among women, senior and skilled staff, and vacancies taking about 23% longer to fill (Ding et al., 2025; correlation, not proof). In a randomised trial, two days at home cut quitting by a third with no performance difference (Bloom, Han & Liang, Nature, 2024). In fintech, the people you most want to keep are the ones with the most options.

**The email** is saved as `announcement-email.md`. It states the CEO's wish and reasons plainly, says it is leadership's proposal and leadership's to answer for, and promises that nothing changes until the councils have agreed. It protects remote contracts and individual cases. It lists what is open (days, date, exceptions) and gives four ways to give input. Fill in the dates, and send it only after the councils and managers have heard it in person first. I changed the "decided" framing for the legal reasons above. I also left out any claim that the mandate fixes culture, because we have no evidence yet.

**What I'd put to the CEO first, privately.** "Fix our culture" is a solution, not a problem. Before the email goes out, ask which specific thing is broken: onboarding of juniors, collaboration between teams, or managers who don't know their people. Each has a better fix than five days for everyone. Offer a version the councils can accept, such as team-agreed anchor days (for example two or three), notice of at least three months (Booking's court-approved plan had six), a hardship clause, and a review after a quarter. That also gets you to roughly the same place sooner than a fight over consent would.
