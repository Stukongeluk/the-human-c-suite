# Announcement email (draft, not sent)

**To:** All employees, Utrecht and Berlin
**From:** [CEO name], with [HR director name]
**Send:** only after the Dutch works council (OR) and, if one exists in Berlin, the Betriebsrat have been told in person, and managers have been briefed
**Subject:** Where we work together: our proposal, and how you can shape it

---

Dear all,

I want to talk to you about how and where we work, and I want you to hear it from me first and straight.

**What I want, and why.** I believe we are stronger when more of us are in the same room more often. I think we have lost some of what makes this company ours: new colleagues who don't know people outside their team, ideas that stay in one department, and a feeling of a shared culture that I hear is thinner than it was. My starting proposal is that we return to working in the office five days a week from 1 March.

**What this is not.** It is not a decision yet. The proposal is mine, and any trade-off it brings is leadership's to answer for, not yours. I could be wrong about the days, the date, or even the cause. In the Netherlands, changes to our home-working arrangements need the consent of our works council, and in Germany the works council has a say in how mobile work is organised and in moving people from agreed telework to the office. Whatever we end up with, it will be agreed with them. Until then, nothing changes: your current arrangement stays exactly as it is.

**Your contract.** About a third of you were hired as remote or hybrid, and for many of you that was a reason to join. Where your contract or a written agreement says you work remotely or hybrid, it does not change by email, and not without your agreement or a proper legal basis. If you have a medical reason, care responsibilities, or live far away, those situations will be looked at individually and with you, not with a single rule.

**What is still open.**
- How many days, and which ones. Five days for everyone is my starting point. The alternatives we will look at honestly include a few team-agreed anchor days a week, a set minimum, and today's arrangement.
- The date. 1 March is a target, not a promise. If the works councils or the facts say we need more time, such as a longer transition, we will take it.
- What "fixing the culture" should mean in practice, and how we will know it worked.
- Whether we have the desks, rooms and commuting support for the version we choose.

**How you can give input.**
1. Your team lead will hold a conversation in each team by [date]. The question: which work really needs us together, and which days? What would we lose?
2. A short anonymous survey opens on [date] and closes on [date]. Results will be shared with everyone, including the parts we may not like.
3. Open sessions with me and [HR director] in Utrecht on [date] and in Berlin on [date], also online. Bring your hardest questions.
4. You can write to [mailbox] or speak to your works council representatives directly. Their input will be taken into the proposal before a decision is made.

**What happens next.** We will bring everything we hear, with the numbers on attendance, retention and engagement, to the works councils by [date]. A decision is expected around [date], and you will have at least [three months, to be confirmed with the works councils] from that day before anything changes. We will then look at whether it is working after one quarter, and change course if it isn't.

I'm not asking you to agree with me. I'm asking you to help get this right.

[CEO name]

*Questions about your own situation: your manager or [HR contact]. Questions about the process: your works council representatives.*
