# Announcement email (draft, not to be sent until the pre-send steps below are done)

**To:** All employees, Utrecht and Berlin
**From:** [CEO name] and [HR director name]
**Subject:** Where we work from 1 March: our proposal, and how you can shape it

Dear all,

Leadership wants to bring more of us together in the office. Our proposal is that, from 1 March, everyone works from the office five days a week. This is a proposal, not a decision. Nothing about anyone's working arrangement changes until we have heard from you and from your employee representatives, and the decision will be shared with you before it takes effect.

**Why we are proposing this**

We are worried about our culture: [name the specific things the CEO has seen, for example how new colleagues settle in, how teams work with each other, how well managers know their people]. We see that as leadership's responsibility to fix, not something you have caused. Many of you were hired on the understanding that you would work remotely or in a hybrid way, and you have done good work under those terms.

**What is still open**

- Five days is where we start, not where we have to end. We want to hear whether fewer set days, or days agreed team by team, would fix the same problems.
- If we move to more office time, you will have notice measured in months, not weeks, so 1 March may move.
- If you were hired as remote or hybrid, your contract or written agreement stays as it is unless you agree to a change. We will talk to you individually, and we will look at care responsibilities, health, disability and distance case by case.
- We want to discuss what comes with more office time: travel costs covered, enough desks, and, for the time people continue to work from home, a home-working allowance and proper equipment. [Include only what has been approved.]

**How you can take part**

1. We are presenting the proposal to the works council in Utrecht and the works council in Berlin [confirm which bodies exist and have been briefed] this week, and they will advise on it and, where the law requires, must agree to it.
2. Your manager will talk it through with your team in the coming days. Team conversations will be held by [date].
3. You can send your views, questions and concerns to [name/mailbox] until [date], anonymously if you prefer, or raise them through your representatives. We will publish what we heard and what we change in response.
4. We will announce the decision, and the date it takes effect, by [date].

**What we ask of ourselves**

We will tell you what we measure (for example how new colleagues settle in, how teams collaborate, how many people leave) and review it after three months, so you can see whether this does what we said it would.

I know this email lands differently depending on how you were hired and how you live. If it worries you, please talk to your manager or to HR. You can also write to me directly at [address].

[CEO name] and [HR director name]

## Before this email goes out (not part of the email)

1. The CEO and you have the conversation first: what exactly is the culture problem, what evidence is there, and are five days for everyone the only fix (see the reply).
2. Both works councils are briefed with the real numbers before anything goes to staff. In the Netherlands, introducing, changing or withdrawing home-working rules needs the council's consent (art. 27 WOR). In Germany, ask whether there is a Betriebsrat: mobile work and moving people from agreed telework to the office need its involvement (§87(1) no. 14 and §99 BetrVG). Have employment counsel in both countries review this draft. This is not legal advice.
3. Managers get the draft and a short Q&A two days earlier, and can say what is open and what is not.
4. Send mid-week, not on a Friday or before a holiday. Check that no sentence in your own wording says "will", "must" or "from 1 March" as a decided fact.
5. Fill every bracket, or delete the line.
