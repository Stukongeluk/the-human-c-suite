# Where we work: fix the culture problem from 1 March, propose team anchor days, don't mandate five days

A note for the conversation with [CEO name]. Prepared 24 November 2026. This is not legal advice: have employment counsel in the Netherlands and Germany check it before anything goes to staff.

## Recommendation

Keep 1 March as the start date, for the measures that need nobody's consent: onboarding, funded team days and manager training. Put team-agreed anchor days to the works councils as a proposal, with staff input until late January. Don't announce five days as a decision. In Utrecht it would very likely be void, it can't change the contracts of people hired as remote, and it is the option most likely to cost us the people we most want to keep.

## The problem we are solving

"Fix our culture" needs a name before we choose the fix. Each likely candidate has a better fix than five days for everyone:

- **New colleagues and juniors don't learn fast enough:** a buddy, structured first weeks, more office time with their team in the first months.
- **Teams, or Utrecht and Berlin, are drifting apart:** funded team days and meetups across the two sites.
- **Managers don't know their people:** regular one-to-ones, training in leading hybrid teams, spans of control that leave time for it.
- **Something else:** [what the CEO sees, in his words].

## The cost of getting it wrong

- Replacing someone typically costs between half and twice their salary. At an assumed average salary of €[70,000] (replace with ours), every 10 people who leave cost about €350,000 to €1.4 million, before lost knowledge and the time colleagues spend covering.
- Among 54 large US tech and finance firms, turnover rose by about 14% after return-to-office mandates, most among women, senior and skilled employees, and vacancies took about 23% longer to fill ([Ding, Jin, Ma, Xing & Yang, Baylor summary, 2025](https://hankamer.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/return-office-mandates-and-hidden-cost-brain-drain)). That is a correlation, not proof of cause.
- In a randomised trial with 1,612 graduate employees at Trip.com, working from home two days a week cut quitting by a third, with no effect on performance ratings or promotions over two years ([Bloom, Han & Liang, *Nature*, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208135/)). Limits: one company in China, and one author co-founded it.

**Where the CEO is right:** being near colleagues matters for learning. At a Fortune 500 firm, engineers who sat near their teammates got 18.3% more feedback on their code, with the gains concentrated among younger and less experienced engineers, while experienced engineers wrote less code ([Emanuel, Harrington & Pallais, NBER, 2023](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31880)). That argues for targeted time together, such as juniors with their mentors and anchor days, rather than five days for everyone.

## Legal steps (check with counsel)

**Netherlands**
- Introducing, changing or withdrawing a home-working policy needs the Ondernemingsraad's consent (article 27(1)(b) and (d) WOR; [Rendement, June 2025](https://www.rendement.nl/or-en-arbeidsomstandigheden/nieuws/instemming-or-vereist-bij-terugkeer-naar-kantoor.html)).
- Caterpillar Work Tools ordered everyone on site every working day from June 2025 without that consent. The council declared the decision void, the court agreed, and the existing arrangements stayed in force ([Rechtbank Oost-Brabant, 15 September 2025, ECLI:NL:RBOBR:2025:5758](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/organisatie-en-contact/organisatie/rechtbanken/rechtbank-oost-brabant/nieuws/caterpillar-had-or-niet-mogen-passeren-bij-intrekken-van-thuiswerkbeleid)).
- Booking.com's council refused 40% office time. The court gave substitute consent because the plan was moderate: 40% instead of the 50 to 60% first considered, six months' transition, a hardship clause and enough desks ([Rechtbank Amsterdam, published 28 October 2026, ECLI:NL:RBAMS:2026:10590](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/organisatie-en-contact/organisatie/rechtbanken/rechtbank-amsterdam/nieuws/2026/10/rechtbank-stelt-bookingcom-in-het-gelijk-over-thuiswerkbeleid)). The council has appealed, and it sets no general norm ([AWVN, November 2026](https://www.awvn.nl/arbeidsverhoudingen/blog/wat-betekent-de-booking-com-uitspraak-over-thuiswerken-voor-werkgevers/)).
- Employees can ask to change their place of work under the Wet flexibel werken. We must consider each request and answer in writing in good time.
- Any system that records attendance, such as badge data used for this purpose, also needs the council's consent (article 27(1)(l) WOR).
- 2027 allowances: home-working allowance probably €2.50 a day, travel allowance €0.25/km ([Rendement, September 2026](https://www.rendement.nl/werkkostenregeling/nieuws/per-2027-hogere-thuiswerkvergoeding-mogelijk.html)). Per day it is one or the other, not both.

**Germany**
- First check whether there is a Betriebsrat in Berlin.
- There is no general legal right to work from home, but how mobile work is organised is co-determined by the Betriebsrat (§87(1) no. 14 BetrVG). Moving someone from agreed telework to the office for good is a transfer that needs its consent (§99 BetrVG; Federal Labour Court, 20 October 2021, 7 ABR 34/20, [Gleiss Lutz](https://www.gleisslutz.com/de/know-how/bag-rueckkehr-aus-dem-homeoffice-mitbestimmungsrecht-des-betriebsrats)). Technical systems that can monitor attendance need its co-determination too (§87(1) no. 6 BetrVG).

**Both countries**
- Where a contract or a written agreement says someone works remotely, changing it needs their agreement or a contractual basis. An email doesn't change it.
- Disability adjustments still apply.

## What we will measure, and when we review

Team and site level only, never individuals:
- the problem itself, once named (for example first-year retention of new joiners, or survey items on working across teams and sites)
- regretted attrition, overall and among remote and hybrid hires, by gender and seniority
- time to fill vacancies
- engagement and trust in leadership
- office use against desk capacity

Review one quarter after the change, together with the works councils.

## Assumptions, and what would change my mind

- I assume the Dutch entity has an Ondernemingsraad (mandatory from 50 employees) and that today's hybrid working rests on a policy or established practice.
- I would change my view if the diagnosis points to something only daily presence fixes, such as a regulatory requirement for specific roles (then for those roles, not for everyone); if the survey shows most people want more office time; or if the councils propose more themselves.

## If the CEO wants five days on the table anyway

Put it to the councils as a proposal, never as a decision, with months of transition, a hardship clause and enough desks, and expect a refusal: a court approved 40% at Booking.com only because the plan was moderate. Remote contracts stay as they are either way.
