I'd advise against announcing five days as a decision. In Utrecht, changing the home-working policy needs your Ondernemingsraad's consent, and when Caterpillar ordered everyone back daily without it in 2025, a court upheld the council's declaration that the order was void. In Berlin, a Betriebsrat (if you have one) has a say too. So the email I've drafted announces a proposal with a date for input: [announcement-email.md](announcement-email.md).

**What I changed, and why:** the draft proposes team-agreed anchor days instead of five days, starts the parts that need no consent on 1 March, and leaves remote contracts alone. The roughly 265 people you hired as remote or hybrid have arrangements that an email can't change.

**The trap:** nobody has yet worked out what "fix our culture" means, and five days for everyone tends to cost you the people you most want to keep. Among 54 large US tech and finance firms, turnover rose about 14% after mandates, most among women, senior and skilled staff, and vacancies took 23% longer to fill ([Ding et al., 2025](https://hankamer.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/return-office-mandates-and-hidden-cost-brain-drain); a correlation, not proof). In a randomised trial, two home days a week cut quitting by a third with no effect on performance or promotion ([Bloom et al., *Nature*, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208135/)).

**First steps**
1. Talk to the CEO privately before anything goes out. A line you can use: *"I want this to work, so I don't want it voided by a court or paid for in resignations. Tell me what you're seeing go wrong, and let's start fixing that in March."*
2. Get the facts: what the remote hires' contracts say, whether Berlin has a Betriebsrat, how many desks each office has, and the survey and attrition data on whatever the CEO names as the problem.
3. Brief the works councils while the problem is still being defined, before staff hear anything.
4. Brief managers the day before. Send the email on a Tuesday to Thursday morning, not just before Christmas, with a live Q&A in both cities the same day.

The email says plainly that if the culture has slipped, it's leadership's job to fix it, not the employees' fault. That shapes how people read everything after it.

For the CEO conversation there's a short note: [ceo-briefing.md](ceo-briefing.md). It sets the options side by side with costs and covers what is right in the CEO's view, the legal steps in each country, a timeline and what to measure. This isn't legal advice, so have employment counsel in both countries read the email before it goes out.

Two questions would change the draft most:
- What exactly is the CEO seeing? New joiners who don't settle in, Utrecht and Berlin drifting apart, or managers who don't know their teams? Each has a better fix than five days for everyone.
- What do the contracts of the people you hired as remote or hybrid actually say?
