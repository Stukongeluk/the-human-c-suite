# Company-wide email on office time: draft

**For:** the CEO to send, co-signed by the HR director
**Status:** draft, not sent. Fill in the [brackets] and check the legal points with employment counsel in both countries before use.
**One change from the original ask:** this email announces a proposal and how it will be decided, not a final five-day rule from 1 March. In Utrecht the works council has a consent right on a change like this, and contracts or written agreements that say remote can't be changed by a company email.

## Before you send

1. **Talk to the CEO privately first.** Agree what "fix our culture" means in two or three concrete problems, and how you'll know they are fixed.
2. **Tell the Ondernemingsraad and, if Berlin has elected one, the Betriebsrat** before anyone else, with the problem, the data and the proposal. They should never learn about this from the all-staff email.
3. **Brief every manager** with this email and the FAQ below, a day before it goes out.
4. **People whose contract or a written agreement says remote or hybrid hear it from their own manager**, in person or by video, the same morning, before the email.
5. **Send on a Tuesday or Wednesday morning.** Either by 9 December or from 5 January; not in the last two weeks before Christmas.
6. **Q&A sessions in both offices and online** within two days, with the CEO and HR director at each.

## The email

**Subject:** More time together in our offices: what we're proposing and how we'll decide

Hi all,

I want to tell you about a change we're planning, why we want it, and how we'll work it out with you before anything is final.

**Why**

In four years we've grown to around 800 people in Utrecht and Berlin, and about a third of you joined us on remote or hybrid terms. A lot of that has worked well. But there are problems I don't want to ignore: [two or three concrete examples, said plainly, for example: "new colleagues tell us it takes them months to find their way", "teams in Utrecht and Berlin barely know each other", "decisions that used to take one conversation now take a week of threads"]. I believe more time together in person is part of the answer, especially for people who are new to us or early in their careers.

**What we're proposing**

[Version A, recommended:] Each team spends three days a week together in the office, on anchor days the team chooses itself, and new colleagues spend more of their first three months in the office with their team.

[Version B is below, if you keep five days as the starting proposal.]

**What's decided, and what isn't**

- **Decided:** we want more time together, and we'll check whether it actually fixes the problems above.
- **Not decided:** the number of days, how teams choose them, when it starts, and how individual situations are handled. We'll work these out with our Ondernemingsraad in Utrecht [and our Betriebsrat in Berlin], who have a formal say, and with you.

**What it means for you now**

- **Nothing changes today.** We'll publish the final policy by [1 March 2027], and nothing new starts before [1 June 2027].
- **If your contract or a written agreement says you work remotely or hybrid, this email doesn't change it.** Your manager has spoken with you or will this week, and we'll talk with you personally about what the proposal would mean for you.
- **If you have care responsibilities, a health condition or disability, a long journey, or anything else that would make this hard,** tell your manager or [HR contact]. We'll look at each situation individually, and raising it will never count against you.
- **Before anything starts,** there will be a desk for everyone who is expected in, and we'll set out how travel costs are covered. [Keep this line only once facilities and payroll have confirmed it.]

**How we'll know if it's working**

We'll track [how quickly new colleagues settle in, the collaboration and belonging questions in our engagement survey, and retention] by team, not by person, and review the policy with the works councils [six months] after it starts. If it doesn't fix what it's meant to fix, we'll change it.

**Talk to us**

- Q&A in Utrecht: [date, time, room]. Berlin: [date, time, room]. Online: [date, time, link]. [HR director] and I will be at all three.
- Questions you'd rather ask anonymously: [link]. We'll publish the answers in the FAQ [link] by [date].
- Your Ondernemingsraad [and Betriebsrat] members want to hear from you too: [contacts].

Use only if the CEO keeps five days as the starting proposal after your conversation:

What to expect with this version: strong pushback from the Ondernemingsraad, and if it refuses consent, a court would weigh the proposal against the Booking.com ruling (below), where 40% office time with six months' transition and a hardship clause was found reasonable. Expect resignations from the remote group while it is being decided.

## FAQ for managers

**"I was hired as fully remote. Do I have to come in?"**
Nothing in your contract changes through this email. We'll talk with you personally about what the proposal would mean for you, and we'll respect what we agreed with you.

**"I live too far away to commute every day."**
Tell us. Distance is one of the situations we'll look at person by person.

**"I have care duties, or a health condition."**
Tell your manager or [HR contact]. Adjustments for a disability or chronic illness are a legal duty, not a favour, and care duties will be taken into account.

**"Is this decided?"**
The direction is. The details aren't: the works councils have a formal say, and so do teams.

**"Will there be enough desks? Who pays for travel?"**
[Answer once facilities and payroll have checked capacity and costs.]

**"Will my attendance be tracked?"**
[Recommended answer:] We won't track individual attendance. Any measurement will be by team, and only as agreed with the works councils.

**"Is this a way to cut jobs?"**
[Only if true:] No. This isn't about reducing headcount.

**"What if I disagree?"**
Tell your manager, HR or your works council members, and come to the Q&A. In the Netherlands, once you've worked here six months, you can also make a formal request about where you work under the Flexible Working Act (Wet flexibel werken), and we'll answer it in writing.

## Notes for you (not for sending)

**Why the email is a proposal, not a decision**

- **Netherlands:** a home-working policy counts as a working-conditions arrangement that needs works council consent (article 27(1)(b) and (d) WOR; [Rendement, June 2025](https://www.rendement.nl/or-en-arbeidsomstandigheden/nieuws/instemming-or-vereist-bij-terugkeer-naar-kantoor.html)). When Caterpillar withdrew its home-working policy to follow a global every-day-in-the-office decision without OR consent, the court found the OR could declare it void, and existing arrangements stayed in force ([Rechtbank Oost-Brabant, 15 September 2025](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/organisatie-en-contact/organisatie/rechtbanken/rechtbank-oost-brabant/nieuws/caterpillar-had-or-niet-mogen-passeren-bij-intrekken-van-thuiswerkbeleid)). Booking.com did get substitute consent in October 2026, for at least 40% office time, after dropping a 50–60% plan, with six months' transition, a hardship clause and a duty to provide enough desks; the court accepted that voluntary attendance had not worked ([Rechtbank Amsterdam, ECLI:NL:RBAMS:2026:10590](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/organisatie-en-contact/organisatie/rechtbanken/rechtbank-amsterdam/nieuws/2026/10/rechtbank-stelt-bookingcom-in-het-gelijk-over-thuiswerkbeleid)).
- **Germany:** without a contract term saying otherwise, the employer sets the place of work with reasonable discretion (§106 GewO), and the prevailing view is that it may end mobile work without co-determination; how mobile work is organised is co-determined (§87(1) no. 14 BetrVG) ([CMS, April 2024](https://cms.law/en/deu/legal-updates/back-to-the-office-fuenf-massnahmen-fuer-eine-neue-praesenzkultur)). An existing works agreement binds you: a Munich employer that ordered office days against one had to withdraw the order ([LAG München, 10 August 2023, 8 TaBVGa 6/23, via Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/personal/arbeitsrecht/zurueck-ins-buero-nicht-ohne-den-betriebsrat_76_609664.html)).
- **Contracts that say remote, in both countries:** changing them needs the person's agreement or a contractual basis. In the Netherlands, that is a written change clause plus a substantial interest (article 7:613 BW), or a reasonable proposal the employee can't reasonably refuse; in Germany, agreement or a socially justified change notice (Änderungskündigung). Check each case with counsel.
- **Attendance tracking** with badge or Wi-Fi data is its own consent and co-determination item (article 27(1)(l) WOR; §87(1) no. 6 BetrVG). Report by team only.

**What the evidence says**

- **For more time together:** at a Fortune 500 firm, engineers whose team sat in one building got 22% more feedback on their code, mostly juniors; sitting together also cut how much code engineers wrote, most of all for seniors ([NBER digest of Emanuel, Harrington & Pallais](https://www.nber.org/digest/202402/intertemporal-tradeoffs-remote-work)). This supports targeted in-person time for new and junior people, not five days for everyone.
- **Against a blanket mandate:** in a randomised trial of 1,612 employees at Trip.com, two home days a week cut quitting by a third with no effect on performance or promotions; the drop was largest for women, non-managers and long commuters ([Bloom, Han & Liang, *Nature*, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11208135/)). Limits: one company in China, and a co-author co-founded it. Among 54 large US tech and finance firms, turnover rose about 14% after office mandates, most among women, senior and skilled staff, and vacancies took about 23% longer to fill ([Ding et al., Baylor summary, 2025](https://hankamer.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/return-office-mandates-and-hidden-cost-brain-drain)). Limits: these are correlations.
- **Cost:** replacing someone costs half to two times their annual salary ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-problem-costs-businesses-trillion.aspx)). If 50 of the roughly 265 people hired remote or hybrid left, that is a seven-figure cost before lost knowledge (my estimate, not a forecast). Add office space and travel allowances on top.

**Numbers to pull before the CEO conversation** (from the HR system and payroll, aggregated, no names)

- How many contracts or written agreements say remote, hybrid or a home location
- Commuting distance in bands for that group, and how many live outside commuting range
- Regretted attrition and engagement by team, office and contract type, especially the questions on collaboration and belonging
- How long new joiners take to settle in, by team
- Desks per office against headcount, and today's attendance by team
- A check by group (gender, age, part-time, carers where known in aggregate) of who would be hit hardest
