I wouldn't send "five days a week from 1 March". In Utrecht, changing the home-working policy needs your works council's consent, and without it the council can declare the decision void: a court backed exactly that against Caterpillar in 2025. In Berlin, if you have a Betriebsrat, moving people who work mostly from home into the office counts as a transfer that needs its consent. And where the contracts of the roughly 265 remote hires say remote, an email can't change them.

The draft in **announcement-email.md** is the version you can send: the problem, the CEO's proposal as a proposal, and how and when you'll decide.

**Step 1: talk to the CEO privately before anything goes out.** You could open with: *"I want to fix the culture too, but we can't announce this as decided. The works council can void it, and we'd lose the people we most need. Can we announce the problem and your proposal, decide with the councils by end of February and leave 1 March out?"*

**What to bring:**
- **What a court found reasonable.** In October 2026, Booking.com got substitute consent for 40% office time. It had shown voluntary attendance wasn't working, and offered six months' transition and a hardship clause. The works council has appealed. Five days on 14 weeks' notice is far from that.
- **The cost.** In a randomised trial, hybrid working cut resignations by a third, with no loss of performance or promotions. After mandates at large US tech and finance firms, turnover rose about 14%, most among senior and skilled people and women, and vacancies took 23% longer to fill.
- **The CEO's case has evidence too.** Engineers sitting near teammates get about 18% more code feedback, mostly the less experienced. That supports targeted office time (onboarding, juniors, team days), not five days for everyone.
- **Two offices.** Five office days still leave Utrecht–Berlin teams on video. If that's where culture breaks, the fix is different.
- **Ownership.** "To fix our culture" makes culture sound like the staff's problem. In the draft, the CEO says it's leadership's, and leaders go first.
- If part of the hope is that some people leave, that's a headcount reduction by another route and must be handled as one.

**Order:** works council and Betriebsrat first, then managers (remote hires hear it from their own), then the CEO live at an all-hands with this email the same day. Input to mid-January, a decision with reasons, then at least three months before anything changes.

**Two questions that would change the draft:**
1. What does "culture" mean in data: engagement scores, onboarding time, who leaves, Utrecht–Berlin friction?
2. How many of the ~265 have remote in their contract or live over an hour from an office, and does Berlin have a Betriebsrat or a works agreement on mobile working?

This isn't legal advice: have employment counsel in both countries check the plan and the email. What am I missing?
