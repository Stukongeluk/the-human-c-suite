I'd recommend against the 10% quota with a fixed 60-day plan and an end-of-Q3 exit, and I've drafted a version that keeps the CEO's goal, faster action on real underperformance, without it. The process and manager briefing are in `performance-process-and-manager-briefing.md`.

**Why not a quota**
- A quota fills the bottom group whether or not anyone is underperforming. In a 30-year simulation of "rank and yank", the gains came mostly in the first few years and shrank, and they depended on reliable ratings (Scullen et al., 2005). Microsoft dropped stack ranking in 2013 because it hurt teamwork.
- It is a legal problem in both of your offices. In the Netherlands the works council must consent to an appraisal system (art. 27(1)(g) WOR), and the cantonal court expects a person to have been told in good time, supported and given a real chance to improve (art. 7:669(3)(d) BW). A ranking is not that evidence. In Germany, a works council in Hamburg (if there is one) must agree to appraisal principles (§94 BetrVG), and a performance dismissal usually needs a written warning and proof of sustained underperformance. "Leaves by end of Q3" can't be promised by anyone.
- A plan that starts from a quota rather than a specific gap is what an employee's lawyer will pick apart, and rating down part-time staff or people on leave can be indirect discrimination.

**What the draft does instead**
Four levels against written role expectations. Calibration that checks ratings by group without forcing a shape. "Does not meet" only with two dated examples the person has already heard. Improvement plans of two to four months with support; 60 days only for narrow gaps. An appeal route, and works council input first. The briefing tells managers: no quota, no ranking colleagues, no promised outcome.

**First step: talk to the CEO privately before anything goes to managers or councils**
His view, fairly put: weak performance went unaddressed and he wants a visible standard. Ask what exactly worked there, since the evidence says the effect fades. Then offer the alternative, with something he can show the board: how many real improvement plans started within 60 days of a gap being identified, and how each ended. One line you can use: "I want the same result you do, faster action on real underperformance. A quota gets us a bottom 10% in every team, including the strong ones, and sets the councils against us before we start."

**Questions that change my answer**
1. Does Hamburg have a works council, and how many employees sit in each legal entity?
2. Is this tied to any cost-cutting plan? If cuts are coming, selection follows Dutch and German legal rules, not ratings.
3. How did last cycle's ratings look by manager and group?
