# Performance reviews and improvement plans: process and manager briefing

Draft for the CPO, to take to the CEO and then to the works councils. Not legal advice; Dutch and German employment counsel should review it before use. Legal references checked November 2026.

## Part 1. The process

### Purpose
Raise the standard of work, and deal early, fairly and decisively with real underperformance. Ratings describe the work against the role. They never describe a share of the team.

### What is different from the original proposal, and why
| Original | This process | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 10% of every team in the bottom category | No quota. Anyone whose work is clearly below the expectations of the role is identified, with evidence. A team can have none | A quota produces a bottom group whether or not anyone underperforms. In a team of eight it means naming one person regardless; in a strong team it means penalising someone who meets the standard |
| 60-day improvement plan | Plan of up to 90 days with a check-in every two weeks and a mid-point review. Shortened only if the employee agrees | Dutch courts expect notice in good time, support and a real chance to improve (art. 7:669(3)(d) BW). A plan that is the first step towards a fixed exit date is hard to defend as a real chance |
| Leave by end of Q3 if not improved | Outcome is open. If the gap is closed, the plan closes. If not, extend once, move to another role, or open a separate exit step with HR and counsel | A decided outcome before the plan starts is not a plan, and in both countries it is the first thing a court or works council will look at |

### Step 0. Works councils first (before any manager is briefed)
- Netherlands: the works council must consent to an appraisal system (art. 27(1)(g) WOR) and to rules on dismissal and promotion (27(1)(e)). Without consent the rules are not valid.
- Germany: the works council (if there is one; confirm for Hamburg) must agree general appraisal principles (section 94(2) BetrVG). Ratings made without it cannot safely be used for personnel decisions.
- Bring them the problem and the data, not the finished design, so they can change it.
- Timeline consequence: this year's cycle cannot start until consent is given. Plan for several weeks.

### Step 1. Expectations before ratings
- Each role family has written expectations (what good looks like at this level), shared with everyone before the review period starts. Criteria and weights are fixed before anyone is rated.
- Rating scale: describes the work (for example: exceeds, meets, partly meets, does not meet expectations). No target shares.

### Step 2. Manager assessment
- Evidence-based: dated examples of work, outcomes, feedback from colleagues and customers. Not activity counts, not hours online, not AI-tool usage.
- Adjustments for part-time hours, leave (sick, parental, care), and time in role are made so people are judged on the work done, not the time available.
- The employee writes their own view first, and it goes into the record.

### Step 3. Calibration (to align standards, not to force a shape)
- Managers meet with HR to compare ratings against the written expectations, and check for inconsistency between managers.
- Allowed: moving a rating because the evidence does not support it. Not allowed: moving a rating to fit a distribution.
- Equality check before ratings are final: ratings by gender, age band, part-time, leave, location and manager. A pattern is investigated, not explained away.
- A manager who rates nobody low is asked for the evidence, not told to find a name. A manager whose team rates far below others is also asked whether the cause is the team's workload or their own management.

### Step 4. The conversation
Every employee hears their rating from their own manager, in person, with the reasons and the evidence, before anything is written down or shared. They can add comments and appeal to HR or a second manager.

### Step 5. Before any improvement plan
The manager and HR check first:
- Were the expectations clear and were they communicated?
- Workload, tools, team situation, and the manager's own part.
- Health: if the employee raises it or there are signs, it goes to the company doctor or occupational health, never to the manager. In the Netherlands, an employee who is ill is largely protected against notice (art. 7:670 BW).
If the cause is in the work or the management, fix that and do not start a plan.

### Step 6. The improvement plan
Use the template in Part 3. Key rules:
- Starts from a specific gap against the role with dated examples. Never from a rank.
- Up to 90 days, check-ins every two weeks, a written record of each, support actually delivered (coaching, training, time, a buddy, adjusted tasks).
- The employee can bring a colleague or a representative to the meetings.
- HR attends the start and mid-point review.

### Step 7. Outcomes (all open at the start)
1. Gap closed: plan ends, no residue in the record beyond a note.
2. Progress but not complete: extend once, with reasons.
3. Role mismatch: move to another role inside the company.
4. Gap persists after real support: HR and counsel decide the next step.
   - Netherlands: by agreement (settlement with a fair package), or by application to the cantonal court with the file showing the gap, the support and the chance. A ranking or quota is not evidence.
   - Germany: where the Dismissal Protection Act applies, usually a written warning (Abmahnung) first, and proof of sustained, clear underperformance; works council hearing before any dismissal.
   No one leaves because of a date. Exit in either country is a separate decision, taken individually, after the plan, and communicated in person first by their own manager.

### Step 8. Measuring whether it works (team level, reviewed quarterly)
- Regretted attrition, and attrition of top-rated people
- Internal moves
- Engagement and trust scores (including "my ratings were fair")
- Share of plans that closed the gap vs. ended in exit
- Rating patterns by group and by manager
- Time to fill and cost of replacing exits (estimate half to two times annual salary per exit)

## Part 2. Manager briefing

Use this as a script and handout. Hold the briefing in small groups, with HR present, after works council consent.

### What this is for
We want everyone to know what is expected of them, get honest feedback, and, where the work is clearly below the standard of the role, get real support and a real chance to improve. If it does not improve, we deal with that fairly and individually.

### What you do
1. Rate each person against the written expectations for their role. Not against each other.
2. Write down dated examples. If you cannot, the rating is not ready.
3. Talk to each person in person before they see anything in writing.
4. If someone is clearly below the standard, talk to HR before you start a plan. Together you check whether the cause is the work, the expectations or the support first.

### What you do not do
- You do not have to find anyone to put in a bottom category. If nobody is below the standard, that is a valid and good result.
- You do not promise or hint at a dismissal date.
- You do not use sick days, part-time hours, parental or care leave, age, or activity counts as evidence.
- You do not ask about health. If someone raises it, you send them to the company doctor or occupational health.
- You do not tell the team about someone's plan.

### How to open the conversation (words you can use)
> "I want to go through how I see your work against what the role needs, and I want to hear how you see it first. I've written down examples so we can talk about specifics. Nothing is final today. You can add your own comments, and you can ask HR to look at it again."

For a person who is below the standard:
> "On [two or three examples], the work fell short of [what the role needs]. Before we decide anything, I want to understand what is getting in the way: the work, the priorities, what you need from me. If we start a plan, it will last up to 90 days, with a check-in every two weeks and real support. The aim is that it ends with the gap closed."

### Likely questions
**"Is this a ranking? Is 10% of the company going to be told to leave?"** No. There is no quota and no target number. People are judged against the expectations of their role.

**"Is a plan the first step to dismissal?"** No. It is a chance to improve with support. Most plans should end with the gap closed. If it does not, HR and I decide the next step individually, with the employee heard.

**"I work part-time, or I was on leave this year. Does that count against me?"** No. You are judged on the work in the time you were working.

**"I think my rating is unfair."** You can write your view into the record and ask HR for a second look. Tell me what evidence you think was missed.

**"What if I'm the one who thinks the plan is not fair?"** You can bring a colleague or a representative, and you can go to HR or the works council.

### Support for managers
- Practice session on a difficult conversation before the cycle opens.
- A named HR contact per manager.
- Your own team's results are also looked at: if the team's ratings or attrition stand out, we ask what we can do for you.

## Part 3. Improvement plan template

```markdown
# Improvement plan: <name>, <role>

**The gap:** <what the role requires, and where the work falls short, with two or three dated examples>
**What we checked first:** <workload, clarity of expectations, tools, team situation, the manager's own part; health questions go to the company doctor or occupational health, not here>
**What good looks like by the end:** <specific, observable, achievable in the period>
**Support we give:** <coaching, training, time, a buddy, adjusted tasks>
**Period and check-ins:** <up to 90 days; a check-in every two weeks; a mid-point review with HR>
**Possible outcomes:** the plan is closed because the gap is closed; extended once with reasons; a move to another role inside the company; only then other steps, with HR and counsel
**Signed:** <employee, manager, HR>, with the employee's own comments
```

## Part 4. Pre-mortem to run with the CEO and works councils
"It is next November and this process clearly failed. Why?" Likely answers to prepare for: managers inflated or deflated ratings to game the system; the best people left; colleagues stopped sharing knowledge; a claim of discrimination from part-time or leave data; a works council challenge. For each, name a mitigation and an early warning signal.
