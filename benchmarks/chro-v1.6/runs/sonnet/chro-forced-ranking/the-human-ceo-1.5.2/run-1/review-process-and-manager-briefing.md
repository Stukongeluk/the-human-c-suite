# Year-end performance review: process and manager briefing

Draft for the Chief People Officer. Not legal advice: have Dutch and German employment counsel review before launch.

## Part 1. Process

**Purpose.** Everyone knows what is expected, gets honest feedback, and anyone who is not meeting the standard gets a real, supported chance to reach it. If they still cannot, we act on documented evidence, through the legal route that applies where they work.

**What is different from the forced-ranking proposal.** There is no quota and no pre-set exit date. The number of people who need support is whatever the evidence says it is, team by team. We expect it to be small but not 10% everywhere.

| Step | When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Consult | Now, before anything is announced | CPO, works councils (Amsterdam; Hamburg if one exists) | Share the real design and numbers. Their consent is needed on assessment and monitoring systems (NL: art. 27 WOR; DE: §87(1) no. 6 and §94 BetrVG, counsel to confirm). Let them change it. |
| 1. Standards | Dec | HR with managers | One page per role family: what "meets expectations" looks like, in observable outcomes. No ticket counts, velocity, hours or activity. |
| 2. Manager preparation | Early Jan | Managers | Briefing below, plus a 90-minute practice session on evidence and difficult conversations. |
| 3. Self-review and manager assessment | Jan | Employee, then manager | Employee writes first. Manager rates against the standard, with examples. Three levels: exceeds, meets, needs support. |
| 4. Calibration | Late Jan | Manager groups, HR facilitating | Check that ratings are consistent across teams and free of bias. Review every "needs support" rating and every outlier. The expected spread is a sanity check, never a target. |
| 5. Conversation | Feb | Manager, employee | Rating shared in person first, with evidence. Employee sees what is written and can add a reply, bring a colleague or representative, and appeal to HR. |
| 6. Support plan | Within 2 weeks of a "needs support" rating | Manager, employee, HR | Written plan: specific gaps, examples, what good looks like, support offered (coaching, training, a buddy, workload or role fit checked), weekly check-ins. First formal review after 60 days. |
| 7. 60-day review | About April | Manager, HR, a second reviewer | Outcomes: goals met (plan ends); progress but not yet there (plan continues with new dates); fit problem (redeploy); or still not meeting after real support (HR and counsel decide next steps). Never decided by the manager alone. |
| 8. Leadership | Same cycle | CEO and executive team | Leaders go through the same standards and calibration, first. |

**Where exits can happen.** Only at step 7, case by case. Counsel confirms the route per country before any conversation about leaving:
- Netherlands: dismissal for poor performance goes to the cantonal court, which expects timely notice of the problem, support and a real chance to improve (art. 7:669(3)(d) BW). A ranking or quota is no evidence. The transition payment applies. A settlement agreement is the usual route and must be voluntary.
- Germany: where the Dismissal Protection Act applies, performance-based dismissal normally needs documented problems and prior warnings, and the works council must be heard. Counsel to confirm.
- Counsel also checks whether the programme, as a whole, could be treated as a collective dismissal (NL: WMCO; DE: §17 KSchG).

**Measures we review, as teams, never as a league table of people.**
- Share of employees with a written standard and a feedback conversation.
- Share of "needs support" ratings reversed or changed in calibration, and the pattern by team, gender, age, location and part-time status.
- Plan outcomes at 60 and 120 days.
- Regretted attrition and engagement pulse in the quarter after reviews. Reviews should not push out the people we want to keep.

## Part 2. Manager briefing (to be given live, with the one-page version handed out)

**Why we are doing this.** We want a clear standard, honest feedback and fast, fair help for people who are struggling. Managers carry this, so here is exactly what is asked of you and what is not.

**What we are asking of you**
1. Rate each person against the written standard for their role, using evidence from the year: outcomes, customer or colleague feedback, specific examples. Not against the person next to them.
2. Write down at least two concrete examples for any "needs support" rating. If you cannot, the rating is not ready.
3. Say what you rated before the conversation, not after. No surprises: if this is the first time someone hears about a problem, the rating is wrong and the failure is ours, not theirs.
4. Offer help, and mean it: clear expectations, time, training, a buddy, a role check.

**What we are not asking**
- You do not need to place anyone in a bottom group. If nobody on your team needs support, say so. If three do, say so and bring the evidence to calibration.
- No ranking people from first to last, and no use of ticket counts, velocity, commits, hours or response times as the judgement.
- Nobody's outcome is decided in advance. A support plan is a real plan.

**Watch for these**
- Recency: the last two months outweigh the first ten.
- Likeness: we rate people like us higher.
- Visibility: remote, part-time and quieter people are easy to overlook. Illness, care duties, parental leave and a new manager are context, not poor performance.
- Anything touching protected characteristics, a works council role, a recent complaint, a sick leave or pregnancy must go to HR before you write a rating.

**In the conversation**
- Start with the standard and the evidence, then ask for their view. Listen.
- Use plain words: "Here is what I saw, here is the standard, here is the gap, here is what I will do to help."
- Say what happens next, with dates.
- Do not promise outcomes either way, and do not use the words "bottom 10%".

**Support plan basics.** Specific goals; a first review at 60 days with a second reviewer; weekly 1:1s documented in a shared note the employee can see; the employee may bring a colleague or representative; HR is involved from day one. At 60 days the options are: goals met, continue, redeploy, or HR and counsel decide next steps. You never decide exit alone.

**Confidentiality and data.** Ratings are shared only with the employee, you, your manager, HR and the calibration group. Do not post, discuss or screenshot them. Employees may ask to see everything written about them; write nothing you would not say to their face.

**Where to get help.** Your HR partner, before the conversation, not after. Questions or objections to this process are welcome: send them to HR, and we will answer them in writing and change the process where you are right.
