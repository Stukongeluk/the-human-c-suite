I'd not run the 10% quota with a fixed exit date. I'd run a calibrated review with a real support track instead. That still addresses underperformance and still fits the CEO's Q3 timeline for genuine cases. Both documents are drafted in `review-process-and-manager-briefing.md`: the process, the works council steps and the manager briefing.

**Why the quota fails here, and what you can tell the CEO**

1. **It doesn't work at team level.** 10% of 1,200 is 120 people. In a team of six, 10% is 0.6 of a person, so someone is picked whether or not anyone is struggling. Strong teams lose good people, and managers learn to hide talent or trade names in calibration. Microsoft, a well-known user, [ended stack ranking in 2013](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2013-11-13/microsoft-kills-its-hated-stack-rankings-dot-does-anyone-do-employee-reviews-right) after years of criticism. "It worked at his last company" is a claim to test: ask him which measure improved (output, attrition, quality), over what period, in which country, and whether the people who stayed did better afterwards.
2. **Netherlands.** A performance dismissal goes to the cantonal court. It expects the employee to have been told in good time, supported and given a real chance to improve (art. 7:669(3)(d) BW). A rank or quota is no evidence, and a plan whose end is fixed in advance would look like a formality.
3. **Germany.** Where the Dismissal Protection Act applies, a dismissal for performance normally needs documented shortfalls and prior warnings. Counsel should confirm for your Hamburg setup.
4. **Works councils.** In the Netherlands, systems that can assess performance need council consent (art. 27(1)(l) WOR). In Germany, where a council exists, §87(1) no. 6 BetrVG gives similar co-determination, and §94 BetrVG covers assessment principles (counsel to confirm). The CEO cannot launch this alone, so consult them first, with the real design.
5. **Scale risk.** If about 60 people in one location were to leave at once, counsel should check whether it triggers collective-dismissal rules (WMCO in NL, §17 KSchG in DE).
6. **Cost and trust.** 120 exits means 120 replacements, lost knowledge, and survivors who start looking elsewhere. Pull your regretted-attrition and time-to-hire data and put a real number on it. I don't have your figures, so I'm not guessing one.

**Options side by side**

| | A. As asked | B. Calibrated review plus support track (recommended) |
|---|---|---|
| Quota | 10% in every team | None; calibration reviews outliers |
| Plan outcome | Exit unless improved by end of Q3 | Decided on evidence at 60 days: met, continue, redeploy, or exit route via HR and counsel |
| Legal exposure | High in NL, material in DE | Lower, because evidence and support are documented |
| Works council | Contested | Co-designed |
| Speed for genuine cases | Fast on paper, slow after challenge | Q3 is realistic where the evidence supports it |
| Manager behaviour | Gaming, name-trading | Evidence and honest feedback |

**How to take it to the CEO.** Raise it privately first, before any wider forum. For example: "I want the same outcome you do: a higher standard and faster action on real underperformance. This design gets us there without a legal and retention risk I can show you. Let me bring a version with the dates." Offer him two things that meet his intent: leaders go through the same calibration first, and a quarterly report on how many people are on support plans and how they end.

**What I'd start now (all reversible):** write the role standards, brief the works councils, and train managers on evidence-based ratings. **What must wait:** any rating distribution, any exit date, and any announcement.
