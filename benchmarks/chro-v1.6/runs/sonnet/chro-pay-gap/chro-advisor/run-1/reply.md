# CHRO: Pay gap response (NL and BE, 650 people)

## Bottom line

Do not stay silent and do not wait five years. Fix the unexplained 6% inside the next one or two pay cycles (target: effective 1 January or 1 April 2027), tell your works councils and employees, and prepare for pay-transparency reporting. Confidence: medium-high. The legal timing needs counsel to confirm (see below).

## What

- **The 6% is the problem. The 14% is a different problem.** The 6% is a gap between men and women in the same role and grade. Unless your job grading is itself biased, there is no good reason for it, and it is the number a claimant or regulator would use. The 14% mostly reflects who sits in which roles (fewer women in senior and higher-paid engineering roles). Fixing that takes years of hiring, promotion and retention work, so a five-year horizon is realistic for the 14% but not for the 6%. [Medium: inferred from your two figures, I haven't seen the data]
- **Your own analysis created knowledge you can't unlearn.** Once HR and the CFO know about a 6% unexplained gap, a plan to leave it in place for five years is a documented decision to keep underpaying women. That is the worst fact pattern in an equal-pay claim or labour inspection. Equal pay for equal work is already a legal requirement in both countries, and claims can include back pay. [Medium: general principle, jurisdiction-specific limits to be confirmed by counsel]
- **The EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970) is the deadline that matters.** Member states had to transpose it by 7 June 2026. The directive provides for the following [Medium: from my knowledge of the directive; verify the current NL and BE transposition status and dates with counsel before quoting them to anyone]:
  - Employees get a right to information on pay levels for equal work, and pay ranges must be disclosed to candidates.
  - Employers with 250 or more staff report their gap data annually, with the first report due in June 2027, so you are likely in scope and 2026 data will be the baseline.
  - If a gap of 5% or more in a worker category can't be justified by objective, gender-neutral criteria and isn't fixed within six months, a joint pay assessment with employee representatives is required. Your 6% is above that line.
  - The burden of proof shifts to the employer in disputes.
- **"Keep it internal" will not hold.** Reporting and information rights are coming regardless. You have around 40 to 100 or more people (HR, finance, managers, analysts) who know or will learn the number, and works councils likely have consultation or consent rights over pay systems in both countries (NL: Works Councils Act; BE: works council or union delegation). Being found to have sat on it is far more damaging than having fixed it. [Assumed: confirm co-determination rights with counsel]
- **The budget argument is weaker than it sounds.** The skill's benchmark is a remediation budget of 0.5 to 1% of payroll in the first year, shrinking after. A rough illustration: [Assumed: I have no payroll data] if about 20% of your people are women (~130), average salary is about 65k EUR, and the unexplained gap is 6%, closing it costs roughly 3.9k EUR per woman, so about 0.5m EUR base, or about 0.65m EUR with employer social charges. That is about 1.5% of a ~45m EUR payroll, and it is a one-time step up in run-rate, not a recurring surprise. Replace these inputs with your real numbers before showing the CFO.

## Why this matters

A phased five-year plan saves little cash (you still pay the same total, just later), while it accrues back-pay exposure every year, risks losing women engineers in a tight market (attrition replacement costs far more than the adjustment), and puts you out of step with the reporting regime you will have to follow from 2027.

## What to tell the CFO

1. "We don't have a choice about whether this becomes visible, only about whether we look like we acted. Reporting and employee information rights arrive in 2026 to 2027, and the 6% is above the 5% trigger for a joint assessment."
2. "A five-year glide path doesn't save money. It spreads the same cost out while accumulating back-pay exposure and flight risk. A written decision to keep underpaying people we know are underpaid is the worst thing we could have on file."
3. "The real ask is smaller than it feels: roughly 1 to 1.5% of payroll one time (we will validate with real numbers by [date]), then it falls away if we fix hiring and promotion practices."
4. "The compromise I can accept: adjust the largest, least-explainable gaps immediately in the next cycle, spread any remainder over at most 12 to 18 months, and fund the 14% pipeline work over several years. Those are different problems with different timelines."
5. "On communication, what I propose is not a press release. It is the works councils, an employee-facing explanation of pay principles, and being ready for the first statutory report. No public announcement is required today, and I'm not suggesting one."

## How to act

1. Validate the analysis (regression controlling for role, grade, tenure, performance, location; check that grading itself isn't biased) and separate explainable from unexplained gaps at individual level → Head of Total Rewards or external pay-equity analyst → 3 weeks.
2. Get legal advice on NL and BE transposition status, reporting dates, works council rights, back-pay limitation periods, and privilege for the analysis → General Counsel / employment counsel → 2 weeks.
3. Cost the remediation properly (individual adjustments plus social charges, tiered by size of gap) and bring the CFO a funded options paper → CHRO with CFO → 4 weeks.
4. Brief the works councils (NL and BE) and agree the communication approach before any adjustments land → CHRO, with legal review → before the January 2027 pay cycle.
5. Make the structural fixes: published pay bands per grade, a rule that offers are made within band, promotion and calibration review by gender, no salary-history questions, and manager training on the new information rights → CHRO and Heads of Practice → by Q1 to Q2 2027.

## Risks

- If the regression finds the real unexplained gap is larger than 6% (e.g. because the grading system embeds the gap), the cost goes up. Better to learn it now.
- If you adjust women's pay without explaining it, you may create perceived unfairness among men. Fix with a transparent, criteria-based method (everyone is placed against the same band logic), not a gender-only top-up.
- Remediation without fixing hiring and promotion practices means the gap re-opens next year.
- Treating the 14% as "solved" once the 6% closes. It won't be.

## Your decision

- **Option A: Fix the 6% within 1 to 2 cycles, inform the works councils and employees, and prepare the statutory report.** Costs more in year one, lowest legal and retention risk, positions you well on the directive.
- **Option B: Fix over 12 to 18 months, front-loading the biggest gaps, with the same disclosure.** Lower year-one cost, a defensible compromise if the CFO needs it.
- **Option C (the CFO's plan): silence plus five years.** I recommend against this. It has the highest total risk and I wouldn't sign off on it.

Recommendation: Option A, with B as the fallback if you need to give the CFO something. Whichever you choose, get it in writing that the plan was reviewed with legal.
