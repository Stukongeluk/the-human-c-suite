Don't take the CFO's plan to the board as it stands. Close the same-role gap within two to three pay rounds, tell your people and the works council what you found, and give the CFO a costed budget instead of a five-year drift. The 6% gap between men and women in the same role and grade is the part that carries legal exposure, and the CFO's plan treats it as a budget timing question.

**Why quiet and slow doesn't work**

- **Equal pay is already law.** Equal pay for equal work is already required (Article 157 TFEU, plus Dutch and Belgian national law). A 6% gap in the same role and grade is an exposure today, whatever the date of the new transparency rules. Five years of knowingly paying women less for the same work is a worse position in a claim than one year.
- **It will come out anyway.** With 650 employees you fall in the group that reports every year under the EU Pay Transparency Directive. Reporting starts in 2027 in the directive's timetable, and the Dutch bill puts the first report on 7 June 2028. Employees will also be able to ask for pay averages by sex for their own role. If a gap of 5% or more in a category of workers can't be explained by objective, gender-neutral factors and isn't fixed within six months, you must carry out a joint pay assessment with employee representatives. Your 6% is above that line, if it holds up. Staying quiet only delays that moment, and the story then becomes the hiding.
- **Keeping it internal isn't really possible in the Netherlands.** The works council has a consent right on the pay system (article 27(1)(c) WOR). Under the Dutch bill, the action plan from a pay evaluation also needs its consent. Belgium has its own gender pay gap rules and employee representation, so check that side with counsel.
- **The CFO is half right.** Money matters, and the real number may be smaller than it looks. That is why the first step is to verify the figure, not to announce it.

**What I'd do, in order**

1. **Check the 6% in the next four to six weeks.** Have the analysis run again in code, by country, and show how many people sit in each role and grade cell. Groups under five are reported only in aggregate. Separate what objective factors explain (real differences in skills, scope, location, tenure where it truly matters) from what they don't. Also check that your grades are not carrying the gap. Women may have been graded lower for similar work, or hired at lower starting salaries within a grade. The 14% overall gap is a different problem, about who sits in which roles and grades, and it needs a separate, longer plan (hiring, promotion criteria, part-time and leave effects).
2. **Cost the fix with the CFO.** The cost is the sum of the individual unexplained shortfalls. Raise the people who are underpaid; don't freeze anyone else's pay. Here is a rough illustration, to be replaced with your payroll data. If 130 women are on an average of EUR 70,000 and the unexplained gap is 6%, the fix is roughly EUR 550,000 a year. Spread over two or three pay rounds, that is roughly EUR 180,000 to 275,000 extra a round. The CFO should compare that with the cost of a claim, back pay, losing people (replacing an engineer costs half to two times salary) and a public report in 2027.
3. **Fix the causes in the same year.** Set starting-salary rules so offers aren't negotiated case by case. Give bonus and promotion decisions written criteria. Check part-time and leave effects. Without this, the gap reopens.
4. **Tell people.** Brief the works council and Belgian employee representatives first, while the plan can still change. Then tell all employees what you found, what you will do and by when, and how they can ask about their own pay. Managers hear it before everyone else, so they can answer questions.
5. **Write a dated plan.** Use scope, gaps, remedy and cost, causes fixed, representatives, communication, and the date of the next review with a named owner.

**Options side by side**

| | Cost | Risk | Trust |
|---|---|---|---|
| CFO plan: quiet, five years | Lowest per year, but back pay and claim risk grow | High: information requests, the 2027 report, a claim | Poor if discovered |

We can't hold it for five years and we can't keep it quiet: the works council has a say in the pay system, and from 2027 we have to report.
