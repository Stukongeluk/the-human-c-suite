The problem is also with the method, not the numbers. The sick-day and part-time criteria are likely unlawful in both countries, and the plan skips legally required steps in both.

**1. Sick days and part-time status**

- **Sick days.** Ranking people on absence will catch people with a chronic illness or disability, and people on pregnancy or maternity-related absence. In the Netherlands, a dismissal motivated by sickness is generally prohibited, and the UWV will not grant permission for it. Selecting on sickness-related absence also invites a discrimination claim under Dutch equal-treatment law (disability and chronic illness). In Germany, the AGG (disability) and the rules on social selection make absence-based selection very risky.
- **Part-time.** Using part-time status as a ranking factor is direct discrimination on working hours under Dutch law. It also works as indirect sex discrimination in both countries, because part-timers are disproportionately women. German law (TzBfG) prohibits disadvantaging part-time staff as well.
- **Performance.** A single year's rating can be a legitimate input. But in both countries it is not the main test for a redundancy selection, as covered below. It also needs to be calibrated across managers and documented. Ratings given by different managers are often not comparable.

**2. The managers' keep-list**

A list of "people we want to keep" made before any criteria are applied is the weakest part of the process. If it isn't tied to documented, job-related reasons, it looks like favouritism and hides bias. In an audit or a tribunal it will be read as the real selection, with the ranking added afterwards to justify it. Keeping a few people for a stated skill or business need is allowed, but each case needs a written reason that you could defend to a court.

**3. The lawful routes differ by country**

I'm not a lawyer, so confirm these with Dutch and German employment counsel. In outline:

- **Rotterdam.** A redundancy dismissal needs UWV permission. Selection normally follows the "afspiegelingsbeginsel" (mirror principle). Within each group of interchangeable roles, you dismiss by age bracket, last-in-first-out within each bracket. Performance is not the standard test. With 20 or more dismissals in one work area within three months, you must also notify the unions and the UWV before you proceed (the collective-redundancy rules). The works council (OR) must be given the chance to advise before the decision is taken.
- **Munich.** A redundancy dismissal needs a "Sozialauswahl", a social selection weighing tenure, age, dependants and disability. Performance-based exemptions are narrow. The Betriebsrat must be consulted before any dismissal. Mass-redundancy thresholds probably apply, which means a notification to the Agentur für Arbeit before notices go out. Dismissals notified without that process can be void.
- **Two separate pools.** You need two separate selection pools, one per country, each following its own rules. A single combined ranking of all 300 people won't work.

**4. Timing**

The 15th is only workable if you are announcing the start of a consultation and notification process. It is not workable as the day people are told they are out. Dutch and German law both require the employee-representation steps and, in the Netherlands, UWV permission before notice. Telling people early also creates its own exposure.

**5. What I'd do instead**

1. Confirm the board's decision is a defined reduction of 40 roles. Document the business rationale and which roles are affected, by location and function.
2. Group roles into interchangeable pools per country. Drop the sick-day and part-time columns from the spreadsheet entirely.
3. Apply the lawful selection method for each country. Use performance only where the law allows it, and only on calibrated data.
4. Get a written reason for every name on the managers' keep-list. Decide which reasons are defensible.
5. Run an adverse-impact check on the draft outcome by sex, age, disability and part-time status, and fix it before anyone is informed.
6. Open consultation with the OR and the Betriebsrat, make the UWV and Agentur für Arbeit filings, and agree the severance and support package (outplacement, redeployment options).
7. Set a communication date once counsel confirms the timeline.
