## What is wrong with the current approach

**1. Ranking on sick days is the biggest problem.** [confidence: high]
- Netherlands: employers may not process individual sickness data for this purpose under the GDPR (AVG). Selecting on absence also invites claims under the equal-treatment law for disability and chronic illness, and the dismissal ban during illness applies.
- Germany: absence is not a valid social-selection criterion. Illness-related selection also risks AGG discrimination claims.
- In both countries it is also the clearest evidence a works council, union, UWV or labour court would look for.

**2. Ranking on part-time status is direct discrimination.** [high]
- Both countries prohibit less favourable treatment of part-timers (Dutch law on differentiation by working hours; German TzBfG section 4). Part-time work also skews by gender, which adds indirect sex-discrimination exposure.

**3. Managers' "keep" lists cannot override the selection rules.** [high]
- Netherlands: selection runs by the proportionality principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel). Within each group of interchangeable roles, employees are sorted into age bands, and the last in are the first out within each band. Individual performance and manager preference are not criteria. A manager may name someone as indispensable, but you have to justify that to UWV with specific, documented skills or role needs.
- Germany: section 1 KSchG requires a social selection (Sozialauswahl) by length of service, age, dependants and severe disability. A small group of genuinely essential performers can be excluded, but you must document the business need. A general "managers' favourites" list is not enough.
- Last year's rating alone is also weak evidence. It was not calibrated for this purpose, and managers who protect their own people create a bias risk.

**4. The timeline does not work.** [high on the principle, medium on exact durations]
- Netherlands: this is a collective redundancy. You have to notify the unions and UWV, consult the works council (OR) before the decision is final, and obtain a UWV dismissal permit. That usually takes several weeks, and notice periods come after it. Telling people before the permit is the usual way to lose the case.
- Germany: you must consult the works council on the redundancy plan and negotiate the reconciliation of interests and social plan (Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan). The mass-dismissal notification to the employment agency must be filed before the dismissals, and the works council must be heard on each dismissal. Whether Munich hits the mass-dismissal thresholds depends on headcount at that site.
- Check the split. Thresholds and selection pools are per country and per location, so "40 across both" is not one exercise. How many roles go in each office has to come from the business case, not from one ranking.

## What I would do instead

1. **Fix the 40 roles, not the 40 people.** For each office, state which roles or activities are disappearing and why. The works council and UWV will test this business case first.
2. **Define the selection pools.** Group interchangeable roles in each office. Do the Dutch age-band calculation and the German social-point calculation within each pool.
3. **Handle the managers' keep list as exception requests.** For each person, write down the specific skill or responsibility that makes them indispensable. Expect to defend each one. Keep the list short.
4. **Delete the sick-day and part-time columns** from the spreadsheet. Keep a record that they were not used. Restrict access to the file, since it holds special-category data.
5. **Check the result for adverse impact** (age, sex, disability, part-time, parental leave, pregnancy, works council members, who have special protection) before it goes to anyone.
6. **Offer alternatives first:** redeployment, voluntary exits with a settlement agreement, and hiring freezes. Voluntary exits can reduce the number of forced dismissals and lower the legal risk.
7. **Bring in employment counsel in both countries now.** This is not something to run through HR software alone.
