Don't tell the board "stable with some pockets of concern." The data doesn't say that, and it's the one line you can't take back. Burnout risk is up sharply in two departments, and trust in senior leadership is at 41%. That second number is company-wide, so it isn't a pocket. If it comes out later (a works council question, a bedrijfsarts report, a wave of sick leave in Claims), you will be the person who framed it as stable.

Resilience training alone is the wrong fix. Claims and Customer Service burnout usually comes from workload, staffing, targets and queue pressure. Training people to cope with those conditions tells them the problem is them, and it will probably lower trust further. It's fine as an add-on, not as the plan.

**What I'd bring to the board**

1. **The honest headline.** "Burnout risk is up sharply in Claims and Customer Service, and trust in senior leadership has dropped to 41%. We're treating both as priorities."
2. **Find the causes in the next 2-3 weeks.** Run short sessions with Claims and Customer Service staff and their team leads. Ask what changed: volumes, headcount, sick leave, overtime, turnover, targets. Compare those against HR data you already have (absence, attrition, overtime). Don't present a fix before you know the driver.
3. **Fix work, not people.** Expect actions like workload and queue limits, filling vacancies, removing the worst targets or reporting pressure, and giving team leads room to push back. Resilience or wellbeing support can sit alongside these.
4. **Trust is the CEO's job, not an HR program.** Ask leadership for a visible response: share the results openly with staff, say what they heard, and commit to two or three specific changes with dates. A softened message to the board followed by a softened message to staff is exactly what keeps trust at 41%.
5. **Cover the Dutch legal side.** Psychosocial workload (PSA) falls under the Arbowet. Involve your arbodienst or bedrijfsarts, check that the RI&E covers PSA, and bring in the ondernemingsraad early. Under the Arbowet you must act on this, and the OR will see the results anyway.
6. **A follow-up check.** A pulse survey in Claims and Customer Service in about 3 months, with results reported back to the board.

**With the CEO, before the meeting**

Go in with a proposal, not a refusal. Something like: "If we call this stable and the numbers get worse, the board will ask why we didn't flag it. I'd rather show we saw it early and have a plan." Offer to keep the training as one part of the plan, which gives the CEO something to approve. If the CEO still insists on "stable," put the actual figures in your slides and let them speak. Don't state something you believe is untrue.

I haven't seen your underlying data. If the Claims and Customer Service numbers are driven by a single team or a recent reorganisation, the plan changes, so check that first.
