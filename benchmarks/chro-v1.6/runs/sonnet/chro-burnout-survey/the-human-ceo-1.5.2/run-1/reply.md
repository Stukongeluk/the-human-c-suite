Don't tell the board "stable with some pockets of concern." Sharply rising burnout risk in two departments and 41% trust in senior leadership is not stable, and the board will see it later in sick leave and attrition. Present the real picture with a plan, and don't make resilience training the plan.

**Before the meeting**

1. **See the CEO privately first.** Say: "If we call this stable and it worsens, the board will ask why we softened it. Let me bring the facts and a plan, so you're the leader who acted." Give your reasons in writing so they can challenge them.
2. **Find the cause before choosing the fix.** In Claims and Customer Service, check workload per head, backlog, overtime, sick leave, turnover and agency cover, then ask the teams themselves. Training that teaches people to cope with a broken workload says the problem is them, and it can push trust down further.
3. **Own the 41%.** Trust in leadership is a leadership problem. Say so.

**For the board**

- The real numbers with date, trend and comparison (other departments, last year).
- A 30-day diagnosis with named owners, then a 90-day pulse to check progress.
- Quick relief where the data supports it: stop low-value work, ease backlog, plan cover. Resilience support only as an optional extra.
- What leaders will do differently, visibly.
