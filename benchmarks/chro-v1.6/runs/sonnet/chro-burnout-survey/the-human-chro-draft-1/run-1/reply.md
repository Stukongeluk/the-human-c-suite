Don't present "stable with some pockets of concern", and don't make resilience training the answer. Take the real numbers to the board, with a short action plan. Start by talking to your CEO privately, before the board sees anything.

**Why**
- Burnout rising sharply in two departments, plus trust in leadership down to 41%, is not stable. Staff who answered the survey will notice if the board hears something softer, and that costs you more trust.
- Resilience training treats burnout as a personal weakness. A 2024 study of 46,336 UK workers in 233 organisations found people on resilience, mindfulness or wellbeing-app programmes were no better off than those who weren't (Fleming, *Industrial Relations Journal*). Burnout comes from workload and control.
- In the Netherlands, work pressure is also a legal duty. The Arbowet requires a policy to prevent or limit psychosocial workload, and it belongs in your RI&E. A board told "stable" has been given a wrong picture of a legal risk.

**Your first steps**
1. **See the CEO this week, one-on-one.** Frame it as protecting them. A board that later sees the raw data, or a spike in sick leave or resignations in Claims, will ask why it was softened. Offer a version they can own: "We have a real problem, here is our plan, I own it."
2. **Find the causes before the board meeting.** Pull aggregate data for Claims and Customer Service: open roles and vacancy time, overtime, absence, regretted attrition, claim volumes per handler, and the survey comments. Check for clusters by manager or team. Report no groups under five people.
3. **Treat the 41% as a leadership result.** Senior leaders should say what they heard, what they will change and by when. Then ask the works council (OR) to help shape the actions, since they have a say on social policy.

**What to put to the board**
- The honest scores, the trend and the spread.
- Two or three workload fixes, chosen with the teams: staffing or backfill, fewer handling-time pressures, clearer escalation routes, manager support.
- Individual support (occupational health, confidential counselling) offered alongside these fixes, not instead of them.
- Indicators reviewed quarterly: absence, regretted attrition, burnout and trust scores in both departments, and a re-survey in six months.
- "Do nothing" with its cost. Replacing an employee costs half to two times their salary, and sick leave in the Netherlands runs up to two years.

**Assumptions:** the survey is reliable and the drop isn't down to one reorganisation or a single team. I haven't seen your absence or attrition data.

**What would change my mind:** if the data shows the problem is concentrated in two or three teams, the plan can be narrower. That is still not "stable".

This is not legal advice. Check the Arbowet and RI&E points with your arbo adviser.
