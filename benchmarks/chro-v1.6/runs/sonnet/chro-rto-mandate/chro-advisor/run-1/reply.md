**People — Return-to-office announcement**

**Bottom line:** Here is the email, written so it can be sent. I would not send it until three things are settled (see "Before this goes out"). For roughly 270 people hired on remote or hybrid terms, a five-day mandate from 1 March is probably a change to their employment terms, not just a policy update. In the Netherlands and Germany that normally means the works councils and legal have to be involved first. Confidence: medium. I can't see your contracts, your works council agreements or your attrition data, and the legal points below need confirming by counsel in each country.

## The email

## Before this goes out

1. **Legal position on the contract changes (🟡 medium confidence, needs counsel).**
   - Where remote or hybrid working is written into an employment contract, or is an established term, an employer generally cannot change it unilaterally. In the Netherlands, a one-sided change usually needs a valid change clause and a weighty business reason (art. 7:613 BW), and you would have to show that the employee's interest does not outweigh it. In Germany, the employer's right to direct work location (Weisungsrecht) is narrowed by what the contract says. Ask your General Counsel or external employment lawyers for both countries which of the roughly 270 people are covered and what the risk is.
   - Handled badly, this can mean constructive-dismissal claims, claims for commuting or home-office costs, and the best people leaving. Ask legal what the exposure is for the people hired with explicit remote terms specifically.
2. **Works council and Betriebsrat (🟡 medium).** At 800 people you almost certainly have both. Rules on working location and working arrangements typically carry consent or consultation rights (WOR art. 27 in the Netherlands; BetrVG section 87 in Germany). If you announce a final decision before they are consulted, you can end up with an injunction, a delayed start date, or a damaged relationship with the council. If this has not been taken to them yet, the email needs the more cautious wording: "The leadership team intends to...", and it should say consultation is under way. I have written the email so that line 1 can be softened that way.
3. **Timing.** From today there are about 14 weeks to 1 March. Consultation, individual conversations and notice periods all have to fit inside that, and it is tight. If the start date cannot hold, a phased start (for example three days from 1 March, five from 1 June for those with remote contracts) avoids a forced decision between a legal mistake and a public reversal.

## What I would raise with the CEO (you may want to do this first)

- **"To fix our culture" is not a measurable goal.** Ask what outcome it should produce and how you will know in six months. If the goal is collaboration, onboarding or speed, a defined set of anchor days (three or four, with a purpose) usually gets most of the benefit at far less cost than five days. The evidence I would lean on: flexibility is a real retention driver, and the reasons people give for leaving are mostly manager quality and growth, not the number of office days.
- **Regrettable attrition is the main risk, and it will not be evenly spread.** The people with the most options (senior engineers, security, risk and data people in a fintech) are the ones who can get remote offers elsewhere. Before you commit, get a view on: who among the roughly 270 are in critical roles, what replacement cost and time-to-fill look like (and your current numbers), and what a 10 to 15 percent attrition increase in that group would cost. I do not have your figures, so I will not put a number on it. Have the CFO or finance partner model it.
- **Hiring.** Berlin and Utrecht both compete for the same engineers, and many of them filter for hybrid. Expect offer acceptance to fall. Check what the current pipeline looks like for the next two quarters.
- **Exceptions need a rule, not a favour.** If some people will be exempted (a manager who negotiates one, a role that cannot move), decide the criteria now, and write them down. Ad hoc exceptions are the fastest way to turn this into a fairness problem.
