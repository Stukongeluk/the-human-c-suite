# Draft: all-company email (for the CEO to send, after managers and employee representatives have been told)

Review with employment counsel in both countries before sending. Items in [brackets] must be filled in or confirmed; delete anything you cannot stand behind.

**Subject:** Our proposal for working from the office from 1 March, and how you can change it

Hi all,

Your manager and our employee representatives in Utrecht and Berlin heard this before you did, and your team has had a first conversation about it. I want to set out what I'm proposing, why, and what is still open.

**The proposal.** From 1 March 2027, we would work from our offices in Utrecht and Berlin five days a week.

**Why.** [Name the specific problems and the evidence, with dates and figures: for example onboarding speed, delivery across teams, engagement or attrition data. If the case rests on judgment rather than data, say so in one sentence: "This is my judgment, and I want you to test it."] I believe we have lost some of the daily contact that makes us good at this. I may be wrong about the cause or the cure, and that is why I'm asking before we finalise.

**What I know this asks of you.** About a third of you joined on remote or hybrid terms, and many of you built your lives around them: where you live, care duties, commutes, childcare. Your employment contract is not a detail to me. Nobody's terms change because of this email. [Counsel to confirm: any change to individual terms is discussed with you personally first and, where the law requires it, needs your agreement.]

**What is decided, and what is open.**
- Decided: we will make a decision about how we work together by [date, e.g. 22 January], after hearing from you.
- Open: the number of office days, the start date, exceptions and phasing, what we do for people on remote or hybrid contracts, and support such as travel costs or relocation help.

**How you can shape it.**
- Until [date, two weeks from today], you can send input to [mailbox or form], including anonymously.
- [Dates]: open Q&A sessions in Utrecht and Berlin, and a session for each team with your manager.
- The works council in Utrecht has been asked for its advice, with the same figures you see here. [Berlin: works council (Betriebsrat), if one exists; otherwise a named employee panel.] Its advice can change the plan.
- By [date], I will publish the decision with the reasons, what we changed because of your input, and what we did not change and why.

**Leaders go first.** [Confirm before including: the executive team will be in the office five days a week from [date], before anyone else is asked to.]

**How we will know whether it works.** After six months we will look at [measures agreed up front, such as regretted attrition, engagement scores, delivery measures, time to hire] and share the results, including the unwelcome ones.

If you see a problem I haven't, please tell me. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?

[CEO name]

---
Notes for the sender, not for the email:
- Order: CEO and exec team, then the works council and its advice request, then managers (with a Q&A sheet), then this email, then the Q&A sessions. Managers must not hear this from the email.
- Until the works council has advised, keep the wording "proposal" or "intended decision". Announcing a final decision first and "consulting" afterwards can be challenged.
