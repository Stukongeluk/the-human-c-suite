I'd advise against sending an email that announces this as a final decision on 1 March; send one that announces it as a proposal, with a real input window, and only after managers and employee representatives have been told. The draft is in `email-draft.md`, next to this reply.

**Why not the straight announcement**
- A third of your people have remote or hybrid terms. In the Netherlands and Germany, a single email can't change those terms; counsel needs to say what you can change unilaterally and what needs agreement. Expect regretted leavers, and your best people are the ones who can leave.
- Utrecht's works council must be asked for advice while it can still change the plan (art. 25 WOR), and it may have consent rights over working arrangements. If a Berlin Betriebsrat exists, the same applies. Announcing first and consulting afterwards can be challenged.
- "To fix our culture" is a conclusion, not a diagnosis. An email that gives no evidence invites the reaction that the decision was made by rank.

**What the draft does instead**
- States the proposal and the date plainly, so there is no spin.
- Separates what is decided from what is open: number of days, phasing, exceptions, and support for the remote and hybrid hires.
- Opens a two-week input window, with Q&A sessions in both cities and the works council consulted. It promises to publish what changed and what didn't.
- Has leaders go first and a six-month review against agreed measures.
- Has no placeholders for facts I don't have. The reasons paragraph is yours and the CEO's to fill, and I'd rather it say "this is my judgment" than pretend to data.

**Before you send it, two questions for the CEO**
1. What exactly is broken, and what evidence is there (attrition, engagement, onboarding, delivery)? If five days is the answer, why not three or four? A short pilot with a measure is a reversible alternative to a company-wide order.
2. Do you have desks for 800 people five days a week in both cities, and what does losing, say, 5 to 10% of that third cost you in hiring and lost knowledge? I can't see your numbers, so treat that percentage as an illustration only.

If the CEO has already decided and won't move, you can still use the draft. Call it an "intended decision" until the works council has advised, and keep the open items open.

**Sequence:** exec team, then works council advice request, then managers (with a Q&A sheet), then the email, then the sessions. Managers shouldn't learn this from the email.

This isn't legal advice. Have employment counsel in both countries check the contract and consultation points before anything goes out.
