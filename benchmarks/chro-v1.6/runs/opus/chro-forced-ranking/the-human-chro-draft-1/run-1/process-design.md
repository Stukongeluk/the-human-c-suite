# Performance reviews 2026–27: process design

*Draft for the CEO to decide on, then for the works councils in Amsterdam and Hamburg. This is not legal advice: Dutch and German employment counsel should check it before it is used. The dates assume reviews are written in January 2027; move them if your cycle is different.*

## 1. Recommendation

Run this year's reviews against clear role expectations, calibrate them across managers, and put every real performance gap on an individual, dated improvement plan, with a decision on each case by 30 September 2027. Don't set a share of people for the bottom category, and don't link a rating to an automatic exit.

This gives the CEO what he is after: honest ratings, real underperformance dealt with, and decisions before the end of Q3. It does so in a form the works councils can agree to and that holds up if a case reaches a Dutch or German court.

## 2. What changed from the proposal, and why

| Proposal | In this design | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Every manager puts 10% of their team in the bottom category | No quota. A rating below expectations needs a documented gap against the role, with dated examples. Calibration tests every rating, high and low | A quota produces a bottom group whether or not anyone is underperforming. In teams of five to nine people it means one person per team, which is 20% of a team of five. Courts in both countries judge each person's own work, not their rank (section 7) |
| The bottom category gets a 60-day improvement plan | Each documented gap gets its own plan of 60 to 120 days (90 by default), with support and a check-in every two weeks | The length has to fit the gap. A Dutch court asks whether the person was told in good time and given a real chance to improve, and whether the problem came from a lack of training or poor working conditions (art. 7:669(3)(d) BW). A plan that starts from a ranking doesn't meet that test |
| Anyone who doesn't improve leaves by the end of Q3 | Every case has a decision by 30 September: closed, extended once, a move to another role, or an exit route with HR and counsel | People still leave where the gap remains. But a plan whose outcome is fixed in advance is hard to defend as a real chance to improve. Some legal routes run past Q3 |
| (Implied) a new rating system | Keep the appraisal system the works councils have already agreed, with sharper guidance and calibration | A new rating system or a forced distribution needs works council consent in both countries (art. 27(1)(g) WOR; §94(2) BetrVG). Working inside the agreed system is the fastest lawful route this cycle |

## 3. What the CEO gets

- **Ratings that mean something.** The same rating means the same thing in every department, because calibration checks the evidence for both high and low ratings.
- **No more silent underperformance.** Every real gap is named, discussed with the person and on a plan by mid-March.
- **A decision on every case by 30 September 2027.**
- **Visibility of managers who avoid the hard conversation.** Calibration asks every manager for evidence, and the monthly report shows each department's ratings and plan outcomes.
- **A monthly one-page report** (section 9) and an honest account of the cycle in October.

## 4. Principles

1. Criteria are written and shared before anyone is rated, and the same rules apply in every team and both countries.
2. People are rated on their work against their role, never against colleagues, a curve, attendance or activity counts.
3. No consequence without a fair chance. A rating reflects the work, but any consequence starts only from the day the person is told clearly what has to change.
4. Everyone can see their review, add their own comments and appeal.
5. People decide, and every decision about a person can be explained to that person.
6. Health questions go to the company doctor (bedrijfsarts in Amsterdam, Betriebsarzt in Hamburg), never to the manager.
7. Ratings are checked by group before they are final.

## 6. The steps

### 6.1 Baseline data, in aggregate only

From the HR system: last cycle's rating distribution by department, manager (teams of five or more), location, gender, age band, part-time status and recent parental or care leave; open performance cases and how long they have been open; regretted and non-regretted attrition in 2026; engagement results by department. Groups of fewer than five are shown only in aggregate.

The purpose is to see where ratings are inflated, where low ratings cluster under one manager (which usually points to the manager, the workload or the role first), and whether any group already gets lower ratings.

### 6.2 Works councils

Bring both councils in at the problem stage, with the real data: the CEO's concern that underperformance goes unaddressed, last cycle's figures, and this design. Ask for their changes and agree the October review. The legal rights involved are in section 7.

### 6.3 Expectations and the scale

- Every person is reviewed against the written expectations for their role and level and the goals agreed for 2026. Where no goals were set, the review uses the job profile and the work the person was actually asked to do, and the conversation sets goals for 2027.
- Keep the existing scale. Add a one-line description per level with an example. For the lowest level: *"A specific, persistent gap against what the role requires at this level, shown by dated examples."*

### 6.4 Writing reviews

Evidence means dated examples of the work: what was delivered, its quality, how the person worked with others, measured against the role. The manager briefing lists what never counts: sick days and absence, part-time hours, leave, pregnancy, age, working pattern, office attendance, activity counts, AI-tool usage, and comparisons with named colleagues.

### 6.5 Calibration

- Per department, chaired by the department head with the HR business partner. Allow about 90 minutes per 40 people.
- **The purpose is to align standards across managers, test the evidence and check for bias. There is no target distribution.**
- Managers present the ratings at both ends, with evidence. Peers challenge "everyone in my team exceeds" as hard as "this person is below expectations". A team with no concerns is fine if the evidence supports it.
- Anyone with a personal relationship with the person being discussed says so and steps out of that discussion.
- Patterns by manager are followed up with that manager as a support question, not a blame question.
- Results stay provisional until the equality check is done.

### 6.6 Equality check

Before ratings are final, HR compares the share of each rating by gender, age (50 and over), part-time status, recent leave, location, and nationality where that is lawful. If a group's share of below-expectations ratings is clearly higher than its share of the department, the department head and HR business partner review those cases before ratings go final. The findings and actions are recorded. Rating patterns that disadvantage part-time staff, people on leave or a protected group can be indirect discrimination under EU law.

### 6.7 Conversations and the right to respond

- The manager holds every conversation in person or by live video, before the person sees anything in writing. Never by email or a system notification first.
- Afterwards the person gets the written review, can add their own comments, and can appeal within 10 working days to their manager's manager together with an HR business partner who wasn't in the calibration.
- Appeals are decided within 15 working days, in writing, with reasons.
- People can also ask for the personal data held about them (GDPR, Article 15).

### 6.8 Improvement plans

- A plan starts from a specific concern about the work, never from a rating band or a share.
- Before a plan starts, the HR business partner checks that the expectation was clear and looks at workload, tools, the team situation and the manager's own part. Any health question goes to the company doctor.
- **Content:** the gap with dated examples; what good looks like by the end; the support given (coaching, training, time, a buddy, adjusted tasks); the length; check-ins; and the possible outcomes. The manager briefing contains the form.
- **Length:** 90 days by default. 60 days only where the gap is narrow and was clearly raised before this cycle; up to 120 days for complex roles. Holidays and sick leave pause the clock.
- **Check-ins** every two weeks, written down, with the HR business partner at the mid-point.
- **Signed** by the manager and the HR business partner. The employee signs to confirm receipt and can add their own comments.

### 6.9 Outcomes

1. **Closed:** the gap is closed, and this is confirmed in writing.
2. **Extended once,** with reasons: real progress, or the plan was disrupted.
3. **A move to another role** that fits, where one exists.
4. **An exit route,** only once 1 to 3 are ruled out. The department head and the CPO decide, with counsel. Either a mutual agreement offered on fair terms with time for independent advice, or the legal route in each country (section 7).

Realistic timing: mutual agreements can be completed by the end of Q3. A court route in the Netherlands, or a written warning followed by a further period in Germany, may run into Q4.

### 6.10 Dignity at exit

- The person hears it from their own manager in person, with the HR business partner present, before any email or system change. Never on a Friday afternoon or just before a holiday.
- Access stays until the agreed last day. If there is a specific security reason to end it earlier, the person is told what it is.
- Support starts on the first day of notice: an outplacement or coaching budget, paid time to look for work, a reference that describes the work fairly, and help with internal openings.
- The team hears what it needs to know on the same day, without the details of the person's case.

## 7. Legal steps by country

| | Amsterdam (Netherlands) | Hamburg (Germany) |
|---|---|---|
| Works council and the rating system | A works council is mandatory from 50 employees. Its consent is needed to introduce or change an appraisal system (art. 27(1)(g) WOR) and rules on dismissal (27(1)(e)). A decision taken without consent is void if the council invokes nullity in writing within a month (art. 27(5) WOR) | Only if one has been elected: check. Its consent is needed for general appraisal principles (§94(2) BetrVG); disagreements go to a conciliation board. Ratings made under unagreed principles can't be used for personnel decisions |
| What a court looks at | Dismissal for poor performance goes to the cantonal court. The employee must have been told in good time and given enough chance to improve, and the problem must not come from illness or from a lack of training or poor working conditions (art. 7:669(3)(d) BW). A ranking or a quota is no such evidence | Where the Dismissal Protection Act applies (more than ten employees, more than six months' service), the employee owes their own reasonable best: "do what they should, as well as they can". The employer has to show a clear, long-term shortfall, for example well behind comparable colleagues (Federal Labour Court, 11 December 2003, 2 AZR 667/02). Being in a bottom 10% is not a breach in itself |
| Before an exit | The file: dated feedback, the plan, check-ins, support given | Usually a written warning (Abmahnung) and a further period first. The works council must be heard before every dismissal, or the dismissal is void (§102(1) BetrVG) |
| Mutual agreement | Settlement agreement; the employee has 14 days to withdraw (art. 7:670b BW). A contribution to the employee's legal costs is common | Termination agreement (Aufhebungsvertrag). The employee may lose up to 12 weeks of unemployment benefit after signing one (§159 SGB III), so allow time for independent advice |
| Money | Statutory transition payment: a third of a month's salary per year of service | Severance is negotiated; no general statutory amount |
| Extra protection | Notice can't be given during illness in most cases, and a court checks that a dismissal request isn't connected to illness (art. 7:670 BW). Protection during pregnancy and leave | After more than six weeks of sickness absence in a year, a workplace integration process (BEM, §167(2) SGB IX) is expected first. Severe disability: approval of the integration office (§168 SGB IX). Protection during pregnancy and parental leave |

## 8. Roles

- **CEO:** sponsor; agrees the design; gets the monthly report (figures, not names).
- **CPO:** owns the process; decides exit routes together with the department head.
- **HR business partners:** brief managers, sit in calibration, check every below-expectations rating and every plan before it starts, and join the mid-point reviews.
- **Managers:** write evidence-based reviews, hold the conversations and run the plans.
- **Managers' managers:** hear appeals.
- **Works councils:** consent or co-determination where the law gives it; review the cycle in October.
- **Counsel (Netherlands and Germany):** review this design now, and every case that goes to an exit route.

## 9. What we measure and report

Monthly to the CEO and quarterly to the works councils, at group level only:

- rating distribution by department and location, against last cycle
- improvement plans started, closed, extended, moved and ended in an exit, by department
- time from the first clearly raised concern to a decision
- appeals: how many, and their outcomes
- equality check: share of each rating by group
- regretted attrition, engagement pulse results and internal moves

How to read it: plans clustering under one manager are a reason to look at that manager, the workload and the role first. A department that never raises a concern while its delivery or engagement data show problems gets a calibration question, not a quota.

## 10. Options side by side

| | A: the proposal as asked | B: this design (recommended) | C: change nothing |
|---|---|---|---|
| What happens | About 120 of 1,200 people are put in the bottom category whatever their work; plans; exits by Q3 | Real gaps go on plans; a decision on every case by Q3 | Current process; underperformance stays unaddressed |
| Legal | Works council consent unlikely in both offices. Without it, a decision can be void (Netherlands) and the ratings can't be used (Germany). Courts judge individual performance, so cases without individual evidence are likely to fail or settle at a higher cost | Runs inside the agreed system; cases built on evidence | No new risk |
| Equality | A forced bottom slot is likely to land on the least visible people: part-time staff, people back from leave, new joiners. This has to be checked, and it is a discrimination risk | Checked by group before ratings are final | Unknown; check anyway |
| Cost (estimate) | If 60 of the 120 leave, replacement alone at half to two times salary ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-problem-costs-businesses-trillion.aspx)) comes to roughly €2–8 million, at an assumed average salary of €65,000. Add transition payments, legal costs and the people who leave next | Driven by real cases: HR and manager time, support and the exits that are justified | The cost of tolerated underperformance |
| Teamwork and trust | Colleagues compete to avoid the bottom slot. Microsoft dropped stack ranking in 2013 because it worked against teamwork ([SHRM](https://www.shrm.org/resourcesandtools/hr-topics/compensation/pages/stack-ranking-microsoft.aspx)). Simulated gains come mostly in the first years and depend on reliable ratings ([Scullen et al., 2005](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2005.00904.x)) | Clear standards, honest feedback, a fair chance | Strong performers see poor work tolerated |
| Reversibility | Low: exits and lost trust don't come back | High: it can be tightened next cycle | High |

On the people who stay: in research on layoffs (a related setting, not performance exits), cutting 1% of a workforce was followed by a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year ([Trevor & Nyberg, 2008](https://clipsheet.strategiccommunication.wisc.edu/?p=218008)).

## 11. If the real goal is cost

If the aim is a smaller payroll, that is a restructuring, and it has its own open, legal route. In the Netherlands: the works council's advice (art. 25 WOR), a UWV permit, selection by the reflection principle, and collective-dismissal notification at 20 or more people in one region within three months. In Germany: a reconciliation of interests and a social plan, social selection (§1(3) KSchG) and advance notification above the thresholds (§17 KSchG). It shouldn't run through reviews. Counsel should also say whether exits driven by a share fixed in advance would count as dismissals for reasons not related to the individual, which would bring in the collective-dismissal rules anyway.

## 12. Assumptions, and what would change this design

- The Amsterdam works council exists (it is mandatory from 50 employees). Hamburg may or may not have one.
- The current appraisal system already has works council consent. If it doesn't, consent is needed this cycle anyway.
- No collective agreement sets its own appraisal or dismissal rules. Check.
- Reviews are written in January 2027.
- What would change it: if the baseline data show a large backlog of documented, ignored underperformance, the number of plans may be more than the HR business partners can support, so add capacity rather than cut the process short. If the goal turns out to be cost, see section 11.
