# Manager briefing: performance reviews 2026–27

*For all people managers in Amsterdam and Hamburg. Draft: send it only once the CEO has agreed the process and both works councils have been briefed. Please read it before your briefing session in the week of 11 January 2027. Items in <angle brackets> are for HR to fill in.*

## In one minute

- **We're making reviews honest.** You rate each person's work against what their role requires, back it with examples, and calibrate with other managers so the same rating means the same thing everywhere.
- **There is no quota.** Nobody will ask you to put a set number or share of your team in any category.
- **Where someone's work falls short, say so clearly,** with examples, and agree a plan with real support. Every plan gets a decision by 30 September 2027.
- **You won't be on your own.** Your HR business partner checks every below-expectations rating and every plan before it starts, and joins the mid-point review.

## Evidence: what counts and what never does

**What counts:** dated examples of the work, measured against the role. What was delivered, its quality and timeliness, how the person worked with colleagues and customers, and what they did with feedback. Include strengths, not only gaps.

**What never goes into a review or a rating:**

- sick days, absence or medical information
- part-time hours or a working pattern agreed with the company
- parental, care or other leave, and pregnancy
- age, or anything about someone's private life
- office attendance, logged-in time, activity counts or AI-tool usage
- comparisons with named colleagues ("she's weaker than Tom")
- anything you haven't seen yourself and can't give an example of

If someone was on leave for part of the year, rate the work they did in the time they worked.

## Before you rate someone below expectations

Go through this with your HR business partner:

- [ ] Was the expectation clear, and written down or said plainly?
- [ ] Have I told the person about the concern before, with examples? If not, this review is that first clear conversation, and any plan starts from today.
- [ ] Have I looked at the work itself: workload, unclear priorities, missing tools or training, a change in the team, my own part?
- [ ] Could there be a health or personal reason? I don't ask about it. I tell the person they can see the company doctor (bedrijfsarts or Betriebsarzt), and I tell my HR business partner.
- [ ] Do I have two or three dated examples that show the gap?

## Calibration: what to expect

- It aligns standards and tests the evidence. **There is no target shape and no number you have to hit.**
- You'll be asked for evidence for both your high and your low ratings. "Everyone in my team exceeds" gets the same questions as "this person is below expectations". A team with no concerns is fine if the evidence shows it.
- If you have a personal relationship with someone being discussed, say so and step out of that part.
- Ratings stay provisional until HR has checked them by group (gender, age, part-time, leave, location). If a pattern shows up, cases get a second look before ratings are final.
- What's said in calibration stays in calibration.

## The review conversation

Plan 45 to 60 minutes, in person or by live video, in a private space. Never by email, chat or a system notification first.

**Structure**

1. Open: what the conversation is for, and that they'll get the written review afterwards and can comment on it.
2. Ask first: "How do you look back on this year? What went well, and what was hard?"
3. Your view, with examples: strengths first and specific, then any gap.
4. Agree what happens next: goals for 2027, development, and a plan where there is a gap.
5. Close: their right to add comments and to appeal within 10 working days, and when you'll next talk.

**Lines you can use**

*Strong year:*
"Your work on <project> made a real difference: <specific result>. That's why I've rated you <rating>. I'd like to talk about what you want to take on next."

*Solid year:*
"You're doing what the role needs, and doing it well. <Two examples.> Let's talk about where you'd like to grow in 2027."

*A gap:*
"I want to be straight with you about one part of your work, because you deserve to hear it clearly from me. The role needs <expectation>. In <month> and <month>, <example>, and in <month>, <example>. That's below what the role requires, and I've rated it that way. Before we talk about a plan, I want to hear how you see it. Is there something in the work, the priorities or the support you get that's getting in the way?"

Then:
"Here's what I'd like to do: agree together what good looks like over the next <90> days, what support you'll get from me and others, and check in every two weeks. My aim is for this plan to close because the gap is closed."

*If they ask whether they're being ranked against colleagues:*
"No. You're rated on your work against what your role needs, and every manager does the same."

*If they ask whether they'll be fired:*
"There's no decision about your job. What I want is for this to work, and the plan is how we get there. If at the end it hasn't worked, we'll look at the options together with HR, including other roles. You'll always know where you stand."

*If they mention health or something personal:*
"Thank you for telling me. You don't have to tell me more. I'd suggest talking to the company doctor, and I'll make sure we take it into account in the plan. I'll let HR know you raised it, unless you'd rather tell them yourself."

*If they're upset or disagree:*
"I can see this is hard to hear. You don't have to respond now. Please add your view to the written review, and if you disagree with the rating you can appeal within 10 working days. Shall we talk again on <day>?"

## The improvement plan

Write it together with the person and your HR business partner, within two weeks of the conversation.

```markdown
# Improvement plan: <name>, <role>

**The gap:** <what the role requires, and where the work falls short, with two or three dated examples>
**What we checked first:** <workload, clarity of expectations, tools, team situation, my own part as manager; no health information here>
**What good looks like by the end:** <specific, observable, achievable in the period>
**Support we give:** <coaching, training, time, a buddy, adjusted tasks>
**Period and check-ins:** <90 days by default; 60 only if the gap is narrow and was raised clearly before; up to 120 for complex roles. A check-in every two weeks; a mid-point review with HR. Holidays and sick leave pause the clock>
**Possible outcomes:** the plan is closed because the gap is closed; extended once, with reasons; a move to another role inside the company; only then other steps, with HR and counsel
**Signed:** <manager, HR business partner; employee to confirm receipt>, with the employee's own comments
```

**Running the check-ins**

- Keep them in the calendar. A cancelled check-in counts against the company, not the employee.
- Each time, write down three things: what improved, what hasn't yet, and what support you're adding. Share the notes with the person.
- Give the support you promised, on time. If you can't, tell HR straight away.
- If the person falls ill, pause the plan and talk to HR.

## What not to do

- Don't mention numbers, quotas, targets or exits to your team, or hint at them.
- Don't decide or promise an outcome either way, and don't offer anyone a deal to leave. Exit routes are decided by the department head and the CPO, with legal advice, and only after a plan.
- Don't discuss anyone's rating or plan with peers outside calibration.
- Don't send the written review before the conversation.
- Don't ask about health, diagnosis, pregnancy or family plans.
- Don't take work, access or meetings away from someone as a signal.
- Don't look for legal answers in search engines or ask other managers. Ask your HR business partner.

## Questions your team may ask

**"Is there a forced curve? Do 10% have to get a low rating?"**
No. There's no quota. People are rated on their own work against their role.

**"Is this a layoff in disguise?"**
No. This process is about each person's performance. Any reduction in jobs would be a separate process, run openly with the works councils under the rules in each country.

**"Can I see what's written about me?"**
Yes. You get your written review after the conversation, you can add your own comments, and you can ask HR for the personal data held about you.

**"What if I disagree with my rating?"**
You can appeal within 10 working days, to your manager's manager together with an HR business partner who wasn't in the calibration. You'll get a written answer with reasons within 15 working days.

**"I was part-time, on leave or ill for part of the year. Will that count against me?"**
No. You're rated on the work you did in the time you worked.

**"How does my rating affect my pay or bonus?"**
<HR to fill in: how ratings feed into the 2027 pay round, with a link to the published pay rules.>

**"What happens if I'm put on a plan?"**
You'll get a clear description of the gap, what good looks like, the support you'll get and the time you have, with check-ins every two weeks. Most plans are meant to close because the gap closes.

## Questions managers often ask

**"Nobody in my team is below expectations. Will I be pushed to find someone?"**
No. You'll be asked for evidence, as for every rating. If the evidence supports it, that's the answer.

**"I've had concerns about someone all year but never said it."**
Say it now, clearly and with examples. The plan starts from this conversation. Consequences can't reach back to a time when the person didn't know there was a problem.

**"I think the problem is the workload, or that I've been unclear."**
Then fix that first, and tell your HR business partner. That's a good outcome of a review.

**"Someone in my team is pregnant, on leave or often ill."**
Rate the work they did in the time they worked, and talk to your HR business partner before the conversation. Extra legal protection applies in both countries.

**"Can't I just agree an exit with someone who's struggling?"**
Not on your own. If someone raises leaving themselves, listen, don't promise anything, and bring in HR.
