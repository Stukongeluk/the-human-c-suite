I've designed the process and the manager briefing, but not in the form your CEO described. A fixed 10% per manager, a 60-day plan and exit by end of Q3 would likely be void, unenforceable or very expensive in both the Netherlands and Germany. It also wouldn't deliver the performance gain he expects. My design keeps his goals: a higher bar, honest differentiation, real consequences for real underperformance, and decisions by Q3. It drops the three parts that cause the problems.

## Why the version as specified breaks

1. **The works councils have to agree first.**
   - **Netherlands:** a new or changed appraisal system needs the OR's consent, and so does a change to dismissal policy (Art. 27(1)(g) and (e) WOR). If you implement without consent, the OR can declare the decision void within a month.
   - **Germany:** if Hamburg has a Betriebsrat, appraisal principles need its consent (§ 94(2) BetrVG). If you can't agree, a conciliation board decides. Any dismissal made without first hearing the Betriebsrat is void (§ 102).
   - Nothing new can go to managers before this happens.
2. **A quota isn't evidence.**
   - **Netherlands:** dismissal for underperformance goes through the kantonrechter. The court has to be satisfied that the person is actually unfit for the role, was told in time, had a real chance to improve, wasn't let down on training, and can't be redeployed.
   - **Germany:** the courts treat someone as a "low performer" at roughly one-third below comparable colleagues over a longer period, and a formal warning is needed first.
   - "The manager had to put 10% somewhere" is the opposite of that evidence. A Dutch court that sees a file built to fill a quota can award extra compensation on top of the statutory payment.
   - Small teams make it arbitrary: a team of six strong engineers still has to name someone.
3. **You don't control the timeline.**
   - People put on a quota-driven plan often report sick. In the Netherlands that generally blocks dismissal while they're ill.
   - German notice periods run up to 7 months. Dismissals are often challenged, and losing means reinstatement with back pay.
   - Clustered Hamburg exits can trigger mass-dismissal notification. Employer-initiated settlements count, and for a site of 60–499 people the threshold is 10%, or more than 25 people, within 30 days.
   - By end of Q3 you'd mostly have the people who took a package, which is the expensive way to get there.

It also doesn't work well on its own terms. The best-known simulation of rank-and-yank found gains of about 16% a year in the first two years, falling to about 2% by year six. After the first round you're cutting solid people. It may well have worked at his last company in year one, especially under at-will employment. Microsoft dropped stack ranking in 2013 and moved to reviews focused on teamwork, which matters a lot in software.

## What I designed instead

- **Calibrated, evidence-based ratings with no team quota.**
  - There's a clear "Does not meet" category with a written evidence standard.
  - Calibration panels challenge managers in both directions. That includes managers who rate nobody low, which is probably the real problem your CEO is reacting to.
  - Option C adds a department-level reference range as a sense check. It never decides an individual rating.
- **A real improvement plan.**
  - 90 days by default. 60 days only for roles with short-cycle, measurable output and shorter tenure, signed off by HR and counsel.
  - Written goals, real support and check-ins every two weeks.
  - Optionally, people can choose an agreed exit at the start instead of the plan.
- **Exits decided case by case, quickly.** The HRBP and counsel review every exit, and the Dutch and German legal routes are spelled out.
- **A timeline that gets decisions done in Q3.**
  - Works council consent December to January, reviews and calibration February to March.
  - Plans April to June, outcome decisions in July, agreed exits completed in Q3.
  - Contested cases may run into Q4.
  - Managers can start individual plans now for underperformance that's already documented, under your current policy. That doesn't need a new system, and it gives your CEO visible action this quarter.

## Assumptions to check

- **Review cycle.** I've assumed it runs January to March. If it has already started, run this year on your current system with stronger calibration and individual plans, and apply the new design next cycle.
- **Works councils and agreements.** I've assumed you have an OR in Amsterdam, possibly a Betriebsrat in Hamburg, and no collective agreement governing appraisals. A European Works Council may also be in scope at your size; counsel should check.
- **Cost figures.** The figures in the process document (roughly €35–100k per exit, so €2–6M for the CEO's version) are illustrative. Replace them with your payroll data before they reach the CEO.
