# Performance review cycle 2026/27: process design

**Status:** Draft for CEO decision, employment counsel review and works council consultation. Not for managers yet.
**Owner:** Chief People Officer
**Scope:** All employees in Amsterdam and Hamburg (about 1,200)

This is not legal advice. Points marked **[counsel]** need confirmation from Dutch and German employment counsel. Section 14 lists the questions.

---

## 1. What this design changes from the original request

| CEO's request | This design | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Every manager puts 10% of the team in the bottom category | No team quota. There's a written evidence standard for the bottom category. Calibration challenges managers in both directions, including those who rate nobody low. Optional department-level reference range (option C). | A quota is not evidence of underperformance in either country. Small teams make a fixed percentage arbitrary. The system needs works council consent, and a quota makes consent unlikely. |
| 60-day improvement plan | 90 days by default. 60 days only for roles with short-cycle, measurable output and shorter tenure, with HR and counsel sign-off. In the Netherlands, consider longer plans for senior or long-serving staff. | Dutch courts judge the plan against the role and the person's tenure. German law needs a warning and a real chance to improve. |
| Anyone who doesn't improve leaves by end of Q3 | Outcome decisions in July. Agreed exits completed in Q3. Contested cases follow the local legal route. | Sickness protection, notice periods, court routes and works council hearings set the timing, not us. |

What stays the same: a higher bar, honest differentiation, consequences for real underperformance, and decisions by Q3.

## 2. Principles

1. **Ratings follow evidence, not a target.** A rating describes performance against the role's expectations, not against teammates.
2. **Calibrate hard, in both directions.** Inflated ratings are challenged as firmly as harsh ones.
3. **Clear, early and fair.** Anyone rated "does not meet" hears it plainly, with examples, and gets real support and a fair chance to close the gap.
4. **Exits are individual decisions.** They're made quickly, documented properly and reviewed before they happen.
5. **One standard, two legal routes.** The same rating standard applies in Amsterdam and Hamburg. Outcomes follow Dutch or German law.

## 3. Rating scale and definitions

Keep your current scale if it already has a clear bottom category, because every change widens what the works councils must consent to. If it doesn't have one, use this reference scale:

| Rating | Definition | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **Exceeds** | Delivers well above what the role and level require, in outcomes and in how they work with others. | Recognition and reward, stretch opportunities |
| **Meets** | Fully delivers what the role and level require. Most people should be here, and it's a good rating. | Normal development |
| **Partially meets** | Clear gaps in some outcomes or behaviours, but overall contribution isn't seriously below the role. | Development plan managed by the manager, reviewed after 3 months. No formal process. |
| **Does not meet** | Material, sustained gaps in core outcomes or behaviours for the role. | Diagnosis, then a formal improvement plan, redeployment or an agreed exit (see §5) |
| **Not rated** | Joined or changed role less than 6 months ago, or absent for more than half the review period | Normal feedback, no rating |

**Evidence standard for "Does not meet"** (all must be true):

- At least three specific, dated examples tied to the role's agreed expectations (goals, job profile, level framework).
- Measured against what is expected at that level, not against the rest of the team.
- The manager has considered other causes: unclear expectations, workload, tools, training, manager support, health and personal circumstances.
- It records whether the gap was raised before. If it wasn't, the rating conversation is the first notice and the improvement plan must allow for that.

**Special cases:**

- **Part-time staff:** judge them against goals scaled to their agreed hours. Lower output because of fewer hours is not underperformance. Unequal treatment of part-timers is unlawful in both countries.
- **Parental, sick or other leave:** rate the period actually worked. Absence itself counts neither for nor against.
- **Recently promoted or moved:** no "Does not meet" in the first 6 months without HRBP review.
- **Disability or chronic illness:** consider reasonable adjustments before any rating consequence.
- **Works council members and other protected employees:** rate normally. HRBP and counsel review any low rating before it's shared.

## 4. Calibration

**Format:** one session per department (about 30–100 people), chaired by the HRBP, with the department head and all people managers. Hold at least one cross-site session per function so Amsterdam and Hamburg use the same yardstick.

**Inputs from each manager:**

- proposed ratings
- a one-paragraph evidence summary for every "Exceeds", "Partially meets" and "Does not meet"
- goals, and peer or stakeholder input where you already collect it
- last year's rating

**Challenge questions the chair asks:**

- "What would this person need to do differently to be rated one level higher or lower?"
- "Walk us through the three people in your team furthest from what their role requires. Where are they against the standard?" (The answer can be "Meets". The point is to look, not to find someone.)
- "Is this rating based on the whole period, or on the last few weeks?"
- "Would you rate this the same way if the person worked full-time, hadn't been on leave, or was more like you?"
- "Has this person heard any of this before?"

**Reference range (option C only):** a department-level range agreed with the CEO and works councils, for example 5–10% "Partially meets" or "Does not meet" combined, shown only for departments of 50 or more people. If a department is well outside it, the chair asks for evidence. A rating never changes because of the range, only because of evidence.

**Outputs:** final ratings signed off by the department head and HRBP, with every change logged and the reason given.

**Bias check before ratings are released:** People Analytics compares the share of low ratings by gender, age band, part-time status, site and recent leave. It adds other characteristics only where we hold the data lawfully, uses aggregated data only, and checks the processing basis with the data protection officer. Any group with a materially higher share is reviewed case by case before ratings are communicated. Aggregated results go to the works councils.

## 5. From rating to action

Within a week of calibration, the manager and HRBP work out the cause for every "Does not meet":

| Likely cause | Route |
|---|---|
| Health-related | Occupational health route (bedrijfsarts in NL, Betriebsarzt / BEM in DE). Not an improvement plan. |
| Wrong role or skills mismatch | Explore redeployment or a role change first |
| Capability or behaviour gap in the current role | Formal improvement plan (§6) |
| Employee says they'd rather leave | Agreed-terms conversation with HR (§6, choice model) |
| Unclear expectations or poor support | Fix that first, then a development plan rather than a formal plan |

"Partially meets" leads to a development plan owned by the manager, not a formal process.

## 6. Improvement plan

**Contents (written, agreed at the start):**

1. The specific gaps, with examples.
2. The standard expected by the end, which must be measurable, achievable for a reasonable person in the role, and the same standard peers at that level are held to.
3. The support we'll provide: training, coaching, a mentor, manager time, and workload adjustment where needed.
4. Check-ins every two weeks. A written summary goes to the employee within two working days, and the employee can respond in writing.
5. A midpoint review with the HRBP.
6. What happens at the end, stated plainly: if the standard isn't met, the next step may be redeployment or ending employment.

**Duration:**

- 90 days by default.
- 60 days only where output is short-cycle and measurable and tenure is short, with HRBP and counsel approval.
- Netherlands: consider longer (often 3–6 months in practice) for senior or long-serving staff, because courts expect more of a large employer and for longer service **[counsel]**.

**Quality gate:** the HRBP reviews every plan before it starts and asks: would an independent judge see a genuine chance to succeed? If the answer is no, the plan doesn't start.

**Pauses:** if the employee reports sick, the plan pauses and occupational health takes over. Managers don't discuss the plan with someone who is off sick.

**Outcomes:**

- **Met:** plan closed, with a 3-month follow-up.
- **Substantially improved but not fully met:** one extension of up to 60 days.
- **Not met:** HRBP and counsel review, then the exit routes in §7.

**Health signal:** we track the share of plans that succeed. If almost none succeed, the plans are being used as exit paperwork. A court will see that too.

**Choice model (optional, CPO decision):** at the start of a plan, HR may offer a choice between the supported plan and leaving on agreed terms. Safeguards:

- The offer is written and made by HR, not the manager.
- There's no deadline pressure: at least two weeks to decide, plus the statutory cooling-off period in NL.
- We contribute to the employee's legal advice.
- Declining has no effect on how the plan is run.

This lets people who'd rather go leave early, with dignity, and it helps the Q3 timeline. It is only fair if the rating behind it is sound.

## 7. Exit routes by country

No exit happens without a review by the manager, skip-level manager, HRBP and employment counsel. That review checks: the evidence, the plan record, protected status, redeployment options and the overall bias picture across all exits.

### Netherlands

| Route | What it takes | Timing and cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Agreed exit** (vaststellingsovereenkomst) | The employee's agreement. A statutory 14-day cooling-off period. Structured so the employee keeps unemployment benefit (employer initiative, notice period observed) **[counsel]**. We usually contribute to legal fees. | Fastest route. Costs at least the statutory transition payment, usually more where the file is weak. |
| **Court** (kantonrechter, ground d: unfitness) | Unfitness for the role, not caused by illness. Told in time. A sufficient chance to improve. Not caused by lack of training or poor working conditions. No suitable redeployment within the notice period. | Typically a few months from filing to decision, plus notice **[counsel]**. Transition payment of 1/3 monthly salary per year of service (capped at €102,000 in 2026, or one year's salary if higher). Extra "fair compensation" if the court finds the employer acted seriously culpably, which is a real risk with quota-driven files. A combination of incomplete grounds (ground i) can add up to 50% more transition payment. |
| **Blockers** | Sickness (dismissal is generally prohibited during the first two years of illness), pregnancy and maternity leave, works council membership, and other protected situations. | The plan and the process pause. |

### Germany

| Route | What it takes | Timing and cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Agreed exit** (Aufhebungsvertrag) | Written form. Counsel structures it to limit the employee's unemployment-benefit suspension risk (Sperrzeit) **[counsel]**. | Severance is negotiated. The common reference point is 0.5 monthly salary per year of service, and it's higher where a dismissal would be weak. |
| **Ordinary dismissal** for conduct or personal reasons (KSchG applies after 6 months' service) | Conduct route: culpable underperformance and a formal warning (Abmahnung) that didn't lead to improvement. Personal route: sustained underperformance (case law uses roughly one-third below comparable colleagues), a negative forecast, and no milder option such as training or redeployment. Both routes need a balancing of interests, and the works council must be heard before notice (§ 102 BetrVG), or the dismissal is void. Notice must be in writing. | Notice of 1–7 months depending on tenure (§ 622 BGB). The employee has 3 weeks to file a claim (§ 4 KSchG). If we lose, employment continues with back pay. Many cases settle at the first court hearing. |
| **Blockers and extra steps** | Pregnancy and maternity, parental leave, severe disability (consent from the Integrationsamt), works council members, carer's leave. Mass-dismissal notification if employer-initiated exits, including agreed exits, cross the § 17 KSchG thresholds within 30 days (for a site of 60–499 people: 10% or more than 25 people). | Spread decisions over time and check thresholds before issuing notices or agreements **[counsel]**. |

## 8. Works council route

Start informally in early December, before the formal request. Be open about the goal and show the data. Offer monitoring rights, such as aggregated distributions, bias-check results and plan outcomes each cycle. That offer does more than anything else to make consent likely.

**Netherlands (ondernemingsraad):**

- Written consent request covering personnel assessment (Art. 27(1)(g) WOR) and, if exit rules change, dismissal policy (Art. 27(1)(e)). Include the regulation text, reasons and expected consequences.
- The OR decides after at least one consultation meeting. Allow 4–8 weeks.
- Without consent, the OR can invoke nullity within a month. The employer can ask the court for replacement consent, but only if the OR's refusal is unreasonable or the decision is needed for weighty business reasons, and it takes time.

**Germany (Betriebsrat, if one exists in Hamburg):**

- Appraisal principles: consent under § 94(2) BetrVG, recorded in a works agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung). If there's no agreement, the conciliation board decides, which takes weeks to months.
- Also relevant:
  - § 87(1) No. 6 if software is used to track performance
  - § 87(1) No. 10 if ratings drive pay
  - § 95 if we adopt selection guidelines for dismissals
  - § 102 hearing before every dismissal
  - § 82(2): employees may bring a works council member to their assessment discussion
- Allow 6–10 weeks if the talks are constructive.

**Also check [counsel]:** any collective agreement (CAO or Tarifvertrag) covering appraisal. Also check whether a European Works Council exists or could be requested. The company may be above the thresholds (1,000+ employees in the EU, 150+ in each of two countries), and a cross-border policy may need to be shared with it.

| When | What |

## 10. Roles and decision rights

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| CEO | Sets the bar and sponsors the change. Agrees the option (B or C) and the non-negotiables. Doesn't decide individual cases. |
| CPO | Owns the process. Final escalation on disputed calibrations. |
| HRBPs | Chair calibration. Run the plan quality gate. Co-sign every exit review. |
| Employment counsel (NL and DE) | Review the design, the works council documents and every exit before it happens |
| Department heads | Co-own calibration outcomes and sign off final ratings |
| Managers | Evidence-based ratings, honest conversations, run plans, keep records |
| Skip-level managers | Review every "Does not meet" and every exit |
| Works councils | Consent. Receive aggregated monitoring reports. |
| Occupational health | Handle any health-related case |

## 11. Cost comparison (illustrative)

These are planning assumptions, not benchmarks. Replace them with payroll data before using the figures. Assumptions: average gross salary about €6,000/month, average tenure 4 years. For option A: 120 people in the bottom category, half of whom exit.

| Cost per exit | Low | High | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Severance or settlement | €8k | €25k | NL transition payment about €8k (1/3 × €6k × 4 years) as the floor, more where the file is weak. DE reference 0.5 × monthly salary × years (about €12k), up to 1.0 or more in weak cases. |
| Paid notice or garden leave | €6k | €12k | 1–2 months released from work |
| Legal and HR cost | €2k | €10k | Counsel review, more if litigated |
| Recruiting the replacement | €10k | €20k | Agency fee or internal recruiting cost |
| Vacancy and ramp-up | €10k | €30k | 3–6 months at reduced productivity |
| **Total per exit** | **~€35k** | **~€100k** | |

- **Option A (60 exits):** about €2–6M, plus management time for 120 plans (roughly 4,000 hours of manager and HR time). Not included: regretted attrition among strong performers, back pay if German cases are lost, and the cost of a works council dispute.
- **Options B and C:** the same cost per exit, but only for people with documented gaps. Settlement premiums are likely lower because the files are stronger, and nobody is exited just to fill a number.

## 12. How we'll know it worked

| Measure | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Rating distribution by department, site and manager | Clear movement from last year's inflated pattern, and consistency across sites |
| Bias check on ratings and exits | No group with a materially higher share of low ratings that the evidence doesn't explain |
| Improvement plan outcomes | A meaningful share succeed. Near zero means the plans are paperwork. |
| Regretted attrition (Exceeds and Meets) in the 6 months after reviews | Flat or down. This is the guardrail. |
| Quality and time-to-fill of replacements | Replacements rated Meets or better after 6 months |
| Fairness items in the engagement survey | Stable or up |
| Legal disputes and outcomes | Few disputes, few losses |
| Team-level delivery measures | Up over the year. Measured at team level, not individual. |

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Managers keep inflating ratings | Calibration challenge questions, department heads co-own outcomes, cross-site sessions, and the reference range in option C |
| Managers use "Does not meet" to clear out people they find difficult | Evidence standard, skip-level review, bias check, and every exit reviewed by counsel |
| Sick reports during plans | Plans designed as genuinely fair, occupational health route, plan pauses. Managers trained not to escalate. |
| Strong performers leave because the process feels threatening | No quota, clear communication that "Meets" is a good rating, and attrition tracked as a guardrail |
| Works councils refuse or delay | Early informal briefing, monitoring rights, and the honest design (B or C) |
| The CEO sees this as softening | Weekly progress view, Q3 decision milestones, and metrics that show the bar rising |
| Ratings that drive pay face scrutiny under incoming EU pay-transparency rules | The evidence standard and bias check make the criteria defensible **[counsel]** |

## 14. Questions for employment counsel

1. NL: what improvement-plan length is defensible for our main role families and tenure bands?
2. NL: does adding calibration, a reference range or a new bottom category require OR consent under Art. 27(1)(g) and (e)? What's the realistic timeline?
3. NL: how should agreed exits be structured so employees keep unemployment benefit, and what settlement levels are typical for files of this quality?
4. DE: do we have, or are we likely to face, a Betriebsrat in Hamburg? What does a § 94(2) works agreement need to contain?
5. DE: for typical roles, can performance be measured objectively enough for a conduct-based or personal-reason dismissal, or is an agreed exit the realistic route?
6. DE: how should agreed exits be structured to limit Sperrzeit risk?
7. Both: could clustered exits trigger collective-redundancy duties (§ 17 KSchG, WMCO, EU Directive 98/59/EC), especially if the reason looks like a target rather than individual performance?
8. Both: is there a collective agreement covering appraisal, and is a European Works Council in scope?
9. Both: what processing basis, retention period and access rules apply to calibration notes and bias-check data under GDPR?
