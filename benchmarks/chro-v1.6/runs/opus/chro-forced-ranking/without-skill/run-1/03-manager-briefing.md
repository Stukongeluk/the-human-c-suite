## 1. The short version

- **We're raising the bar.** This year's ratings must reflect real performance against the role, and they'll be calibrated hard across Amsterdam and Hamburg.
- **There's no quota.** Nobody has to put a set percentage of their team in any category. You do have to back every rating with evidence, and you'll be challenged if your ratings look too generous or too harsh.
- **"Meets" is a good rating.** Most people should be there.
- **"Does not meet" leads to action.** That means a clear conversation, a fair improvement plan with real support, and a decision at the end. You don't run this alone: your HR business partner (HRBP) is with you at every step.
- **No exit happens without review.** Exits are decided case by case, after a review by your skip-level manager, your HRBP and employment counsel.

## 2. Why we're doing this

For several years almost everyone has been rated "meets" or better. That doesn't help anyone:

- People with real gaps don't hear about them until it's too late to fix them.
- Strong performers don't see their contribution recognised.
- Problems that a team has to work around are left unaddressed for too long.

This cycle we're fixing that by being honest and specific with everyone, not by hitting a number.

## 4. Rating well

**The scale:**

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Exceeds | Delivers well above what the role and level require, in outcomes and in how they work with others |
| Meets | Fully delivers what the role and level require. This is a good rating. |
| Partially meets | Clear gaps in some areas, but not seriously below the role. Leads to a development plan you manage, with no formal process. |
| Does not meet | Material, sustained gaps in core outcomes or behaviours for the role. Leads to formal action, with your HRBP. |
| Not rated | Joined or changed role less than 6 months ago, or absent for more than half the period |

**Rate against the role, not the team.** Ask "is this person delivering what someone at this level in this role should?", not "who's weakest in my team?". In a strong team, everyone can be "Meets" or better.

**Evidence for a "Does not meet"** (your HRBP will check all four):

1. At least three specific, dated examples tied to the person's goals, job profile or level expectations.
2. Measured against the standard for their level, not against colleagues.
3. You've thought about other causes: unclear expectations, workload, tools, training, your own support, health and personal circumstances.
4. You note whether you've raised this before. If you haven't, say so honestly. The plan will allow for it.

**Check yourself for these common errors:**

- **Recency:** rating the last six weeks instead of the whole year.
- **Halo or horns:** one big success or failure colouring everything.
- **Similarity:** rating people who work like you more generously.
- **Visibility:** confusing loud with effective, or quiet with weak.
- **Penalising part-time work or leave:** judge part-timers against goals for their agreed hours. For anyone who was on leave, rate the time they actually worked. Treating part-timers or people on leave worse is unlawful, not just unfair.

**Talk to your HRBP before rating** anyone who:

- was recently promoted or moved
- has a known health condition or disability
- is pregnant, on or just back from parental leave
- is a works council member

You still rate them normally. The HRBP makes sure the process protects them and us.

## 5. Calibration

**Bring:**

- your proposed ratings
- a short evidence summary for every rating other than "Meets"
- each person's goals and last year's rating

**Expect questions like these:**

- "What would this person need to do differently to move one level up or down?"
- "Walk us through the three people furthest from what their role requires. Where are they against the standard?" It's fine if the answer is "Meets". The point is that you looked.
- "Is this the whole year, or the last few weeks?"
- "Would you rate this the same way if they were full-time, hadn't been on leave, or worked more like you?"
- "Has this person heard any of this before?"

*[Option C only]* **Reference range:** for departments of 50+ people, we show a reference range for how many "Partially meets" and "Does not meet" ratings we'd typically expect. It's a prompt to look harder, not a target. **No rating is changed to fit the range. Ratings change only on evidence.**

**After calibration:** ratings may change. If yours do, your HRBP will tell you why. Don't share any rating until final ratings are released and the bias check is done.

## 6. The "Does not meet" conversation

**Before:**

- Agree the message and the next step with your HRBP.
- Book 45–60 minutes in a private room or a camera-on video call. Avoid Fridays and the day before someone's holiday.
- Have the examples written down.

**In the conversation:**

1. **Get to it quickly.** "I want to talk about your rating and what happens next. Your overall rating is 'Does not meet expectations'."
2. **Be specific.** "Here's what I've seen: [example, date, impact]. The standard for your role is [expectation]."
3. **Listen.** "How do you see it? Is there anything I'm missing, at work or outside it, that's affecting this?" If they raise health, stress or personal issues, take them seriously and tell your HRBP.
4. **Explain the next step.** "We're going to put together a plan with clear goals and real support, so you have a fair chance to close the gap. Your HRBP and I will go through it with you within [a week]."
5. **Be honest about the stakes, once and calmly.** "I want you to succeed. I also have to be clear that if the gaps aren't closed, this could ultimately affect your employment here."
6. **Close.** "This is a lot to take in. You can come back to me with questions, and you're welcome to bring someone you trust to the next meeting." In Germany, the employee may bring a Betriebsrat member to a discussion of their performance assessment.

**After:** within two working days, send a short written summary of what was said and agreed. Copy your HRBP.

**Never say:**

- "HR told me I had to put someone in the bottom." This isn't true, and it destroys the rating.
- "We need to cut people" or "there's a target."
- "This is just a formality" or "the decision has already been made."
- "You'd be better off taking a package." Only HR discusses exit terms.
- Anything about the person's age, health, pregnancy, family situation, part-time hours, nationality or works council role as a reason for the rating.

**Assume anything you say or write could be read out in court.** That includes chat messages, emails, calendar invites and notes. Never write about "managing someone out". In the Netherlands, employees may lawfully record conversations they take part in. Stick to facts, examples and next steps.

## 7. Running an improvement plan

Your HRBP drafts the plan with you. It must include:

- **The gaps:** specific, with examples.
- **The standard:** what "good enough" looks like by the end. It must be measurable, realistic for a reasonable person in the role, and the same standard as peers at that level.
- **The support:** training, coaching, a mentor, time with you, and lighter workload if needed. You must actually provide it.
- **Check-ins:** every two weeks. Send a written summary within two working days. The employee can reply in writing.
- **Duration:** usually 90 days. Your HRBP will confirm.
- **The end:** what happens if the standard is or isn't met.

**During the plan:**

- **Give the support you promised.** Missed coaching sessions or cancelled check-ins weaken the plan, both for the person and in any later legal review.
- **Recognise progress honestly.** If they're improving, say so. The goal is for the plan to succeed.
- **If they report sick, the plan pauses.** Don't contact them about performance. Occupational health and your HRBP take over.
- **If they ask whether they're "being managed out",** answer truthfully: "No decision has been made. The plan is real, and so is the support. What happens at the end depends on whether the goals are met."
- **If they ask about leaving on agreed terms,** don't negotiate or speculate. Say "That's a fair question. I'll ask HR to talk to you about it," and tell your HRBP the same day.

**At the end:**

- **Goals met:** close the plan, tell them clearly, and follow up after 3 months.
- **Substantial progress but not fully there:** your HRBP may extend it once.
- **Not met:** your HRBP, your skip-level manager and employment counsel review the case before any decision. You'll be told the outcome and how to communicate it. Don't announce anything yourself.

## 8. What you must not do

- Don't put a percentage of your team in any category to hit a number.
- Don't share ratings before calibration and release.
- Don't start an improvement plan without your HRBP.
- Don't discuss exits, settlements or packages. That's HR only.
- Don't contact someone on sick leave about performance.
- Don't record conversations. In Germany, secretly recording a conversation is a criminal offence.
- Don't discuss individual ratings with anyone other than your skip-level manager, HRBP and the calibration group.
- Don't change the goals or the standard partway through a plan without your HRBP.

## 9. FAQ

**Do I have to put someone in "Does not meet"?**
No. You have to rate honestly and back each rating with evidence. If your whole team really meets expectations, that's the right answer, and calibration will ask you to show it.

**My team is small. How does a reference range apply?**
It doesn't. The range (if used) applies only to departments of 50 or more people.

**Someone has been rated "Meets" for years but isn't performing. Can I rate them "Does not meet" now?**
Yes, if the evidence supports it. Be honest that this is the first time they're hearing it clearly. The plan will give them proper time, and your HRBP may suggest a longer one.

**What if the person disagrees with their rating?**
They can put their view in writing, and it's attached to the review. They can also ask your skip-level manager or HRBP to review it. In Germany, they can involve the Betriebsrat.

**What if someone resigns after a low rating?**
Accept it normally, tell your HRBP, and don't encourage or discourage it.

**Are Amsterdam and Hamburg handled differently?**
Ratings use the same standard in both offices. What happens after a plan follows Dutch or German law, and your HRBP handles that.
