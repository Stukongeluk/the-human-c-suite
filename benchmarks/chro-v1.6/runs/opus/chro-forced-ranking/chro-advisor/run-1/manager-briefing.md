# Manager Briefing: This Year's Performance Reviews

**For:** all people managers in Amsterdam and Hamburg
**Format:** a 75-minute session (HRBP-led), with this document as the pack
**Status:** DRAFT. Issue it only once the review design is approved, Dutch and German counsel have signed off, and works-council consent (Amsterdam) and agreement (Hamburg, if applicable) are in place. Fill in the dates in [brackets] before issuing.

---

## 1. Why we are doing this

We are raising the bar on two things: **how honestly we rate** and **how quickly we act** when someone is not meeting the standard of their role.

- When everyone is rated "meets", the rating tells people nothing, and our best people notice.
- When underperformance goes unaddressed, the colleagues around that person carry the load, and they are often the ones who leave.

So this year every rating has to be backed by evidence. Every low rating gets a clear plan within 30 days. Every plan ends on time, with a real decision at the end.

This is not a target exercise. Nobody has a quota for their team. Your job is to rate each person honestly against what their role requires, and to back it up.

---

## 2. What is changing this year

1. **Role standards, not rankings.** Rate each person against the standard for their role and level (see the rating anchors for your job family), not against their teammates.
2. **Evidence for every rating.** For each person, bring at least three concrete examples from across the year. Ratings of 1, 2 and 5 need skip-level approval.
3. **Calibration that goes both ways.** You will be challenged on low ratings without evidence, and equally on teams where everyone is rated "meets" or above while team results say otherwise.
4. **Low ratings lead to action within 30 days.** That is a documented feedback phase or an improvement plan, with HR support.
5. **Plans are real.** The outcome of an improvement plan is not decided in advance. If someone improves, that is a success.

**About the numbers:** we use a company-wide expected range to spot rating inflation across functions of about 50 people or more. Roughly 10–17% of people are expected at ratings 1–2. It is **not** a target for your team. A team of 7 might honestly have nobody below "meets", or two people. What matters is that you can show your evidence.

## 4. How to rate

**The scale**

| Rating | What it means |
|---|---|
| 5 Exceptional | Consistently well beyond the role; sets the standard for others |
| 4 Exceeds | Regularly beyond the role standard |
| 3 Meets | Delivers the role standard reliably. This is a good rating, not a disappointing one. |
| 2 Needs improvement | Meets some but not all of the role standard |
| 1 Does not meet | Clearly below the role standard on outcomes or behaviour |
| Too new to rate | Less than 6 months in the role |
| Insufficient period | Worked less than half the cycle (for example because of leave). Talk to your HRBP. |

**Evidence rules**

- Use at least three concrete examples covering the whole year, not just the last two months.
- Base them on outcomes and observable behaviour: what was delivered, by when, at what quality, and how the person worked with others.
- Compare the person to the role standard. Do not compare them to a named colleague.
- **Periods of leave** (parental, sick, care) do not count against anyone. Rate the period they worked.
- **Part-time colleagues** are rated against expectations that are pro rata to their hours.
- **Write as if the employee and a judge will read it.** Employees can request access to their personal data, and that can include your notes.

**Watch for these biases**

- Recency: the last month dominates.
- Halo or horns: one strong trait colours everything.
- Similarity: people like you look better.
- Visibility: remote, part-time and quieter people get less credit for the same work.
- Leniency: "I don't want the conversation."

## 5. Before you propose a 1 or a 2

All of these must be true:

- [ ] You can describe what "meets" looks like in this role, specifically.
- [ ] You have concrete examples, with dates, of where the person fell short.
- [ ] You told the person about it during the year, and you can show when (a dated email, written 1:1 notes or a summary). *If you didn't, still give the honest rating. The next step will then be a feedback phase, not an improvement plan. Tell your HRBP.*
- [ ] You have considered context: workload, unclear priorities, team changes, your own management, and possible health issues. If health might be involved, **talk to your HRBP before anything else.** Do not ask the employee about a diagnosis.
- [ ] You know what support you would offer: training, coaching, clearer goals, more frequent check-ins.

## 6. Calibration: what to bring and what you'll be asked

**Bring** checklist A for each person, and a 3-line rationale for every 1, 2 and 5.

**Expect questions like these:**

- "What did you tell them, and when?"
- "What does 'meets' look like in this role?"
- "Your team missed [goal]. Why is everyone at 3 or above?"
- "Would the same work get the same rating in [other team]?"
- "What support have they had?"

Ratings can move up or down in calibration. That is the point of it. Once ratings are final, you own them. Never say "calibration (or HR) changed your rating". Explain the rating on its merits.

## 7. The review conversation

**For every rating**

1. **Open:** "I want to go through your rating for this year and what comes next."
2. **Rating and evidence:** state the rating, then give 2–3 specific examples.
3. **Their view:** "How do you see it?" Listen, and don't argue each point.
4. **Next steps:** goals, development, and pay where relevant.
5. **Close:** "You'll get a written summary from me within two days."

**For a 2 or a 1: a structure you can use**

> "Your rating this year is [2 – Needs improvement / 1 – Does not meet]. I want to be clear about why, and about what happens next.
>
> In your role, meeting the standard means [specific standard]. Over the year, [example 1, with date], [example 2], [example 3]. We discussed [issue] on [date(s)].
>
> How do you see it?
>
> *[listen]*
>
> Here's what happens next. Over the next [4–6 weeks for a feedback phase / 60–90 days for an improvement plan], we will focus on [1–3 measurable outcomes]. I'll meet you every week, [HRBP name] will join at the start, middle and end, and you'll have [specific support].
>
> I want this to work. The outcome isn't decided. If things don't improve enough, we will discuss whether this role is the right fit, which could mean a different role or leaving the company. I'm telling you now so there are no surprises later."

**If they ask "Am I being fired?"**

> "No decision has been made. The plan is real, and I'll be honest with you at every check-in about how it's going."

**If they disagree with the rating**, explain the review route: they can ask the skip-level and the HRBP to review it within 10 working days.

## 8. Never say this

| Don't say | Why |
|---|---|
| "We had to put 10% of the team in the bottom" / "I had to pick someone" | It isn't true, and it would undo the whole rating. In a dispute this sentence is the employee's best evidence. |
| "HR / the CEO / calibration made me do this" | You own the rating. Passing blame destroys trust. |
| "You're in the bottom 10%" / "You're the weakest in the team" | We rate against the role standard, not against each other. |
| "Compared to [colleague]…" | It breaches the colleague's privacy and shifts the discussion away from the standard. |
| "This is just a formality" / "You'll be gone by Q3" | The outcome is open. A plan with a predetermined outcome is unfair and won't hold up in a Dutch or German court. |
| "Maybe it's time to think about your future elsewhere" (during the plan) | Exit discussions are for HR, and only at the end of the process. |
| Anything about age, health, pregnancy, family, part-time hours, religion, nationality or union/works-council activity | It is discriminatory or protected, and it is never relevant to the rating. |
| "Off the record…" | Nothing is off the record. |
| Any offer of a package, reference or exit date | Only HR and legal handle separations. |

## 9. After the conversation: your role in the feedback phase or plan

**Feedback phase (4–6 weeks)**, used when there was no prior documented feedback:

- weekly 1:1s focused on the specific gaps
- a short written summary after each one, sent to the employee and copied to your HRBP

**Improvement plan (60–90 days; your HRBP sets the length per role and country)**

```
Name:                     Manager:                 HRBP:
Start date:               Mid-point review:        End date:

Current performance issues
- [Specific, observable behaviour or result, with examples and dates]
- [Metric not met: target X, actual Y, over Z weeks]

Required outcomes (measurable, dated)
1. [Outcome] by [date]
2. [Outcome] by [date]
3. [Outcome] by [date]

Support provided
- [Training / coaching / clearer priorities / pairing / tools]

Check-ins
- Weekly with manager (written summary after each)
- HRBP joins at start, mid-point and end

If the outcomes are met:      the plan closes; normal cycle continues
If the outcomes are not met:  we will discuss a different role or leaving the company
```

**Your obligations**

- Hold every check-in. A cancelled check-in is a broken promise.
- Write down progress honestly, including improvement.
- Give the support you promised.
- Don't move the goalposts.

**At the end**, you, your skip-level and your HRBP meet to decide:

- Met → the plan closes.
- Clear progress → one extension of up to 30 days.
- Not met → HR leads on a role change or separation.

You do not make or announce that decision alone.

## 10. Special situations

| Situation | What you do |
|---|---|
| The employee reports sick or says they're burnt out | Say "Thank you for telling me, let's get you the right support." Inform your HRBP the same day. The plan pauses. Do not ask about the diagnosis. In Amsterdam the occupational doctor (*bedrijfsarts*) takes over the medical side. |
| The employee is pregnant, on or returning from leave, or has a disability | Talk to your HRBP before the conversation. The standard doesn't change, but the process may. |
| The employee brings a lawyer, union rep, or (in Hamburg) asks for the Betriebsrat | "That's your right." Hand it to your HRBP. Don't discuss the case with them yourself. |
| The employee asks about leaving, a package or a reference | "Let me connect you with HR." Do not negotiate or hint at terms. |
| The employee resigns | Accept it professionally and inform your HRBP. Don't comment on the plan. |
| Your own manager asks you to "just get someone into the bottom" | Don't. Refer them to your HRBP or the CPO. Ratings without evidence are not accepted in calibration. |

## 11. Amsterdam and Hamburg: what's different for you

The ratings, calibration and conversations are the same in both offices. The legal steps after a plan differ, and HR handles them. What you need to know:

- **Amsterdam:** the works council (OR) has approved this process. Dismissal for performance requires that the employee was told clearly and in time and given a genuine chance to improve. That is why your dated feedback and check-in notes matter so much. Sickness changes the process completely; involve HR immediately.
- **Hamburg:** if a dismissal is ever considered, the works council (Betriebsrat, if one is in place) must be consulted before anything happens. German law judges performance against what the individual can reasonably deliver, so your evidence must be specific and documented. Where the issue is effort or conduct, HR may issue a formal warning (*Abmahnung*) first. Managers never issue warnings or dismissals on their own.

## 12. FAQ

**Do I have to put someone in rating 1 or 2?**
No. You have to rate everyone honestly against their role. If you believe nobody in your team is below "meets", bring the evidence to calibration. You'll be asked for it, just as you'd be asked for evidence behind a 1 or a 2.

**Is there a percentage I need to hit?**
No. There is a company-wide expected range that we use to spot inflation across large functions. It is not applied to your team.

**I never told this person about the problem during the year. Can I still rate them a 2?**
Yes, if it's the honest rating. The next step is then a feedback phase, not an improvement plan. It also means you need to give feedback earlier next time, and that will come up in your own review.

**Someone was on parental or sick leave for four months. How do I rate them?**
Rate the period they worked. Leave never counts against anyone. If they worked less than half the year, talk to your HRBP about "insufficient period".

**How do I rate part-time colleagues?**
Against the role standard, pro rata to their hours.

**Can the plan be shorter than 60 days if it's obvious?**
No. If you believe the outcome is already obvious, talk to your HRBP. A plan that is a formality is unfair, and we don't run them.

**What happens to me as a manager?**
Your own review includes how you handled performance in your team. That counts both ways: avoiding hard conversations, and making ratings you can't support.

**Who sees my calibration notes?**
Your skip-level, HR and the calibration group. The employee may also request them, so write them accordingly.
