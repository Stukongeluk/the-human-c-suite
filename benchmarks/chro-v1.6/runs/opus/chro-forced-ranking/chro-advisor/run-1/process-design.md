# Performance Review Cycle: Process Design (Amsterdam and Hamburg)

**For:** Chief People Officer, CEO, ExCo. Use the same document as the basis for the works-council submissions.
**Status:** Draft for decision. The legal points are my reading of statute and guidance, not legal advice. Dutch and German employment counsel must confirm them before anything goes to managers or to the works councils.
**Not for managers.** Managers get `manager-briefing.md`.

**Confidence tags:** [Verified] = checked against statute text or a primary or official source. [Medium] = reasonable inference or a single secondary source; application to our facts needs counsel. [Assumed] = not verified; replace it with our own data.

No company context file was provided, so every company number below (team sizes, salaries, tenure, HR headcount, review calendar) is [Assumed].

## 1. What the CEO wants, and what this design keeps

As I read the request, the CEO wants four things:

1. **Honest differentiation**, with no more "everyone meets expectations".
2. **Real consequences** for sustained underperformance.
3. **Speed**, so the problem is dealt with this year and not "next cycle".
4. **One simple standard** across the company.

This design keeps all four. It drops one mechanism: the per-manager 10% quota with a fixed exit date. That mechanism is the part that fails legally in the Netherlands and Germany, and the part that is unfair in small teams.

| CEO's element | In this design | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A bottom category | **Kept** (ratings 1 and 2) | Underperformance has to be named. High performers notice when it is tolerated. |
| 10% of every manager's team | **Changed** to a company-wide expected range of about 10–17% at ratings 1–2. It is checked at function level (about 50+ people) and never applied per team. | In a team of 6, "10%" means 1 person, which is 17%, whether or not anyone is underperforming. A rating a manager was *obliged* to give is weak evidence in an NL or DE court. [Medium] |
| 60-day improvement plan | **Kept as the minimum** where expectations were already documented and output is measurable. **90 days is the default** for knowledge roles in NL. | UWV guidance says six weeks can be enough for unskilled work. Expectations rise with tenure and employer size. [Verified: UWV] |
| "Doesn't improve → leaves" | **Consequence kept** (role change or exit). **Predetermination removed.** | A plan whose outcome is fixed in advance is a pro forma plan. NL requires a genuine chance to improve. DE requires a real chance after a warning. [Medium] |
| "Gone by end of Q3" | **Replaced by:** a decision at the end of the plan, then an exit by agreement within about 4–6 weeks | Neither country allows a guaranteed exit date for performance. See section 9 for realistic timing. [Medium] |

## 2. Settle these with the CEO before designing further

**Questions to ask him:**

1. "When you say it worked, which metric moved, by how much, and over what period?"
2. "What happened to regretted attrition, referrals and hiring in years 2 and 3?" The best-known simulation of forced distribution found an average improvement in workforce potential of about 16% a year in the first two years. That fell to about 2% by year six and about 1% by year ten. It assumed reliable ratings and better replacement hires, and it measured workforce potential, not company performance. [Verified: Scullen et al., *Personnel Psychology* 2005, via press release]
3. "Was your last company in an at-will environment, such as the US?" Exit cost and speed are very different there.
4. "What problem are we solving: rating inflation, specific underperformers that managers won't address, or cost and headcount?" If the answer is headcount, see section 14.
5. "What would you need to see by mid-year to believe this is working?" His answer becomes the dashboard in section 11.

**Data to bring (People Analytics, this week):**

- Last cycle's rating distribution, by function and by manager.
- The number of people with *documented* performance concerns today.
- Regretted attrition over the last 12 months, by function.
- eNPS and manager-effectiveness scores.
- Improvement-plan count and outcomes for the last 2 years.

If last cycle had 95% of people at "meets" or above, the CEO's diagnosis of rating inflation is right. In that case calibration (section 6) has to be visibly tough.

## 3. The design on one page

**Principles**

1. **Rate against the role standard, not against teammates.**
2. **Force the conversation, not the distribution.** Calibration challenges inflation and avoidance as hard as it challenges unsupported low ratings.
3. **No rating without evidence. No improvement plan without prior documented feedback.** If there was no prior feedback, start with a short feedback phase.
4. **Every rating of 1 or 2 gets a documented action within 30 days.** A low rating with no follow-up is not an option.
5. **Improvement plans have an open outcome and real support, and they end on time.**
6. **One process, two legal tracks.** Ratings and calibration are the same in Amsterdam and Hamburg. Only the consequences follow local law.

## 4. Rating scale

| Rating | Meaning | Expected company-wide range | Next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Exceptional | Consistently well beyond the role; sets the standard for others | 5–10% | Fast-track, equity or bonus refresh |
| 4 Exceeds | Regularly beyond the role standard | 20–25% | Merit increase, stretch work |
| 3 Meets | Delivers the role standard reliably | 55–65% | Market adjustment, development |
| 2 Needs improvement | Meets some but not all of the role standard | 8–12% | Documented feedback phase, or an improvement plan if earlier feedback is documented |
| 1 Does not meet | Clearly below the role standard on outcomes or behaviour | 2–5% | Improvement plan if earlier feedback is documented, otherwise a feedback phase first. Role change or exit if the plan fails. |
| *Too new to rate* | Less than 6 months in role | n/a | Onboarding review |
| *Insufficient period* | Worked less than half the cycle (for example because of leave) | n/a | Rate the worked period only if it is long enough; otherwise no rating |

**How the expected range is used.** It is a check at company level and at function level (about 50 people or more). A function that lands outside the range explains why, with evidence, and can stay outside it. The range is **never** applied to an individual team and **never** described to managers as a target. Managers are told honestly that it exists and what it is for.

The ranges come from our own calibration framework. [Verified: internal framework]

## 6. Calibration protocol

- **Format:** 8–10 managers from one function, a 2-hour session, facilitated by the HRBP and chaired by the skip-level.
- **What each manager presents:** a 3-line rationale for every 1, 2 and 5, plus a rationale for any team where **everyone** is rated 3 or above.
- **Challenge in both directions:**
  - *Inflation or avoidance:* "Your team missed [goal X] and everyone is at 3 or above. What explains that?"
  - *Unsupported low rating:* "What did you tell them, and when? What does 'meets' look like in this role? What support did they get?"
  - *Consistency:* "Would the same work get the same rating in [other team]?"
- **Hard rules:**
  - No 1 or 2 without an evidence pack.
  - A rating stays honest even without prior feedback, but the next step is then a feedback phase, not an improvement plan.
  - Periods of leave are excluded, and the rating covers the period worked.
  - Expectations for part-time staff are pro rata to their hours.
- **Notes:** write them as if the employee and a judge will read them. Under the GDPR, employees can request access to their personal data, and these notes may be part of it. [Medium]
- **Manager accountability:** how a manager handles performance feeds their own review. That covers avoiding hard calls and making unsupported ones.

## 7. Fairness controls: adverse-impact check

Before ratings are final, People Analytics compares the share of 1–2 ratings across these groups:

- gender
- age band (under 30, 30–49, 50+)
- part-time vs full-time
- leave during the cycle (parental, sick)
- location (Amsterdam vs Hamburg)
- tenure band
- remote-heavy vs office-heavy

A group whose rate of 1–2 ratings is markedly higher than the overall rate (as a working heuristic, 1.25 times or more) triggers a case-by-case review before ratings are released. [Assumed threshold; agree it with counsel]

- Use only data we already hold lawfully, and report it in aggregate. Do not collect ethnicity data for this.
- Legal hooks: Dutch equal-treatment law, including the ban on part-time discrimination, and the German AGG (equal-treatment act) and §4 TzBfG (part-time and fixed-term act). [Medium]
- Why this matters more under forced ranking: quotas tend to land on the least visible people, such as part-timers, people back from leave and remote workers. That is where discrimination claims come from.

## 8. Improvement plans

**Step 1: Diagnose (weeks 1–2).** Is the problem skill (can't), will (won't) or context (workload, unclear role, the manager, health)?

- If health may be involved, involve HR and the occupational doctor (*bedrijfsarts* in NL, *Betriebsarzt* in DE) **before** a plan starts.
- In 2019 the Dutch Supreme Court held that the dismissal protection during illness can block a performance-based dissolution when the underperformance may have been caused by illness. [Verified: commentary on HR 19 July 2019]

**Step 2: Feedback phase (only if there was no prior documented feedback).** It lasts 4–6 weeks. Feedback is specific ("your last three releases shipped with X open defects"), and a written summary goes out after every conversation.

**Step 3: Improvement plan.** Use the template in the manager briefing. It covers:

- issues, with dates and examples
- measurable outcomes, each with a date
- the support provided
- the consequence if the outcomes are not met
- weekly manager check-ins, with the HRBP at the start, middle and end

**Plan length** (counsel to confirm per role):

- **NL:**
  - 60 days minimum, only where expectations were already documented and output is measurable.
  - 90 days is the default for knowledge roles.
  - Longer for people with long tenure. Expectations of the employer rise with tenure and company size. [Verified: UWV guidance; application Medium]
- **DE:**
  - The Federal Labour Court measures performance against what *this* employee can do: "Der Arbeitnehmer muss tun, was er soll, und zwar so gut, wie er kann" ("the employee must do what he is supposed to do, as well as he can").
  - For quantitative work, significant underperformance means persistently more than about one third below comparable colleagues over a longer period.
  - Where the issue is effort or conduct, it usually has to come after a formal warning (*Abmahnung*).
  - A bottom-10% ranking proves none of this. [Verified via secondary sources on BAG 2 AZR 667/02; application Medium]

**Rules:**

- At most one extension, of 30 days, and only where there is clear progress.
- The outcome stays open.
- **No plan as a formality.** If the decision is already made, do not run a fake plan. HR holds an honest exit conversation with a fair package instead.

**If the employee reports sick:**

- The plan pauses, and no manager asks about the diagnosis.
- **NL:** wages continue for up to 104 weeks (at least 70%). Re-integration duties apply, and dismissal protection may apply. [Medium]
- **DE:** the employer pays 6 weeks of sick pay. Illness does not automatically bar dismissal, but it changes the legal basis. [Medium]

**HR capacity:** at our framework ratio of one HRBP per 75–100 employees, we have about 12–16 HRBPs. [Assumed]

- Under the CEO's original design, 120–150 plans would run at the same time. That is 8–12 per HRBP, each with weekly check-ins, on top of the normal workload.
- Under this design, cycle 1 starts fewer plans, because undocumented cases go through a feedback phase first.
- Budget temporary HR support if the load goes above about 6 active plans per HRBP.

## 9. Outcomes and separations

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Met | Close the plan and monitor for 6 months within the normal cycle |
| Partly met, with clear progress | One extension of 30 days |
| Not met | Role change if a suitable vacancy exists (in NL, checking for redeployment is a legal requirement for this dismissal ground [Verified: UWV]). Otherwise, separation. |

**Who decides:** the manager, skip-level and HRBP together, with counsel for every exit. Never the manager alone.

**Netherlands**

- **First route: a mutual termination agreement** (*vaststellingsovereenkomst*). It should include:
  - the statutory transition payment (1/3 of monthly salary per year of service) as the floor
  - paid notice or garden leave
  - a contribution to legal fees
  - a neutral reference
  - outplacement support
- The employee has a 14-day cooling-off period. If the agreement is drafted properly, unemployment-benefit eligibility is preserved. [Medium]
- **Fallback: the sub-district court (*kantonrechter*) on the d-ground** (dismissal for unsuitability). The employer must show:
  - the employee was told in time and concretely
  - they had sufficient opportunity to improve
  - the problem was not caused by a lack of training
  - redeployment is not reasonably possible

  This takes months. The court can add "fair compensation" (*billijke vergoeding*) if the employer acted seriously culpably, and a dossier built on a quota is the obvious risk here. [Verified: UWV for the requirements; Medium for the compensation risk]

**Germany**

- **First route: a termination agreement** (*Aufhebungsvertrag*), which must be in written form. The market rule of thumb for severance is 0.5 monthly salary per year of service, and more when our file is weak. Add garden leave and a written reference (*Arbeitszeugnis*). The employee risks a suspension of unemployment benefit (*Sperrzeit*), which is usually priced into the deal. [Medium]
- **Fallback: ordinary dismissal.**
  - If there is a Betriebsrat, it must be heard before every dismissal, and a dismissal without that hearing is void (§102 BetrVG). [Verified: statute]
  - Statutory notice periods apply.
  - The employee has 3 weeks to sue. Most cases end in a settlement. [Medium]

**Collective-redundancy check (both countries):** if exits are driven by a quota rather than by individual performance, they may be treated as terminations "for reasons not related to the individual workers concerned". The EU directive counts employer-initiated terminations, termination agreements included, toward the collective-redundancy thresholds once there are at least five redundancies. [Verified: Directive 98/59/EC art. 1] Counsel should check the numbers per location against the Dutch WMCO (collective dismissal notification act) and §17 KSchG (German dismissal protection act) before any exit.

**Budget:** the CFO approves a separate severance budget up front (section 12).

**Realistic timing** [Assumed calendar: conversations end in week 0, around late February; no extensions]

| Case | Plan starts | Decision | Earliest exit by agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Underperformance already documented before the review | Weeks 2–4 | Weeks 11–17 | Weeks 13–21 (about June–July) |
| First time the employee hears it is this review | Weeks 8–10 (after the feedback phase) | Weeks 17–23 | Weeks 19–27 (about July–early September) |

On paper, both groups can be out by the end of Q3, but only if nothing slips. Several things can push an exit into Q4 or later:

- One extension adds 4 weeks.
- Sick leave pauses the plan.
- An employee who refuses an agreement adds months: the court route in NL, a lawsuit in DE.

Tell the CEO that most documented cases should be resolved in Q3, and that a meaningful share of the rest will land in Q4. Saying so now is better than missing his date later.

## 10. Works councils and legal checklist

**Netherlands: ondernemingsraad (OR)** [Verified: WOR art. 27]

- The OR's consent (*instemming*) is required for any regulation on personnel assessment (art. 27(1)(g)). It is also required for regulations on appointment, dismissal or promotion policy (art. 27(1)(e)). Tying ratings to exits brings the design under (e) as well.
- Submit a written request with the reasons and the expected consequences for employees, and discuss it in a consultation meeting with the OR (*overlegvergadering*). [Medium on the procedure detail]
- **Without consent the decision is void** once the OR invokes this in writing within one month.
- The sub-district court can give replacement consent only if the OR's refusal is unreasonable, or if there are compelling business-organisational, economic or social reasons.
- **Practical point:** a per-team quota is the element the OR is most likely to refuse. Pre-consult the OR chair informally and lead with the fairness controls.

**Germany: Betriebsrat (if Hamburg has one)**

- §94(2) BetrVG: general appraisal principles need the Betriebsrat's consent. If there is no agreement, the conciliation board (*Einigungsstelle*) decides. [Verified: statute]
- §95 BetrVG: if ratings are used to *select* who is dismissed, they act as selection guidelines, which need consent. [Medium]
- §87(1) no. 6 BetrVG: co-determination applies if a new HR tool records performance data. [Medium]
- §102 BetrVG: the Betriebsrat must be heard before every dismissal, and a dismissal without that hearing is void. [Verified: statute]
- **If there is no Betriebsrat:** there is no co-determination, but the KSchG still protects employees with more than 6 months' service in establishments with more than 10 employees. [Medium]

**Both countries**

- Ratings and calibration notes are personal data, so set a retention period and expect access requests.
- No automated decisions.
- Run the equal-treatment check in section 7.

## 11. How we will know it worked

The CEO gets a dashboard monthly during the cycle and quarterly after it.

| Metric | Target or signal |
|---|---|
| Rating distribution by function vs the expected range | Within range, or explained |
| 1–2 ratings with a documented action within 30 days | 100% |
| Improvement-plan outcomes (improved / role change / exit) and median time to resolution | Resolved within about 6 months |
| Regretted attrition, overall and among people rated 4–5 | Below 10% a year; flat or falling |
| Adverse-impact ratios by group | No unexplained flags |
| eNPS and manager-effectiveness score | eNPS above 30; manager effectiveness above 3.8/5 |
| Disputes and settlement cost vs budget | Within budget; zero voided decisions |

**Definition of "it worked" at 12 months:**

- an honest distribution
- every low rating resolved within about 6 months
- top-performer attrition not rising
- no fairness flags
- cost within budget

## 12. Cost model (CFO to validate)

[Assumed] Average gross salary €71k a year (about €5.5k a month including holiday allowance). Average tenure 4 years.

| Cost per exit | Range | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Settlement | €12–25k | NL statutory floor is about €7.3k at these assumptions, and settlements usually exceed it. DE is 0.5–1.0 month per year of service. |
| Legal and HR time | €2–5k | |
| Replacement hiring | €8–18k | Internal recruiting up to agency fees |
| Ramp-up loss | €9–18k | 3–6 months at about 50% productivity |
| **Total** | **€31–66k** | |

- **Per 100 exits: roughly €3–6.5M.** This is before any court awards, the voluntary departure of strong performers, and management time.
- Under the CEO's original design, the number of people labelled is 120–150. With predetermined outcomes, most of them would exit.
- Under this design, cycle 1 produces fewer exits, at a lower cost per exit, because the files hold up and the packages are lower.

## 13. If the CEO chooses his original design anyway

The CEO decides. If he keeps the per-manager quota, these conditions are not optional in NL and DE:

1. Written sign-off from Dutch and German counsel on the design.
2. OR consent, plus Betriebsrat agreement if there is one, **before** first use.
3. **Evidence override.** No manager may put anyone in the bottom category without an evidence pack. If fewer than 10% qualify, the quota gives way.
4. Open-outcome plans with real support and no fixed exit date.
5. Exits by agreement, within a CFO-approved severance budget, after the collective-redundancy check.

Once these are applied, his design matches this one in everything except the quota label, and the quota label is the part that creates the risk.

---

## 14. If the real goal is reducing headcount

Say so, and run it as a restructuring:

- a business case
- the OR's advice right on reorganisations and significant workforce reductions (WOR art. 25)
- the UWV route or social selection (*Sozialauswahl*)
- collective-redundancy consultation where the thresholds are hit [Medium]

This is cleaner and, over time, cheaper. Above all, it does not brand people as poor performers when they are not.
