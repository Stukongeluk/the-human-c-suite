# Manager briefing: performance review 2026

**For:** all people managers in Amsterdam and Hamburg · **From:** the CEO and the Chief People Officer
**Status:** draft, 23 November 2026. It becomes final once the Ondernemingsraad has given its consent and the works agreement with the Betriebsrat is signed. Dates and details may change in that process.

## In one minute

- We're raising the bar on performance, and we want honest ratings. Each person is assessed against the written expectations for their role and level, not against their colleagues.
- **There is no quota for any rating.** Some teams will have nobody "not meeting expectations". Others will have more than one person.
- Calibration checks that ratings are consistent across teams, and it challenges ratings in both directions.
- Anyone not meeting expectations gets a clear, fixed-term plan, with real support and consequences known from the start. Most people improve once they have clear expectations and real support.
- **No rating should come as a surprise.** If you haven't raised a concern with someone before, start with a development plan, not an improvement plan.
- Call your HR partner before any "not meeting expectations" conversation, and in the situations listed below.

## Rating scale

**Exceptional** · **Fully meets** · **Partly meets** · **Not meeting expectations**

## Step 1: Prepare your assessment

**Use:**
- the written expectations for the person's role and level
- the goals agreed for the year, and anything that changed along the way
- examples from the whole year, not only the last two months
- input from people they work with, where the agreed procedure includes it
- context: absence, leave, part-time hours, changes of role or manager, workload

**Don't use:**
- tickets closed, commits, lines of code, story points, pull requests, hours logged or online status. These measure activity, not contribution. Delivery metrics belong in team conversations, not in anyone's review
- comparisons with named colleagues
- health, family situation or anything else about someone's private life

**Check yourself for:**
- recency: the last month weighs too heavily
- halo: one strength or weakness colours everything else
- similarity: people like you look better
- visibility: you see less of remote and part-time colleagues
- reactions to challenge: research finds that managers tend to rate employees who challenge them as weaker than those who agree (Burris, 2012). Useful challenge is a contribution.

## Step 2: Calibration

**Bring** your proposed rating for each person, two or three examples per person, and the goals they had.

**How it works:** you meet by department, with an HR partner and a manager from another department. Every "Exceptional" and every "Not meeting expectations" is discussed. Other ratings are discussed when someone asks.

**What it's for:** making ratings consistent against the written standards. You may be asked "Where's the evidence?" for a low rating. You may also be asked "Why is everyone 'Fully meets' when the team missed its goals?"

**What it's not for:** filling a curve. Nobody will ask you to move a rating to hit a number. If anyone does, tell your HR partner.

**Afterwards,** HR checks the combined results for patterns by part-time status, leave, gender, age band and nationality. If a pattern shows up, calibration may revisit ratings before they're final.

## Step 3: The review conversation

- Hold it in person or by live video, with a week's notice. Never by email.
- Share your written assessment a day ahead, so the person can prepare.
- Plan 45 to 60 minutes: their view first, then your assessment with examples, then what's next. Record their comments.

**Lines you can use:**
- *Opening:* "I'd like to hear how you see your year first. Then I'll share my view against what we agreed for your role, and we'll agree what's next."
- *Not meeting expectations:* "In [area], your work hasn't met what we agreed for your role. Here are two examples: [...]. I want to help you get there. Here's the plan, the support you'll get and the dates. If we're still not there by [end date], we'll need to talk about whether this role is right for you, and HR will be part of that conversation."
- *If they disagree:* "Thanks for telling me. Let's add your view to the record. You can also ask for a review within ten working days, and I'll send you how."

**Don't say:**
- "HR made me pick someone" or "The CEO wants a number." There is no quota, so neither is true.
- "You're in the bottom 10%." We don't rank people against each other.
- "It's just a formality," or anything else that promises an outcome.
- Anything that compares them with a named colleague.

## Step 4: When someone isn't meeting expectations

**Choose the track:**

| Situation | Track |
|---|---|
| Partly meets | Development plan |
| Not meeting, and you haven't raised concerns before in writing | Development plan first |
| Not meeting, and the gap is in skills or knowledge | Development plan with training |
| Not meeting, and you raised concerns before in writing, with examples | Improvement plan |

**A development plan** lasts three months. It has clear goals, paid learning time during working hours, a mentor and a check-in every month. No dismissal consequence is attached. If there's no progress, talk to HR about moving to an improvement plan.

**An improvement plan** must include:
- a start date and an end date: usually three months, at least two, up to six for complex roles, with one extension possible
- three to five goals that are concrete and achievable, each with examples of the current gap
- named support: coaching, training during working hours and an adjusted workload where needed
- check-ins every two weeks, each with a short written summary that includes the employee's view
- your HR partner at the start, the midpoint and the end
- what happens if the goals are met, partly met or not met, stated at the start

Keep the plan in the person's own role and team. A plan in another department may not count as a real chance to improve. The aim is improvement, so plan for the person to succeed.

## Step 5: Outcomes

- **Goals met:** close the plan, and say so clearly to the person.
- **Partly met:** extend the plan once, with HR.
- **The role is the wrong fit:** look at internal roles with HR first.
- **Not met:** HR and counsel review the file, and HR joins you for the next conversation. Never discuss ending someone's employment, notice or a settlement on your own.
- **If someone leaves:** they leave on fair terms, with paid time to look for work, outplacement support and an agreed reference. You tell them yourself, in person, with HR there.

## Call your HR partner first when

- you're considering a "not meeting expectations" rating
- the person is ill or recently back from sick leave
- the person is pregnant, or on or recently back from parental leave
- the person has a disability or a chronic condition
- the person is a works council member or candidate (in Hamburg, also the representative for severely disabled employees)
- the person has recently raised a complaint, a grievance or a report of wrongdoing
- the person has been in their role for under six months. They can't be rated "not meeting"; set clear expectations instead
- the person changed teams or managers during the year. Get input from their previous manager

## Amsterdam and Hamburg: what's different

**Amsterdam**
- The assessment procedure is agreed with the Ondernemingsraad. Follow it as written.
- If a plan ever ends up in court, the court checks whether the person was told in good time and got real support and a real chance to improve. Your check-in notes are that evidence.
- Hold the conversation in Dutch or English, whichever the employee prefers.

**Hamburg**
- The criteria and procedure are set in the works agreement with the Betriebsrat. Follow it exactly. An assessment made outside the agreed procedure may have to be removed from the file.
- Only HR issues a formal warning (Abmahnung), never you.
- The Betriebsrat is heard before any notice of termination. HR handles this.
- Hold the conversation in German or English, whichever the employee prefers.

## Records and privacy

- Write notes only in [HR system]. Never in chat, email or personal documents.
- Employees can see their own records, so write as if they will read them.
- Don't share ratings with anyone outside the calibration group and HR.

## What's expected of you

- You're assessed on the quality and timeliness of your feedback and on how your people develop, not on how many people you rate low.
- ExCo and directors are assessed first, against the same standards.
- If your team fell short because its goals, staffing or tools were wrong, say so. That's a problem for leadership to fix, not something to put in an individual's rating.

## Questions managers ask

**Is there a target percentage for any rating?**
No.

**My whole team is strong. Can I rate nobody below "Fully meets"?**
Yes, if the evidence supports it. Calibration will ask for evidence in both directions.

**What if more than 10% of my team isn't meeting expectations?**
Rate what you see, then talk to your own manager and HR. That many usually points to something in the system, such as goals, staffing, onboarding or tooling, and we'll fix it together.

**Do I have to wait for the review to raise a concern?**
No. Raise it when you see it, with examples, and tell HR. A rating should never be the first time someone hears about a problem.

**What do I say if my team asks about "forced ranking"?**
"There's no quota. Each person is assessed against the expectations for their role, and we check consistency across teams."

**Can the employee disagree?**
Yes. Their view goes into the record, and they can ask for a review by your manager and an HR partner within ten working days.

**Can I use Jira or GitHub data in someone's review?**
No. Team-level delivery data is for team conversations. Use examples of outcomes, quality and collaboration.
