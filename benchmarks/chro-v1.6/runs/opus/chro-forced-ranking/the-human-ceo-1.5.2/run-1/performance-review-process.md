# Performance review 2026: proposed process

**From:** Chief People Officer · **To:** CEO and ExCo · **Status:** draft for decision, 23 November 2026
To be shared with the Ondernemingsraad (Amsterdam) and the Betriebsrat (Hamburg) at the problem stage, before anything is final.

## Recommendation

This year, everyone should be assessed against written, absolute standards for their role and level. Ratings get calibrated across teams, and "not meeting expectations" is a real category. Anyone in it gets a fixed-term, supported improvement plan, with the consequences agreed at the start. No rating gets a quota, and nobody sets the number of exits in advance.

This gives us what the forced-ranking proposal is meant to achieve: a higher bar, ratings that managers can't soften, and real consequences for sustained underperformance, with outcomes known by the end of Q3 2027. It can also win works council consent in both countries and hold up in court. A fixed 10% can't. It would put about 120 of our 1,200 people in the bottom category whatever their performance.

## 1. The problem we are solving

We need to confirm this with the CEO before ExCo. Forced ranking is a solution, and the right design depends on which problem it is meant to solve:

| If the problem is... | ...the evidence would be | ...and the right tool is |
|---|---|---|
| Soft ratings: managers avoid hard conversations | Most people rated "meets" or higher while team and company goals are missed; few improvement plans | Written standards, calibration and manager accountability (this proposal) |
| Specific teams underperforming | Missed outcomes concentrated in a few areas | A team-level diagnosis of goals, staffing, skills, tooling and leadership |
| Cost or headcount | A budget gap | A separate decision (section 10), not a performance label |

Data to add from the HR system before ExCo:

- last cycle's ratings by department
- improvement plans over the last two years and how they ended
- voluntary and regretted attrition over the last 12 months
- team sizes
- engagement results on clear expectations and fair reviews

## 3. What the case for forced ranking gets right, and why it doesn't carry over here

The case is real. A forced distribution counters lenient ratings and makes managers have conversations they avoid. A well-known simulation found that dismissing the lowest-rated employees each year raised workforce potential by about 16% a year in the first two years, falling to about 2% by year six (Scullen, Bergey and Aiman-Smith, 2005). That model assumed reliable ratings and better replacements. It left out morale, collaboration and recruiting.

Why it doesn't fit us:

- **In small teams, the quota is arbitrary.** Under a 10% rule, a team of four, five or six still has to name one person. That is 25%, 20% or 17% of the team. A strong team loses someone it would fight to keep, and a weak team gets off lightly.

  | Team size | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | One person as a share of the team | 25% | 20% | 17% | 14% | 13% | 10% | 8% |

- **In both our countries, the law judges the individual, not the curve.** In 2020 the Amsterdam cantonal court refused to end a Booking.com employee's contract when his performance had been measured against colleagues' averages. The court held that adequate performance shouldn't depend only on the average of all employees (ECLI:NL:RBAMS:2020:4487). German courts require proof of a clear, long-term shortfall against comparable employees. Performing about one-third below average is an indicator, not a rule (BAG, 11 December 2003, 2 AZR 667/02).
- **Others have dropped it.** Microsoft ended stack ranking in 2013, after criticism that it pitted staff against each other. The message to staff said "no more curve" and "no more rankings". When Meta cut about 5% of its staff as "low performers" in February 2025, it left out Germany, the Netherlands, France and Italy because of local regulations.
- **A 60-day plan with the outcome fixed in advance isn't an improvement plan.** Courts expect the employer to offer a serious, real chance to improve. A plan built to produce a number will be read as building a dismissal file. In the Netherlands, that also exposes us to extra compensation on top of the statutory payment.

## 4. The process

**Rating scale** (labels to be agreed with the works councils): Exceptional · Fully meets · Partly meets · Not meeting expectations. No level has a target percentage.

1. **Standards (December–January).** Each job family gets written expectations per level, covering outcomes, quality, collaboration and behaviour. Where these are missing, People and the department lead write them with two or three people who do the role. This year's individual goals are checked against them.
2. **Evidence (February).** The employee writes a short self-reflection. The manager assesses the whole year with examples. We don't use tickets closed, commits, lines of code, story points, hours or online status. Delivery and flow metrics stay at team level.
3. **Calibration (early March).** By department, managers compare their ratings against the written standards, with an HR partner and a manager from another department. Calibration challenges in both directions: a team rated "fully meets" across the board while it missed its goals, and a "not meeting" with no examples. HR checks the combined results for patterns by part-time status, leave, gender, age band and nationality, in groups of at least ten. Calibration revisits any pattern before ratings are final.
4. **Conversation (second half of March).** The employee's own manager holds it in person or by live video, never by email. No rating should come as a surprise: "not meeting expectations" requires that concerns were raised earlier in the year, with examples. The employee adds their view to the record. Within ten working days they can ask for a review by their manager's manager and an HR partner who wasn't in the calibration.
5. **Plans (April–July).**
   - *Development plan*: for "partly meets", and for "not meeting" where concerns weren't raised before or the gap is in skills or knowledge. It has clear goals, paid training time, a mentor and monthly check-ins. No dismissal consequence is attached. If there is no progress after three months, it can become an improvement plan.
   - *Improvement plan*: for "not meeting" where concerns were raised before, in writing. It has:
     - fixed start and end dates, usually three months: at least two, up to six for complex roles, extendable once
     - three to five concrete, achievable goals, with examples of the current gap
     - named support: coaching, training in working hours and an adjusted workload where needed
     - check-ins every two weeks, recorded with the employee's view
     - an HR partner at the start, midpoint and end
     - the possible consequences, stated at the start

     The plan runs in the employee's own role and team.
6. **Outcomes (July–September).**
   - Goals met: the plan closes.
   - Partly met: the plan is extended once.
   - The role is the wrong fit: look for an internal move first.
   - Not met: HR and employment counsel review the file. Then comes a conversation about leaving by mutual agreement on fair terms, or the legal route.

   Anyone who leaves gets at least the statutory payment, paid time to look for work, outplacement support and an agreed reference.
7. **Review (October).** ExCo and both works councils review the cycle against the indicators in section 9 and change what didn't work.

## 6. Safeguards

- **Protected situations.** Managers call HR before any rating conversation with someone who is:
  - on sick leave or recently back from it
  - pregnant, or on or recently back from parental leave
  - disabled or living with a chronic condition
  - a works council member or candidate, or in Hamburg the representative for severely disabled employees
  - someone who has recently raised a complaint or a report of wrongdoing
- **New in role.** Nobody with under six months in their role is rated "not meeting". Their manager sets clear expectations instead.
- **Part-time staff.** They are assessed against expectations scaled to their hours.
- **People decide, not tools.** No tool scores, ranks or selects people. Employees can see and challenge their own records (GDPR Articles 15 and 22). Before launch, the data protection officer assesses whether we need a data protection impact assessment.
- **Records.** They are kept only in the HR system, for as long as the retention policy allows. Never in chat, email or personal files.

## 7. Legal and consultation touchpoints

This is not legal advice. Employment counsel in both countries must confirm it before launch.

| | Netherlands (Amsterdam) | Germany (Hamburg) |
|---|---|---|
| The assessment system | Works council consent right over personnel assessment (art. 27(1)(g) WOR). If the works council invokes it within a month, a decision taken without consent is void | Co-determination over general assessment principles (§94(2) BetrVG) and over selection guidelines for dismissals (§95 BetrVG). §87(1) no. 6 also applies if a software tool is used. Without agreement, the conciliation board (Einigungsstelle) decides. First check that an elected Betriebsrat exists |
| Dismissal for performance | Decided by the cantonal court (art. 7:669(3)(d) BW). The employee must have been told in good time and had a real chance to improve, and the shortfall must not come from a lack of training or support. Recent rulings expect fixed start and end dates, concrete and achievable goals, support, recorded evaluations and consequences known in advance. Statutory transition payment (art. 7:673 BW) | The Dismissal Protection Act applies after six months' service. The employer must prove a clear, long-term shortfall against comparable employees. A warning (Abmahnung) is usually required where the underperformance is conduct-related. The works council is heard before every dismissal (§102 BetrVG) |
| Notice | One to four months, by length of service | Up to seven months, by length of service (§622 BGB) |
| If a number drives the exits | Counsel to assess whether this is a reorganisation in substance. If so, the works council's advice right (art. 25 WOR) applies, and collective dismissal notification (WMCO) for 20 or more people in three months | Counsel to assess collective dismissal notification (§17 KSchG, which counts termination agreements the employer initiates) and social selection (§1(3) KSchG) |

## 8. Leadership goes first

- ExCo and directors are assessed first, against the same written standards. The combined results are shared with all managers.
- The CEO and CPO jointly own this process and its results. Where calibration shows that teams fell short because goals, staffing, skills or tooling were wrong, that is a leadership finding, and ExCo says so in its communication.
- Managers are assessed on the quality and timeliness of their feedback and on how their people develop, not on how many people they rate low.

## 9. How we'll know it works

We agree these indicators now. ExCo reviews them monthly while plans run, and quarterly with the works councils:

- ratings by department, and patterns by group (combined, groups of ten or more)
- improvement plan outcomes: completed, extended, moved role, left
- review requests and their outcomes
- regretted attrition (target: no increase) and voluntary attrition
- pulse survey statements: "I know what is expected of me", "My review was fair", "I can raise concerns without fear"
- team-level business results: customer outcomes, delivery flow and reliability

## 10. If the real goal is cost

Then it is a separate decision, and it shouldn't run through the performance review. We would start with spend, hiring and natural attrition. At an illustrative 10% voluntary attrition rate, about 120 people leave a company our size every year anyway (to check against our actual rate). Any reduction would go through works council advice and the collective dismissal rules, and leaders' pay and bonuses would be addressed first.

## 11. Assumptions, and what would change this proposal

- We assume our real underperformance problem is smaller and more uneven than 10% in every team. Last cycle's data may show otherwise. If so, this process handles it, because it has no ceiling.
- If ratings in this cycle still don't match team results after calibration, we strengthen manager accountability and revisit the design with the works councils.
- If the works councils propose a better design, we adopt it.
- If the CEO's previous company has data showing lasting gains in a comparable legal setting, we will look at it together.

## Appendix: cost estimate for option A

These figures are illustrative assumptions, to be replaced with payroll data:

- average gross salary: €75,000
- average length of service: four years
- replacement cost: half to twice annual salary (Gallup, 2019, for US employers)
- exit payment, before any negotiated premium:
  - low end: the Dutch statutory transition payment, one-third of a month's salary per year of service (€8,333)
  - high end: half a month's salary per year of service, the formula in §1a KSchG that German settlements often start from (€12,500)

The estimate leaves out legal costs, managers' time, lost knowledge and regretted leavers.
