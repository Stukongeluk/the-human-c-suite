**My recommendation: don't run the 10% quota with fixed exits. Take the CEO a review with a real "not meeting expectations" category, calibration that challenges soft ratings, and a firm, supported improvement plan instead.** The quota would put about 120 of your 1,200 people on an exit track whatever their performance, in the two countries where that's hardest: Meta left Germany and the Netherlands (and France and Italy) out of its 2025 "low performer" cuts because of local rules ([Reuters, 8 Feb 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/02/08/meta-prepares-for-layoffs-on-monday--internal-memo)).

Why it fails here:

- **Amsterdam:** a court ends a contract for poor performance only if the person was told in time and had a real chance to improve. It turned Booking.com down for judging an employee against colleagues' averages ([2020 ruling](https://www.binnenlandsbestuur.nl/bestuur-en-organisatie/capra-advocaten/pas-op-onder-het-gemiddelde-presteren-is-nog-geen)). Your works council has a consent right over the assessment system (art. 27(1)(g) WOR).
- **Hamburg:** a "low performer" label carries no weight in a dismissal by itself. If Hamburg has an elected Betriebsrat, it co-determines the assessment principles (§94 BetrVG) ([NJW, Jan 2024](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/aus-der-njw/forum/njw-forum-2024-5-grenzen-arbeitsrechtlicher-bewertungssysteme-2024-01-29)).
- **The maths:** in a team of six, 10% means one person (17%), even if all six are good.
- **The cost:** replacing someone costs half to twice their salary ([Gallup, 2019](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-cost-replacing-employee.aspx)). With exit payments, if 60 of the 120 leave, that's roughly €2.75–9.75M at an assumed €75k average salary.

He still gets what he's after: a higher bar, ratings managers can't soften, real consequences for real underperformance, and outcomes known by the end of Q3 2027.

**First steps**

1. **Talk to the CEO privately this week, before ExCo or any manager hears about it.** A line you can use: *"I want what you want: a higher bar and real consequences for real underperformance. The quota version would likely fail in court in both our countries, and it needs works council consent we're unlikely to get. Let me show you a version that gets the outcome by Q3 and survives both."*
2. **Ask him two questions.** What problem is this solving: soft ratings, specific teams, or cost? And what did "it worked" mean at his last company? Measured how, and under which law?
3. **If it's cost, take it out of the review.** That's a separate decision. Spend, hiring and natural attrition come first, and any reduction goes through works council advice and the collective dismissal rules.
4. **Before ExCo, pull last cycle's ratings by department, past improvement plans and their outcomes, attrition and team sizes.** I don't have these numbers, and they show how big the real problem is.
5. **Bring both works councils in during December**, while they can still change the design.
