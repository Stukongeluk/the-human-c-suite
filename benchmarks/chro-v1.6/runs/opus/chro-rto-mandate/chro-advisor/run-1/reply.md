# Company-wide email: return to the office

## Bottom line

The email is below, and it's written so you can actually send it. The CEO's version ("everyone in five days a week from 1 March, decided") would very likely be void in Utrecht. A Dutch court blocked that exact kind of mandate in 2025 because the works council hadn't consented. It also can't override the contracts of the roughly 265 people you hired as remote or hybrid.

So the draft keeps five days, but as the CEO's *intended* decision. It also takes out "fix our culture", protects the agreements people were hired on, and sets out a real process. Confidence: medium-high. Have employment counsel in NL and DE check it before it goes out.

## The email

Send it from the CEO, not from HR. It's his decision, and people will want his reasons in his words. Fill in the bracketed parts before sending. The dates assume it goes out in the week of 30 November.

---

**Subject:** Working together in Utrecht and Berlin: what we're proposing and what happens next

Hi everyone,

I want to tell you about a change we intend to make, why we want to make it, and what happens before anything changes.

**What we're proposing**
We intend to work together from our Utrecht and Berlin offices five days a week, starting Monday 1 March 2027.

I know this is a big change. For many of you it affects your commute and your family logistics, and for some of you it affects where you live. About a third of you joined us on a remote or hybrid basis over the last four years, on terms we offered you. So I want to be clear about what is decided and what isn't.

**Why**
In four years we've grown to 800 people in two cities, and many colleagues have never worked in the same room. We see the cost of that in [two or three concrete, observable problems, for example: how long it takes new colleagues to find their feet, how often work between Utrecht and Berlin teams stalls, or what you told us in the last engagement survey]. We believe spending more time together is the best way to fix that.

This is not a judgement on anyone who works remotely. Many of you have done excellent work from home. It is also not a way to reduce headcount. We want everyone who is here today to stay.

**What this email does not do**
- It doesn't change anyone's contract or agreed arrangement. If you joined on a remote or hybrid contract, or you have a written home-working agreement, that agreement stands. We'll talk with each of you individually [and we won't change your agreement without your consent].
- It isn't final yet. In the Netherlands, our works council (OR) has to consent before this can take effect. In Germany, our Betriebsrat has a say in how it's applied. We shared the proposal with both today. If they need more time, we'll move the date rather than rush it.

**If your situation makes this hard**
Maybe it's your health, a disability, caring for children or relatives, a long commute or something else. Tell HR in confidence at [email]. You don't have to explain it to your manager first. We'll look at every situation individually, and the final policy will include a clear way to ask for an exception.

**What happens next**
- **Week of 7 December:** Q&A sessions in Utrecht, in Berlin and online, with me and [HR director name]. You can also ask questions anonymously at [link], and we'll publish the answers.
- **By 18 December:** if you're on a remote or hybrid contract, your manager and HR will get in touch to set up a one-to-one conversation. These will all take place by 22 January.
- **Alongside this:** we'll make sure both offices have room for everyone every day: desks, meeting rooms and quiet space for focused work. We'll also look at support for the extra travel costs.
- **By 29 January:** we'll share the final policy, after the works councils have given their view.
- **1 March:** the intended start.

Please come to the sessions, ask the hard questions and talk to your works council members. We want to get this right with you, not just announce it to you.

[CEO name]
CEO

---

## What you need to know before it goes out

1. **Utrecht: without the OR's consent, there's no policy.** Changing where people work needs the OR's consent under art. 27 WOR (the Dutch Works Councils Act). In September 2025 the Oost-Brabant court ruled against Caterpillar over its all-days-in-the-office mandate: the OR's consent was required, so the OR could treat the decision as void, and existing home-working agreements stayed in force. *(Verified: court ruling.)* Neither country gives employees a general legal right to work from home. The Dutch Senate rejected the *Wet werken waar je wilt* in 2023. Your real constraints are the works councils and the contracts.
2. **If the OR says no, five days from 1 March is unlikely to hold up in court.** In the one reported case where a court overrode an OR refusal (Booking.com, November 2026), the mandate was 40% office time. It came with a six-month transition and an exception for personal circumstances, and the OR has appealed. Five days on three months' notice goes far beyond that. *(Medium: my reading of one case.)*
3. **Berlin: the Betriebsrat is involved, person by person.** Whether you offer mobile work at all is the employer's call. How it's organised is something the Betriebsrat co-decides (§ 87(1) no. 14 BetrVG). Ordering someone who has worked mainly from home back to the office full-time generally counts as a transfer, which needs Betriebsrat consent (§ 99 BetrVG; BAG 20.10.2021, 7 ABR 34/20). Where home office is written into the contract, you need either the employee's agreement or an *Änderungskündigung* (a dismissal combined with an offer of new terms), and the legal bar for that is very high. *(Medium-high: law-firm summaries, not yet checked by counsel.)*
4. **The people you'd lose are the ones you least want to lose.** Ding et al. studied 54 large US tech and finance firms that brought in return-to-office mandates. Turnover beyond the normal rate rose by 13–14% on average. Women, senior managers and highly skilled staff left most, and roles took 23% longer to fill. In a randomised trial published in *Nature* (2024), Bloom et al. found that two home days a week cut resignations by a third, with no effect on productivity or promotions. *(Medium: US and Chinese data, but large samples, and the first one is close to your sector.)*
5. **People will read "fix our culture" as "remote people broke it".** Nobody can act on it either. Culture is behaviour, so the email needs the two or three concrete problems the CEO wants to solve. If he can't name them, have that conversation before anything is sent. *(Assumed: I haven't seen your engagement, exit or onboarding data.)*

## Why this matters

If this goes out as a final decision, the likely result is that the OR has it declared void in Utrecht, the Betriebsrat blocks individual returns in Berlin, and your best remote hires start interviewing in January. The CEO would end up with neither the office culture he wants nor the people.

## How to act

1. **Map every remote and hybrid contract** (place-of-work clause, home-working addendum, any revocation clause, where the person lives), plus any existing home-working policy or Betriebsvereinbarung (works agreement) and its notice period. Check that both offices can actually hold everyone five days a week. Owner: HR and Facilities. Deadline: Fri 4 Dec.
2. **Get NL and DE employment counsel** to review the draft and the timeline. Owner: you. Deadline: Fri 4 Dec.
3. **Take Option A vs. B (below) to the CEO.** Start from one question: "Which behaviour do you want to change, and how will we measure it in six months?" Owner: you. Deadline: Wed 2 Dec.
4. **Send the formal consent request to the OR and open talks with the Betriebsrat** before the email goes out, or on the same day at the latest. Owner: CEO and you.
5. **Run the Q&A sessions and finish one-to-ones** with every remote and hybrid hire by 22 Jan. Owner: CEO, managers, HR.

## Risks

- **Cost of leavers:** if 15% of the ~265 remote and hybrid hires leave, that's about 40 roles to refill. At roughly €50k per replacement (recruiting, vacancy, ramp-up), that comes to around €2m. *(Assumed figures: put your own numbers in before you meet the CEO.)*
- **The headcount sentence:** keep "this is not a way to reduce headcount" only if the CEO will stand behind it. If the real aim is to get people to resign, that's a different and much riskier plan, and the email shouldn't pretend otherwise.

## Your decision

**Option A: five days from 1 March (the draft as written).** It sends the strongest signal and matches the CEO's ask. It's also the least likely to win OR consent or court approval, and it carries the highest risk of losing people.

**Option B: three office days, plus in-person onboarding for new joiners, phased in from 1 March and reviewed after six months against the measures from step 3.** Each team picks anchor days for the work that's best done in person. It's a weaker statement, but much easier to defend with the OR and the courts. It matches the Bloom trial, and it keeps the flexibility that holds on to people. To switch the email to B, replace the "What we're proposing" paragraph with:

> We intend to work together from our Utrecht and Berlin offices three days a week, on anchor days each team chooses for the work that's best done in person. We'll build up to this from Monday 1 March 2027 over [three] months. New colleagues will spend their first [four] weeks in the office.

**Recommendation:** argue for B this week. If the CEO insists on five days, send A, but phase it in and keep the bracketed promise not to change remote contracts without consent. In practice you'd need each person's agreement or a formal contract-change procedure anyway.

## Where I could be wrong

Your exit interviews, 90-day turnover or engagement data may already show that remote work causes a specific problem, such as new joiners not getting up to speed or Utrecht–Berlin work stalling. In that case the CEO's instinct has more behind it than "culture" suggests, and the email should open with that evidence. Dutch case law is also still moving: the Booking.com ruling is under appeal, and further appeals are pending. This is general information, not legal advice.
