# Rotterdam and Munich: reducing up to 40 roles lawfully

**Selection method, timeline and the 15 December announcement**

Draft for the leadership team, HR and employment counsel in the Netherlands and Germany · Prepared 23 November 2026 · Status: draft for counsel review. This is not legal advice; counsel in each country must confirm every step before anything is sent or signed.

---

## 1. Summary

- **The ranked spreadsheet is withdrawn.** It ranks people on last year's performance rating, sick days and part-time status. It will not be used, in whole or in part, to select anyone. Managers' lists of people to keep are withdrawn as lists; any exemption follows sections 6 and 7.
- **15 December is the plan announcement, not the names.** Staff hear why, how many roles per area, how selection will work, the timeline and the support. Nobody is told individually that day.
- **Selection follows each country's rules, after consultation,** from a list rebuilt with lawful fields only (section 8).
- **The number can still go down.** Consultation in both countries must cover ways to avoid or reduce the redundancies, so the board's approval is treated as an intended decision, with up to 40 roles as the ceiling.
- **Leadership owns the result.** The announcement says plainly that this follows from leadership decisions, and states what the leadership team gives up before any role ends.
- **Estimated exits:** Rotterdam roughly April to August 2027 (voluntary leavers earlier), Munich roughly April to October 2027. These are estimates; they depend on consultation, permits and individual notice periods.

## 3. Why the original criteria can't be used

| Criterion | Rotterdam | Munich | Also |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part-time status | Treating employees differently because of their working hours when a contract is ended is prohibited unless objectively justified (art. 7:648 BW). | A part-time employee may not be treated worse because of part-time work unless there are objective reasons (§4(1) TzBfG). | Most part-time workers in both countries are women, so it is also indirect sex discrimination. |
| Sick days | People who are sick generally can't be given notice through UWV during the first two years of illness. Selection follows the reflection principle, which has no absence criterion; workers with a work limitation can be protected, not selected out. | Not one of the four social-selection criteria. Absence alone does not justify keeping a less protected colleague instead (BAG case law). Long-term illness can amount to a disability, which is protected. | Absence records are data about health, a special category under GDPR (art. 9); data collected to manage absence can't be repurposed to choose who leaves. |
| Last year's performance rating | Not part of selection for economic dismissal. Poor performance is a separate route through the cantonal court, which expects timely warning, support and a real chance to improve. | Not part of social selection. Performance can only justify exempting a specific person, with written reasons, weighed against the colleague who would go instead (§1(3) KSchG). | The ratings were not designed or calibrated for this decision, and the works councils will ask for them. |
| Manager keep-lists | Only an individually documented exception for indispensable knowledge or skills. | Only an individually justified exemption, disclosed to the Betriebsrat in the hearing for each dismissal. | A list of names chosen before the method is agreed is what a court or UWV looks for first. |

Rules for this step:

- Name the work that stops (products, services, projects, reports), so the people who stay don't carry the same work with fewer colleagues.
- Define job groups by job content, required knowledge and skills, level and pay. Don't draw groups or "unique roles" around particular people; UWV and German courts test for exactly that.
- Count in FTE. Contracted hours are used only to count positions, never to rank people.

## 6. Selection in Rotterdam: the reflection principle

Per business location and per category of interchangeable jobs in which positions disappear:

1. End the work of external workers first: agency workers, secondees, self-employed contractors and staff borrowed from another location.
2. Then apply the reflection principle across the remaining staff, in this order of groups: employees above state pension (AOW) age; on-call (zero-hours) workers; fixed-term employees whose contract ends within 26 weeks of UWV's decision; permanent employees and longer fixed-term contracts.
3. Spread the exits across five age bands (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55 and over) so the age mix stays about the same. Within each band, the most recently hired go first.
4. Exceptions, each documented: an employee whose special knowledge or skills make them indispensable (with evidence); an employee with a work limitation (may be protected); a dismissal ban that won't lapse in time (the next person in the same band goes instead); a collective-agreement dismissal committee with its own order, if your agreement has one.
5. Protections to check per person (counsel to complete): illness, pregnancy and maternity leave, works council membership and other dismissal bans.
6. Route: a UWV permit for each dismissal, or a voluntary settlement agreement. Employees are entitled to the statutory transition payment; a social plan is negotiated on top. Employer notice is one to four months by length of service, minus the time the UWV procedure took, with at least one month remaining.

## 7. Selection in Munich: social selection

1. Confirm that a Betriebsrat exists and that the Dismissal Protection Act applies (more than ten employees; the person has more than six months' service).
2. Form groups of comparable employees per establishment: the same level, interchangeable by contract and skills. Part-time and full-time staff doing the same work are normally compared together.
3. Weigh the four statutory criteria: length of service, age, maintenance obligations and severe disability. Agree the points scheme with the Betriebsrat as a works agreement; selection guidelines need its consent (§95 BetrVG), and an agreed weighting can then be reviewed by a court only for gross error (§1(4) KSchG).
4. Exemptions (§1(3) sentence 2 KSchG): only individually, naming the specific knowledge, skills or performance and the business consequence of losing that person, weighed against the more protected colleague who would go instead. A rating alone is not enough, and a colleague's sickness absence on its own is no reason to exempt someone. Each exemption is disclosed to the Betriebsrat in the hearing for each dismissal (§102 BetrVG).
5. Optional: a name list agreed with the Betriebsrat in the reconciliation of interests (§1(5) KSchG). Dismissals on it are presumed to be operationally necessary and the selection is reviewed only for gross error. This is the one lawful way to finalise a list of names in Munich, and only together with the Betriebsrat.
6. Protected people need prior consent from the authority or are excluded: severe disability (Integrationsamt), pregnancy and parental leave, Betriebsrat members (counsel to complete).
7. Sequence: negotiate the reconciliation of interests and the social plan (§§111–112 BetrVG) and run the written consultation on the collective dismissal (§17(2) KSchG); only once that consultation has ended, file the collective dismissal notification with the employment agency; then hear the Betriebsrat on each dismissal (§102 BetrVG); then give notice. Since 1 April 2026, the Federal Labour Court holds that notices are void if the notification is missing or was filed before consultation with the Betriebsrat ended. Statutory notice periods run up to seven months to the end of a month (§622 BGB).

| Field | Rotterdam | Munich | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site / establishment | Yes | Yes | Selection unit |
| Job title and job group | Yes | Yes | Interchangeable jobs / comparable employees |
| Contract type | Yes | Yes | NL order of groups; DE comparability |
| Date of birth | Yes (age band) | Yes (age) | Selection criterion |
| Start date | Yes | Yes | NL: most recently hired first within band; DE: length of service |
| Contracted hours | Yes | Yes | Counting FTE only, never ranking |
| Maintenance obligations | No | Yes | DE criterion; from payroll data, confirmed by the employee |
| Severe disability (where known) | No | Yes | DE criterion, protective |
| Exemption and written reason | Yes | Yes | Documented exceptions only |
| Legal protection flag | Yes | Yes | Kept by HR and counsel only, access-restricted |

**Never in the list:** sick days or any absence or health information, performance ratings, part-time status as a criterion, leave history, complaints or concerns raised, union or works council activity, nationality.

**Transparency before anything is final:**

- Each employee in an affected group receives the data used for them (job group, start date, age band, contract type, and in Munich the social criteria) and has ten working days to correct it.
- The works council and Betriebsrat see the method and an anonymised outcome per site: exits by age band, gender and part-time share, against the group's starting mix. Any skew is explained or fixed before decisions.
- A person decides each case; the list is the input, not the decision.

## 11. Support for anyone whose role ends

- Above-statutory terms where the company can afford them, and paid notice so people can job-hunt without losing income
- From the first day of notice: paid time to search, coaching, a training budget per person, outplacement, CV and LinkedIn review
- Written references and a promise of a positive reference call; priority if the company rehires; an alumni network
- Keep the laptop or phone where possible, after company data is wiped
- Confidential psychological and financial support
- Extra care for people on a work visa, close to retirement or on leave
- On the day: each person hears it from their own manager or a senior leader with HR present, in person or live; access changes happen after the conversation; a written summary the same day; a chance to say goodbye

## 12. The people who stay

- Tell them the same day what happened, why, and what it means for them. Say the round is over only if it is true.
- Re-plan work and priorities with each team: stop work, don't just redistribute it.
- 1:1s within two weeks; watch engagement, absence and regretted exits for six to twelve months. In one well-known study, a 1% workforce reduction was associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the following year.
