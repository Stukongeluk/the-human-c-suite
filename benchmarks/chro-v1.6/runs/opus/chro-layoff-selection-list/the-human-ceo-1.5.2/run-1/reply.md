I wouldn't finalise this list, and I'd stop it circulating today. Two of its three criteria, sick days and part-time status, are unlawful grounds for selection in both the Netherlands and Germany, and neither country lets you rank people out on last year's rating or on managers' keep-lists. Use the 15th to announce the plan and open consultation; names come after that, through each country's own selection rules.

Two facts decide the timing:

- **40 of 300 is 13%.** That almost certainly makes this a collective dismissal in at least one office: in the Netherlands from 20 people in one UWV region within three months, in Germany from 10% or more than 25 people at a site of 60 to 499. In Rotterdam that brings a one-month waiting period after UWV confirms the notification, and settlement agreements count too ([UWV, October 2026](https://www.uwv.nl/nl/ontslag/collectief-ontslag)).
- **In Germany, notices are void** if the collective dismissal notification is missing or was filed before consultation with the Betriebsrat ended ([Federal Labour Court, 1 April 2026](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/en/presse/massenentlassung-rechtsfolge-von-fehlern-im-anzeigeverfahren/)). Names announced on the 15th couldn't lawfully be acted on.

**Why the spreadsheet can't be fixed by tweaking it**

- **Part-time:** treating someone worse in a dismissal because of their hours is prohibited unless objectively justified (art. 7:648 BW; §4 TzBfG). Most part-timers are women, so it is also indirect sex discrimination.
- **Sick days:** absence records are health data under GDPR, collected to manage absence, not to choose who leaves. The criterion lands on people with chronic illness or a disability, who are protected in both countries, and in the Netherlands people who are sick generally can't be given notice through UWV anyway.
- **Last year's rating:** Dutch selection follows the reflection principle (by job group, age band and hire date), not performance. German social selection weighs service, age, maintenance obligations and severe disability; performance can only justify exempting a named person, with written reasons.
- **Keep-lists:** they survive only as individually documented exceptions, and in Munich the Betriebsrat must be told who was exempted and why.
