# Reducing up to 40 roles in Rotterdam and Munich: selection plan and announcement for 15 December

**Status:** working draft for the executive team, HR and employment counsel in both countries. Confidential. It contains no names and none should be added until the steps in section 4 allow it.
**Date:** 23 November 2026
**Not legal advice.** Every step, threshold and date below has to be checked by Dutch and German employment counsel before anyone acts on it.

---

## 1. The decision in one paragraph

The board has approved reducing up to 40 of about 300 roles. We will not use the current HR spreadsheet, because it ranks people on sick days and part-time hours, which the law in both countries forbids, and on last year's rating, which neither country's selection rules use. On 15 December we announce the proposal, the reasons, the process and the support. Before that, the works councils in both offices hear it. We do not announce names on that day. People are selected per location under the law that applies there: the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) in Rotterdam and social selection (Sozialauswahl) in Munich. Before that, we reduce the number of people who have to be dismissed.

## 2. What changes from the current spreadsheet

| Criterion in the spreadsheet | Can we use it? | Why | What replaces it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number of sick days | No | Counting absence can be indirect disability discrimination (CJEU, *Ruiz Conejero*, C-270/16, 2018). In the Netherlands, an employee who is ill is in most cases protected against notice (art. 7:670 BW). It also means using health data, a special category under GDPR art. 9, for a dismissal decision | Nothing. Illness is a reason a person may be protected, never a reason they are selected |
| Part-time hours | No | Treating part-time staff less favourably is prohibited (NL art. 7:648 BW; DE §4 TzBfG; Directive 97/81/EC). Most part-time staff are women, so it is also indirect sex discrimination | Nothing. Hours play no part in the order |
| Last year's performance rating | Not as a ranking criterion | Neither selection method uses ratings. In Germany, ratings count for personnel decisions only if the appraisal principles behind them had works council consent (§94 BetrVG) | The legal order per location (section 4). Performance can only come in through the narrow exceptions in section 5 |
| Managers' lists of who to keep | Only as requests | Manager preference is not a legal ground in either country | An exception request per person, with a written, job-related reason, reviewed by HR and counsel (section 5) |

**What happens to the spreadsheet now:** no further copies and no use for any selection. Access is limited to HR leadership and counsel. Counsel and the data protection officer decide how it is kept. Do not delete it on our own initiative.

**Aggregate check, this week:** HR compares the people the spreadsheet would have selected with all 300. The comparison covers the share of women, part-time staff, people over 50, people on leave and people with recent long-term sick leave, per location. These are totals only, with no names. The board then sees the legal risk we are avoiding in numbers.

## 3. Before anyone is selected: fewer dismissals

The board approved a reduction of up to 40 roles. That is not the same as dismissing 40 people. Each line below is a role that can go without a forced exit.

Report the result to the board as "up to 40 roles, of which X closed without dismissals". That figure is the real number of people who face forced selection.

## 4. Selection method per location

### Rotterdam (Netherlands)

1. **Groups of interchangeable jobs at the Rotterdam location.** Jobs are interchangeable when they are similar in content, required knowledge and skills, level and pay. HR draws the groups and counsel checks them. A group is not drawn so that it captures particular people.
2. **Order within each group in which roles go.** External staff (agency workers, freelancers, secondees, staff borrowed from another location) go first. After them come employees at state pension (AOW) age, then on-call or zero-hours employees, then fixed-term contracts ending within 26 weeks of the UWV decision, and then everyone else.
3. **Within each of these groups, the reflection principle applies.** Staff are sorted into five age bands (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55 and over), and exits are spread across the bands so that the age mix stays the same. Within each band, the person with the shortest service goes first.
4. **Narrow exceptions,** each documented (section 5): an employee shown with documents to be indispensable because of special knowledge or skills, or an employee with a work disability (arbeidsbeperking). If an employee is protected by a dismissal ban, for example illness, pregnancy or works council membership, the employer may propose the next person in line in the same age band instead. Counsel confirms the conditions.
5. **A collective agreement may set its own order** through an independent dismissal committee. Check whether ours does.
6. **Process:**
   - The works council gives advice on the proposed decision (art. 25 WOR). The request goes out while its advice can still change the plan. If the final decision departs from that advice, it is suspended for one month.
   - If 20 or more dismissals fall in one UWV work area within three months, we notify UWV and the unions and consult the unions (WMCO). The one-month waiting period runs from UWV's confirmation that the notification is complete. During it, no contract may end, including through a settlement agreement, unless the unions agree to shorten it. Individual conversations are allowed.
   - Then comes a UWV permit application per person, with the reflection overview, or a settlement agreement.
   - Everyone dismissed gets the statutory transition payment (transitievergoeding).
   - The notice period is one to four months depending on service, minus the time the UWV procedure took, with at least one month left.

### Munich (Germany)

1. **Is there an elected works council (Betriebsrat)?** Confirm this first. The process differs materially.
2. **With a works council:**
   - We negotiate a reconciliation of interests (Interessenausgleich) and a social plan (§§111–112 BetrVG) before the change is carried out.
   - Selection guidelines, such as a points scheme, need its consent (§95 BetrVG).
   - Before any notice is given, we consult it in writing on the collective dismissal (§17(2) KSchG) and hear it on each dismissal (§102 BetrVG). A dismissal without that hearing is invalid.
   - If a named list is wanted, the route that holds up best is a list agreed with the works council inside the reconciliation of interests. Courts then presume the operational need and review the social selection only for gross error (§1(5) KSchG). The §102 hearing still applies.
3. **Social selection** compares employees who could do each other's jobs, at the same level, within the Munich establishment. It weighs length of service, age, maintenance obligations (for example children or a dependent partner) and severe disability (§1(3) KSchG). A weighting agreed with the works council can only be challenged for gross error (§1(4) KSchG).
4. **Narrow exception (§1(3), second sentence, KSchG):** an employee can be left out of the comparison only where keeping them is in a justified operational interest, because of their knowledge, skills or performance or to keep a balanced staff structure. This is weighed against the social protection of the person who would then go instead. A manager's preference does not meet this test.
5. **Special protection:**
   - Severely disabled employees: the Integration Office must consent (§168 SGB IX).
   - Pregnancy and the period after birth (§17 MuSchG), and parental leave (§18 BEEG): the competent authority must consent.
   - Works council members can't be given ordinary notice (§15 KSchG).
6. **Notification:** above the thresholds (for example 10% or more than 25 people in an establishment of 60 to 499 within 30 days), we notify the employment agency before any notice is given (§17 KSchG). Termination agreements the employer initiates count towards the threshold. Mistakes in the notification can make the dismissals invalid, so counsel checks it before filing.
7. **Notice periods** grow with service, up to seven months to the end of a month after 20 years (§622 BGB).
8. **Without a works council:** social selection, the §17 notification and the special protections still apply. Only the negotiation steps fall away.

## 5. Exception request (one per person a manager wants to keep)

Each request is decided by HR and counsel, not by the requesting manager. The result is recorded either way.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Location and job group | |
| The specific knowledge, skill or operational need | Concrete and job-related. "Strong performer" or "key person" is not enough |
| Why nobody else in the group can cover it, and how long transferring it would take | |
| How the business would cope during this person's holiday or illness | (If it already copes, the person is not indispensable) |
| Who goes instead, and that person's protection | Name withheld until the legal process allows |
| Evidence attached | Documents, not opinions |
| Decision, by whom, date, reason | |

Expect most of the managers' current names not to meet this test. Tell managers that early, so nobody hears an informal promise that the process can't keep.

## 8. Support from the first day of notice

- **Netherlands:** the statutory transition payment, plus a social plan agreed with the works council: severance above the statutory amount where affordable, outplacement, a training budget per person and paid time to look for work.
- **Germany:** severance under the social plan. Also look at a transfer company (Transfergesellschaft), where people can receive transfer short-time allowance for up to 12 months while they retrain and look for work (§111 SGB III). Counsel and the works council check whether it fits.
- **Everyone:** coaching and job-search help from the day notice is given. Laptop or phone kept after the company data is wiped, where possible. Written references and a promise of a positive reference call. Priority if we hire again. Confidential psychological and financial support, available from 15 December including over the holidays.
- **Extra care** for people on a work permit or residence permit linked to their job, people close to retirement, and people on leave.

## 9. Communication

- Works councils hear it first, before 15 December.
- Managers are briefed on 14 December with a script and answers to likely questions, and have someone to talk to themselves.
- On 15 December, the CEO announces it live per location, in person or on a live call, with Q&A. The written version follows the same day. No mass email as the first news, and no access changes.
- Each team has a meeting with its own manager on 15 or 16 December.
- Later, each affected person hears the news personally from their own manager, with HR present, before anything in writing. They get a written summary the same day: package, deadlines, contacts. Access changes happen after the conversation, never as the way someone finds out.
- The people who stay hear the same day what changed, what it means for them, and whether the round is over (only if that is true). They have 1:1s within two weeks and a check-in after two and six weeks.

## 11. What we will watch afterwards (team and location level, never individuals)

Regretted leavers in the six to twelve months after the change. Absence. A short engagement and trust pulse at six weeks and six months. Internal moves made. Time to fill critical roles. The outcome of the equality check per location. Downsizing tends to raise voluntary turnover among the people who stay (Trevor & Nyberg, *Academy of Management Journal*, 2008), so this is a real cost to plan for, not an afterthought.
