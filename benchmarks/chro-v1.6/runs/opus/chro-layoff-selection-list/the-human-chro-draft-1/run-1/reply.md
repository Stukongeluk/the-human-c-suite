**What's wrong with the current list**
- **Sick days.** Counting absence can be disability discrimination (CJEU, *Ruiz Conejero*, C-270/16, 2018). In the Netherlands, an employee who is ill is also protected against notice in most cases (art. 7:670 BW). And it means using health data to decide a dismissal, which is a GDPR problem on its own.
- **Part-time.** Treating part-timers worse is banned in both countries (NL art. 7:648 BW, DE §4 TzBfG, Directive 97/81/EC). Most part-timers are women, so it's also indirect sex discrimination.
- **Last year's rating.** Neither country's selection method uses it.
- **Managers' keep lists.** These can only be requests. Both countries allow narrow exceptions: in the Netherlands, an employee shown with documents to be indispensable; in Germany, a justified operational interest weighed against the person who would go instead (§1(3) KSchG). Each needs a written, job-related reason. Expect most names not to pass, and tell managers before anyone promises someone their job.

A list that's quick to build tends to end up in front of a judge. Dutch UWV checks the selection order person by person before it grants a permit.

**What the law sets instead**
- **Rotterdam:** selection is per group of interchangeable jobs at the location.
  - Agency staff, freelancers and secondees go first. Then, in order, people at state pension age, on-call staff, fixed-term contracts ending within 26 weeks, and everyone else.
  - Within each group, the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) applies: five age bands, with the shortest-serving going first in each band.
  - The works council gives its advice on the reorganisation (art. 25 WOR) while that advice can still change the plan.
  - At 20 or more dismissals in the region within three months, you notify UWV and the unions. A one-month waiting period follows in which no contract can end, settlement agreements included.
- **Munich:** first confirm whether there is an elected works council (Betriebsrat).
  - Selection is a social selection (Sozialauswahl) based on length of service, age, maintenance obligations and severe disability.
  - With a works council, you negotiate a reconciliation of interests and a social plan, and selection guidelines need its consent. It must be heard before every notice, or the dismissal is invalid.
  - If you want a named list, agree it with the works council in the reconciliation of interests. Courts then review it only for gross error (§1(5) KSchG).
  - Severely disabled staff, pregnant staff and people on parental leave need an authority's consent before notice.
