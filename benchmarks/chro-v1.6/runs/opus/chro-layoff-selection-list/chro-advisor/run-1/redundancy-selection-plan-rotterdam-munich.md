# Redundancy selection plan: Rotterdam and Munich

Prepared 23 Nov 2026 for the CEO, HR lead and Legal. Confidential.

**Status:** this is a draft. Dutch and German employment counsel must confirm it before any of it reaches staff.

**How the tags work:**
- **[Verified]:** checked against a current official or legal source, listed at the end.
- **[Medium]:** established law or a reasonable inference that I haven't re-checked today.
- **[Assumed]:** depends on facts about your company that I don't have.

---

## 1. Starting point

- **The cut:** 40 of 300 roles (13%) across two countries. I don't know how the 40 split between the sites [Assumed].
- **Work from roles, not people.** The board approved a number of roles. In both countries the law starts from positions that disappear. It then applies fixed rules to decide which people in comparable roles are affected.
- **The order of work:**
  1. Decide which positions go, and why.
  2. Find out who can be moved into open roles.
  3. Select people under the local rules.
  4. Add documented exceptions.

  Ranking everyone and cutting from the bottom is not lawful in either country.
- **Each country's rules apply to its own staff.** Selection runs separately for each site. Cuts can't be shifted between Rotterdam and Munich to get around either country's rules [Medium].

## 2. The HR spreadsheet, column by column

### Sick days

**Rotterdam (NL):**
- Sick days are not a selection criterion [Verified].
- Anyone in their first two years of illness can't be given notice (the Dutch ban on dismissal during illness) [Verified].
- EU courts have held that long-term illness can count as a disability (CJEU, HK Danmark, 2013), so this is a discrimination risk [Medium].
- Absence caused by pregnancy makes it sex discrimination [Medium].
- Sickness records are health data, a special category under GDPR Art. 9 [Medium].

**Munich (DE):**
- Sick days are not one of the four German selection criteria [Verified].
- The same disability and sex discrimination risks apply under the AGG, Germany's general equal treatment law [Medium].
- Dismissal for sickness is a separate legal route with its own strict tests. It can't be built into a redundancy ranking [Medium].
- The same GDPR issue applies [Medium].

**What to do:** remove the column. Don't use it as a tiebreaker or behind a stand-in label such as "reliability".

### Part-time status

**Rotterdam (NL):**
- Treating staff differently because of their working hours is prohibited (art. 7:648 BW) [Medium].
- The afspiegelingsbeginsel (section 3) doesn't look at working hours [Verified].
- Where part-timers are mostly women, this is indirect sex discrimination [Medium].

**Munich (DE):**
- Part-timers may not be treated worse because they work part-time (§4 TzBfG) [Verified].
- In the selection pool, part-timers generally count as comparable with full-timers. The exception is a documented business decision that a role needs particular hours [Medium].
- The same indirect sex discrimination risk applies under the AGG [Medium].

**What to do:** remove the column. If a role genuinely needs full-time cover, record that as a decision about the role, made before selection and reviewed by counsel.

### Last year's performance rating

**Rotterdam (NL):** the rating is not a selection criterion. It can only come in through two routes:
- An "indispensable" exception: the person has special knowledge or skills, and you document why losing them would seriously harm the business [Verified].
- A talent scheme of up to 10% of a role category, only if your CAO allows one [Verified; the source is from 2018, so confirm it is still current].

**Munich (DE):** the rating is not a selection criterion. It can help justify keeping a named high performer out of the pool. That is only allowed where keeping them serves a legitimate business interest. The exception is narrow, and the employer must prove it [Verified].

**What to do:**
- Use the rating only as one piece of evidence for an exception justified person by person.
- First check whether people who were on leave or part-time were rated lower across the board. If they were, the rating carries the same bias [Medium].
- A single annual rating is weak evidence on its own.

### Managers' "keep" names

These have no legal standing in either country [Medium]. Section 5 sets out what to do with them.

## 3. Rotterdam: how selection has to work

**Confirm first:**
- **CAO (collective agreement):** it may set its own rules, such as a dismissal committee, a talent scheme or a social plan.
- **OR (works council):** it exists. It is mandatory at 50 or more employees [Medium].
- **Unions:** which ones have members in the company.

**Steps:**

1. **Business case for each position.** Say which positions disappear, and in which category of interchangeable roles. Roles are grouped by content, required knowledge and skills, level and pay [Verified].
2. **Redeployment.** Before anyone is dismissed, check open vacancies and suitable roles. Include training where that is reasonable [Medium].
3. **Order within each category** [Verified]. Each group is cleared before the next:
   - External workers: temps, payrolled staff, seconded staff and freelancers in those roles.
   - Employees who have reached state pension (AOW) age.
   - Zero-hours and on-call contracts.
   - Fixed-term contracts that end within 26 weeks.
   - Permanent staff and longer fixed-term contracts. These go through the afspiegelingsbeginsel:
     - Split them into age bands: 15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55 and 55+.
     - Each band loses its proportional share of the cuts.
     - Within each band, the most recently hired go first.
4. **Allowed exceptions, each documented** [Verified]:
   - An indispensable employee with special knowledge or skills.
   - An employee with a recognised work limitation (the target group of the national scheme for jobs for people with disabilities).
   - A person under a dismissal ban that won't lift within four weeks. The next person in the same age band is selected instead.
   - The CAO talent scheme, if there is one.
5. **Dismissal bans to check for each person** [Verified]:
   - Illness, during the first two years.
   - Pregnancy, maternity leave, and six weeks after return.
   - OR members and candidates, until two years after their membership ends.
   - Union membership or union activity.
   - A request for parental or care leave.
6. **Procedure.**
   - **OR advice request** (WOR art. 25). Ask while the advice can still change the decision. If the final decision departs from the advice, there is a one-month standstill [Medium].
   - **Collective-redundancy notice (WMCO).** This applies if 20 or more terminations fall in the Zuid-Holland/Zeeland work area within three months. Mutual agreements count toward the 20. You must:
     - notify UWV and the unions
     - consult the unions
     - wait one month before dismissing, unless the unions state in writing that they were consulted and agree [Verified]
   - **Dismissal itself:** each person needs either a UWV permit or a mutual termination agreement. UWV checks the business reason, redeployment and the afspiegeling [Medium].
   - **Costs:**
     - Everyone gets the statutory transition payment: one-third of a monthly salary per year of service, plus any social plan [Medium].
     - Time spent on the UWV procedure can shorten the notice period, but at least one month must remain [Medium].

## 4. Munich: how selection has to work

**Confirm first:**
- **KSchG (Dismissal Protection Act) applies.** This needs a workplace with more than 10 employees, and covers employees with more than six months' service [Medium].
- **Workplace headcount.** This is needed for the mass-dismissal thresholds.
- **Betriebsrat (works council).** Does one exist?
- **Tarifvertrag (collective agreement).** Does one apply?

**Steps:**

1. **Documented business decision:** which positions go, and why [Medium].
2. **Redeployment.** Offer free comparable positions in the company first [Medium].
3. **Comparable groups.** Group together employees who are interchangeable. They have comparable qualifications, sit at the same level, and could be moved between roles by instruction [Verified].
4. **Social selection on four criteria** [Verified]:
   - Length of service.
   - Age.
   - Legal maintenance obligations to dependants.
   - Severe disability: a disability degree of at least 50, or at least 30 for people officially treated as equivalent.

   Use a points scheme. Ideally, agree it with the Betriebsrat as a selection guideline (§95 BetrVG). Once agreed, a court will only check the scheme for gross errors (§1(4) KSchG) [Verified].
5. **Exclusions from the pool:**
   - Only named high performers can be excluded. Each needs a concrete, documented business interest such as knowledge, skills or performance.
   - Alternatively, there must be a documented need to keep a balanced staff structure.
   - Courts read this narrowly, and the burden of proof is on you [Verified].
6. **Special protection.** Dismissal needs approval from an authority, or is barred [Medium]:
   - Severely disabled employees need consent from the Integration Office (Integrationsamt) (§168 SGB IX).
   - Pregnancy, and up to four months after birth (§17 MuSchG).
   - Parental leave (§18 BEEG).
   - Betriebsrat members (§15 KSchG), and other protected roles such as the data protection officer.
7. **Procedure with the Betriebsrat:**
   - **Information and consultation on the restructuring** (§111 BetrVG) [Medium]:
     - Try to reach a reconciliation of interests, the agreement on whether and how the cuts happen (Interessenausgleich).
     - Negotiate a social plan, which sets severance and support for those leaving (§112).
     - If you implement without trying for the reconciliation of interests, employees can claim compensation (§113).
   - **Written information and consultation under §17(2) KSchG.** It must cover the reasons, the numbers and job groups affected, the time period, the selection criteria and how severance is calculated [Verified].
   - **Mass-dismissal notice to the Agentur für Arbeit (federal employment agency)** [Verified]:
     - It is required if, within 30 days, the dismissals reach:
       - more than 5 in a workplace of 21–59 employees
       - 10% or more than 25 in a workplace of 60–499 employees
       - 30 or more in a workplace of 500 or more
     - File it only after consultation is complete.
     - Dismissals without the notice are void and can't be fixed afterwards (EU Court of Justice, 30 Oct 2025).
     - A notice filed before consultation is complete also makes the dismissals void (Federal Labour Court, 1 Apr 2026).
   - **Hearing for each person.** The Betriebsrat must be heard before each individual notice (§102 BetrVG). Without the hearing, the notice is void [Medium].
   - **The notice itself** [Medium]:
     - It must be in writing with a wet-ink signature (§623 BGB).
     - Notice periods run from four weeks to seven months depending on service (§622 BGB). Contracts or the Tarifvertrag may set longer periods.
     - Employees have three weeks to go to court (§4 KSchG).
   - **If there is no Betriebsrat:** social selection and the mass-dismissal notice still apply [Medium].

## 5. The managers' keep lists

- **Treat each name as a request, not a decision.**
- **Each request needs:**
  - the person's role and comparable group
  - the specific knowledge, skills or contribution
  - the evidence behind it
  - for Rotterdam: why it can't be covered or handed over in reasonable time
  - for Munich: the concrete business interest
- **Run a check for skew, led by counsel and covered by legal privilege.** Compare the keep lists with the pool by part-time status, gender, age band and leave status (parental, sick), using totals only, never names. If the lists are skewed, redo them.
- **Rejected requests** put the person back in the normal selection.
- **Correct any promises.** If a manager has told someone they are "safe", correct it now.

## 6. The spreadsheet and data protection

- **The sick-day column is health data.** It wasn't collected to select people for redundancy, and there is no lawful basis for using it that way [Medium].
- **Remove it from the working file and restrict access to the original.** The data protection officer and counsel decide how long to keep it. It may be needed as evidence later, so don't delete it without counsel's advice.
- **Build the new file with only the fields each country's rules need** (section 10). Keep selection in a controlled file, not an automated scoring tool. In Munich, the Betriebsrat has a say over technical systems that monitor performance or behaviour (§87(1) no. 6 BetrVG) [Medium].

## 8. The 15th: what to say, and in what order

**Order:**
1. **Works councils first.** Give the OR (Rotterdam), the Betriebsrat (Munich) and the Dutch unions the written proposal privately. Ideally do this a few days before, and the same day at the latest.
2. **Managers of affected teams.** Brief them and prepare them for questions.
3. **All staff,** in person or live at each site, on the same day. Send the written note afterwards, not before.
4. **Clients and others outside the company,** only if they are affected.

**What to cover:**
- why the cuts are needed
- how many roles are at risk, per site and function
- how selection will work: under the legal rules, not a ranking
- the timeline
- the voluntary scheme
- the support on offer
- who people can talk to

**What to avoid:**
- No names.
- No individual "you're safe" or "you're at risk" messages.
- No email before the in-person announcement.
- Nothing shared about the keep lists.

## 9. Treating people well

**People who leave:**
- Offer a voluntary scheme first, open to everyone in the affected groups on the same terms.
- Give them priority for open roles.
- Pay severance above the legal minimum where you can afford it.
- Provide outplacement support and paid time to look for work.
- Agree references in advance.
- Offer an employee assistance programme (confidential counselling).
- Hold individual conversations in person and in private, with HR present, and follow up in writing the same day.

**People who stay:**
- Explain why the cuts are happening and how selection was done.
- Managers hold team conversations within 48 hours.
- Be honest about what changes in workload. Don't pretend it's business as usual.

**People on sick leave or parental leave:**
- They are protected, and they should hear the general announcement personally, by phone or a visit. Not by email, and not from colleagues.
