CHRO: Rotterdam and Munich redundancy selection

BOTTOM LINE
I won't finalise this list, and I'd ask you not to let it go out. In both the Netherlands and Germany, it's unlawful to pick people for redundancy because of their sick days or because they work part-time. In Rotterdam, last year's rating isn't an allowed selection criterion either. You can still announce on the 15th. Announce the proposal and the process, without names. Confidence: high.

WHAT
- **Sick days and part-time status have to come out.** Ranking on sick days risks disability discrimination, and sex discrimination where the absence is pregnancy-related. It also uses health data. In the Netherlands, no one can be given notice during their first two years of illness [Verified]. Ranking on part-time status treats part-timers worse because of their hours, which the law forbids (§4 TzBfG [Verified]; art. 7:648 BW [Medium]). Part-timers are mostly women, so it is also indirect sex discrimination [Assumed for your workforce].
- **Rotterdam has to follow fixed selection rules, not a ranking.** For each group of interchangeable roles, temps and contractors go first. Then the afspiegelingsbeginsel applies: staff are split into age bands, and within each band the most recently hired go first. UWV checks this before it grants a dismissal permit. Performance only counts through narrow, documented exceptions. One is an indispensable employee. The other is a talent scheme, if your CAO allows one, capped at 10% [Verified].
- **Munich has to use social selection.** This compares people in similar roles on length of service, age, maintenance obligations (dependants) and severe disability. A rating can at most support keeping a named high performer, and each case needs its own documented justification. Courts read that exception narrowly [Verified].
- **The managers' keep lists don't count as they stand.** Each name has to become a documented exception request that fits the rules above. If it doesn't, that person goes back into the normal selection. The lists were drawn up alongside this spreadsheet, so they also need a check for skew by part-time status, gender, age and leave [Medium].
- **Board approval is not consultation.** Rotterdam needs:
  - the works council's (OR) advice
  - a collective-redundancy notice to UWV and the unions, if 20 or more of the cuts fall there, followed by a one-month wait
  - a UWV permit or a mutual agreement for each person

  Munich needs:
  - the Betriebsrat (German works council) process, if there is one
  - a mass-dismissal notice to the employment agency, filed only after consultation is complete
  - a Betriebsrat hearing for each person

  In Germany, a missing or premature mass-dismissal notice makes the dismissals void, and that can't be fixed afterwards [Verified for the rules; Assumed for your headcounts and works councils].

WHY THIS MATTERS
This ranking would push out people who were ill, people who care for children or relatives and people with a disability. They are the ones least able to absorb losing their job, and the reasons have nothing to do with which roles the business no longer needs. All 40 exits could then be challenged: UWV refusing permits, void dismissals in Munich, discrimination claims and back pay. The 260 people who stay will also judge the company by how this was done.

HOW TO ACT
1. Take sick days and part-time status out of the selection file. Restrict access to the original and give it to counsel. Tell managers in writing that these factors are not used → HR lead + data protection officer → Thu 26 Nov
2. Instruct Dutch and German employment counsel. Confirm the facts the plan depends on: headcount per site, works councils, collective agreements (CAO in Rotterdam, Tarifvertrag in Munich), unions, temps and contractors → HR lead + Legal → Fri 27 Nov
3. Rebuild the cut as roles, not people: which positions go at each site, by group of comparable roles, after checking open vacancies for redeployment. Turn the keep lists into documented exception requests → HR lead + business heads + counsel → Fri 4 Dec
4. Prepare the consultation packs:
   - Rotterdam: the OR advice request and the collective-redundancy notice
   - Munich: information for the Betriebsrat, and a plan to negotiate the restructuring terms and a social plan (severance and support for those leaving)
   - Both sites: a voluntary-departure offer open to everyone in the affected groups on the same terms

   → HR lead + counsel → Thu 10 Dec
5. On 15 Dec, brief the OR and the Betriebsrat first. Then announce the proposal to staff in person at both sites: why, how many roles per site and function, the process, timeline and support. No names → CEO + CHRO → Tue 15 Dec

RISKS
- **If names go out on the 15th from this spreadsheet:** permits are refused in Rotterdam, dismissals are void in Munich and discrimination claims follow in both countries. The restructuring then has to restart months later, after trust is gone.
- **Exit dates move later.** Realistically, people leave in spring 2027 in Rotterdam and between spring and autumn 2027 in Munich, depending on consultation and notice periods [Assumed]. The CFO should redo the savings and severance figures on those dates before the board finds out from someone else.
- **The keep lists may already be known.** Keep them confidential and confirm nothing to individuals until selection is done.

YOUR DECISION
- **Option A:** announce the proposal on Tue 15 Dec as above. This keeps the date and the momentum, but people carry the uncertainty through the holidays.
- **Option B:** brief the works councils by 15 Dec and announce to staff on Tue 5 Jan. This avoids a pre-Christmas announcement, but it is three weeks later and could leak in between.
- **Not an option I'll prepare:** a named list on the 15th built from this spreadsheet.

Recommendation: Option A if both works councils can be briefed properly a few days before the 15th; otherwise Option B.
