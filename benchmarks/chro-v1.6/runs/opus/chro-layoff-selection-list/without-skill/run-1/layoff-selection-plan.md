# Rotterdam and Munich workforce reduction: lawful selection and timeline

Working draft for HR, leadership and employment counsel, prepared 23 November 2026. This is not legal advice. Dutch and German employment counsel need to sign off before anything is sent to employees, works councils or authorities.

Assumption: "the 15th" means Tuesday 15 December 2026.

---

## 1. Why the current spreadsheet can't be used

| Criterion in HR's sheet | Rotterdam (NL) | Munich (DE) |
|---|---|---|
| Last year's performance rating | Not a selection criterion. Selection for redundancy follows the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel). Underperformance is a separate dismissal ground that needs its own file and goes to the subdistrict court (kantonrechter). | Not one of the four social-selection criteria in §1(3) KSchG. Performance can only be used to *exclude* a specific person from the pool when keeping them is in a demonstrable business interest, justified one person at a time. |
| Number of sick days | Absence data is health data (GDPR Art. 9), and the Dutch DPA has fined employers for over-processing it. Employees who are sick are protected against notice during the first two years of illness. Using it also creates disability-discrimination exposure. | Health data (GDPR Art. 9). Not a selection criterion. Creates disability-discrimination exposure under the AGG. Illness-based dismissal is a separate route with its own strict test, and you can't fold it into a redundancy. |
| Part-time status | Distinguishing on working hours is prohibited (art. 7:648 BW, the former Wet onderscheid arbeidsduur) unless objectively justified. Most part-timers are women, so this is also indirect sex discrimination. | Part-timers may not be treated worse because they work part-time (§4(1) TzBfG). Also indirect sex discrimination under the AGG. |
| Managers' keep list | Only a narrow exception applies: an employee whose knowledge or experience is indispensable, which you have to prove with documents. | Only via §1(3) sentence 2 KSchG (knowledge, skills, performance, or keeping a balanced personnel structure). The reason has to be concrete, documented per person and weighed against the social criteria. |
| One ranking across both offices | Selection happens per establishment (bedrijfsvestiging) and per category of interchangeable roles. | Selection happens per establishment (Betrieb) and per group of comparable roles at the same level. |

**Exposure if it goes ahead as is.** In Germany, evidence of a discriminatory criterion shifts the burden of proof to the employer (§22 AGG) and opens compensation claims (§15 AGG) on top of unfair-dismissal claims. In the Netherlands, UWV checks the reflection principle and will refuse permits that don't follow it. Settlement agreements built on these criteria are open to discrimination complaints at the Netherlands Institute for Human Rights (College voor de Rechten van de Mens). And with 40 people leaving, the pattern (part-timers, people who were ill) will be visible to everyone who stays.

## 2. Decide on roles first, then people

1. From the business case the board approved, set out **per location** which positions disappear and how many in each category of roles. Managers' input belongs here, in shaping the future organisation, not in naming individuals.
2. Shrink the forced number before any selection (see section 5).
3. Only then apply each country's selection rules to the affected categories.

## 3. Rotterdam (Netherlands)

### 3.1 Process

1. **Works council advice (art. 25 WOR).** Ask the OR for advice on the *proposed* decision while it can still have a real influence. Present the board approval as a proposed decision, not a final one. The request should include the reasons, the expected effects on employees and the measures planned in response, including a social plan if there is one. If the final decision departs from the OR's advice, its implementation has to be suspended for one month (art. 25(6) WOR). An OR is mandatory from 50 employees.
2. **Collective dismissal notification (WMCO).** This applies if you plan **20 or more** dismissals within **one UWV region** (Rotterdam falls under Zuid-Holland/Zeeland) within **3 months**. Settlement agreements, UWV permits and court terminations all count towards the 20. Notify UWV and the unions that have members in the company, and consult the unions if they respond. A **one-month waiting period** applies to notices, court terminations and settlement agreements. It falls away if the unions confirm in writing that they were consulted and agree.
3. **Collective agreement (CAO) and social plan.** Check whether a CAO applies and whether it sets up an independent dismissal committee with its own selection order. Negotiate the social plan with the unions where relevant.
4. **Exit routes.** Voluntary settlement agreements (vaststellingsovereenkomst) or a UWV permit for business-economic reasons. The statutory transition payment (transitievergoeding) is owed when you dismiss through UWV. It isn't strictly owed in a settlement agreement, but in practice it's the floor for what you offer.

### 3.2 Selection: the reflection principle

Per establishment, for each category of interchangeable roles where positions disappear:

- **Step 1: Categories.** Group roles that are comparable in content, required knowledge and competencies, level and pay.
- **Step 2: Order of groups.** (1) external workers (agency staff, secondees, freelancers, people loaned from another location); (2) employees at state pension (AOW) age; (3) zero-hours contracts; (4) fixed-term contracts ending within 26 weeks of UWV's decision; (5) permanent staff and longer fixed-term contracts.
- **Step 3: Within groups 2 to 5.** Use five age brackets (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55+). Spread the dismissals so the age profile stays roughly the same. Within each bracket, the most recent hire leaves first.
- **Allowed deviations.** An indispensable employee, with documentary proof. An employee with an arbeidsbeperking (a condition that limits their ability to work) may be treated differently. If the selected person is protected against dismissal (for example, sick), you may propose the next person in the same age bracket.
- **Protected employees to flag:** illness (the protection doesn't apply if the illness started after UWV received the application), pregnancy and maternity leave plus 6 weeks after return, OR members and candidates (up to 2 years after membership), employees taking parental or care leave, union activity.

### 3.3 Data HR needs per Rotterdam employee

Role and category of interchangeable roles; contract type and end date; date of birth; start date of employment; part-time hours (only to calculate positions in FTE, never as a criterion); flags for protection against dismissal (illness start date only, never diagnosis; pregnancy or leave; OR membership); arbeidsbeperking where the employee has disclosed it; documented justification for any "indispensable" exception.

## 4. Munich (Germany)

### 4.1 Process

1. **Betriebsrat (if you have one).** A pure headcount reduction of this size is very likely an operational change (Betriebsänderung, §111 BetrVG). Inform the Betriebsrat in good time and in full, *before* telling employees. Attempt an Interessenausgleich (going to the conciliation board, the Einigungsstelle, if needed) and negotiate a Sozialplan. A Sozialplan can be enforced through the Einigungsstelle where §112a BetrVG thresholds are met, for example 20% or at least 37 employees in an establishment of 60–249 people. Two tools are worth using:
   - **Selection guideline (§95 BetrVG, §1(4) KSchG).** If the weighting of the social criteria is agreed with the Betriebsrat, courts only review it for gross error.
   - **Name list in the Interessenausgleich (§1(5) KSchG).** If the affected employees are named in an agreed Interessenausgleich, the operational reasons are presumed, and selection is only reviewed for gross error. This is the lawful version of "finalising the list".
2. **Mass dismissal procedure (§17 KSchG).** It applies within 30 days if the establishment has 21–59 employees and more than 5 are dismissed; 60–499 employees and 10% or more than 25; 500+ employees and at least 30. Employer-initiated settlement agreements count as dismissals. First consult the Betriebsrat in writing (reasons, numbers and job groups, period, selection criteria, severance calculation). Only then notify the Agentur für Arbeit, and only after that give notice.
   - On 1 April 2026 the Federal Labour Court (BAG) ruled that dismissals are ineffective if the required notification is missing, *or* if it was filed before the Betriebsrat consultation was complete. On 30 October 2025 the ECJ (*Tomann*, C-134/24; *Sewel*, C-402/24) held that a missing notification can't be cured afterwards. Get the sequence and content exactly right.
3. **Hearing before each dismissal (§102 BetrVG).** The Betriebsrat has to be heard before every individual notice. A notice given without the hearing is void.
4. **Prior approvals for protected employees.** Severely disabled or equivalent employees need consent from the Integrationsamt (in Bavaria, the Inklusionsamt) under §168 SGB IX. Pregnancy and maternity (§17 MuSchG) and parental leave (§18 BEEG) need approval from the competent authority, which in Bavaria is the Gewerbeaufsichtsamt. Works council members are largely protected from ordinary dismissal (§15 KSchG). Also check people on care leave, trainees and the data protection officer.
5. **Notice.** In writing with a wet-ink signature (§623 BGB). Notice periods follow §622 BGB, the contract or the collective agreement (up to 7 months by length of service).
6. **No Betriebsrat?** There's no Interessenausgleich or Sozialplan, but social selection (§1(3) KSchG) and the mass dismissal notification still apply.

### 4.2 Selection: social selection (Sozialauswahl)

- **Pool.** Per establishment, employees in comparable, interchangeable roles at the same hierarchy level. Part-timers and full-timers are generally in the same pool. They can only be separated if there's a genuine organisational decision requiring specific working hours, and counsel should test that.
- **Criteria (only these four).** Length of service, age, statutory maintenance obligations (dependants), and severe disability. They're usually weighted in a points scheme agreed with the Betriebsrat.
- **Exclusions.** Specific people may be kept out of the pool for documented, concrete business reasons (knowledge, skills, performance) or to keep a balanced age structure (forming age groups is permitted). Each exclusion has to be justified individually and weighed against the social hardship for the others. A manager's preference is not enough.

### 4.3 Data HR needs per Munich employee

Establishment and comparable role group; hierarchy level; start date; date of birth; maintenance obligations (verify with the employee, don't rely only on payroll tax data); severe-disability or equivalent status (where disclosed); special-protection flags (Betriebsrat, pregnancy, parental or care leave, trainee, DPO); written justification for any §1(3) sentence 2 exclusion.

## 5. Reduce the number of forced exits first

- Freeze hiring and use open vacancies for redeployment between teams and sites.
- End agency and freelance work in affected roles. In the Netherlands this goes first anyway.
- Count natural attrition and planned retirements in the period.
- Offer an open voluntary departure scheme with clear, neutral terms. You can reserve the right to refuse volunteers in critical roles for business reasons, but never on sick days or part-time status. Voluntary exits still count towards the WMCO and §17 thresholds. In Germany, settlement agreements can trigger a blocking period on unemployment benefit (§159 SGB III), so have counsel structure them.
- Offer reduced hours on request, but never impose them as a hidden criterion.

## 6. Realistic timeline (assuming no consultation has started yet)

| When | Rotterdam | Munich |
|---|---|---|
| By Fri 4 Dec | Counsel engaged; roles-not-people plan per site; check CAO; draft OR advice request | Counsel engaged; check Betrieb size and thresholds; draft §111 information pack and §17(2) consultation letter |
| Week of 7 Dec | Submit OR advice request; WMCO notification to UWV and unions if ≥20 dismissals | Inform Betriebsrat (§111) and start §17(2) consultation; propose selection guideline and points scheme |
| Tue 15 Dec | All-staff announcement of the *plan* (no names), after the OR has been briefed | All-staff announcement of the *plan* (no names), after the Betriebsrat has been briefed |
| Dec–Jan | OR advice (allow 4–8 weeks with the holidays); social plan talks; voluntary scheme window; draft reflection-principle selection | Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan negotiations (often 6–12 weeks; longer if it goes to the Einigungsstelle); voluntary scheme window |
| Late Jan–Feb 2027 | Decision after advice (+1 month if it departs from the advice); individual conversations; settlement offers or UWV applications | — |
| Feb–Apr 2027 | UWV procedure, then notice | Interessenausgleich with name list → §17 notification → §102 hearings and authority approvals → written notices |

If either works council is already engaged, these dates can move forward. They can't skip steps.

## 7. What can be announced on 15 December

**Conditions:** the OR has received the advice request and the Betriebsrat has received the §111 information *before* the all-staff message. Nobody gets an individual "you're affected" message on the 15th.

**Draft all-staff message** (localise into Dutch and German, and agree the text with counsel and both councils):

> Subject: Proposed changes to our organisation in Rotterdam and Munich
>
> Today we want to tell you openly about a difficult proposal. [One or two sentences on the business reason.] To address this, we are proposing to reduce our organisation by around 40 roles across Rotterdam and Munich over the coming months.
>
> No decisions about individual people have been made. In the Netherlands, we have asked the works council for its advice and [have notified UWV and the unions]. In Germany, we have started consultations with the Betriebsrat. The process, including how roles are selected, will follow the law and the agreements we reach with them.
>
> Before any compulsory changes, we will try to limit their number through vacancies, redeployment and a voluntary departure scheme, which we will share with you by [date].
>
> We expect to be able to tell people individually whether their role is affected from [Rotterdam: late January / Munich: date after consultation]. Until then, your manager, HR [names] and the works council are available for questions. [Confidential support / EAP details.]
>
> We know this creates uncertainty, and we will keep you updated at least every [two weeks].

## 8. The existing spreadsheet

- Stop using it now, including as a "tiebreaker". Don't carry the ratings, sick-day or part-time columns into the new selection.
- Restrict access to the few people who need it and don't circulate it further.
- Tell employment counsel and your data protection officer that it exists, and let them decide how it's retained. Don't quietly delete it.
- Brief managers that the keep list is input on future roles only. Any exception has to go through the documented exception route in each country.

## 9. Open points to settle with counsel this week

1. How the 40 roles split between Rotterdam and Munich, and the usual headcount of each establishment (this sets the WMCO, §17 and §112a thresholds).
2. Whether Rotterdam has an OR, whether a CAO applies, and whether it has a dismissal committee.
3. Whether Munich has a Betriebsrat, whether there's an existing selection guideline, and whether you're bound by a collective agreement.
4. Whether either council or any union has already been told about the board decision, and how the decision was minuted.
5. Whether the offices are in separate legal entities, and whether a larger group triggers European Works Council information duties.
6. Budget for the social plan or Sozialplan and the voluntary scheme.
