**Bottom line:** Don't tell the board it's "stable", and don't make resilience training the response. Show them the real numbers with a 90-day plan attached. That version protects your CEO too.
*Confidence: high on the approach. Low on the causes until you've talked to the teams.*

**What the survey says**
- People in Claims and Customer Service are burning out, and it's getting worse fast. "Pockets" fits where it is. "Stable" doesn't fit how fast it's moving. [your survey]
- Trust in senior leadership at 41% covers the whole company, and it's about the people asking for the softer wording. That's not a pocket. [your survey]
- Claims and Customer Service are where customers deal with you. Burnout there tends to turn into long sick leave, people quitting, slower claims and more complaints. In the Netherlands you keep paying wages for up to two years when someone is off sick. [inference + law, medium]

**Why not the CEO's version**
- Making resilience training the answer tells exhausted people the problem is how they cope. When two whole departments are hit, look first at how the work is set up. With trust at 41%, staff will read the training as leadership looking away. It can come later as an optional extra, but never instead of fixing the causes.
- The board will hear the real picture anyway: from the works council (OR), from next quarter's sick-leave figures or from staff. Then the story isn't burnout any more. It's "the board was misinformed", with your name on the slide.
- The Arbowet requires you to have a policy against work pressure and to deal with it in your RI&E (risk assessment) and action plan. The OR has a consent right on that policy (WOR art. 27(1)(d)). "Stable" won't hold up next to that.

**This week**
1. Pull the backing numbers for both departments: response rate, change since the last survey, sick leave, attrition, overtime, vacancies and caseload per FTE. *(You + HR analytics, by midweek.)*
2. See the CEO one-to-one before the deck is final. Bring an alternative, not just an objection: a version that shows the board you're in control. The soft version will look like denial once sick leave rises. *(You, this week.)*
3. Open the board slide with something like: *"Burnout risk has risen sharply in Claims and Customer Service, and trust in senior leadership has fallen to 41%. This is a clear signal, not a stable picture. Here is our 90-day plan."*

**The 90-day plan to present**
1. HR runs listening sessions in Claims and CS, without the teams' own managers in the room, to find the causes. *(HR, 30 days)*
2. Quick relief where the cause is obvious: pause non-essential changes, add temporary capacity, review targets. *(Claims and CS directors, 30 days)*
3. The CEO and leadership team share the results with all staff, commit to 2–3 specific actions and report on progress. Asking and then doing nothing visible does more damage than not asking at all. *(CEO, 30 days)*
4. Involve the OR and the company doctor, and update the RI&E for work pressure. *(HR, 60 days)*
5. Re-survey both departments and track sick leave monthly, then report back to the board. *(HR, day 90)*

**Your decision**
- **A (recommended):** agree the honest framing with the CEO and present it together. It costs you one awkward conversation.
- **B:** if the CEO won't move, present the numbers accurately and let the CEO add their own interpretation. Don't call it "stable" yourself.
- If you're asked to change or drop the numbers themselves, that's no longer framing. Take it to the chair of the supervisory board.
