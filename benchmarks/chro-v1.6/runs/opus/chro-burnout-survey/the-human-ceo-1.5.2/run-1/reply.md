Don't call it "stable" or lead with resilience training. See your CEO privately first, then give the board the real numbers, owned by leadership, with a plan aimed at the causes.

**1. This week, privately with your CEO.** Try: "Burnout up sharply and trust at 41% isn't stable, and the works council will see these figures too. If the board finds out later, we lose its trust as well. Let's bring it as ours, with a plan."

**2. At the board:** the figures against last year, per department; ownership (trust in leadership is a verdict on us, not on staff); the risk (burnout shows up next in absence, claim quality and complaints); the plan, with dates. Headline: "Two serious signals, burnout in two departments and trust at 41%. We own them; here's the plan."

**3. Fix the work, not the people.** Within four weeks, sit with both teams and check caseloads, backlog, overtime and absence (team level only); then fix what drives the load. Resilience training alone tells exhausted people they're the problem; in a UK study of 46,336 workers, participants were no better off ([Fleming, 2024](https://wellbeing.hmc.ox.ac.uk/publications/employee-well-being-outcomes-from-individual-level-mental-health-interventions-cross-sectional-evidence-from-the-united-kingdom/)). The [Arbowet](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0010346/) (art. 3) also requires tackling psychosocial workload at the source. Training: optional, never the answer.

**4. Works council now:** share results and plan; new arbo measures likely need its consent (art. 27(1)(d) WOR; check with counsel).

**5. Re-measure** in three months and report back.

If he insists on "stable", put the figures on the slide and let them speak.
