**Short version: don't soften the numbers, don't make resilience training the response, and settle this with your CEO before the board meeting, not during it.**

**1. Talk to the CEO this week, one-on-one.**
Present it as protecting him. "Stable" won't survive contact with the data. A sharp rise in burnout and 41% trust are the numbers board members will remember, and they'll come out anyway through the works council, sick leave, attrition or exit interviews. If the board later finds out the summary was softened, the story stops being about burnout and becomes about leadership credibility, which is exactly what scored 41%. Give him a better line to use: *"Two clear warning signs. We've seen them, we own them, and here's our plan."* That puts a CEO in a stronger position than "stable" does.

**2. Don't lead with resilience training.**
Burnout in Claims and Customer Service is usually driven by workload, staffing, backlogs, targets and systems, not by people lacking resilience. If training is the only response, exhausted staff hear "the problem is you," and with trust at 41% they will. Dutch law also sets the order: under the Arbowet you have to tackle work stress at the source, and the Labour Inspectorate puts organising the work differently ahead of individual coping measures, though it expects both. Training can be an optional extra, but it can't be the plan.

**3. Before next week, get the hard data for both departments.**
Pull the trends in sick leave, turnover, vacancies, caseload or backlog per FTE and overtime, and compare them with the last survey. That turns "pockets of concern" into a concrete picture, and it usually shows the cause.

**4. Give the board three things:**
- **The facts, plainly.** Burnout risk is up sharply in two departments (show the numbers and the trend), and trust in senior leadership is at 41%. Say outright that the trust figure is about leadership, not about Claims or CS.
- **What you don't know yet.** Run listening sessions in both departments within 30 days, with the works council involved.
- **The plan and when they'll hear back.** Start with fast workload relief (temporary capacity, backlog, targets). Leadership shares the results with all staff and says what will change. A pulse survey in about 3 months shows whether it's working. Ask the board for a concrete decision, such as budget for extra capacity, so this isn't only bad news.

**5. Involve the works council (OR) now.** Decisions that affect working conditions, including the RI&E and its action plan, generally need OR consent under art. 27 WOR. Check the details with your employment lawyer. Treating the OR as a partner from the start also helps rebuild trust.

**6. If the CEO still insists on "stable":** let him choose the tone, but keep the real numbers on the slide. Don't present a summary you know is misleading. If it comes to that, raising it with the board chair is legitimate, and it's part of your job. Step 1 usually makes this unnecessary.
