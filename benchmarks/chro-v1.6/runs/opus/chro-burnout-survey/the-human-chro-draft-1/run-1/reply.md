Don't tell the board "stable". Show the real numbers with a plan to fix the work in Claims and Customer Service, and agree that with your CEO privately first.

Why: burnout comes from chronic work stress that isn't managed ([WHO, 2019](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-05-2019-burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon-international-classification-of-diseases)). In a study of 46,336 UK workers, those who took resilience training or similar courses were no better off than those who didn't ([Fleming, 2024](https://wellbeing.hmc.ox.ac.uk/news/more-ambition-needed-to-improve-workplace-wellbeing/)). Training alone tells those teams the problem is them, and everyone who answered will hear how it was framed.

**This week**
1. **Talk to your CEO alone.** A line you can use: "If we call this stable and absence in Claims climbs next quarter, the board will ask what we knew in November. Customers feel it there first. The number with our plan next to it protects us better than softer wording."
2. **Find the cause.** Absence, turnover, caseload per FTE and vacancies in both departments, and any recent system or target change.
3. **Bring in the works council.** Dutch law requires a policy on work pressure, recorded in your RI&E (article 3(2) Arbowet), and the council has a consent right on working-conditions policy (article 27(1)(d) WOR). Check with counsel; this isn't legal advice.

**At the board**
- The bad news in plain words, with scores and trend.
- Two or three actions with owners and dates: workload and staffing first, resilience support as well, not instead.
- The 41% is about leadership, so the CEO says what leaders heard and will change.
- Teams pick their own fixes; report back before the next survey.

If he still wants "stable", don't sign off on that wording; ask for the full results to go in the board pack.
