**The numbers.** If closing the gap costs C a year, two rounds cost 4.5C over five years and the five-year route 3C. The 1.5C difference is pay women go without, and claims recover it: the directive allows full back pay, uncapped ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/european-union/employment-and-hr/eu-pay-transparency-directive)). In Belgium a claim can already win six months' gross pay as a lump sum ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-on-discrimination-in-the-workplace/belgium)). Roughly, C is at most 1.5% of payroll.

**Why silence won't hold**
- **Belgium:** the works council should already get a pay-structure analysis every two years, and decides with you whether an action plan is needed ([FPS Employment](https://werk.belgie.be/nl/themas/gelijkheid-en-non-discriminatie/gelijkheid-vrouwen-mannen-de-loonkloof?id=8486)).
- **Netherlands:** the minister says the new law can't start on 1 January 2027 as planned; no new date is set ([AWVN](https://www.awvn.nl/equal-pay/nieuws/wellicht-toch-uitstel-wet-loontransparantie/)). Once it's law, staff can ask for average pay by sex for their work. An unexplained gap of 5% or more in any category, unfixed six months after a report, triggers a joint assessment with representatives ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/european-union/employment-and-hr/eu-pay-transparency-directive)).
