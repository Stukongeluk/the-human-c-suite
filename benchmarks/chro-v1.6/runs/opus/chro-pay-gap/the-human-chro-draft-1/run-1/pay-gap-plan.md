# Closing our gender pay gap: plan and note for the CFO

Draft for discussion, 23 November 2026. For the CFO and CEO first; a version for the works councils follows once the numbers are checked. This is not legal advice: employment counsel in the Netherlands and in Belgium should check it before anyone acts on it.

## Recommendation

Close the unexplained pay gap between men and women in the same roles and grades in two pay rounds (2027 and 2028), from a separate budget line. Tell both works councils and all staff what we found and what we are doing about it. Treat the 14% overall gap, which mostly reflects where women sit in our grade structure, as its own three-to-five-year plan with yearly targets.

## 1. Note for the CFO

### What we found

- **Overall gap: 14%.** All women compared with all men, before taking role or grade into account.
- **Same role and grade: about 6%.** Men and women doing the same work, paid differently.
- This is our first analysis, so it needs checking before any figure leaves the leadership team (section 3).

### Where you are right

- We should not share a number we haven't checked.
- The budget has to be predictable. The proposal is two fixed amounts, known now, not an open-ended commitment.
- The 14% won't close quickly. Changing who gets hired and promoted into senior grades takes years, and five years is a realistic horizon for that part.

### Why the five-year, say-nothing route doesn't work for the 6%

1. **It's unlawful now.** Equal pay for equal work, or work of equal value, is already law in both countries (Article 157 TFEU, and Dutch and Belgian equal treatment law). The new reporting dates are slipping; the obligation to pay equally is not.
2. **It doesn't save money; it moves it into a liability.** Every route ends at the same yearly cost. The only "saving" from going slowly is pay withheld from women doing the same work, which is what a claim recovers (section 2).
3. **Silence isn't available.**
   - In Belgium, employers with 50 or more workers must give the works council (or, failing one, the union delegation) a detailed pay-structure analysis every two years, within three months of the financial year closing, and decide with it whether an action plan is needed ([FPS Employment](https://werk.belgie.be/nl/themas/gelijkheid-en-non-discriminatie/gelijkheid-vrouwen-mannen-de-loonkloof?id=8486)).
   - In the Netherlands, the works council already has a consent right on the pay system (article 27(1)(c) WOR) ([Rendement](https://www.rendement.nl/instemmingsrecht-artikel-27-wor/nieuws/instemmingsrecht-or-beoordeling-en-beloning.html)). Under the bill now in parliament, its consent right also covers the action plan that follows a pay evaluation ([CMS, April 2026](https://cms.law/en/nld/publication/implementation-of-the-pay-transparency-directive-an-update)).
   - Once the directive is in national law, every employee can ask in writing for their own pay level and the average pay, by sex, of people doing the same work or work of equal value, and must get an answer within two months ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/european-union/employment-and-hr/eu-pay-transparency-directive)).
4. **Knowing makes it harder to defend.** We now have the analysis. Under the directive, once a worker shows facts that suggest discrimination, the employer has to prove there was none; compensation includes full back pay with no upper limit; and limitation periods are at least three years and don't start until the worker knows ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/european-union/employment-and-hr/eu-pay-transparency-directive)). In Belgium, a discrimination claim can already recover six months' gross pay as a lump sum, or the actual loss ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-on-discrimination-in-the-workplace/belgium)).
5. **The 5% test is per category, not company-wide.** Where a report shows a gap of at least 5% in any category of workers that objective, gender-neutral factors don't explain, and it isn't fixed within six months, the employer must do a joint pay assessment with workers' representatives ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/european-union/employment-and-hr/eu-pay-transparency-directive)). Our 6% is an average, so some categories will be above it, and a slow plan would still leave several over 5% when the first report is due.
6. **The people involved will find out.** Hidden gaps come out through information requests, reports and claims, and the story then becomes the hiding. The women affected are engineers and consultants in a tight market.

### What I'm asking for

- Agreement in principle to close the same-role gap in two pay rounds.
- A separate budget line, sized once the analysis is checked. Indicative figures are in section 2; replace them with ours.
- A joint session with the CEO before [date], so we bring one plan rather than two positions.

## 2. The cost, in numbers

**Formula.** Annual cost of closing the gap (C) = the sum, over each person below the fair point for their role and grade, of (fair point − current pay) × (1 + employer on-costs). The fair point should come from the checked analysis, not from the 6% average.

**Illustration only.** Our real figures replace these assumptions:

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---|
| Women in roles with a same-work comparison | 160 (about a quarter of 650) |
| Their average annual salary | €60,000 |
| Unexplained gap to close | 6%, applied to all of them (an upper bound: in practice only people below the fair point get an adjustment) |
| Employer on-costs (social security, pension, Belgian holiday pay and similar) | 30% |

C ≈ 160 × €60,000 × 6% × 1.3 ≈ **€750,000 a year**. With the same average salary for everyone, payroll is about €50.7 million, so C is at most about **1.5% of payroll**.

**Three routes over five years (2027 to 2031), at C = €750,000:**

| Route | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 | 2031 | Paid over five years | Underpayment women still carry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two pay rounds (recommended) | €375k | €750k | €750k | €750k | €750k | €3.38m | €0.38m |
| Three pay rounds (if the budget truly can't carry two) | €250k | €500k | €750k | €750k | €750k | €3.00m | €0.75m |
| Five years, quietly | €150k | €300k | €450k | €600k | €750k | €2.25m | €1.50m |

**How to read it.** Each row adds up to the same €3.75 million: what we pay plus what women go without. From 2031 every route costs the same, €750,000 a year. The five-year route "saves" €1.1 million against two rounds only because women doing the same work go on being paid less, and that is exactly what back-pay claims recover.

**Not in the table:** back pay for the years before 2027 (the same on every route, and a question for counsel), damages and legal costs, a joint pay assessment, management time, and the cost of the people who leave when they find out.

## 3. Checking the analysis first (by mid-January 2027)

- **Per employer.** Analyse the Dutch and Belgian entities separately. Reporting is generally per legal entity, and pooling mixes two pay markets, which can create or hide a gap.
- **Hourly pay,** so that part-time work doesn't distort the comparison.
- **All pay,** not just base salary: bonus, overtime, lease car or mobility budget, pension contribution, and in Belgium group insurance, meal and eco vouchers, and the 13th month.
- **Categories of workers** built on objective criteria (skills, effort, responsibility, working conditions), so that both "equal work" and "work of equal value" are covered. This is also the job evaluation system the Dutch bill will require of every employer ([CMS, April 2026](https://cms.law/en/nld/publication/implementation-of-the-pay-transparency-directive-an-update)).
- **Explained and unexplained.** A factor only explains part of the gap if it is objective, gender-neutral and applied the same way to everyone. Performance ratings count only if the rating process has itself been checked for differences between men and women.
- **Group sizes.** Say how many people sit in each cell, and show groups of fewer than five only in aggregate.
- **The individual list.** Each person below the fair point for their role, grade and relevant experience, with the gap in euros. This list drives the budget and stays with HR and the reward team.
- **An independent check** by an external pay-equity specialist or counsel. Access to individual data stays restricted, and everything shared outside HR is aggregated.

## 4. Pay-gap plan

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| Scope | Dutch entity: about 420 employees; Dutch rules once the transposition law is in force (first report for 150 or more employees was planned for 7 June 2028, on 2027, but the start date is now slipping). Belgian entity: about 230 employees; the 22 April 2012 pay gap act applies now, and the directive isn't yet in Belgian law. [Confirm the legal entity structure.] |
| Job architecture | Roles grouped into categories by skills, effort, responsibility and working conditions, the same method in both countries. [Owner, date.] |
| Gaps | Mean and median, overall and per category, per entity; the share objective factors explain and the share they don't; group sizes, with groups under five shown only in aggregate. [From the checked analysis.] |
| Remedy | Everyone below the fair point for their role and grade gets an adjustment: at least half in the 2027 pay round (no later than [1 July 2027]), the rest in the 2028 round. Largest gaps first. The underpaid are raised; nobody else's increase is held back to fund it. Budget: [€ in 2027, € from 2028], agreed with finance as a separate line. Whether to add a back-pay element: decided with counsel. |
| Causes fixed | Published pay ranges per role and grade; starting pay from the range, with no questions about pay history; written criteria for promotion and bonus decisions, checked by gender each round; no penalty for part-time work or parental and care leave in progression or bonus. |
| The 14% | Targets for the share of women per grade, promotion rates and hiring into senior roles; criteria for promotion published; progression for part-timers and people returning from leave reviewed. Yearly targets, three to five years. Targets for the pipeline, not quotas for individual decisions. |
| Representatives | Netherlands: works council consulted at the start with the real numbers; consent for any change to the pay system (article 27(1)(c) WOR), and for the action plan under the new law once it applies. Belgium: findings and plan discussed with the works council as part of the pay-structure analysis, with the action plan agreed there; union delegation involved. Joint pay assessment if a gap of 5% or more in a category remains unexplained and unfixed six months after a report. |
| Communication | Works councils first, then managers, then each affected person from their own manager, then all staff the same week. Everyone is told how to ask for their own pay level and the average for their work, by sex. |
| Review | Re-run the analysis every spring and report it to the works councils and the leadership team. Done means: no unexplained gap of 5% or more in any category, and the average unexplained gap close to zero. Owner: [CHRO], with [reward lead] and finance. |

## 6. Communication

**Order:** CFO, CEO, works councils, managers, the people whose pay changes, then everyone, in the same week. Nobody learns about their own adjustment from an all-staff email.

**To all staff (once the figures are checked):**
"This year we analysed pay by gender for the first time. Overall, women here earn on average [14]% less than men, mainly because fewer women work in our senior grades. Among people in the same role and grade, we found a gap of about [6]% that factors such as experience don't explain. That isn't acceptable, and we're correcting it in two steps, in [month 2027] and [month 2028], in agreement with the works councils. We're also changing how we set starting salaries and decide on promotions and bonuses, and we'll publish the pay ranges. You can ask HR at any time for your own pay level and the average pay, by gender, for your role. We'll repeat the analysis every year and share the results."

**From a manager to a person whose pay is adjusted:**
"We've reviewed pay for everyone in your role and grade. Your salary is lower than it should be compared with colleagues doing the same work, and it has nothing to do with your performance. We're correcting it: from [date] your salary rises by €[x] a year, and from [date] by a further €[y]. HR can walk you through how we calculated it, and you can ask questions or raise concerns at any time."

Have counsel review both texts before they go out. Don't ask anyone to sign a waiver in return for an adjustment.

## 7. Questions for employment counsel

**Netherlands**
- Status and start date of the transposition bill (Wet implementatie Richtlijn loontransparantie, bill 36949); which obligations apply from day one.
- Which parts of the plan need works council consent now, and which under the new law.
- Back-pay exposure for past years, and whether to include a back-pay element.
- Any collective agreement that sets pay scales for part of our staff.

**Belgium**
- Whether the two-yearly pay-structure analysis has been done and given to the works council; if not, how to put that right.
- Our joint committee and whether its job classification is gender-neutral.
- Exposure under the Gender Act of 10 May 2007 and wage-claim limitation periods.
- Status of the transposition.

**Both**
- Wording of the messages to staff and to affected individuals.
- Data protection for the analysis and the individual list.

## 8. Assumptions and what would change this

- **Assumptions:** the 6% compares people in the same roles and grades and is mostly not explained by objective factors; there is a works council in both entities; the pay budget can carry roughly 1% to 1.5% of payroll a year for two years; no collective agreement fixes these salaries.
- **What would change the plan:** if the check shows most of the 6% is explained by objective, gender-neutral factors applied consistently, the remedy and the budget shrink. If the company is in real financial difficulty, three pay rounds with fixed dates instead of two, still not five years and still not silent.
- **What would not change it:** a later reporting date. The duty to pay equally applies now.
