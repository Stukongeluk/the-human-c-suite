# Decision: closing our gender pay gap

**Decision needed by:** before the 2027 budget is final (proposed: 12 December 2026) · **Decision owner:** CEO, with the CFO and HR director · **Prepared:** 23 November 2026 · **Status:** draft for discussion

**Recommendation in brief:** correct the unexplained gap within the same roles and grades effective 1 January 2027. The central estimate is €0.81M a year, about 1.4% of payroll. Tell the works councils first, then all staff, and give the 14% overall gap a three-to-five-year plan with yearly targets.

## The problem in one sentence

Our first pay analysis shows a 14% gender pay gap overall and about 6% between women and men in the same roles and grades. Whatever part of the 6% we can't explain with objective, gender-neutral factors is pay we owe under existing equal-pay law. Employees and regulators will be able to see it under the new pay transparency rules, and every year we leave it open adds to what we owe.

## This is ours

The gap comes from pay decisions that we, the leadership team, made or approved over many years: starting salaries, raises, bonuses and promotions. Nothing the women affected did caused it. Fixing it is our responsibility, and we'll say so when we communicate.

## What it costs

Estimate = women affected × their average pay × unexplained gap × 1.3 employer costs. Total employer payroll is assumed at €57.5M (650 people × €68k × 1.3).

| Scenario | Women | Average pay | Unexplained gap | Cost per year (employer) | Share of payroll |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 130 | €55k | 4% | €0.39M | 0.7% |
| Central | 162 | €60k | 6% | €0.81M | 1.4% |
| High | 195 | €65k | 6% | €1.05M | 1.8% |

In the central case the average correction is about €3,800 gross a year per woman, or about €5,000 including employer costs. The cost is permanent whichever option we choose. The options only differ in when we start paying it.

## Options

| Option | 2027 cost | Total 2027–2031 | Withheld from affected women | People affected, and how | Hidden costs and risks | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Say nothing; close in five equal steps, 2027–2031 | €0.16M | €2.43M | €1.62M | Women stay underpaid for up to five years | Back-pay claims with the burden of proof on us. Some categories likely still at 5% or more when the first report is due, which triggers a joint assessment. Loss of works council trust. Departures once people use their right to information | The delay can't be undone, and the cost lands anyway |
| B. Correct in one step, effective 1 January 2027 | €0.81M | €4.05M | €0 | Corrections for women in affected roles; nobody's pay goes down | Pressure on the 2027 budget; questions from colleagues who don't get a correction | Pay rises are permanent, as they should be |
| C. Two steps in 2027 (half on 1 January, the rest on 1 July) | €0.61M | €3.84M | €0.20M | As B, with half the correction six months later | As B, with less pressure on 2027 | As B |
| D. Do nothing | €0 | €0 | €4.05M | The gap stays | Exposure grows by about €0.8M a year; the same risks as A, but larger | — |

Option A looks €1.62M cheaper than B over five years, but that saving is the pay we'd withhold from the women affected. It ends at the same €0.81M a year.

**From each seat.** *Finance:* B or C is a defined line of about 1.4% of payroll. *Clients:* no effect on delivery; the only conversation with clients is the 2027 rate indexation. *Delivery:* keeping experienced women engineers protects billable capacity, and every departure costs recruitment fees, a vacancy and ramp-up time. *People:* trust that pay here is fair, and a works council relationship that can carry later decisions.

## Recommendation

Option B, with C as the fallback if 2027 cash is really constrained. Validate the analysis by 31 January 2027. Correct the unexplained like-for-like gap effective 1 January 2027, paid with the March payroll. Run a separate three-to-five-year plan for the 14% overall gap. Tell the works councils first, then all staff.

## How we fund it

1. **The leadership bonus pool first.** Leaders contribute before anyone else does.
2. **2027 rate indexation.** If staff costs are 60–70% of revenue, €0.81M is roughly 1% of revenue. Finance to check this against actual revenue and the contracts that allow indexation.
3. **A separate pay-equity line**, not taken from the normal raise budget, so nobody's raise shrinks to fund it. If that proves impossible, we take part of it from the raise budget and say so openly.
4. **The timing in option C**, if needed.

## The CFO's concerns, and how this plan answers them

| Concern | Answer |
|---|---|
| Budget certainty | One defined line in the 2027 budget (central €0.81M, range €0.39–1.05M), with the final amount known by the end of January |
| Telling people invites claims | The exposure exists whether we speak or not. Fixing it quickly reduces it, and counsel reviews the wording before anything goes out |
| Everyone will expect a raise | Corrections apply only where a gap is unexplained, on criteria shared with the works councils. The normal raise round is unchanged |
| Spreading it over five years softens the blow | It doesn't reduce the cost: we reach the same €0.81M a year in 2031 and withhold €1.62M along the way |
| The new law isn't in force yet | True, and the Directive doesn't bind us directly until it is. But equal pay for equal work already applies, and Belgium already requires the two-yearly analysis to go to the works council |

## The 14% overall gap

We expect this gap to come mostly from representation, with fewer women in the senior, better-paid grades; the data has to confirm that. We build the plan with the works councils:

- Analyse promotion rates, hiring and attrition by gender and grade, including time to promotion after parental leave and for part-timers.
- Set yearly targets for representation per grade, reviewed before each promotion round.
- Publish pay ranges per grade internally, set rules for starting salaries, stop asking about salary history and put ranges in vacancies.
- Check raise and bonus proposals for gender gaps before they're final.
- Review progress every year in the same report to the works councils.

## Who hears what

- **Works councils:** first, before staff, with the real numbers and the method.
- **Managers:** briefed before staff, with a script and answers to likely questions.
- **Affected employees:** privately, from their own manager, with their new pay and how it was set.
- **All staff:** the same week. We tell them what we found (aggregates only, with no group small enough to identify anyone), what we're fixing, by when, and how to ask about their own pay.
- **Externally:** no announcement needed now; statutory reporting will follow.

## Human impact

- About 160 women get a correction: an estimate, and fewer if part of the gap turns out to be explained. In the central case that averages about €3,800 gross a year.
- Nobody's pay goes down.
- Colleagues who don't get a correction hear the criteria, and their normal raise round is unchanged.
- Individual corrections stay private and reporting is aggregated only. Groups below a minimum size aren't shown.
- What we watch: attrition of women, engagement by gender, and the number of pay questions and how fast we answer them.

## Measures

We review these quarterly in 2027, then before every pay round.

- Unexplained gap per category and entity: no category at 5% or more, with an aim of below 2%.
- Overall gap per entity, year on year.
- Promotion and attrition rates by gender and grade.
- Share of new hires paid within the published range.
- Pay-information requests answered within two months.

## Assumptions, and what would change my mind

- **Assumption:** women are about 25% of staff at about €60k average pay, with 30% employer costs. **Would change my mind:** the payroll data in January. The decision holds anywhere in the €0.39–1.05M range.
- **Assumption:** most of the 6% is unexplained. **Would change my mind:** a validation showing that objective, gender-neutral factors explain much of it (for example, Dutch and Belgian pay levels mixed in one analysis). The correction would then shrink, though we'd still check that those factors are neutral themselves.
- **Assumption:** 2027 cash allows €0.81M. **Would change my mind:** if it doesn't, we choose option C, not option A.
- **Assumption:** Dutch rules start on 1 January 2027. **Would change my mind:** nothing in the recommendation. A delay to mid-2027 or 2028 moves the reporting date, not the equal-pay obligation that already applies.
