Don't stay silent, and don't take five years over the like-for-like part. Split the two gaps: correct the 6% gap within the same roles and grades in 2027, which on my rough estimate costs about €0.8M a year (around 1.4% of payroll). Then put the 14% overall gap on an honest multi-year plan that you share with both works councils and your staff.

**Why the CFO's plan doesn't hold**

- **The 6% isn't a budget item; it's pay you owe.** Equal pay for equal work has been EU law since 1957 and already applies in both countries. Whatever part of the 6% that objective, gender-neutral factors can't explain is a liability today, not a future cost.
- **Silence won't last.** Belgium's 2012 pay gap law already requires firms with 50+ staff to give the works council a pay-structure analysis every two years ([FOD WASO](https://werk.belgie.be/nl/themas/gelijkheid-en-non-discriminatie/gelijkheid-vrouwen-mannen-de-loonkloof?id=8486)). Under the Dutch bill as submitted, employers with 150+ staff file their first report by 7 June 2028, covering 2027 ([Freshfields, June 2026](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/dutch-pay-transparency-legislative-proposal-submitted-to-parliament-102mxc2)). So next year's pay is probably the first that gets reported. Once the rules are in force, any employee can ask for average pay by sex in their category and must get an answer within two months ([Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2025/employment/eu-pay-transparency-directive-countries-implemented-prepare/)).
- **6% is over the line.** Under the Directive, an unjustified gap of 5% or more in a category that isn't fixed within six months of reporting triggers a joint pay assessment with employee representatives ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2023/09/27/pay-transparency-directive-what-is-a-joint-pay-assessment)). Claimants can recover back pay in full, and the burden of proof shifts to the employer ([Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/pay-transparency/)). A gap you documented and chose not to fix is the weakest position you can be in.
- **Five years isn't cheaper.** You end up at the same ~€0.8M a year. In between, you withhold about €1.6M (two years' worth of the gap) from the women affected, which is roughly what they could later claim.

**What to do**

1. **Validate by end of January, and keep to that deadline.** Recalculate per legal entity, on hourly pay, with every pay element (bonus, lease car, allowances), comparing roles of equal value, not just matching titles. Check that the explanations themselves, such as starting salaries and ratings, are neutral.
2. **Correct the unexplained part effective 1 January 2027**, paid once each case is checked. Nobody's pay goes down. Each person hears about it privately from their own manager.
3. **Works councils first, then everyone.** Bring in the OR and the Belgian ondernemingsraad now, with the method and the numbers. Then tell all staff what you found, what you're fixing and by when, and how to ask about their own pay. Share aggregates only, with no group small enough to identify anyone. You don't need a press release, but you do need to tell your own people.
4. **Fix what created the gap:** pay ranges per grade, rules for starting salaries, no questions about salary history, a gender check before every raise and bonus round, and protected progression during leave and part-time work. Build this with the OR, which has a consent right on pay systems (art. 27(1)(c) WOR, [AWVN](https://www.awvn.nl/medezeggenschap/hr-van-a-tot-z/bevoegdheden-ondernemingsraad-bij-beloningsbeleid/)).
5. **Give the 14% its multi-year plan.** It's mostly about who reaches the senior, better-paid grades. Look at promotion, hiring and attrition by gender and set yearly targets. This is where the CFO's five-year horizon is right.
6. **Own it as leadership.** Say plainly that the gap comes from pay decisions leadership made or signed off, not from anything the women did.

**What to tell the CFO**

Start by agreeing with what's right: budget certainty matters, and the 14% will take years. Then:

> "Let's give the structural gap a five-year plan. The like-for-like gap is different: that's pay we owe people for the same work. The law already requires it, Belgium already makes us share the analysis with the works council, and soon any employee can ask for the numbers. Spreading it out doesn't make it cheaper. It keeps €1.6M from our colleagues and turns it into a claim we've documented ourselves. So let's find about €0.8M for 2027 together."

Where the money can come from:
- The leadership bonus pool first, so leaders pay before anyone else.
- 2027 rate indexation: roughly one extra percent on rates on my estimates (check against your revenue).
- If cash is truly tight, two steps (January and July), which bring 2027 down to about €0.6M.

If you can, keep this separate from the normal raise budget so nobody's raise shrinks to pay for it.

This isn't legal advice. Get employment counsel in both countries before you communicate anything.
