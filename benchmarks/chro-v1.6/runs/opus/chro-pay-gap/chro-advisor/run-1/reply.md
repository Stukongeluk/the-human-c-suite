# CHRO: your gender pay gap, and what to tell the CFO

## Bottom line

Treat the two numbers as two separate problems. The 14% overall gap can have the five-year plan the CFO wants. The 6% gap between men and women in the same role and grade can't wait five years. Check how much of it is really unexplained, close that part within about 12 months, and tell your own people once the fix is in place. Paying women less for the same work is already unlawful in both countries. Staying silent won't last, and the money a five-year phase-in seems to save is mostly money you would owe back anyway.

Confidence: high on the direction. Medium on the euro figures, which use assumed inputs until finance reruns them on real payroll.

## What

- **The 14% and the 6% are different problems.** The 14% mostly reflects who sits where: fewer women in senior, better-paid roles. That isn't unlawful in itself, and it takes years to change, so a five-year plan suits it. The 6% means women earn less than men in the same role and grade. Unless objective, gender-neutral factors explain it, it is unequal pay, and that is already prohibited: Art. 157 TFEU at EU level; in the Netherlands art. 7:646 BW and the Equal Treatment of Men and Women Act (WGB); in Belgium the Gender Act of 2007. [verified: statute]
- **The people behind the 6%.** As an illustration, assume 25% women and an average pay for women of EUR 60k. That gives about 160 women who each earn roughly EUR 3,800 a year less than men in the same jobs. Under the CFO's plan, each of them would give up roughly another EUR 7,700, plus the pension that pay would have built, before reaching parity. [assumed: replace with your data]
- **"Say nothing" won't hold, in either country.**
  - **Belgium.** The 2012 Pay Gap Act already requires a gender analysis of your pay structure every two years. That analysis is presented to the works council, which then decides with you whether an action plan is needed. The social balance sheet in your annual accounts already splits staff data by gender.
  - **Netherlands.** The pay transparency bill (36 949) is still in the Tweede Kamer. The target start is 1 January 2027, but 1 July 2027 or 1 January 2028 now look more likely. Once it applies, any employee can ask for average pay by gender for their category of work, and you must answer within two months. With 250+ staff, your Dutch entity will report every year, first by 7 June 2028 on 2027 pay.
  - [verified: Dutch government note to parliament of 2 Sep 2026; FOD Werk. medium: Belgium's private-sector timing, where there is still no bill]
- **A five-year glide path is exactly what the EU directive is built to stop.** Take any category of workers doing the same work or work of equal value. If the gap there is 5% or more, can't be justified on gender-neutral grounds and isn't fixed within six months of the report, you must run a joint pay assessment with workers' representatives. The Dutch bill also gives the works council (OR) consent rights over how unjustified gaps are fixed. On the CFO's timeline you would be about 40% of the way when the first Dutch report is due in mid-2028. The OR, not finance, would then set the pace. [verified: directive Art. 10; CMS on the Dutch bill]
- **Fixing the gap costs less than delaying it.** Closing a 3-6% like-for-like gap costs an assumed EUR 0.4-0.8M a year fully loaded, about 0.7-1.4% of payroll.
  - Compared with fixing it at once, a linear five-year phase-in only defers about two years of that cost (EUR 0.8-1.6M in total).
  - The deferred money is pay women can claim back. In the Netherlands, pay claims go back five years. In Belgium, a claimant can ask for a flat six months' pay instead of proving her loss: about EUR 30k, or roughly eight years of one woman's gap.
  - The directive bans caps on compensation and shifts the burden of proof to the employer.

## Why this matters

Now that you have run the analysis, the company knows. From here on, every month the gap stays open is a choice you knowingly made and wrote down. If it ever reaches the Dutch human rights board (College voor de Rechten van de Mens), a Belgian labour court or your own works councils, a minute saying "we found it and chose five years" is the worst document you could have. Women engineers are also among the hardest people you hire. The ones you lose when they find out will cost you billable capacity, not just recruiting fees.

## How to act

1. **Hold the decision.** Don't adopt the five-year plan for the like-for-like gap. Take the split proposal below to the CEO and CFO. Owner: you. By Friday 11 December 2026.
2. **Stress-test the 6%** with employment counsel in both countries.
   - Analyse each legal employer separately, because NL and BE pay markets differ.
   - Use hourly or FTE pay, and include bonus, allowances and lease car.
   - Control for gender-neutral factors such as tenure, experience and performance.
   - Check that those factors aren't biased themselves, for example by part-time work, parental leave or salary history at hire.
   - Output: the unjustified gap for each person.
   - Owner: head of reward and counsel. By Friday 29 January 2027.
3. **Close the unjustified part within two pay cycles.**
   - From 1 April 2027: close the largest gaps in full and bring everyone else at least halfway. Everything else is closed from 1 January 2028.
   - Fund it from a separate pay-equity pool, not from the merit budget.
   - Raise women's pay. Don't cut or freeze men's pay to close the gap.
   - Ask counsel whether to make one-off back payments.
   - Owner: CFO (budget) and you. Pool approved by Friday 26 February 2027.
4. **Involve the works councils early.**
   - Belgium: put the findings into the two-yearly pay analysis and agree an action plan with the works council.
   - Netherlands: share the findings with the OR. Get its consent wherever you change the pay or job-evaluation system.
   - Owner: you and the country HR leads. During Q1 2027.
5. **Tell people, then fix the causes.**
   - In the week before 1 April 2027, talk one-to-one with everyone whose pay changes.
   - Then send an all-staff note covering both numbers, what you have fixed and what comes next.
   - In parallel, stop new gaps forming: salary ranges in job ads, no questions about previous pay, and a gender check in every pay-rise and promotion round.
   - Set a five-year plan for the 14%, with yearly targets for women's share of senior grades and their promotion rates.
   - Owner: you and the management board. Structural plan approved by 30 June 2027.

## What to tell the CFO

Agree with the CFO before you disagree. Something like:

> "You're right on three things. We don't go public now, we protect the budget, and part of this does take five years. The 14% is about who's in senior roles, and that one gets a five-year plan. The 6% is different. It's women paid less for the same job, and it's unlawful today, not in 2028. We can't keep it quiet either. In Belgium the works council has to see this analysis every two years. Once the Dutch law starts, any employee can ask for these figures and we must answer within two months. On money, the five-year glide only defers about two years of the fix, and that's back pay we'd owe with interest. A single Belgian claim at six months' pay costs more than that woman's gap would cost us in seven or eight years. My proposal: close the unjustified part in two pay cycles, April 2027 and January 2028, from a separate pool of about 1% of payroll. Compared with your plan, that's roughly EUR 100-220k a year extra, averaged over five years, and it buys off a liability at least that size. Can finance work out the real number with me on actual payroll?"

## Risks

- **The 6% could shrink or grow once you test it.** Some of it may be justified, which makes the fix cheaper. But the directive compares work of *equal value*, not only the same role. Comparing different roles of equal value could reveal gaps that your role-and-grade view misses. Budget for the range, not the midpoint.
- **The Belgian entity may already be out of step with the 2012 Act.** If this is truly your first pay analysis, it probably hasn't produced the two-yearly report. Put that right in the next cycle, with counsel.
- **The gap will come back if you only fix pay.** If starting offers, negotiation and pay-rise calibration stay as they are, the gap reopens within two cycles.

## Your decision

- **Option A, the CFO's plan.** Internal and silent, closed over five years. Lowest cash outlay in 2027-2030. But the back-pay liability keeps growing, it fails the directive's six-month rule, and the works council gets to set the pace from 2028.
- **Option B, the split plan.** The like-for-like gap is fixed within about 12 months and the overall gap over five years. Staff are told in April 2027, and external reporting happens when the law requires it. Roughly EUR 100-220k a year more than A, on average.
- **Option C, everything at once.** Fix the like-for-like gap fully on 1 April 2027, pay back pay and publish voluntarily. Highest one-off cost, but the strongest employer brand and the lowest legal risk.
- **Recommendation: B.** Decide on the back-pay part of C once counsel has priced your past exposure.
