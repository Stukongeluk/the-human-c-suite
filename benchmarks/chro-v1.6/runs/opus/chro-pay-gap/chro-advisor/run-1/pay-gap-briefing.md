# Gender pay gap: briefing for the CHRO

650 staff: 420 in the Netherlands, 230 in Belgium. Prepared 23 November 2026.

All euro figures are illustrative. The inputs are assumptions, not your data, and finance should rerun them on actual payroll. The legal points reflect the position as of November 2026. Employment counsel in each country should confirm them before you act, because both countries' pay transparency rules are still in the legislative process.

Assumption: the Dutch and Belgian staff are employed by separate legal entities, one in each country. Pay reporting under the directive runs per employer.

---

## 1. Two gaps, two problems

| | 14% overall gap | 6% gap in the same role and grade |
|---|---|---|
| What it measures | Average pay of all women against all men | Women against men doing the same job at the same level |
| Usual cause | Representation: fewer women in senior, better-paid roles | Pay decisions: starting offers, negotiation, raises, bonuses |
| Lawful? | Not unlawful in itself, but must be reported once the directive applies | Unlawful unless objective, gender-neutral factors explain it |
| Right timeline | 3-5 years, with yearly targets | Close the unjustified part within about 12 months |
| Fix | Hiring slates, promotion calibration, progression for part-timers and people returning from leave, senior pipeline | Individual pay adjustments, plus changes to how pay is set |

## 2. Legal position by country

### Applies today in both countries

- **Equal pay for equal work, or work of equal value.**
  - EU: Art. 157 TFEU.
  - Netherlands: art. 7:646 BW, arts. 7-12 of the Equal Treatment of Men and Women Act (WGB), and the General Equal Treatment Act (AWGB).
  - Belgium: the Gender Act of 10 May 2007.
- **Netherlands.** A pay claim can go back five years. Once an employee shows facts that suggest sex-based unequal treatment, the employer must prove it did not discriminate. An employee can also complain free of charge to the College voor de Rechten van de Mens.
- **Belgium.** A victim of discrimination in employment can claim either actual damages or a flat sum of six months' pay, which needs no proof of loss. Since 2023, courts can add a further lump sum for each additional ground in cases of multiple discrimination. An employer may not take prejudicial measures against an employee who has complained.
- **Belgium, 2012 Pay Gap Act.** This applies to employers with on average 50 or more workers.
  - Every two years, the employer must make a detailed analysis of its pay structure and present it to the works council within three months of the financial year end. If there is no works council, it goes to the union delegation.
  - The works council and the employer then decide whether an action plan is appropriate.
  - The works council can propose appointing a mediator.
  - The social balance sheet splits staff data by gender.
  - Failures carry sanctions under the Social Criminal Code (level 2 or 3).

### Coming: the EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970)

| | Netherlands | Belgium |
|---|---|---|
| Status, Nov 2026 | Bill 36 949 is in the Tweede Kamer. Plenary debate is planned for week 50 of 2026 and week 2 of 2027, then it goes to the Eerste Kamer. | Deadline missed. Belgium asked for six more months. A preliminary private-sector draft exists, but no bill. Advisers expect late 2026 or early 2027. |
| Entry into force | Target 1 January 2027. Commentators now expect 1 July 2027 or 1 January 2028. | Not yet known |
| Your entity | 420 staff, so 250+: a report every year | 230 staff, so 150-249: under the directive minimum, a report every three years. Belgium may set different thresholds. |
| First report | By 7 June 2028, on calendar year 2027. No report is required on 2026 pay. | Not yet fixed |
| Works council | Must be consulted on the report. Keeps its consent rights over pay and job-evaluation systems, the classification of worker categories, and how unjustified pay differences are fixed, including the pay evaluation and action plan. | Expected through the existing works council and the collective agreements (NAR/CNT CAO 25 and 38), which need amending |

**Key mechanics of the directive, once it is in national law:**

- **Right to information.** Any worker can ask for their own pay level and the average pay by sex for workers doing the same work or work of equal value. The employer must answer within two months and must remind all staff of this right every year.
- **Joint pay assessment.** It is triggered when the report shows a gap of 5% or more in a category of workers, the employer can't justify it on objective, gender-neutral criteria, and it hasn't remedied it within six months of reporting. The employer then runs the assessment with workers' representatives and agrees remedial measures with them.
- **Remedies.** Full compensation, including back pay, bonuses and payments in kind. Member states may not cap it. The burden of proof shifts to the employer.
- **Recruitment.** Candidates must be given the pay range before negotiations. Employers may not ask about previous pay.
- **Direct effect.** Private-sector employees cannot rely on the directive directly until it becomes national law. The equal pay rules above already apply.

## 3. Cost model (illustrative)

### Inputs (all assumed)

- Women make up 25% of staff, about 162 people.
- Average annual pay for women is EUR 60,000. Average pay for men follows from the 14% overall gap: about EUR 69,800.
- Employer costs, blended across NL and BE, add 27%.
- Total payroll comes to about EUR 43.8M gross, or EUR 55.6M fully loaded.
- The unjustified like-for-like gap is somewhere between 3% and 6%, until it is validated.
- Raise needed to close a gap g: g / (1 - g). For example, closing a 6% gap needs a 6.4% raise.

### Cost of closing the gap (permanent, per year)

| Unjustified gap | Raise needed | Annual cost, fully loaded | Share of payroll | Average gap per woman per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0% | 3.1% | EUR 383k | 0.7% | EUR 1,860 |
| 4.5% | 4.7% | EUR 583k | 1.0% | EUR 2,830 |
| 6.0% | 6.4% | EUR 790k | 1.4% | EUR 3,830 |

### CFO plan against the recommended plan, 2027-2031

- **CFO plan:** 20% of the fix each year, 2027 through 2031.
- **Recommended plan:** 50% from 1 April 2027 and 100% from 1 January 2028.

Extra cash under the recommended plan, compared with the CFO plan:

| Gap | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 | 2031 | 5-year total | Average per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.0% | 67k | 230k | 153k | 77k | 0 | EUR 0.53M | EUR 105k |
| 4.5% | 102k | 350k | 233k | 117k | 0 | EUR 0.80M | EUR 160k |
| 6.0% | 138k | 474k | 316k | 158k | 0 | EUR 1.09M | EUR 217k |

Compared with fixing everything at once, the CFO plan defers two full years of cost (EUR 0.8-1.6M). Every euro of that is pay that women doing the same work did not receive, and so a potential claim.

### Exposure the CFO plan doesn't make go away

- **Netherlands, past underpayment.** If the unjustified gap has existed for five years, the existing back-pay exposure in NL alone is of the order of EUR 1.2-2.6M, plus statutory interest and legal costs. Fixing now stops it growing. The CFO plan lets it keep growing for four more years.
- **Belgium, per claim.** A flat sum of six months' pay is about EUR 30k per claimant, roughly eight years of an average woman's gap. It can be cumulated where more than one ground applies.
- **Netherlands, from 2028.** If gaps of 5% or more are still unremedied six months after the first report, you must run a joint pay assessment. The OR then has consent rights over the fix.

## 4. Checklist: validating the 6% (by 29 January 2027)

1. **Analyse each legal employer separately.** NL and BE pay levels differ. If women are spread unevenly across the two countries, a pooled figure can overstate or hide the real gap.
2. **Use hourly or FTE pay.** Annual pay is distorted by part-time work, which is very common among women in the Netherlands. The directive works from hourly pay.
3. **Include all pay.** Base pay, holiday pay, 13th month, bonus, allowances, lease car and other benefits in kind, overtime, and any shares or profit share.
4. **Run a regression** that controls for gender-neutral factors: grade, role, tenure in grade, relevant experience and performance rating. The gap that remains is what you have to fix.
5. **Test the controls themselves for bias.**
   - Do part-timers or people returning from parental leave get lower ratings?
   - Were starting salaries based on previous pay?
   - Are men more often hired at the top of the range?
6. **Look beyond role and grade.** Compare roles of equal value across job families, for example a female-dominated support role against a male-dominated technical role at a similar level. This needs a gender-neutral job-evaluation system, which the Dutch bill requires every employer to have.
7. **Go down to the individual.** Produce a list of everyone with an unjustified gap and the amount, so the fix can be done person by person.
8. **Check progression rates.** Compare promotion rates and time-to-promotion by gender. This is the main driver of the 14% and the measure for the five-year plan.

Do the analysis with employment counsel in each country, so you get candid legal advice on justification and past exposure. Privilege must not become a way of hiding the result. The findings will go to the works councils and to staff.

## 6. Communication sequence

- **Now.** Keep the circle small while you validate: CEO, CFO, CHRO, reward, counsel. Internal first is fine. Staying silent indefinitely isn't.
- **Works councils, before staff.** In Belgium this is a legal requirement. In the Netherlands it builds the trust you will need for consent on any changes to the pay system.
- **The affected individuals, before everyone else.** Tell each person what changes, from when, and why: "We reviewed pay across comparable roles. Your pay was below where it should be and we are correcting it." Have counsel review the wording for accuracy, not to soften it.
- **All staff.** Give both numbers. Explain the difference between them, what you have fixed and the five-year plan for the rest. Saying it first and with a plan is far better than having it come out through an information request in 2028.
- **External.** Required by law from 2028 in NL; Belgium's timing is not yet known. Voluntary publication earlier is an option (Option C).

## 7. One-page note for the CFO (draft)

> **Subject: Pay gap - proposal that protects the budget and closes the legal risk**
>
> Our first pay analysis shows two different gaps.
>
> 1. **14% overall.** This is mainly because fewer women hold senior roles. It is lawful, but it will have to be reported. I agree with a five-year plan for it, with yearly targets.
> 2. **About 6% in the same role and grade.** Women are paid less than men for the same job. Unless objective factors explain it, this is unlawful today in both countries, not only once the EU directive arrives.
>
> **Why five years doesn't work for the 6%:**
>
> - **Belgium:** the works council has to see a gender pay analysis every two years under existing law.
> - **Netherlands:** once the new law applies (expected 2027-2028), any employee can ask for average pay by gender for their category, and we must answer within two months. Our first Dutch report, on 2027 pay, is due by 7 June 2028. If a gap of 5% or more is not fixed within six months, we must run a joint assessment with the OR, which then has consent rights over the fix.
> - **The money:** a five-year glide defers about two years of cost. That is pay women can claim back: five years back in NL, and in BE a flat six months' pay per claim. Compensation can't be capped.
>
> **Proposal:**
>
> - Validate the 6% with counsel by end of January.
> - Close the unjustified part in two steps, from 1 April 2027 and 1 January 2028.
> - Fund it from a separate pool of about 1% of payroll (estimated EUR 0.4-0.8M a year once complete).
> - Compared with your plan, the extra cash averages roughly EUR 100-220k a year over five years.
> - Five-year plan for the 14%.
> - Communicate internally once the first step is in place; publish when the law requires.
>
> Could we rerun these numbers together on actual payroll before 11 December?
