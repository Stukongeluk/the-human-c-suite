You're right to doubt it, and you can say so without being difficult: start with a private conversation with the VP, framed around what they are trying to achieve, and bring an alternative they can use instead of just an objection.

**Why the ranking fails (your evidence)**
- Copilot acceptance rate measures tool usage, not value. It can rise while quality falls.
- PRs merged rewards volume, which AI makes cheap. It punishes the people who review, mentor, fix incidents and delete code.
- Once a number decides who gets a plan, people optimise the number. A fixed "bottom 10%" also means someone is always bottom, even in a strong team.

**What to do, in order**

1. **Ask for a 30-minute one-to-one with the VP.** Don't start with "this is wrong". Start with the goal: "I want this to work. What problem are you trying to solve: speed, quality, or whether the Copilot spend pays off?"

2. **Offer something in place of the ranking.** For example: "Could we show team-level delivery trends instead? Lead time, review wait time, change failure rate, plus a short developer survey. Those show where we're stuck, and I'll help set it up."

3. **Raise the legal point as a process question, not a threat.** In the Netherlands, a company of 300 must have a works council (ondernemingsraad). It has a consent right (art. 27 WOR) on systems suited to monitoring employees' performance. A per-person dashboard that feeds improvement plans very likely falls under it, and GDPR applies too. Ask: "Has the OR been involved? I'd hate for this to get blocked after launch." If you don't know whether there's an OR, ask HR. If there is one, talking to a member is a safe, legitimate step.

4. **If the VP won't move, go one step up, not around.** Talk to your own manager or HR about the process (who decides, who was consulted), not about the VP. Write down dates and facts in neutral language. Not legal advice: improvement plans in Dutch employment law need a fair process and real feedback, and a dashboard ranking is a weak basis.

**A line you can use**
"I'm in favour of knowing how we're doing. My worry is that these two numbers will tell us who uses Copilot most, not who helps the company most, and people will start gaming them. Can I propose something that gets you the visibility and keeps the OR on side?"

**Honest risk:** speaking up carries some personal cost. A private, solution-first approach keeps it low, and the choice is yours.
