You can push back without being difficult: start with a private conversation with the VP, lead with their goal rather than your objection, and bring one alternative they can say yes to. Don't start in a team channel or by going over their head.

**Why the plan is weak (for your own clarity)**
- Copilot acceptance rate measures tool usage, not value. It can rise while quality falls.
- PRs merged per person rewards volume, and AI makes volume cheap. It punishes the people who review, mentor, fix incidents and delete code.
- Once a ranking decides who gets an improvement plan, people optimise the number. A forced bottom 10% always has someone in it, even on a strong team.

**What you can actually do**

1. **Ask first, in a 1:1.** "What problem are we trying to solve: delivery speed, quality, or specific people's performance?" Each has a different fix, and the answer shows whether the VP wants outcomes or control.
2. **Make it about their goal.** Say: "I want this to work. My worry is that these two numbers will get gamed, and the best reviewers and mentors will land at the bottom. Can I bring an alternative?"
3. **Offer a team-level option.** Cycle time, review wait time, change failure rate and recovery time (the DORA metrics), plus a short developer-experience survey. These show where work gets stuck, and the VP still gets a weekly view. If they want AI numbers, measure team delivery and quality before and after Copilot, not per-person acceptance.
4. **Propose a six-week trial.** It is reversible, and it lets the VP save face.

**The Dutch angle (this strengthens your case without making it personal)**
At 300 people you almost certainly have a works council (ondernemingsraad, mandatory from 50 employees). Under art. 27(1)(l) WOR it has a consent right over systems suited to monitoring employees' performance, and a weekly individual ranking looks like one. Ask neutrally: "Has this been through the OR and HR/privacy?" That is a process question, not a rebellion. Improvement plans tied to a ranking are also a dismissal-law matter, so HR and legal should be in the room. This is not legal advice.

**If the VP won't move**
Write your concerns down in neutral language, then talk to your manager and HR, and let the works council know. Don't rally colleagues behind the VP's back. How far you go is your choice, and you don't owe your career as a sacrifice.

**A line you can use**
"I'm with you on accountability. I just don't think these two metrics measure it, and I'd hate for us to lose good people over a number we can game. Can I show you something that gives you a clearer picture?"
