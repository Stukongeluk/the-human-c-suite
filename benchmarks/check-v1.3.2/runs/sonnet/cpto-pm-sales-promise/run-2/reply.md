Don't absorb this quietly, and don't refuse it either: take the CEO a one-page trade-off he can decide on, in a private conversation before any wider meeting. The promise is made; your job is to turn it into a decision about what moves, so he chooses it openly instead of finding out in March that something broke.

**Before you talk to him (a day or two)**
1. **Get a rough estimate from the engineers who would build it.** Ask for a range per feature and what they'd need to learn first. Without that, you only have an opinion against his.
2. **Understand the customer's problem behind each feature.** Ask sales what the customer actually needs and what was said, and in what words. Often one feature is a real need, one is a nice-to-have, and one has a workaround or a smaller version.
3. **Write down current capacity.** What the team is committed to in Q1, who is on it, and what you'd drop to fit each feature.

**The conversation with the CEO**
Start with what you share: you want this customer happy. Then show facts, not a complaint:
- "Here's the Q1 plan and the capacity behind it. These three features cost roughly X to Y weeks. To fit them, either A, B or C slips, or we reduce scope."
- Give him two or three options, for example: (1) all three, with these roadmap items dropped, which affect N customers and this revenue; (2) the one or two features the customer needs most by end of Q1, the rest in Q2; (3) a smaller first version of each, plus a pilot with the customer.
- Say which you'd choose and why, then ask him to decide. Overtime is not an option I'd put on the table: a plan that only works with overtime tends to break in the quality of everything else.

**A line you can use**
"I want to deliver this for them. The team is full, so every feature we add pushes something else out. Can I show you what moves for each option, so you can pick the one that's best for the customer and the company?"

**Afterwards**
- Offer to join the customer conversation, so the date they hear comes from an estimate the team has made.
- Suggest a simple rule going forward: customer commitments get a quick estimate from product and engineering first. Frame it as protecting his word, not limiting him.

**Keep in mind**
He may still say "do it all". Then you have made the trade-off visible and he owns it; put the decision and what slips in writing, neutrally, and protect your team from the squeeze.
