Don't absorb it quietly, and don't refuse it. Size the three features with your team this week, then take the CEO a private choice instead of a complaint: which roadmap work moves so his promise can be kept, or which features get smaller or later. One comparison decides it: the weeks the three features need against the weeks free in Q1. With your team at capacity, that's close to zero, and he needs to see it now, not in March.

**This week**

1. **Find the real need.** Ask the account owner or the CEO what the customer is trying to achieve, why Q1, and what's at stake: a renewal, an expansion, a contract clause? Often one feature matters most and another can be covered by configuration or a workaround.
2. **Get a rough estimate from the team.** Ranges, in a day or two. Then list the roadmap items that would slip to fit all three, and who each one affects.
3. **Talk to the CEO one to one, before any roadmap meeting.** He probably promised under pressure to keep the account. Your job is to help him keep his word, not to prove him wrong. Bring options:
   - All three by end of Q1, and these named roadmap items move to Q2.
   - The most important one by Q1, the others on a dated plan he shares with the customer now.
   - Smaller first versions of all three, tried out with the customer.

   Not on the list: all three on top of the current roadmap. That only fits with overtime or skipped testing, and it comes back as bugs at your biggest customer and a slower Q2.
4. **Let him choose, then confirm it in two written lines:** what changes and why, so the roadmap update doesn't look like your team slipping.
5. **Tell your team what's happening,** and that nothing is added until the trade-off is decided.

**A line you can use**

"I want to help you deliver what you promised them. The team sized the three features at about [X] weeks, and Q1 is already full. Here are the ways we can make it work. Which do you prefer, and which roadmap items are you comfortable moving?"

**Once it's settled:** suggest one habit. Before a date goes to a customer, you give him a rough sizing within 48 hours. It protects his credibility.

Two answers would change the details: is the promise contractual or verbal, and would these features help other customers too? If so, some may belong on the roadmap anyway.
