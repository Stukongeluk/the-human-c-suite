Don't refuse it, and don't quietly absorb it. Turn the promise into a choice the CEO makes, in private, with the trade-off in front of him. Your team is full, so every week these three features take pushes a week of the current roadmap out. Which work moves should be his decision now, not his surprise in March.

What damages the relationship isn't pushing back now. It's saying yes and then missing the date with his biggest customer.

**This week, before you meet**

1. **Find the real need.** Ask the CEO or the account manager what problem these features solve for the customer, and exactly what was promised. A date in a contract is different from "we're working on it", and the need is often smaller than three features.
2. **Get a rough estimate** from your engineering lead: ranges, not a commitment, with no pressure to make it fit.
3. **Map what slips.** For each feature, name what moves and who that affects. Note which features help many customers and which only this one; a one-off build is maintained forever.

**The 1:1 with the CEO**

Start from his goal: keeping this customer and keeping his word. Then bring options, not a verdict:

- **A:** all three by end of Q1; these named roadmap items move to Q2.
- **B:** the feature that matters most by Q1, the others dated for Q2, with an early version the customer can try.
- **C:** cover part of it another way: a smaller version, configuration or a workaround.

Not an option: everything at once. That only works with overtime or cut testing, and it comes back as bugs at this same customer.

A line you can use: *"I'm not saying no. The team is full, so something has to give, and I'd rather you choose what than have it happen by accident in March."*

**Afterwards**

- The same day, email him what you agreed and what moved.
- Tell your team what's in and what's out, so they aren't carrying both.
- Propose one rule for next time: before a date reaches a customer, product and engineering get two days to estimate. Frame it as protecting his credibility, not your turf.

To be fair to him: if this customer is a big share of revenue, these features may deserve to beat your roadmap. The point is to choose that with open eyes. Two answers would change my advice: is their renewal at risk, and has the date already gone to them in writing?
