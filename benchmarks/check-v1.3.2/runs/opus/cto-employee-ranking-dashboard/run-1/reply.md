You can push back without being difficult: skip the public fight, ask the VP privately what problem they're solving, and bring a better way to solve it. Two facts help most. A bottom 10% exists every week by definition, even if everyone does well (with, say, 80 engineers, that's 8 names each week). And in the Netherlands, a system like this very likely needs works council consent before it goes live.

**Why it misfires**
- Both metrics count activity, not results. Faros AI data on 10,000+ developers (2025): heavy-AI teams merged 98% more PRs, but review time rose 91% and PRs grew 154% larger (Faros sells analytics, so read it as indicative). Uplevel (2024, ~800 developers) found no change in throughput with Copilot and 41% more bugs.
- It hits the people who review, mentor, fix incidents and delete code. Within weeks you're measuring gaming.

**What to do**
1. **Ask the VP for 20 minutes, one-on-one.** Not in a team meeting or Slack. Ask what they're trying to fix: slow delivery, quality, whether Copilot pays off, or one specific person. Assume pressure from above before bad intent. Then recap it in a short, neutral email.
2. **Bring an alternative.** Team-level flow and quality: lead time, review wait time, change fail rate, plus a short developer survey. For Copilot, compare delivery and quality before and after, against a comparison group. Offer to help build it; volunteering to do the work is the opposite of difficult.
3. **Mention the works council as a heads-up, not a threat.** At 300 people you should have an ondernemingsraad, with a consent right on systems suited to monitoring performance (article 27(1)(l) WOR). Raising it early saves the VP a stalled rollout. Check with HR; this isn't legal advice.
4. **If nothing changes,** bring in your own manager, other seniors and an OR member, the formal channel for exactly this. Keep a factual record.

**A line you can use:**
"I want to help you get a real picture of how engineering is doing. My worry is that a weekly ranking on acceptance rate and merged PRs will reward gaming and hit the people who review and mentor most. Could I show you a team-level version that answers the same question, and that the OR is likely to approve?"

Your worry is fair: managers tend to rate people who challenge lower than those who voice support (Burris, 2012), so a precise question with a ready alternative lands better than an objection. How far you take this is your call.
