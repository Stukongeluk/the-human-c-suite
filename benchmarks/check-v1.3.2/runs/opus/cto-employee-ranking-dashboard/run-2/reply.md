You can push back without being difficult: offer the VP a better way to get what they want, privately first, instead of arguing against measurement. And you have a strong fact on your side: at a Dutch company of 300, a system that tracks individual performance needs the works council's consent before use (article 27(1)(l) WOR).

**Why the ranking misfires**
- Acceptance rate shows how much someone uses Copilot, not whether the code is good. PR counts reward splitting work and punish reviewing, mentoring and incident work. On high-AI-adoption teams, [Faros AI](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-productivity-paradox-report-2025) found 98% more PRs merged, but review time went up 91% (2025; Faros sells engineering analytics).
- A fixed bottom 10% always exists, so someone goes on a plan every week even if everyone is doing well. People soon work the numbers, not the product.

**What to do, in order**
1. **Assume a fair question behind it.** The VP probably wants to know whether Copilot pays off and where delivery is slow. Aim at that.
2. **Ask for 20 minutes alone with the VP.** Start with questions, then make an offer: "What problem should the ranking solve? What would show it's working? Could we try team-level measures for one quarter instead (lead time, review wait time and change failure rate, before and after Copilot) while managers handle individual concerns the normal way?"
3. **Follow up with a short, friendly email** about what you agreed. That's a record, without drama.
4. **Find allies quietly:** other senior engineers, your manager, HR. HR will care because under Dutch law an improvement plan has to rest on real shortcomings and a fair chance to improve, and a relative ranking shows neither.
5. **If it goes ahead anyway,** ask a works council member whether the council has given consent. That's their job, so you aren't making trouble. A decision taken without consent is void if the council invokes that in writing within a month ([Dirkzwager on the Dutch DPA's guidance](https://www.dirkzwager.nl/kennis/artikelen/instemmingsrecht-van-de-or-volgens-het-or-privacyboekje-van-de-ap)).

**A line for the meeting:** "I want this to work. I'm worried this version measures Copilot usage instead of results and puts good people on plans. Can I propose a team-level version, and we compare after a quarter?"

**The personal risk:** a private question with an alternative is about the safest way to disagree, and asking for reasons isn't disloyalty. How far you take it is still your call. If there's no works council or the VP shuts the conversation down, the next steps change.

This isn't legal advice; the works council or an employment lawyer can confirm the details.
