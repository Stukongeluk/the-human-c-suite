Book a private 1:1 with your CEO within 48 hours, bring rough effort estimates from your engineering lead, and ask him to choose between three concrete trade-offs—because your team is at 100% capacity with 0% buffer for three unestimated features. Do not push back in a wider meeting, and do not absorb the work quietly with overtime; that turns one awkward conversation into missed commitments across the board, incidents, and attrition.

Start from his side: he was in front of your biggest customer protecting renewal or expansion revenue, and he likely did not see the full Q1 queue in that moment. Treating him as an ally trying to solve a revenue problem—rather than someone breaking the process—protects your relationship.

**Your first steps:**

1. **Align with your engineering lead today.** Get rough-order estimates for the three features and identify what the customer actually needs to achieve by end of Q1. Often one feature is urgent, one can be a configuration or workaround, and one can wait for Q2.
2. **Meet the CEO privately before any leadership or team forum.** Never put him on the spot in public.
3. **Present three options, not a flat no:**
   - **Swap:** Deliver all three by end of Q1, and defer specific existing Q1 items of equal effort (naming the customers and revenue affected).
   - **Phase:** Ship the smallest viable version or workaround in Q1, and move the rest to Q2.
   - **Hold Q1:** Keep the current roadmap intact and jointly reset a firm Q2 date with the customer.
4. **Set one simple rule for next time:** Leadership and sales can promise discovery and a date for an answer, never a delivery date before engineering estimates it.

**A line you can use:**
> "I want us to deliver for [Customer] and protect that revenue. Right now our Q1 capacity is 100% committed to [X and Y]. Adding three features without dropping anything means missing our Q1 commitments or burning out the team and risking quality. Here are three ways we can make this work—which trade-off do you want to make?"

**Key assumptions:** You have an engineering lead who can estimate quickly, and your CEO will engage with clear revenue-versus-capacity numbers in private.
