**CPO: Skipping the pilot**

**BOTTOM LINE**
Skipping the pilot gains you no time, because the works council and UWV set the 30 June date. Instead I've folded the pilot into the rollout: go live in stages from 1 March while the legal steps run, with stop rules written in advance. That way you have six weeks of real data before the first step you can't undo in mid-April, and the end date is the same.
Confidence: medium-high on the timing logic, low on the thresholds until you have a baseline.

**WHAT**
- **The pilot isn't what decides the end date.** [MEDIUM: Dutch law from working knowledge; confirm with counsel]
  - The fixed sequence is: works council advice request mid-January, collective-dismissal notice early March, UWV permits or settlement agreements mid-April, notice end of May.
  - A staged go-live on 1 March sits inside that sequence and costs zero days.
  - Dropping it moves nothing forward.
- **The savings don't arrive any sooner either.** The team is paid through its notice until 30 June whatever you do with the pilot. Skipping it saves no money. It only removes the one check you get before committing.
- **Without live testing, the first full-volume day is 1 July**, the day after the team has left. If it goes wrong then, nobody is left to fix it or take the calls.
- **"Competitors did this" shows they announced it, not that it worked.** [ASSUMED: I haven't seen what your competitors did or how it went]
  - Klarna announced the same in 2024 and was hiring humans again in 2025. [MEDIUM: not re-checked today]
  - A one-day check tells you more. Are they still advertising support jobs? Do recent reviews on G2, Capterra or Trustpilot mention support? What do your sales team hear from customers switching away from them?
- **Their results won't transfer to you, and being second costs you nothing.**
  - Resolution rates depend on your ticket mix, knowledge base and languages. The same vendor gives different results at different companies.
  - Every competitor can buy the same vendors, so going live three months earlier is no moat.
  - If their support has got worse, good human escalation for enterprise customers becomes something you can sell.

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
Once UWV permits are filed and notices go out, the decision is effectively final. The staged go-live is the only thing that tells you whether the AI works before that point. It costs you no calendar time and no money.

**HOW TO ACT** (fast track. Full timeline in `support-ai-plan.md`, section 9, now updated)
1. **Start the legal track.** Engage employment counsel and inform the works council. → CPTO + HR → Fri 4 Dec 2026
2. **Test vendors offline, with no customers involved.** Run 2–3 vendors on about 500 past tickets in Dutch and English, graded blind by your senior agents, and choose one. This takes about two weeks and runs in parallel. → AI agent PM → Fri 22 Jan 2027
3. **Write the stop rules before go-live.** Have the CPTO sign them and share them with the works council. Examples: CSAT more than 5 points below today's baseline for 2 weeks, re-contact more than 1.5× baseline, any severe error, time-to-human worse than your SLA. → CPTO + Head of Support → Fri 26 Feb 2027
4. **Go live in stages.** Start with one segment in week 1 and add segments weekly, so all chat is covered by the end of March and email from April. → AI agent PM → Mon 1 Mar 2027
5. **Hold a go/no-go on six weeks of live data** before filing UWV permits or signing settlement agreements. If a stop rule has tripped, pause the rollout and move the UWV step back. → CPTO → Wed 14 Apr 2027

**RISKS**
- **The stop rules get ignored under deadline pressure.** By April the momentum will be strong. They only work if they're written down, signed and visible to the works council.
- **Your knowledge walks out.** The AI is built from your senior agents' knowledge while they're losing their jobs. Pay retention bonuses for staying until 30 June and include them in the one-off costs.
- **The works council sees a decision, not a proposal.** If the advice request reads as a decision already made, the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer) can reverse it.
- **Enterprise customers aren't told in time.** They still need subprocessor notice before go-live, often 30 days, so send notices by mid-January.

**YOUR DECISION**
- **Option A1:** No live testing at all; the AI takes full volume when the team leaves. It looks fastest, but you get no data before committing and have no fallback on 1 July. I'd advise against this.
- **Option A2:** Replace all 25 by 30 June, using the staged go-live from 1 March and the go/no-go on 14 April. Same date, same savings, and you'll know whether it works before you're committed.
- **Option B:** As in my first plan. Make the AI the front door by 30 June and decide headcount in Q3 on evidence.

**Recommendation:** B is still my view of the overall plan, but the call is yours. If you're set on Q2, take A2 over A1. It costs no time and gives you a brake.

**CRITIC-SCREEN**
- **Weakest point:** the six weeks of data cover chat first. Email and enterprise volume reach full scale only in May and June, after the go/no-go.
- **Missing perspective:** what your competitors' customers actually experienced. That's unknown until someone does the one-day check.
- **If wrong, the cost is:** about €0.5M a year for each extra point of churn (assuming ~€50M ARR), plus rehiring costs, with no team left to fall back on.
- **Proceed:** A2 with these risks noted.
