**CPO: Who to tell, and when**

**BOTTOM LINE**
You can wait to tell the support team until January, but not the works council. If you sign a binding contract to replace the team before asking the works council's advice, you hand it the strongest possible case at the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer). That could stall the 30 June date by months, which is the delay you're trying to avoid. The lawful way to keep this quiet is to tell the works council first in confidence and sign the contract subject to its advice. Then tell the team in mid-January, together with a retention package, because a retention package is what actually stops early departures.
Confidence: medium-high on the direction. The legal points come from my working knowledge of Dutch law, so your employment counsel should confirm them before you act.

**WHAT**
- **Signing first turns the advice into a formality, and the law doesn't allow that.** [MEDIUM]
  - The works council must be asked for advice while it can still change the decision (WOR art. 25(2)).
  - Once a production contract to replace the team is signed, the decision has in effect been taken.
  - The works council can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber, which can order the decision withdrawn, its consequences undone and further implementation stopped (art. 26). That could include unwinding the vendor contract, and it would push everything back by months.
- **The works council can be told and still keep it secret.** [MEDIUM]
  - If you mark the information confidential, works council members are legally bound to keep it to themselves (art. 20).
  - Signing contracts "subject to works council advice" (onder voorbehoud van OR-advies) is standard Dutch practice. You keep the vendor, the price and the timeline, and add an exit right in case the plan changes.
- **Holding off on the team is possible, but only until mid-January.**
  - The rules don't force you to tell the team in December. My first plan briefed them on 11 December. The fast track can move that to the week the advice request goes in (15 January), with the timing agreed with the works council.
  - Until then, the only vendor agreement you sign is a short evaluation agreement for the offline test. It commits you to nothing.
- **This won't stay secret for two months anyway.** [ASSUMED]
  - Ticket exports, vendor access to past tickets, the privacy impact assessment (DPIA), the engineers on the integration and the people grading the vendor test all touch it.
  - In a 350-person company, the likeliest outcome is a leak before 22 January. Then people learn it from rumour, with no package on the table, and your best agents leave first, which is exactly what you want to prevent.
- **Retention is what stops early departures, not secrecy.**
  - Put the package on the table the day people hear the news:
    - a stay bonus payable on the last working day, roughly 1–2 months' salary each, about €0.1–0.2M for the team [ASSUMED]
    - an enhanced social plan and outplacement
    - first call on internal vacancies
    - paid time to look for work
  - People with five months' notice and a reason to stay mostly stay. Your other 325 employees will also see how these 25 were treated, and that shapes how much they trust you the next time you change something.

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
Telling people at the right moment is what keeps the 30 June date safe. Skipping the works council is the one move in this plan that gives an outside party the power to stop it.

**HOW TO ACT** (updated in `support-ai-plan.md`, section 9)
1. **Brief employment counsel on the confidential timeline below** and get their sign-off on the sequence. → CPTO + HR → Fri 4 Dec 2026
2. **Tell the works council chair and members in confidence.** Mark the information confidential under art. 20 and agree with them when and how the team will be told. → CPTO + HR → Fri 4 Dec 2026
3. **Submit the advice request and brief the team the same week**, with the retention package and the social plan outline. → CPTO + HR + Head of Support → Fri 15 Jan 2027
4. **Run the offline vendor test under a short evaluation agreement only** (4–22 Jan). Have it graded by a small confidential group: the Head of Support plus 2–3 senior agents, each with a signed confidentiality agreement and a stay bonus. → AI agent PM → Fri 22 Jan 2027
5. **Sign the production contract subject to works council advice**, with an exit right if the plan changes after the advice. → CPTO + Legal → Fri 22 Jan 2027

**RISKS**
- **A leak before 15 January.** Have a short, true statement ready ("we're evaluating AI for support, the works council is involved, nothing is decided"). Brief the team within 48 hours rather than denying it.
- **The confidential group feels caught in the middle.** You're asking 3–4 people to keep a secret from their colleagues. Keep the group small, pay for it, and keep the period short.
- **The works council refuses confidentiality or takes a long time.** If it wants to consult the team, agree an earlier briefing rather than going around it. That takes days; an Enterprise Chamber case takes months.
- **The retention package is treated as optional.** Without it, a January briefing leads to departures in February, just when the knowledge base needs your senior agents.

**YOUR DECISION**
- **Option A:** As you asked. Sign the vendor contract first, then tell the works council and the team. Nobody is told before late January, but you hand the works council a strong case at the Enterprise Chamber, a ruling against you could stall Q2 by months, and the plan is likely to leak first anyway.
- **Option B:** Tell the works council in confidence in early December, sign subject to its advice, and tell the team on 15 January with a retention package. The team still doesn't hear until January, the contract still gets signed on 22 January, and the plan stays on the legal path.

**Recommendation:** B. It gives you the secrecy you're after without giving anyone the power to stop the plan.

**CRITIC-SCREEN**
- **Weakest point:** a confidential works council process still depends on its members' discretion and goodwill. If trust is low today, they may resist confidentiality.
- **Missing perspective:** the works council's own view on timing. Ask the chair before you decide.
- **If wrong, the cost is:**
  - B: some people may leave in February despite the package. That's partly priced in through the stay bonus.
  - A: months of delay if the Enterprise Chamber rules against you, plus unwinding costs on the vendor contract.
- **Proceed:** B, after counsel confirms the sequence.
