Agreed: I've taken out the separate pilot phase. The result is that the AI handles full volume on chat and email by mid-February, about four months earlier than in my first draft. I've kept two checks inside the rollout, because they don't add any time to the schedule.

**The pilot was never what set your go-live date.** Three other things do, whatever we decide about the pilot:
- the vendor's security review and DPA;
- customer subprocessor notices, which are often 30 days under B2B DPAs;
- the first integrations.

That makes mid-January the earliest realistic go-live. A replay test on your own tickets fits inside that wait, and a staged ramp is simply how the traffic gets switched on.

## Fast-track timeline

| When | What |
|---|---|
| This week | Shortlist two vendors, starting with your helpdesk's own AI agent. Engage employment counsel, brief the works council (OR) chair, and pull the ticket data. |
| 1–18 Dec | Replay test: about 500 of your historical tickets per vendor, graded blind by senior agents. The security review and DPA run in parallel. Choose a vendor by 18 Dec and send the subprocessor notices. |
| Week of 18 Jan | Live on 10% of chat. |
| 25 Jan to 8 Feb | One step a week: 30% of chat, then 60% plus email, then 100% of chat and email for routine questions. |
| Mid-Feb to Apr | Account actions (resets, invoices, user management) once a security red-team has passed, then the remaining channels. |

**Each ramp step goes ahead only if, in the previous week:**
- the reviewed sample has no unresolved wrong answers on pricing, contracts, security or personal data;
- CSAT on AI conversations isn't clearly below your human baseline;
- handoffs to humans work without customers having to repeat themselves.

If a step fails, you hold for a week and fix the cause. The project carries on.

**Why I wouldn't switch on 100% on day one:**
- **It saves no time,** because the notices and security review set the date anyway.
- **You own every answer the agent gives.** In 2024 a tribunal held Air Canada liable for a wrong policy answer from its chatbot. At full volume you'd learn about mistakes like that from customers instead of from a week of review.
- **Untested account actions are a security risk.** An agent that can be talked into resetting someone else's account creates a security incident, not just a support ticket.

**On competitors:** it's worth knowing exactly what they did. Have someone spend an hour as a customer of each of them: ask a how-to question and a billing question, report a bug, and ask for a human. Public "AI replaced our support" stories often turn out to mean an AI front line with people behind it. Their results also can't tell you how an agent will do on your product, your docs and your Dutch and English tickets. The replay test answers exactly that question, and it costs you two weeks you're waiting through anyway.

**What this means for the team decision.** If you still want people gone by 30 June:
- the formal OR advice request and the collective dismissal notification need to go in around early February;
- most exits would have to be settlement agreements.

On this timeline you'd have about three weeks of live data by then, at partial volume. That's thin, but it's real data.
