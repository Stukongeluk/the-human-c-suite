The worry makes sense. Losing your senior agents before the AI is live would hurt the rollout. I can mostly do what you're asking for the support team. I'd handle the works council differently, though, because waiting there creates exactly the delay you're trying to avoid.

## Works council: brief them in confidence before you sign

Under Dutch law, you must ask the works council (OR) for its advice on introducing an AI agent to replace the support team, and on reorganising support. You have to ask while their advice can still change the decision (art. 25 WOR).

If the first thing the OR sees is a signed contract, they have a strong argument that they were asked too late. They can then take it to the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer), which can order you to withdraw the decision and undo what you've done. That would cost you months, far more than a few people leaving early.

The OR doesn't have to be a leak, and there are two ways to protect yourself. I'd do both:
1. **Brief the OR in confidence before signing.** Bring in the chair or the daily board now and impose confidentiality under art. 20 WOR, which legally binds them. Present it as a proposed decision, with the vendor selection and the timeline.
2. **Make the vendor contract conditional.** Add a break clause or condition so that signing doesn't lock in the outcome before the OR has advised. Counsel can draft this so it costs you little.

Ask employment counsel exactly which steps need formal advice. My advice is not to let the OR find out from a signed contract.

## Support team: wait until signing, with two conditions

1. **Tell them on signing day, before the customer subprocessor notices go out.** Those notices go to every customer, and some customers will ask support what this new AI subprocessor is. If your team learns about it from a customer email, you lose the trust you need for the ramp. On this timeline signing day falls around 18 December, just before the holidays. That's not ideal, but it's better than the team piecing it together over Christmas.
2. **Have the retention offer ready to announce in the same meeting.** People leave because of uncertainty, so the first thing they hear should include what's in it for them.

Also expect the news to get out before signing day. The support lead, procurement, security and the data pull for the replay test all involve people. Have a short holding statement ready in case it does.

## Retention fixes early departures

Secrecy only delays the problem. Retention addresses it directly.
- **Identify the people you can't lose** through mid-February, and ideally through Q2: likely your senior agents, tier-2 specialists and whoever knows the knowledge base best.
- **Offer them a retention bonus paid on a fixed date** (for example 30 June). Pay it even if their role is later made redundant; otherwise nobody will believe it.
- **Be clear about redeployment chances and exit terms** from day one.

Other people leaving early doesn't hurt the plan, because you're not backfilling anyway. The risk lies with a specific handful of people, so spend the money there.

## One change to the replay test

I'd planned to have senior agents grade the replay test in December. Under your constraint, the support lead and team leads do the grading instead, under confidentiality. The grading will be slightly less sharp, but it still works.
