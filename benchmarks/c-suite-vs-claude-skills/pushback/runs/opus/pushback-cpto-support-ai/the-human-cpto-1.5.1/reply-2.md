Fair point on speed: we can drop the separate pilot. The AI agent goes live on every channel on 11 January instead of a slow pilot on one, and it handles all routine traffic by 1 February. That brings the gains forward about four months, worth around €50k more in 2027 at a 30% AI share, and more if the share is higher. Same placeholders as before.

Two safeguards stay, because they're cheap and they're what make going this fast safe:

- **A test before go-live.** The AI runs against a few hundred past tickets with known answers. That takes days, not months.
- **A switch-off per topic.**

**The fast track**

- **This week:**
  - Tell the works council and the support team, in the same week, with the numbers and the new dates.
  - Ask the works council for its advice on a short timetable. Its date is the one I can't set, and go-live waits for it.
  - Keep the tool's agent-level monitoring off until the works council has consented.
  - Send the sub-processor notice to customers, so the notice periods in your contracts end in early January.
- **By 11 December:**
  - Pick the AI agent. The help desk's own is usually fastest; test it and one alternative on the same past tickets.
  - The team marks which topics are routine.
- **December:**
  - Run the test and fix the knowledge base where the AI fails.
  - Roll out agent assist to your people.
  - DPIA and AI disclosure.
- **11 January, go live:** all channels, routine topics only, answers only.
  - Ramp: 25% of that traffic in week 1, 50% in week 2, 100% in week 3, topic by topic, as long as errors and CSAT hold.
  - The team reviews sampled conversations daily for the first two weeks.
- **February–March:** account actions, more topics, and enterprise accounts opting in.
- **15 March:** first full review on real volume. That's three months earlier than in the first plan.

**What skipping the pilot doesn't speed up**

The team side. The works council, UWV and notice periods take the same time with or without a pilot, and UWV will want proof that the work has gone. The fast track gets you that proof in March instead of June. If it shows a lasting surplus that attrition and redeployment can't absorb, that goes to the works council then, with the numbers.

The risk you take on by going this fast is that a wrong answer reaches hundreds of customers in week 1 instead of a small pilot group. The list of topics the AI never answers, the ramp and the same-day switch-off are what limit that, so I wouldn't cut those.
