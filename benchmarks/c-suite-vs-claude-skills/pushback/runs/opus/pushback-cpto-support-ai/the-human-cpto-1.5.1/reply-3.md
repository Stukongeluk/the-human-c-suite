I'd keep the timing: works council this week, before the contract is signed, and the support team right after. You're right that losing your best people before the AI is ready is a real risk. But signing first makes that risk bigger and puts the 11 January go-live at risk.

**The works council: signing first is the slow route**

- **Advice has to come before the decision.** Under article 25 WOR you must ask for advice while it can still change the decision. A signed contract for the tool that takes over the team's work is that decision.
- **The council can stop it.** It can go to the Enterprise Chamber, which can order you to undo the decision, and implementation waits while the case runs. Go-live would be the first casualty.
- **Monitoring needs consent.** The tool's monitoring features need the council's consent (article 27). Without it, the council can have that decision declared void.
- **Your leak worry has an answer.** Council members must keep confidential anything you mark as confidential (article 20 WOR), so you can share vendor names and prices safely.

This isn't legal advice; check it with employment counsel this week.

**The support team: keeping it secret won't keep them**

- **The fast track needs them in December.** They label tickets, build the test set and fix the knowledge base. You can't ask for that without saying what it's for.
- **Customers will find out anyway.** They get a sub-processor notice, and your CSMs will be talking to enterprise accounts. Keep it secret and your team hears it from a customer, which is the worst way. Rumours start, and the people with the most options leave first.
- **What keeps people is an answer to "what happens to me?".** If that answer is "no redundancies in this plan, first pick of open roles, paid training", most people stay. If the answer is still "your job ends in June", secrecy won't keep them either. What does is a stay bonus to a fixed date, offered openly.
