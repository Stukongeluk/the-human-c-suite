# Plan: bringing our costs back in line with revenue

**Draft for the board meeting of Thursday 17 December 2026** · Owner: CEO · Prepared: 23 November 2026

**Status:** proposal. Any reduction in roles in this plan is an intended decision (voorgenomen besluit) until the works council has given its advice under article 25 of the Works Councils Act (WOR). Until then, the parts the works council can still change stay open, and every document and message calls it an intended decision.

This plan is not legal advice. Employment counsel confirms each legal step before we rely on it.

---

## 1. The problem

Revenue is 18% below [fill in: comparison period] and our cost base has not followed. As a result we [fill in: lose about €__ a month / reach our cash floor of €__ in __ / are at risk on covenant __ at the test date of __]. The board has asked for 60 fewer roles by the end of March. This plan sets out how to close the same gap faster, with less damage to our ability to recover, and within Dutch law.

## 2. Responsibility

The revenue decline is the leadership team's result. It follows from choices we made about [products, markets, pricing, sales], not from the work of the colleagues whose jobs are now at risk. We will say this to the board, the works council and every employee. The leadership team takes the first and largest cut (section 6). That won't close the gap. It is what makes it fair to ask anything of anyone else.

## 4. Set the target in euros and cash, not in heads

At an estimated €90,000 fully loaded per role (salary, holiday allowance, employer charges and pension), 60 roles is about €5.4m a year, or €450,000 a month. We propose that the board restates its decision as a target:

> **Reduce run-rate costs by €[gap] a year and keep cash above €[floor], by [quarter].**

The number of roles that must go then follows from what is left after faster measures. Sixty becomes a ceiling we test, not a number we fill.

Why:

- **Law.** If the number of roles is fixed before the works council advises, its advice can no longer change the decision, and the decision can be challenged at the Enterprise Chamber (WOR articles 25 and 26).
- **Speed.** Most non-payroll and contractor savings start in the first quarter. In the Netherlands, savings from redundancies start only after the works council, the UWV or settlement process and notice periods, so mostly from May to July (section 9).
- **Recovery.** Revenue fell. Cutting capacity before we know why risks removing the people who win it back.

The case for acting fast is real: at this run-rate, each month without action costs about €450,000 (estimate). So everything that can start this week starts this week.

A public short-time work scheme isn't available for a drop like ours. The current scheme (werktijdverkorting) covers only calamities outside normal business risk, and the bill for a broader crisis scheme (Wet personeelsbehoud bij crisis, 36.940) is still before the Tweede Kamer.

**Together, the reversible measures could cover an estimated €2.6m (if we have no contractors to end) to €5.4m (with 10 contractor FTE) a year.** That would leave between none and about 30 roles to go, instead of 60. We will know which end is right by 11 December.

**Seen from each seat**

- **Customers:** protect support, customer success on at-risk accounts, security and on-call coverage. No change to a customer commitment without telling the customer.
- **Finance:** the whole cost, net and by quarter; cash against the floor; one-off costs arrive before the savings.
- **Technology:** no single points of failure in critical systems after any exit, including contractors; knowledge transfer planned within notice periods; on-call rotas stay sustainable.
- **Product:** what stops is decided before who goes.
- **People:** one round only, and the people who stay know what has stopped.

## 6. Recommended path

1. **Leadership first, this week.** The responsibility statement in section 2, to the board and then to all staff. Leadership pay and bonus measures from 1 January, agreed in writing (variable pay already contractually promised is waived voluntarily).
2. **Fast, reversible savings from this week.** A hiring freeze with a weekly exception review by the CEO, CFO and CTO, with exceptions only for revenue-, security- or customer-critical roles, which go first to people in affected roles. A spend review with one owner per category and a validated figure by 11 December. An inventory of contractors by job group, with notice given in December where their work can stop. Cancel open vacancies not yet filled.
3. **Diagnose the revenue drop by 11 December.** Which products, segments and customers; churn versus lost new business versus price; temporary or structural. Start a revenue track with its own owner.
4. **Decide with the works council, not after it.** A first session on 1 December with the real numbers and these options, and a consultation calendar agreed that day. Treat their objections as free risk analysis and change the plan when they find a better route.
5. **Size any redundancies on validated savings only,** not on hoped-for ones, so that there is one round and no second one.
6. **Cut work along with roles.** The advice request names the work that stops, so the people who stay are not left with the same work and fewer colleagues.

## 7. If roles still have to go

### Legal steps (counsel to confirm each one)

- **Works council:** advice right on the intended decision (WOR article 25), requested while the advice can still change the decision, with the reasons, the consequences for staff and the measures we propose. If the final decision departs from the advice, implementation waits one month.
- **Collective dismissal (WMCO):** 20 or more terminations within three months in one UWV work area means notifying UWV and the unions in writing before the decision is final, and consulting the unions. Terminations by settlement agreement that we initiate count towards the 20. After a complete notification, a one-month waiting period applies during which no contract may end, including by settlement, unless the unions agree to shorten it.
- **Route:** dismissal for business-economic reasons needs UWV permission or a settlement agreement.
- **Who goes:** the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel, Ontslagregeling articles 11–15), per category of interchangeable jobs. Agency workers and freelancers in that category go first. Then employees above state pension age, on-call workers and temporary contracts with at most 26 weeks left. Then the others, spread over five age bands (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55+) so that the age mix stays the same, with the shortest-serving first within each band. The principle doesn't apply when a whole category of jobs or a unique job disappears. A narrow exception for an indispensable employee needs documentation.
- **Never selection criteria:** performance ratings, activity metrics, who we would prefer to keep, who is on leave, who raised concerns, or any protected characteristic. Employees protected by a dismissal ban, such as during illness, are skipped. Underperformance is a separate matter with its own process and does not belong in this round.
- **Management roles are in scope in proportion.** We will not protect layers of management while the people who build and sell the product go.
- **Money and notice:** the statutory transition payment is one third of a monthly salary (including holiday allowance and fixed extras) per year of service, capped at €102,000 in 2026, or one year's salary if that is higher. The cap is indexed every 1 January. Notice periods run from one to four months by length of service; UWV time is deducted, but at least one month remains.
- **Re-employment:** if the same work comes back within 26 weeks of notice, the person must be offered it first.

### Social plan (proposal, to agree with the works council and unions)

- Transition payment at 1.5 times the statutory amount.
- A transition budget of €5,000 per person, about one month's salary, available from the day of notice for coaching, training and job search, with outplacement through a provider of the person's choice. Sweden's job security councils work this way, stepping in from the day of notice; the OECD found that over 85% of displaced Swedish workers found a new job within a year.
- Paid time off during notice for job search and interviews, and payment for independent legal advice on a settlement agreement.
- Keep the laptop after company data is wiped; a written reference and a reference call.
- Priority for rehiring for 12 months, longer than the legal 26 weeks, and an alumni network.
- Confidential psychological and financial support.
- Extra help for colleagues whose residence permit depends on their job (we check the current IND rules on the search period with each person and make a plan together), colleagues close to retirement, and anyone on leave.

### The day itself

- Each person hears it from their own manager with HR present, in person or on a live call, on Tuesday 9 or Wednesday 10 March. Never by email, and never through a login that stops working.
- Access changes after the conversation, not before.
- A written summary the same day: the package, deadlines and contacts.
- Time to say goodbye to colleagues, if they want it.
- Managers get a script, a rehearsal and support for themselves.

### The people who stay

- An all-hands on the afternoon of 10 March: what happened and why, what stops, what it means for them, and that this is the only round (said only if it is true).
- Work re-planned team by team in the following two weeks, and a 1:1 for everyone within two weeks.
- Regretted attrition, absence and engagement watched monthly for 12 months. One study found that cutting the workforce by 1% was associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor and Nyberg, 2008). It is a correlation, but it puts the cost of losing people we want to keep into the business case.

"By the end of March" therefore means decisions made and notices given, not people off the payroll. A settlement agreement can end a contract sooner, but someone who leaves without working their notice period usually starts unemployment benefit later, so a fair settlement pays that period out. Either way, the cost runs into the second quarter.

## 9. What 60 redundancies save, and when (estimate)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Run-rate saving: 60 roles × €90,000 | €5.40m a year |
| 2027 payroll saving, with exits around 1 June (seven months) | €3.15m |
| Statutory transition payments (average three years' service for leavers, monthly salary €5,616 including holiday allowance) | −€0.34m |
| Social plan top-up (+50%) | −€0.17m |
| Transition budget, €5,000 per person | −€0.30m |
| Legal, HR and advisory costs | −€0.15m |
| Replacing about six extra regretted leavers among those who stay (€25,000 each) | −€0.15m |
| **Net effect in 2027** | **about €2.04m** |

Not included: lost productivity during the transition, lost knowledge, and any effect on customers and revenue. Leavers' average service is set below the company average because the shortest-serving in each age band go first. By contrast, the reversible measures in section 5 mostly start in the first quarter with small one-off costs, so most of their annual effect lands in 2027.

## 11. Communication

- Always say what is decided, what is still open, and what happens next, with dates.
- The works council hears everything before staff do; managers hear before their teams; affected people hear before any wider announcement.
- No news to individuals on a Friday or just before a holiday.
- A questions channel for all staff, answered within two working days; weekly contact with the works council during consultation.
- Key customers hear from their account manager, after staff, where it affects them.

## 13. Pre-mortem: it is December 2027 and this failed. Why?

| Reason | Early warning | What we do now |
|---|---|---|
| We removed capacity we needed to win revenue back | Renewals and pipeline keep falling after March | A revenue track with its own owner; stop-work decisions protect revenue roles |
| The people we most wanted to keep left anyway | Regretted attrition above baseline in Q2 | Openness, one round only, stay conversations, workload actually reduced |
| The legal process failed or slipped | Works council or unions object to timing or information | Intended-decision wording throughout, counsel from day one, calendar agreed on 1 December |
| The savings didn't stick: work wasn't stopped, so contractors and hires crept back | Run-rate cost per line above plan | One owner per line, monthly review |
| A second round was needed because the first was sized on hope | Validated savings below plan in January | Size any reduction on validated savings only |

## 14. Assumptions, and what would change this plan

| Assumption | What would change the plan |
|---|---|
| €90,000 fully loaded per role | The real average for the affected job groups from the HR system |
| About €10m non-payroll spend, 10–15% of it avoidable | The general ledger |
| 10% annual voluntary attrition, three in five leavers not replaced | Last year's actual attrition |
| Exits around 1 June 2027 | Faster settlement agreements, or a works council advice we depart from (about four weeks later) |
| The drop is spread across the business | If it sits in one product in structural decline, stopping that product is the real decision. The reflection principle doesn't apply to a category of jobs that disappears entirely, and a reduction close to 60 may be right for that part of the business |
| Cash runway of more than 12 months, no covenant at risk in Q1 | Less runway or a covenant at risk: move faster, still lawfully, and size the reduction on the cash need |
| The drop is structural | If it is temporary, such as a large contract that slipped, voluntary reduced hours across teams, leaders first, is the better bridge |

## 15. Questions for the board
