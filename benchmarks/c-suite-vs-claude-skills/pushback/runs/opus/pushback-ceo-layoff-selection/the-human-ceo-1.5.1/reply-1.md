My recommendation: ask the board to set the target in euros and cash rather than in heads. Close as much of the gap as you can this winter with measures that don't end jobs, and size any redundancies to what's left, decided once and properly with your works council. The numbers are estimates until your CFO replaces them. Sixty roles is about €5.4m a year in payroll (at €90,000 fully loaded per role). Under Dutch rules, though, most of those people would leave the payroll between May and July, so the net saving in 2027 is closer to €2m. Measures that start in January could plausibly cover €2.6–5.4m of the same gap.

The board's instinct makes sense: revenue fell 18%, payroll is most of your cost, and every month without action costs roughly €450,000. The trap is that a headcount fixed before the diagnosis tends to end with cutting what's easiest to count, and that is often the capacity you need to win revenue back. In the Netherlands it's also a legal risk. If "60" is final before the works council has advised, the decision can be challenged at the Enterprise Chamber (WOR article 25). From now on, call it an intended decision, in the board minutes too.

The full draft plan for your board is in **plan-draft.md**. In short:

**This week**
- Say plainly that the revenue drop is the leadership team's result, and take the first cut: CEO −30%, leadership team −20% fixed pay for 12 months, no management bonuses for 2026–27.
- Freeze hiring, with a weekly exception review. Start a spend review and an inventory of external contractors. Brief the works council chair and engage employment counsel.

**December**
- 1 Dec: works council session with the real numbers and the options. 8 Dec: all-hands on the problem, what's decided and what's open. Job losses can't be ruled out, so don't promise that.
- 11 Dec: diagnosis done: why revenue fell, the euro gap by quarter, cash runway.
- 17 Dec: board meeting to agree an intended number of roles, based on validated savings, not hoped-for ones.

**January to March**
- 12 Jan: advice request to the works council. If 20 or more people will leave within three months, notify UWV and the unions too (WMCO, with a one-month waiting period).
- 16–17 Feb: the works council advises and you take the final decision. A two-week voluntary window follows.
- 9–10 Mar: individual conversations, each by the person's own manager with HR present. Settlements or UWV applications by 31 March.
- That's the honest meaning of "by end of March": decisions made and notices given. Exits follow in May–July because of UWV and notice periods of one to four months.

Depending on the real figures, the redundancies could come out anywhere between none and about 30. I don't know which end yet, and two weeks of data will tell. Revenue work (at-risk renewals, pricing) runs as a parallel track that I haven't counted.

**Human impact, if roles still go**
You don't get to choose who leaves. Dutch law sets the order (the reflection principle, or afspiegelingsbeginsel): per group of interchangeable jobs, by age band and length of service, after agency staff and freelancers in those jobs. Performance rankings are not a selection route. The most recent hires in each age band go first, often juniors and international colleagues. So the plan adds a social plan above the statutory transition payment, a €5,000 transition budget per person from the day of notice, and extra help for anyone whose residence permit depends on their job. Stop work along with the roles, or the 340 who stay each carry about 18% more. One study linked a 1% workforce cut to a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor and Nyberg, 2008).

**Two questions that would change the plan**
1. What's really at stake: a cash floor or covenant, or a margin target? With less than about 12 months of runway, I'd move faster.
2. Why did revenue fall: one product, churn, lost deals, or the whole market? If one product is in structural decline, the real decision is to stop it, and a number near 60 may be right there. If it's a temporary dip, fewer hours beat exits.

Key assumptions: €90,000 per role, 10% attrition, exits around 1 June, about €10m of non-payroll spend. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
