I won't draft that formula. For this round it isn't lawful in the Netherlands, and it would put your 31 March deadline at risk. Below is the selection method that will get through UWV, followed by the levers you really have for keeping your strongest people.

**Why ratings and Copilot data won't work here**
- **Performance ratings:** for business-economic dismissals, UWV checks the reflection principle, and performance isn't part of it. Applications that select by ratings get refused, and the timeline goes with them. Targeting low scorers with settlement offers doesn't get around this. Nobody has to sign, and anyone who refuses goes to UWV under the normal order.
- **Copilot usage:** it measures how often someone uses a tool, not how good their work is. Usage is naturally low in architecture, support, management and work on restricted code. Using it to judge individuals turns it into a performance-monitoring system. That needs your works council's consent (WOR article 27(1)(l)). It also runs into the GDPR's purpose limitation, because the data was collected to manage licences, not to choose who gets dismissed.
- **Discrimination risk:** ratings and tool usage often track age, part-time work, leave and illness. A selection that falls harder on any of those invites claims under equal treatment law.

**The selection criteria HR applies, per category of interchangeable jobs**
1. Define each category honestly: jobs with similar content, level and pay. UWV checks this.
2. End agency workers and freelancers in the category first.
3. Next: employees at or above state pension age, on-call workers, and temporary contracts with at most 26 weeks left.
4. Spread the remaining number over five age bands (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55+), in proportion to each band's size, so the age mix stays the same. Counsel applies the official rounding.
5. Within each band, the shortest-serving employee goes first.
6. Skip anyone under a dismissal ban, such as illness, pregnancy or works council membership, and take the next person in the same band.
7. People who leave in the voluntary window reduce the number selected in their category.

**Where you do have a choice**
- **Which work stops.** When a whole category of jobs ends, the reflection principle doesn't apply to it. It has to be work you really stop, not a relabelling.
- **Indispensable employees.** This is a narrow exception. You document why one person's knowledge is essential, and the next person in line is selected instead. It covers a handful of people, not ratings by another route.
- **A collective agreement.** If one applies and allows it, up to 10% of the dismissals can depart from the order.
- **Retention.** Tell the people you most need, early and honestly, that their work continues.

**If some people really underperform:** that's a separate track. Each person must have been told clearly and given a real chance, with support, to improve. The dismissal then goes through the subdistrict court, not UWV, and takes months. Last year's ratings alone are rarely enough, and none of it fits this round or this deadline.

Copilot data is fine at team level, aggregated, with the works council's agreement, for example to decide how many licences you pay for.
