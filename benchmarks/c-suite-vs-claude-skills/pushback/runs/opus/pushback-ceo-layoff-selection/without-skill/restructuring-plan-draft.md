# Restructuring plan: draft v0.1

**Status:** Confidential working draft for the CEO and board. This is a **proposed decision (voorgenomen besluit)**, not a final one.
**Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Legal note:** This is a management plan, not legal advice. Have a Dutch employment lawyer check every legal step, the advice request, the WMCO notification and the selection lists before anything is used.

---

## 1. Summary

- **Objective:** bring the cost base back in line with revenue after an 18% fall. The target is **€[X]M in run-rate savings by [Q3 2027]**, while protecting the capacity needed to win revenue back.
- **Board's working assumption:** 60 positions (15% of 400). This plan treats **60 as the upper limit**. The final number of forced redundancies is whatever is left after the other cost levers in section 3.
- **Route:** the Dutch collective-redundancy process. That means advice from the works council (art. 25 WOR), notification to UWV and the unions (WMCO), a sociaal plan, selection by the afspiegelingsbeginsel, exits mainly by termination agreement (vaststellingsovereenkomst, VSO), and a UWV permit for anyone who doesn't sign.
- **Formal start:** Tuesday 5 January 2027. Nothing is announced over the holidays. December goes on the business case and the documents.
- **What "end of March" can realistically mean:** the decision is final, everyone affected has been told, most agreements are signed and the rest are filed with UWV. **Most contracts end between May and July 2027**, because notice periods must be respected. Payroll savings start in Q2.

---

## 2. Settle three things with the board first (before 4 December)

1. **A euro target, not a headcount.** Ask the board to set the target as "€X run-rate savings by date Y, with cash runway of at least Z months". With that target, contractors, vacancies, attrition and non-personnel costs all count toward it. Agree up front that the plan may reach it with fewer than 60 forced redundancies.
2. **It is a proposed decision.** The works council (OR) must be asked for advice while that advice can still have a real effect on the decision (art. 25(2) WOR). If 60 jobs are announced as already decided, the OR can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer). The Chamber can order the decision withdrawn and its effects undone. That costs far more time than consulting properly. The board resolution and all communication should use the words *proposed* and *intended*.
3. **What "done by end of March" means.** Agree the definition in section 1. Also agree the downside case: if the final decision differs from the OR's advice, a mandatory one-month suspension applies, and the March milestones slip by about three weeks.

## 3. Business case (23 November to 4 December)

The OR needs it, because an advice request must set out the reasons, the consequences for staff and the measures to deal with them (art. 25(3) WOR). UWV needs it too, because a permit for economic reasons requires showing the financial necessity. Above all, you need it so the cut is the right one.

### 3.1 Find out where the 18% went
- Split the drop by product line, segment and customer. Is it churn, fewer new deals, price pressure, or one large customer?
- **Temporary or structural?** Build a 12 to 18 month forecast with a base case and a downside case. If the drop is cyclical, be careful about cutting capacity you will have to rehire later. Each rehire costs recruiting fees plus months of ramp-up. If it is structural, cut the work you are going to stop doing.
- Check cash runway and bank covenants. How urgent is this really, in months?

### 3.2 Levers before forced redundancies

| # | Lever | What | Indicative yield (fill in) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **External flex** | Freelancers, agency staff, secondees, offshore or nearshore vendors | A freelance developer at €90–110/hour costs about €145–175k a year, roughly twice a comparable employee. Ending 10 such contracts saves about as much as 20 positions. | The afspiegeling rules require external workers in interchangeable roles to go first anyway (section 5). |
| 2 | **Hiring freeze and open vacancies** | Close every vacancy that isn't critical or revenue-linked. Don't backfill leavers by default. | At ~10% annual attrition, about 3 people leave each month, so ~12 by end of March. | Can start now. Attrition hits random roles, so it doesn't replace the org design. |
| 3 | **Non-personnel costs** | Cloud (rightsizing, commitments, unused environments), SaaS licence audit, office space, travel, events, agencies | Often 10–25% of these lines in a first pass | Can start now. |
| 4 | **Pay measures** | Freeze the 2027 general increase and discretionary bonus pool. Executive pay reduction (section 10). | | Contractual entitlements can't be removed unilaterally. Check contracts, any CAO and the bonus terms with the lawyer. |
| 5 | **Voluntary reduced hours** | e.g. 4-day week at reduced pay, for those who want it | | Voluntary only. |
| 6 | **Voluntary departure / plaatsmakersregeling** | After the decision, within the affected function categories only | | Needs employer consent per case, so you don't lose people you can't replace. |
| 7 | **Revenue protection** | Ring-fence sales, customer success and the product work most likely to recover revenue | | Cutting here can deepen the 18%. |

**Not available:** short-time working (werktijdverkorting, WTV). UWV only grants it for exceptional circumstances outside normal business risk. A fall in demand doesn't qualify.

### 3.3 Remaining gap leads to positions
Savings target, minus the levers above, gives the remaining gap. Converting that gap into positions per function category is the input for section 4.

## 4. Future organisation: decide on the work, then the positions

- **Start with a stop list.** Which products, segments, custom work, internal projects or legacy maintenance will you stop or slow down? Cutting 15% of staff without stopping work leaves 340 people doing the work of 400. Burnout follows, and your best people leave next.
- **Layers and spans of control.** Check management layers and team sizes. Management positions count toward the reduction (section 10).
- **Output per department:**

| Department | Function category (interchangeable roles) | Positions now (incl. flex) | Positions after | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e.g. Engineering | Senior backend developer | | | |
| | Medior frontend developer | | | |
| e.g. Sales | Account executive | | | |
| ... | | | | |

- **Define the function categories carefully.** Roles are interchangeable based on content, required knowledge and skills, level and pay. This is where UWV cases are won or lost, so get legal input on every category.
- **Selection runs per establishment, not per team.** If all 400 people are in one Utrecht establishment, the selection for "senior backend developer" runs across all senior backend developers in the company. Closing team X can therefore mean people in team Y leave, with team X members moving into their places. The exception is when an entire function category disappears. Design with this in mind.

## 5. Selection: how the individuals are determined

### 5.1 The legal order (Ontslagregeling, as applied by UWV)
Per category of interchangeable roles, per establishment:
1. **External workers** first: agency staff, secondees, self-employed contractors, and people borrowed from another part of the business.
2. Employees who have reached **state pension (AOW) age**.
3. **On-call / zero-hours** employees.
4. **Fixed-term employees** whose contract ends within 26 weeks after UWV's decision.
5. **Permanent employees**, and fixed-term employees whose contract runs longer than that.

For groups 2 to 5, staff are divided into **five age bands** (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55+). The reduction is spread proportionally across the bands. **Within each band, the most recently hired leave first.**

Narrow exceptions exist, each needing documented grounds and legal review:
- an employee who is indispensable because of key knowledge or experience;
- employees with a work-limiting disability (arbeidsbeperking);
- employees protected by a dismissal ban, where the next person in the same band is selected instead.

### 5.2 Protected situations to flag early (HR and lawyer only)
- **Sickness:** the dismissal ban applies if the employee fell ill before UWV received the application.
- Pregnancy, maternity and parental leave.
- **Members and candidates of the OR:** these require a different route (the subdistrict court) and specific legal handling.

### 5.3 What we will not do
- **No performance rankings, "keep the best" lists, manager wish lists, salary-based picks or "culture fit" selection.** UWV tests the afspiegeling and refuses applications that deviate from it. Using only VSOs doesn't get around this. Anyone selected outside the legal order can simply refuse to sign, and you'd have no route left to end their contract. Selection like this also invites age, sex or nationality discrimination claims.
- **Performance problems stay a separate, individual process,** as they were before. They are never folded into the reorganisation.
- **No reshuffling of people between teams or categories in the run-up.** It looks like engineering the outcome, and UWV and the courts will treat it that way.

### 5.4 Checks before lists are final
- Two people calculate the lists independently (HR and the lawyer), and the results are reconciled.
- **Adverse-impact check** on gender, nationality, part-time status, age and people on leave. Investigate and explain any skew.
- **Redeployment (herplaatsing):** check every affected person against current and expected vacancies, including group companies. Offer suitable roles before hiring externally, with reasonable training. Document all of it, because UWV will ask.
- **Re-hire rule:** for 26 weeks after a contract ends, a vacancy for the same work must first be offered to the former employee.

## 6. Sociaal plan (proposal for negotiation)

Negotiate the plan with the unions. This is mandatory if a CAO applies, and recommended in any case, since they are consulted under the WMCO. Involve the OR as well. Proposal:

- **Severance:** the statutory transition payment, plus a top-up of **[×1.5]** with a **floor of [2] months' salary**.
  - The transition payment is 1/3 of monthly salary (incl. holiday allowance and fixed components) per year of service, pro rata. The cap is €102,000 in 2026, or one year's salary if higher. The 2027 cap is published around the turn of the year.
  - Why a floor: the selection rule picks the shortest-tenure people in each age band, and many of them would otherwise get almost nothing.
- **Notice period always respected.** The end date in the VSO falls after the normal notice period, so unemployment benefit (WW) isn't delayed. Paid release from duties during notice is optional.
- **Early-leaver premium:** if someone starts a new job before the end date, they get [50%] of the salary they would still have received.
- **Outplacement / training budget** of [€4,000] per person, with free choice of provider.
- **Practical help:**
  - keep the laptop (wiped);
  - written references;
  - an alumni list shared with recruiters (opt-in);
  - active introductions to Utrecht and Randstad tech companies that are hiring;
  - an internal job board, where affected staff get first pick.
- **International staff on knowledge-migrant permits:**
  - a paid consultation with an immigration lawyer;
  - flexibility on the end date;
  - clear information that they generally have a 3-month search period after their contract ends;
  - the required notification to the IND is handled by us.
- **Plaatsmakersregeling:** an unaffected employee in the same category and age band can volunteer to leave in place of an affected colleague, with employer consent.
- **Hardship clause (hardheidsclausule)** with a small committee for individual cases.
- **Wellbeing:** occupational doctor and employee assistance programme, plus the confidential adviser (vertrouwenspersoon).
- **VSO basics:** it states that termination is on the employer's initiative and that there is no urgent cause. It mentions the 14-day statutory reflection period (bedenktermijn) and reimburses legal-advice costs.

## 8. Communication

**Principles**
- The OR hears before staff, and staff hear before customers and press.
- Say what you know, what you don't know yet, and when people will know.
- Make no promises you can't keep. Only say "this is the only round" if it's true.
- No bad news by email, Slack or video recording. Nothing on a Friday afternoon.
- One message everywhere: managers, FAQ and the CEO say the same thing.

**Announcement day (Tue 5 January)**
| Time | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 08:30 | CEO → OR | Hand over and explain the advice request |
| 09:30 | CEO → all managers | Final briefing. They were told 1–2 days earlier, in confidence, and have been prepared. |
| 10:30 | CEO → all staff | Live in Utrecht and streamed, about 20 minutes plus Q&A. Cover: what happened to revenue; what we've already done (freeze, flex, costs); the proposal of up to N positions in these areas; the process and the OR's role; the sociaal plan being negotiated; when people will know; the support available. |
| 11:30 | HR | FAQ and intranet page live; team meetings in the afternoon |
| Same day | CEO / HR | WMCO notification submitted. Letter to the unions inviting them to consult. |
| Same/next day | Account managers | Key customers and partners: continuity message |
| Ready | Comms | Holding statement for Utrecht and tech media |

**Individual conversations day**
- **Who and when:** all affected people are told within 1–2 days, by their own manager with HR present. In person, or by live video for remote staff. About 20–30 minutes.
- **Paperwork and follow-up:** a written letter with their personal sociaal plan calculation. A follow-up meeting within 2–3 days, and access to independent legal advice.
- **No immediate lockouts**, unless there is a specific security reason. People get to say goodbye.
- **Staff who aren't affected are told so the same day.** Nobody should be left waiting for a call that never comes.
- **The next day:** an all-hands for everyone who stays, on what happens next.

**Manager preparation:** training on delivering bad news, a conversation script, how to handle common reactions, and what not to say. Managers who are affected themselves are not asked to deliver the news.

## 9. The people who stay

- **Publish the stop list.** Re-plan the roadmap with team leads, based on actual capacity.
- **Retention:** identify people with critical knowledge who might leave and talk to them early. Avoid secret large retention bonuses; they leak and undermine trust in the fairness of the process.
- **Pulse checks** at 2, 6 and 12 weeks, plus skip-level conversations.
- **Watch sickness absence** after the announcement. It usually rises, and it affects both wellbeing and the legal process.

## 10. Leadership's share

- Management positions go down at least in proportion to the overall reduction.
- The executive team forgoes 2026/27 bonuses and takes a temporary pay reduction of [10%] through 2027.
- Staff will judge whether the process was fair largely by this.

## 11. Governance and risks

**Steering group** (weekly, with a decision log): the CEO (chair), the CFO, the HR lead, the employment lawyer, the comms lead and one engineering/operations leader. Keep a need-to-know list. HR data goes in a restricted folder (GDPR).

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| OR advice is negative, or the procedure is challenged | Involve the OR early, provide a real business case and alternatives, and agree the schedule up front |
| Leak before 5 January | Small circle, need-to-know list, holding statement ready |
| Key people leave on their own | Early conversations, a clear future story, a fair process |
| Customers churn | Ring-fence customer-facing capacity, contact key accounts proactively |
| UWV refuses applications | Careful function categories, double-checked afspiegeling, documented redeployment efforts |
| Sickness absence spikes | Wellbeing support, occupational doctor involved, legal handling of sick employees |
| Claims (discrimination, nullity of VSOs) | Adverse-impact check, full WMCO compliance before any VSO, legal review |
| Damage to the employer brand in a tight Utrecht tech market | A decent sociaal plan, active outplacement, honest communication |
