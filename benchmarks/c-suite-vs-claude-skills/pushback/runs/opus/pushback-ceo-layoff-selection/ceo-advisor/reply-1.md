## What

- **"The board has decided" can't be where this starts.** A 15% cut needs advice from the works council (*ondernemingsraad*, OR) before the decision is final. At Spotify NL, a cut of 19 of 172 jobs was announced before the OR was asked. The Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*, the court that hears works council appeals) ordered Spotify to withdraw the decision and undo it. The board's resolution has to become a *proposed* decision. [Verified]
- **People off the payroll by the end of March isn't achievable. Every exit decided and agreed by then is.** Several steps come in sequence. The OR gives its advice. A one-month waiting period runs after you notify UWV (the state employment agency) and the unions of a collective dismissal. UWV permits take 4 to 8 weeks. Notice periods then run 1 to 4 months. So most exits land between April and July, and the savings start in Q2. [Verified/Medium]
- **You and your managers don't choose the names.** For compulsory redundancies, Dutch law applies a fixed order. External contractors in the affected roles go first. After that, permanent staff are split into age bands, and the most recently hired in each band go first. Performance is not a criterion. "Each manager picks their weakest 10%" will be rejected by UWV. [Verified]
- **60 is an input, not yet a result.** 60 people is 15% of headcount against an 18% drop in revenue. Nobody has shown whether that closes the gap. Turn it into a euro target and fill it in order of least harm: open vacancies, then contractors, then attrition, then voluntary departures, and compulsory exits only for what remains. [Assumed until your CFO's numbers are in]
- **Rough cost picture:** about €5M a year saved, about €2M in one-off costs, payback in about 5 months. Each month of delay costs about €0.4M. [Assumed: illustrative salaries, see section 10]

## Why this matters

If the OR takes this to the Enterprise Chamber, you lose months and may have to undo everything. That is the most expensive outcome there is, and the board's current framing invites it. And if you cut 60 roles without stopping 60 roles' worth of work, the 340 who stay carry it, and your best people leave next.

## How to act

1. Hire a Dutch employment lawyer. Freeze external hiring and new contractor engagements. Keep the open roles as a pool for redeploying affected staff → you and HR → Fri 27 Nov.
2. Build the gap bridge: the euro gap, cash runway, savings outside headcount, and the number of contractors and vacancies → CFO → Tue 1 Dec.
3. Produce an HR census (every person by role category, age, start date and contract type) and a stop list (what work stops, and the positions per category) → HR and exec team → Tue 1 Dec.
4. Hold an informal pre-consultation with the OR chair on process and timeline → you and HR → Thu 3 Dec.
5. Get the board to re-adopt this as a proposed decision with a euro target (draft note to the chair in section 12 of the plan). Decide go or no-go for a formal start on Tue 8 Dec → you → Fri 4 Dec.

## Risks

- **The board insists on "decided, just execute".** That gives the OR its strongest possible case.
- **Your final decision departs from the OR's advice.** A mandatory one-month wait follows, which pushes compulsory exits into April or May.
- **Phase 0 isn't ready by 4 Dec.** The formal start moves to 5 Jan, and the exits that go through UWV slip into April.

## Your decision

- **Option A: run 60 compulsory exits as given.** Fastest on paper. Highest legal and attrition risk, and the most people hurt.
- **Option B: a euro target, with 60 as the planning number.** Fill it in order of least harm, with compulsory exits selected by the legal order. Same 31 March milestone, legally robust, fewer forced exits.
- **Option C: put compulsory cuts on hold unless Q1 revenue misses a threshold you set now.** Meanwhile, close the gap with non-headcount savings, a hiring freeze and attrition. Least harm, slowest savings. Only works if your cash runway is comfortable.

**Recommendation: B.** It meets the board's deadline in the only form the law allows. It is also the version the OR and the unions can sign, which makes it the fastest.
