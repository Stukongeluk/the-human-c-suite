# Workforce reduction plan: draft v0.1

Confidential, for the CEO, until the board has re-adopted it. Drafted 23 November 2026.
This is not legal advice. Your Dutch employment lawyer needs to sign off every legal step below before you act on it.

**Confidence tags:** [Verified] = checked against the named source. [Medium] = established rule or a reasonable inference, not yet checked against your data or re-checked today. [Assumed] = a placeholder until you put your own numbers in.

---

## 1. Bottom line

You can have 60 role reductions decided, communicated and legally closed (signed agreements or UWV permits) by 31 March 2027. You cannot have 60 people off the payroll by 31 March. And you must not run this as a decision the board has already taken. Present it as a proposed decision with a euro target. Fill that target from the least harmful sources first. For compulsory redundancies, Dutch law decides who is selected, not you and not your managers.

---

## 2. Three changes to the board's framing (do these first)

| The board said | Change it to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "We have decided" | "Proposed decision (*voorgenomen besluit*), subject to works council advice" | A 15% cut needs the works council (*ondernemingsraad*, OR) to give advice under art. 25 WOR before the decision is final. At Spotify NL, a cut of 19 of 172 jobs (11%) was announced before the OR was asked. The Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*) ordered Spotify to withdraw the decision and undo its effects. [Verified: Loyens & Loeff] |
| "Cut 60 jobs" | "Save €X a year by date Y. 60 roles is the planning number and can move either way" | 60 people is 15% of headcount. Revenue fell 18%. Whether 60 closes the gap, falls short or goes too far depends on your cost structure, margin target and cash, and nobody has shown that yet. [Assumed until the CFO's gap bridge is done] |
| "By the end of March" | "By 31 March, every decision is final and every affected person's exit is agreed or permitted. Payroll savings start in Q2" | The OR advice, the one-month collective dismissal waiting period, the UWV procedure and notice periods (1 to 4 months) put most actual exits between April and July. [Verified/Medium, see section 5] |

## 3. Three paths (choose one by Friday 4 December)

| | A. Execute 60 compulsory exits as given | B. Euro target, filled in order of least harm (recommended) | C. Defer compulsory exits until a Q1 trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| What | 60 compulsory redundancies via UWV permits or agreements (VSOs, explained in section 5) | Order: unfilled vacancies, then external contractors, then attrition, then voluntary departures, then compulsory exits for whatever remains | Close the gap with non-people costs, a hiring freeze and attrition. Do compulsory cuts only if Q1 revenue misses a threshold set now |
| Upside | Simple to explain. Matches the board's number exactly | Same 31 March milestone. Fewer forced exits. A version the OR and unions can sign, which is also the fastest | Least harm. Keeps capacity for a recovery |
| Downside | Most people hurt. Highest legal risk if run as "decided". Takes no account of contractors, who legally go first anyway | Needs two weeks of real analysis now. The final compulsory number is only known in January | Slowest savings. Second-guesses the board. Only works if cash runway is comfortable |
| Reversibility | Low. Rehiring costs recruitment fees plus months of ramp-up | Medium. Vacancies and contractor spend can come back first if revenue recovers | High |
| Second-order effects | Remaining staff read it as arbitrary. Recent hires leave (see section 6) | Remaining staff see that the order was fair and explained | If the trigger fires, you run the process anyway, three months later |

**Recommendation: B.** Two exceptions. If the gap bridge shows cash runway under 12 months, speed matters more and A's timing comes back into play, still with the framing fixes from section 2. If the bridge shows the revenue drop is temporary and cash covers it, C becomes viable.

## 4. Phase 0: validate the number (23 November to 4 December)

**4.1 Gap bridge (CFO, by Tuesday 1 December)**
- Revenue for the last 12 months against the year before, and why it fell: lost customers, churn, pricing, the market, or one big account.
- The cost base, split into: employees, external contractors (freelancers, agency and seconded staff), cloud and hosting, software licences, office, marketing, other.
- The board's actual target: margin, EBITDA or cash. Months of cash runway at the current burn.
- Result: the euro gap per year, and the date by which it has to be closed.

**4.2 Non-people levers, each with a euro amount and a date**
Cloud and SaaS spend, office space, marketing programmes, travel and events, 2027 variable pay, and leadership pay. A visible cut at the top is a small saving, but it is a real signal when you ask 60 people to leave.

**4.3 Fill the target in this order**
1. Open vacancies you will not fill. Keep a few open as a pool for redeploying affected staff (required, see section 5).
2. External contractors, freelancers, agency and seconded staff in the affected roles. In any category where positions disappear, the law makes these people go first. [Verified: UWV]
3. Attrition between now and the exit dates, with no backfill. Use your own last-12-months figure, because attrition drops in a downturn.
4. Voluntary departures under the social plan. You keep the right to refuse someone with critical skills.
5. Compulsory redundancies for whatever remains, selected by *afspiegeling* (section 6).

Illustration only, not your data [Assumed]: 8 vacancies + 12 contractor roles + 10 voluntary departures could cut the compulsory number from 60 to about 30. Contractors usually cost more per head than employees, so measure each step in euros, not heads.

**4.4 The stop list (exec team, by Tuesday 1 December)**
Every role removed maps to work that stops or shrinks: a product line, a market, an internal project, a service level. Without this, 340 people carry the work of 400, and your strongest people leave next. The stop list is also the case the OR and UWV will ask for: why these positions disappear.

**4.5 HR census (HR, by Tuesday 1 December)**
List everyone, including external contractors, with: function and level, function category, age, start date, contract type and end date, location, and protected status (sick, pregnant, on parental leave, OR member).

**Go/no-go on Friday 4 December.** If the board re-adopts the proposal and the case is ready, the formal start is Tuesday 8 December. If not, the formal start moves to Tuesday 5 January. The decisions and agreements still mostly land by the end of March, but the exits that go through UWV slip into April or May.

## 5. The legal route in the Netherlands

- **Works council advice (art. 25 WOR).** A 15% reduction clearly requires advice; the Spotify NL case is the precedent. [Verified: Loyens & Loeff] The law sets no fixed advice period. Plan for 5 to 7 weeks including the holidays, and agree the timeline with the OR up front. [Verified that there is no fixed term: Dirkzwager. The duration is Medium] If your final decision departs from the OR's advice, you must wait a month before carrying it out, and the OR can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber. [Verified: Dirkzwager] UWV refuses dismissal applications filed before the OR has advised, or during that one-month wait. [Medium: Dirkzwager 2016, citing UWV rules] The OR can bring in an expert at the company's cost. [Medium] I have assumed you have an OR, which is required at your size. [Assumed]
- **Collective dismissal notification (WMCO, the Dutch collective dismissal act).** It applies to 20 or more dismissals within one UWV region in three months. Utrecht falls in the Flevoland and Utrecht region, and exits by mutual agreement count towards the 20. [Verified: UWV] Notify UWV and every union with members in the company. The one-month waiting period starts only once UWV confirms your notification is complete. No contract may end during that month, not even by agreement, unless the unions agree to waive it. [Verified: UWV, CMS January 2026] Individual conversations are allowed during the month, but they must not lead to concrete termination agreements. [Verified: CMS]
- **Two exit routes.**
  - *Mutual termination agreement (vaststellingsovereenkomst, VSO).* This is voluntary for the employee, who gets a 14-day statutory cooling-off period after signing. The end date has to respect the notice period, or the start of their unemployment benefit (WW) is delayed. [Medium]
  - *UWV dismissal permit (ontslagvergunning)* for people who don't sign. In practice it takes 4 to 8 weeks: the employee has two weeks to respond, and there may be a second round. [Medium: Dirkzwager, an older source] You can deduct the procedure time from the notice period, but at least one month of notice must remain. [Verified: UWV] You can ask UWV once per employee to pause the procedure for up to 14 days to negotiate an agreement. [Verified: UWV]
- **Notice periods:** 1, 2, 3 or 4 months for service of under 5, 5 to 10, 10 to 15, and 15 or more years. Check whether any contracts say otherwise. [Medium: art. 7:672 Civil Code (BW), not re-checked today]
- **Statutory severance (transitievergoeding):** one third of a monthly salary per year of service, with the monthly salary including holiday allowance and fixed components. The 2026 cap is €102,000, or the annual salary if that is higher. [Verified cap: Rendement. Formula: art. 7:673 BW] The 2027 cap applies to exits in 2027, so confirm it in January.
- **Redeployment (herplaatsing):** before granting a permit, UWV checks that you had no suitable vacancy for the person, with training if needed, within the notice period. [Medium: art. 7:669 BW] This is why vacancies stay open as a redeployment pool.
- **Protected employees:** people who are sick, pregnant or on maternity leave, and OR members have dismissal protection. Handle each case individually with the lawyer. [Medium]
- **Collective labour agreement (cao):** I have assumed you don't have one. [Assumed] If you do and it sets up a dismissal committee, that committee replaces UWV and can use a different selection order. [Verified: UWV]

## 6. Selection: who is affected

**What you decide:** which work stops, and so how many positions disappear in each function category. You must back this with the stop list and the numbers.

**What you don't decide: the names.** For compulsory exits, the UWV order applies (*afspiegelingsbeginsel*, the "reflection principle"). [Verified: UWV]
1. Group the roles into categories of interchangeable functions within each location. Interchangeable means similar in content, knowledge and skills, level and pay.
2. In each category where positions disappear, people go in this order: external workers (seconded, agency, freelance, loaned from elsewhere), then employees past state pension (AOW) age, then zero-hours workers, then fixed-term contracts ending within 26 weeks, then permanent staff.
3. Permanent staff are split into five age bands: 15 to 25, 25 to 35, 35 to 45, 45 to 55, and 55 plus. Each band gives up positions in proportion to its size, so the age profile stays the same. Within each band, the most recently hired go first.

*Example:* a category of 50 backend engineers loses 10 positions. The age bands hold 20, 15, 10 and 5 people, so they lose 4, 3, 2 and 1 respectively, starting with the most recent hires in each band.

The exceptions are narrow: documented indispensable knowledge, workers whose health limits their capacity, and protected statuses. [Verified: UWV]

**The performance trap.** Performance is not a selection criterion. [Verified: it is not among UWV's criteria] Three consequences:
- Don't ask managers for "their weakest 10%". UWV will reject that list. And if you use VSOs to remove them instead, it reads as a pretext, which raises the price and the legal risk.
- Last-in-first-out within each age band will hit your most recent hires, often the people you brought in for scarce skills. Test the categories now. Where a recent hire's skills really are indispensable, use the documented exception, sparingly.
- Handle genuine performance problems separately, through the normal improvement process, not inside this reorganisation.

**Voluntary departures and *plaatsmakers*.** A voluntary scheme lets people choose to leave. A *plaatsmaker* is a volunteer from the same category and age band who takes the place of a selected colleague. [Medium: common in Dutch social plans, confirm with the lawyer] Write into the scheme that you can refuse volunteers with critical skills.

**Fairness check before anything is final.** Run the outcome against gender, part-time status, nationality, parental leave and sick leave. Check that no category was drawn to catch particular individuals.

## 7. Social plan (sociaal plan): proposed contents to negotiate with the unions and the OR

- Severance: the statutory transitievergoeding as the floor, plus a top-up factor (a budget line for you and the CFO to set).
- Full notice periods respected, with the option of paid release from work after handover.
- Outplacement and job-to-job support: a budget per person (€3,000 to €5,000 [Assumed]), paid time for job applications, and first access to internal vacancies.
- A training budget.
- An early-leaver bonus: part of the saved wages if someone leaves early for a new job.
- The voluntary scheme and the plaatsmaker rule.
- A hardship committee for cases the rules don't fit.
- Practical support: references, keeping their laptop, an alumni network, and a job fair with Utrecht tech employers.

If the unions agree to the plan, you can usually also get their written statement waiving the WMCO waiting month. [Verified that the waiver exists: UWV]

## 9. Communication plan

**Principles:** you tell people yourself, in person. The board, OR, staff and customers all hear the same story. Nobody finds out from a rumour or a locked account. No names on day one. Tell the people who are not affected as early as you can: naming the affected categories on day one means most of the 400 know by lunchtime that they are safe.

**Day one (Tuesday 8 December):**
- 08:00 Final briefing with the exec team.
- 09:00 The OR's executive committee receives the advice request. The WMCO notification is filed. The unions are informed by email and phone.
- 10:30 All-hands, in person and streamed. You cover: what happened to revenue, what we are stopping, the number of positions per category, how selection works (by law, not by managers), the timeline, the support available, and exactly what happens next and when. A written FAQ goes out at the same time.
- 11:30 Manager briefings, with talking points.
- Afternoon: account owners call key customers. A holding statement is ready for the press and LinkedIn.
- From then on: daily HR drop-in hours, and a weekly written update until the process is finished.

**Individual conversations (after the final decision):** the manager and HR together, in person. The news comes clearly in the first minute, with a letter in hand. A follow-up conversation within two working days. No immediate access shut-off unless the role makes it a security necessity.

**The people who stay:** a re-planned roadmap the following week, a workload check, and "this is the only round" only if you can actually back it.

## 11. Stress test

- **Weakest point:** the number 60 doesn't come from a calculated gap yet.
- **Missing perspective:** why revenue fell 18%. If the cause is sales or churn, cutting delivery or customer success deepens the drop. If it is one lost account or a declining product, the stop list is where the answer is.
- **If we get it wrong:** cut too deep and you rehire 15 to 20 people in 2028, paying recruitment fees and 3 to 6 months of ramp-up each, while losing revenue in the gap [Assumed]. Cut too shallow and you run a second round, and the people who stay stop believing the first one.
- **Proceed?** Yes, once the gap bridge and the stop list exist (4 December).
