## €6m is a gross annual run-rate, not what 2027 delivers

€6.0m ÷ 80 = €75k fully loaded per head. That assumes a full year of savings, no work coming back and no exit costs. With realistic Dutch and Belgian timing and costs, the scenarios look like this:

| € m | Base | Downside | Upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline (gross, annual) | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 |
| Net annual run-rate (after leakage) | 4.6 | 4.2 | 4.9 |
| 2027 in-year net savings | 2.5 | 1.5 | 3.1 |
| One-off costs (severance, notice, outplacement, fees, retention) | (3.6) | (4.3) | (3.0) |
| Dis-synergy (replacing extra regretted leavers) | (0.2) | (0.4) | (0.1) |
| **2027 net impact** | **(1.3)** | **(3.1)** | **≈ 0** |
| 2028 full-year net savings | 4.7 | 4.3 | 5.0 |
| Payback (months of net run-rate) | 9 | 12 | 7 |

In the base case, 2027 ends about €1.3m worse off, not €6m better. Cumulative cash turns positive around April 2028.

There's also a marginal effect. In 2027, each exit costs about what it saves: the net effect is roughly −€1k per FTE even before fixed costs. So adding more redundancies won't close a 2027 gap.

## Where the €6.0m goes (base case)

- **Leakage, −€1.4m.** In warehouse and operations roles the work stays. Unless volume falls or a specific process or automation change removes it, it comes back as agency staff and overtime. In NL you generally also have to release agency and other flex workers in the same functions before permanent staff. And if you refill a role within 26 weeks of a UWV dismissal, you have to offer it back to the dismissed employee. So "cut permanent staff, backfill with agency" doesn't work legally or financially.
- **Phasing, −€2.1m.**
  - NL: works council advice first, plus a one-month suspension if you depart from that advice. Then a WMCO notification to UWV and the unions, with a one-month waiting period. Then UWV procedures or mutual terminations, and notice periods.
  - BE: 25 exits count as a collective dismissal if the Belgian entity has 100–299 staff, because the threshold there is 10%. That means the Renault procedure and a 30-day waiting period, which can be extended to 60.
  - Realistic contract-end dates are May–June 2027, and only if consultation starts in December.
- **One-offs, −€3.6m (€45k per FTE).**
  - NL: the transitievergoeding is one third of a month's pay per year of service, capped at €102k in 2026. The base case adds a 1.5× sociaal plan uplift on top.
  - BE: pay in lieu of notice of 24–30 weeks of fully-loaded pay for 7–9 years' service.
  - Plus about €1.0m for fees, works council costs, retention and transition.

**Accounting point for the pack:** if you announce before 31 December, the restructuring provision (IAS 37 / RJ 252) may fall in FY2026. FY2027 would then show +€2.3m. That changes the presentation, not the economics, so show the board both years.

## What last year's restructuring changes

1. **Track record.** The board will ask what last year's round actually delivered: savings realised, headcount regrowth, agency spend. The *Last Year* tab is set up for that. If it fell short, the board will discount this plan. Last year's actual realisation rate is also your best evidence for the leakage assumption.
2. **Sociaal plan.** If last year's plan is still in force, it probably applies here and sets the NL severance cost. That's the biggest single swing in the one-offs.
3. **Baseline.** Check that last year's savings aren't already in the 2027 budget, so nothing is counted twice.
4. **Second-round effects.** A second round within two years raises:
   - regretted attrition;
   - sickness absence (staff on sick leave in NL can't be dismissed, so their exits slip);
   - union resistance.

   All three are scenario levers in the model.

## The board paper itself

In both countries the works councils have to be consulted while the decision can still be influenced (NL: art. 25 WOR; BE: Renault phase 1). Word the board resolution as an *intention*, subject to consultation, not as a final decision on 80 roles. Otherwise you hand the works councils and unions grounds for a procedural challenge.

Suggested wording, base case, to update once real data is in: *"The programme reduces the annual cost base by €4.6m net from 2028 (€6.0m gross). In 2027 it delivers €2.5m of savings against €3.6m of one-off costs, with cash payback expected in Q2 2028."*

## What's in the workbook

- **Inputs:** on-costs, pay indexation, severance rules, fixed costs, scenario levers and the threshold tests.

- **Calc / Phasing:** the per-cohort calculations, plus monthly 2027 savings and cash for any scenario.
- **Alternatives:** the same 80 FTE through a hiring freeze and natural attrition. That gives about +€0.8m in 2027 and a similar run-rate, but it takes about two years to get there and you don't control where the reductions fall. A hybrid (freeze, voluntary scheme, compulsory exits only for the remaining gap) sits in between.
