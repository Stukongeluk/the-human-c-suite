The costs now appear in the reconciliation, which covers the point I raised about the gross page. Four things to get right before it goes in the pack:

1. **"Below the line" only exists in the adjusted measure.** IFRS and Dutch GAAP have no extraordinary items, so in the statutory accounts the costs stay in reported EBITDA. The board pack needs both figures with the reconciliation between them, as the tab shows.
2. **Call it "restructuring costs", not "one-off".** This is the second year running. Two consecutive years of add-backs is exactly what auditors, lenders and any future buyer's quality-of-earnings review will challenge. I've put a memo line for last year's add-back next to this year's, so the board sees the pattern from you rather than from someone else. Fill in Last Year!C7.
   - If you're listed, ESMA's guidelines on alternative performance measures say costs that affected past periods and will affect future ones, with restructuring as the standard example, should rarely be called non-recurring.
   - If you have bank debt, check the covenant definition. Restructuring add-backs are often capped.
   - If you report under IFRS and use adjusted EBITDA externally, IFRS 18 applies from 2027. It requires such measures to be disclosed and reconciled in the notes.
3. **Only genuine restructuring costs go below the line: €3.4m in the base case.** That covers severance, notice in lieu, outplacement, advisers, works council costs and external programme costs.
   - Retention bonuses are a judgement call because they pay staff who stay. Treat them the way you did last year.
   - Agency and overtime backfill, transition productivity loss and replacement hiring stay in adjusted EBITDA. That's where definitions usually get stretched, and where auditors look first.
4. **Adjusted EBITDA won't show the €6.0m.** It improves by about €2.1m in 2027 and €4.7m a year from 2028. If the board reads the gross page as the adjusted EBITDA uplift, a €3.9m gap will show up in the 2027 actuals. Put the adjusted figure on the gross page, or at least in the same section.
