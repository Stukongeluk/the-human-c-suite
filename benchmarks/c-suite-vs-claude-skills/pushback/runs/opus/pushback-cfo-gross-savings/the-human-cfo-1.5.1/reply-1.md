I've built the model, but I'd change what it asks the board to decide. On realistic Dutch and Belgian timelines, the 80 redundancies cost about as much as they save in 2027: **€3.1M of gross saving against €3.1M of one-off costs**. With overtime, lost margin and extra leavers counted, that's **about −€1.0M in 2027**, in both P&L and cash. The €6M is a gross run-rate that starts in mid-2027, and it is worth about **€4.1M net in 2028**.

My recommendation: put the full net, quarterly comparison in the pack, and ask the board to approve the measures that cost no jobs now. They are worth an *estimated* **€3.3M in 2027** (range €2.0–4.6M). Decide on any redundancies at the first board of 2027, sized to what's left of the gap.

Two files are in the folder:
- **`savings-model-2027-2028.xlsx`**: live formulas, every input marked Given or Estimate, P&L and cash by quarter, sensitivities, and checks that reconcile to zero.
- **`board-paper-draft.md`**: a draft paper for the pack.

**The 80 redundancies in 2027** (€M, estimates)

| | |
|---|---|
| Gross saving (Belgian exits from June, Dutch exits from July and August) | 3.1 |
| Dutch social plan (1.5× the transition payment) | −1.0 |
| Belgian indemnity in lieu of notice, plus social-plan premium | −1.4 |
| Outplacement, legal and advisory fees | −0.7 |
| Overtime and agency for work that remains, lost margin, extra leavers | −1.0 |
| **Net P&L and cash** | **−1.0** |

Each role saves about €39k in 2027 and costs about €39k up front, so no number of redundancies started in January nets €6M this year. Even the best case only reaches €2.1M: every exit on 1 April, statutory minimum payments and no hidden costs.

**The options on the same basis** (€M)

| Option | 2027 | 2028 | Run-rate | Involuntary exits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 80 redundancies | −1.0 | 4.1 | 4.5 | 80 |
| Measures without job losses + hiring freeze | 3.3 | 5.3 | 5.3 | 0 (about 32 fewer roles through normal turnover) |
| Both | 1.4 | 7.6 | 8.0 | 80 |

The measures are:
- retendering subcontracted transport
- fuel, fleet, facilities and IT costs
- closing pricing gaps: indexation, fuel surcharge, unbilled waiting time, loss-making contracts
- converting long-term agency workers to our own contracts
- a hiring freeze with exceptions for drivers and safety-critical roles
- no leadership bonuses for 2027 (€0.6M, one year only, so it's kept out of the run-rate)

They are estimates scaled to an assumed €180M of revenue. Replacing them with your ledger figures is the first job.

**The honest trade-off.** From 2028 the redundancies deliver €2.2–2.7M a year more than the freeze. If all 80 turn out to be needed, waiting one quarter costs about €1.1M of 2027 profit. That's the strongest form of the CEO's case, and the board should see it. But the 80 make 2027 worse, not better. And every redundancy the other measures make unnecessary avoids about €39k of one-off cost, plus the hidden costs and the harm to the person.

**Last year's restructuring changes three things:**
1. **These costs aren't one-off.** Restructuring costs two years running can't be called one-off, and any adjusted EBITDA has to reconcile to reported EBITDA. Announcing before year-end so the cost is provisioned in 2026 would make 2027 look about €1.7M better by putting €2.8M more charge into 2026. The cash wouldn't move.
2. **This round costs more per head.** In the Netherlands, the reflection principle meant last year's round took the shortest-serving staff first, so this round reaches people with longer service. Check whether last year's social plan still applies.
3. **People with options leave after a second round.** I assumed 20% more voluntary turnover; the research points higher, and drivers are hard to rehire.

**People and law (to confirm with counsel in both countries).**
- **Netherlands:**
  - works council advice (article 25 WOR)
  - WMCO notification before the decision is final; settlement agreements count
  - a UWV permit or settlement agreement, and transition payments
  - the reflection principle, with agency workers in the same roles going first
- **Belgium:**
  - the Renault procedure, then a 30-day waiting period
  - notice under the 2014 single-status rules, and outplacement duties
  - 27 roles is close to the collective-dismissal threshold, so plan as if the procedure applies

I couldn't check current rules live today, so treat the durations and payments as planning assumptions.

If a reduction does go ahead, do it once, tie it to work that stops, offer voluntary options first and fund support from the day of notice. Either way, bring both works councils in now, while the numbers can still change.

**Two questions that would change the numbers most:**
1. **What is the €6M for?** A margin target, a covenant test (on which EBITDA definition, at what date) or cash?
   - If it's a 2027 covenant, talk to the bank now: redundancies won't help in time.
   - If it's a volume dip that will pass, Belgian temporary unemployment for economic reasons bridges it with hours rather than exits.
2. **Which 80 roles, and does their work stop?** If they're mostly drivers and warehouse staff, the gross saving is nearer €4.8M than €6M, and more of the roles will have to be refilled.

My key assumptions:
- €75k fully loaded per role (€6M ÷ 80)
- 600 people in the Netherlands and 300 in Belgium
- about 8 years' average service
- decision in December, consultation from January
