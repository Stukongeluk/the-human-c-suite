# Cost programme 2027: what 80 redundancies would really save, and the alternatives

**Board paper, draft for the December 2026 meeting** · Prepared by the CFO · Status: draft. Every figure marked *estimate* still has to be replaced with payroll, HRIS, ledger and contract data. The legal points must be confirmed by employment counsel in the Netherlands and Belgium. Model: `savings-model-2027-2028.xlsx`, with live formulas, every input labelled and all checks reconciling to zero.

All amounts are in € millions unless stated, pre-tax, in constant 2027 prices. Savings are positive and costs negative.

---

## 1. Recommendation

The proposal is 80 redundancies to save €6M in 2027. On realistic Dutch and Belgian timelines it doesn't do that. In 2027 the 80 roles cost about as much as they save: **€3.1M gross saving against €3.1M of one-off costs**. Once the work that still has to be done, lost margin and extra leavers are counted, the result is **about −€1.0M in 2027**, in both P&L and cash. The €6M is a gross annual run-rate that starts in mid-2027. Net, it is worth **about €4.1M in 2028 and €4.5M a year after that** (*estimates*).

We recommend that the board:

1. **Approve now** the measures that cost no jobs, worth an *estimated* €2.5M in 2027 and €3.5M a year from 2028: retendering subcontracted transport, fuel and fleet, facilities and IT, closing pricing gaps, and converting long-term agency workers to our own contracts. Each gets a named owner, a date and a measure.
2. **Approve a hiring freeze with exceptions** for drivers and safety-critical roles, with work redesigned or stopped so the people who stay are not overloaded: an *estimated* €0.8M in 2027 and €1.8M in 2028, with about 32 fewer roles through normal turnover.
3. **Leaders first:** pay no leadership bonuses for 2027 (€0.6M, a one-year measure, kept out of every run-rate). The leadership team states plainly that the result is its responsibility.
4. **Not decide on redundancies at this meeting.** Commission a six-week fact base, bring both works councils in at the problem stage, and decide at the first board of 2027 whether a reduction is still needed. If it is, size it to what is left of the gap and tie it to work that stops.

Together, items 1 to 3 deliver an *estimated* **€3.3M in 2027** (range €2.0–4.6M) and **€5.3M in 2028** (range €3.0–7.6M), with no involuntary exits. No option on the table delivers €6M in 2027.

**The price of waiting.** If the full 80 turn out to be needed after all, starting one quarter later costs about €1.1M of 2027 P&L and €0.1M of 2028 P&L. That is the strongest argument for deciding now, and the board should weigh it. Against it: the 80 make 2027 worse, not better. And every redundancy the other measures make unnecessary avoids about €39k of one-off cost, the hidden costs and the harm to the person.

## 2. What the €6M has to solve (open question)

The answer depends on what the €6M protects, and that has not been written down yet:

- **A 2027 margin or EBITDA target.** Redundancies cannot deliver it in 2027 (section 3). Pricing and spend can.
- **A covenant test.** Which EBITDA definition applies, at which test date, and whether restructuring costs may be added back, and up to what cap, is set in the facility agreement. If headroom is tight in 2027, the right first step is an early conversation with the bank, backed by a 13-week cash forecast and base and downside cases. Redundancies consume cash in 2027.
- **Cash.** Collect overdue receivables, bill waiting time and extras on time, and time capex. Do not stretch payments to small suppliers or owner-drivers.
- **A structural margin gap for 2028 onwards.** This is where a targeted reduction may be justified, if the measures above don't close it.
- **A temporary volume dip.** Hours bridge a dip better than exits. In Belgium, temporary unemployment for economic reasons can reduce hours without dismissals (check the current conditions). In the Netherlands, the current short-time work scheme covers only calamities. A broader crisis scheme was before parliament in 2026; check whether it has passed.

Differences of €1k are due to rounding.

**Timing (planning assumptions, to be confirmed by counsel).** The board decides in December, and nothing is announced before the holidays.
- **Netherlands (53 roles, *estimate*):** works council advice and WMCO notification start in January, with advice by about March. Settlement agreements are signed in April and people work out two months' notice, so most leave by the end of June. Those who leave via a UWV permit go about a month later.
- **Belgium (27 roles, *estimate*):** the Renault procedure starts in January, with information and consultation running to about the end of March. Then comes notification and a 30-day waiting period. People leave at the end of May with an indemnity in lieu of notice. With worked notice instead, they would leave around November, and the 2027 result would be about the same.

**One-off costs, 2027 (*estimates*):**
- Dutch social plan at 1.5 times the statutory transition payment (about 8 years' service): €1.0M.
- Belgian indemnity in lieu of 27 weeks: €1.1M.
- Belgian social-plan premium of 8 weeks: €0.3M.
- Outplacement and transition support at €5k a person: €0.4M.
- Legal and advisory fees in two countries: €0.3M.

**Hidden costs (*estimates*, deliberately moderate):**
- Overtime and agency cover for work that remains: 15% of the gross saving in 2027 and 5% after.
- €1M a year of revenue lost through service disruption, at a 30% contribution margin.
- 15% of roles refilled from 2028, at half a year's pay each to recruit and ramp up. Gallup puts replacement at one-half to two times annual salary ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-problem-costs-businesses.aspx)).
- 20% more voluntary turnover in the 12 months after the announcement. In a US study, a 1% workforce reduction was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, *Academy of Management Journal*, 2008), so our figure is conservative.

**Two facts that hold whatever the estimates:**
- Each role saves about **€38.6k in 2027** and costs about **€38.5k up front**. On these timelines, no number of redundancies started in January nets €6M in 2027.
- Even the best case for 2027 delivers only **€2.1M**: every exit on 1 April, statutory minimum payments only, no hidden costs at all.

**Break-even:**
- About €13.5M of lost revenue in 2028, or about 140 extra leavers, would wipe out the 2028 net saving.
- About €10M of lost revenue over 2027–2028 would wipe out the cumulative saving.

## 4. Options side by side, on the same basis (€M)

The measures without job losses are available in every option, so the board's real choice is what the redundancies add on top of them.

| 1. Do nothing | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0 | – |
| 2. 80 redundancies (CEO proposal) | −1.0 | −1.0 | 4.1 | 4.5 | 80 | No |
| 3. Measures without job loss + hiring freeze (recommended now) | 3.3 | 2.7 | 5.3 | 5.3 | 0 (about 32 fewer roles through turnover) | Yes, quarter by quarter |
| 4. Measures without job loss + 80 redundancies | 1.4 | 0.8 | 7.6 | 8.0 | 80 | No |

- **Who carries the cost.** In option 2, 80 people and their families, and the colleagues who absorb the work. In option 3, leaders (through their bonus), suppliers, customers on loss-making contracts, and staff workload if the freeze is badly managed.
- **What the redundancies add.** From 2028, option 4 delivers about €2.2–2.7M a year more than option 3. In 2027 it delivers €1.8M less.
- **Option 3 cash.** The 2027 cash figure is lower than P&L because unpaid 2027 bonuses would have been paid in Q1 2028.

Drivers are also a shortage occupation in both countries, which pushes up the refilling rate.

## 7. What last year's restructuring means for this one

- **The costs are not one-off.** Restructuring charges in two consecutive years are a recurring cost of running the business. They should not be labelled non-recurring. Any adjusted EBITDA in the pack must reconcile line by line to reported EBITDA, and management bonus definitions should not exclude them. US rules for listed companies forbid that label in exactly this situation (SEC Regulation S-K Item 10(e)); we should apply the same test.
- **Provision timing moves profit, not cash.** Announcing a detailed plan before year-end could allow the termination costs to be provisioned in 2026. That would show about +€1.7M in 2027 by putting another €2.8M of restructuring charge into 2026. The 2027 cash would not change. The auditors decide whether the recognition criteria are met. We don't recommend shaping the announcement date around it, or announcing just before the holidays.
- **This round will cost more per person.** In the Netherlands the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) selects the shortest-serving staff first within each age band. Last year's round therefore reached them, and this one reaches longer service, with higher transition payments and longer notice. Check whether last year's social plan still applies to 2027.
- **A second round in two years is when people with options leave**, and trust in the next announcement falls. One well-prepared round does far less damage than repeated small ones. Before a second round, we owe the board an answer to what in our planning made it necessary again.
- **Data we need from last year:** savings delivered against plan, roles refilled since, voluntary turnover in the 12 months after, actual costs against budget, and the time from announcement to exit in each country. These are the best calibration this model can have.

## 8. People, process and law (planning points; counsel to confirm)

This is not legal advice. Rules depend on collective agreements and company size, and several changed in 2025–2026.

**Netherlands**
- **Works council (article 25 WOR):** its advice must be sought while it can still change the decision. If the decision departs from the advice, implementation waits one month.
- **Collective dismissal (WMCO):** dismissing 20 or more people in one UWV work area within three months means notifying UWV and the unions before the decision is final. Employer-initiated settlement agreements count towards the 20. UWV generally waits a month before handling applications unless the unions confirm they have been consulted.
- **Dismissal route and payments:** a UWV permit or a settlement agreement; the statutory transition payment; employer notice of one to four months by service.
- **Selection:** the reflection principle applies, and agency workers in the same roles go first.
- **Collective agreement:** check the applicable CAO and any social plan still in force.

**Belgium**
- **Works council:** mandatory from 100 employees.
- **Renault procedure:** collective dismissals follow it. Phase 1 is information and consultation on the intention, including ways to avoid or reduce dismissals. Phase 2 is notification to the regional employment service, followed by a 30-day waiting period that can be extended to 60.
- **Thresholds:** they depend on the size of the Belgian unit. At 27 roles we are close to them, so we plan as if the procedure applies and do not structure the timing to avoid it.
- **Notice and support:** notice follows the 2014 single-status rules, with longer periods for pre-2014 white-collar service. Outplacement duties apply, plus an employment cell where required. Protected employees and the duty to give reasons for dismissal also apply.

**If any reduction goes ahead after the fact base:**
- **Scope:** one round, tied to work that stops, voluntary options first, and internal vacancies offered first to people in affected roles.
- **Terms:** above statutory minimums, priority for rehiring, and support from the day of notice rather than after the last day.
- **How people hear it:** from their own manager in person, never by mass email or a blocked login, and never just before the holidays. The people who stay hear the same day what happened and why.

## 9. What we will track, and when we review

- **Monthly:** cash and EBITDA against plan, lever by lever, with each owner reporting.
- **Quarterly, next to the financials:**
  - regretted attrition and driver vacancy days
  - overtime and agency hours, and sickness absence
  - on-time delivery, damage claims and customer complaints
  - an engagement pulse
- **Decision gate:** the first board of 2027, on the fact base and the first months of delivery.
