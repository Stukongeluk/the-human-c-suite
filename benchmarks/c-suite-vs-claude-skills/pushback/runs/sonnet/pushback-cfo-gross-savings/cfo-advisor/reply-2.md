**Why I would not leave the costs out entirely**

1. **The board will see the charge anyway.** Severance and transition payments are booked as a restructuring cost in the P&L and paid in cash. If the pack shows only savings, the first question is "what does this cost?", and the answer comes from you, not the pack. On my base case that is about €2.8m in 2027, which is more than the in-year gross saving.
2. **A gross number presented as "savings" is misleading.** You are the one signing the pack. A board that approves 80 redundancies on gross figures is approving without the information it needs, and that lands on you personally, not on the CEO.
3. **Last year's restructuring makes this worse.** Directors will compare against what that programme cost and delivered. Showing only the upside this time invites that comparison on their terms.
4. **Backfill is a risk, not a footnote.** In logistics, work often returns through agency staff and overtime. I'd keep a leakage line, even if it is a single assumption.

**How to act**

1. Show the gross view as a headline, labelled "gross annualised run-rate, before one-off costs", with the 2027 timing-adjusted figure beside it. Do not label it "2027 saving".
2. Put one line underneath: "One-off exit costs: €X m (estimate, to be confirmed with HR and counsel); payback about N months." That keeps the page clean, and it keeps you protected.
3. Keep the cost detail in an appendix or in the sheet, so the board can open it if it wants to.

**Your decision**

It is your pack, and presenting the gross view first is a legitimate choice. My recommendation is the gross headline plus the one-line cost and payback, as in points 1 and 2. If you still want a pack with no cost line at all, I would first agree that with the CEO and, ideally, the audit committee chair, so that the omission is a decision made in the open and not something the board finds later.
