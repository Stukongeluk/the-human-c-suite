- 🟢 The arithmetic. €6m ÷ 80 = €75,000 per head. That only works as a full-year figure if every person costs €75k fully loaded and leaves on 1 January.
- 🟡 Timing. Collective dismissals in both countries run on a statutory clock: employee-representative consultation, and in the Netherlands the UWV route (notice periods up to four months, shortened by the procedure time), and in Belgium the collective-dismissal (Renault) information and consultation procedure plus notice or indemnity. Nobody leaves on 1 January, and notice time is paid time. Have counsel confirm the Dutch and Belgian thresholds and the clock for your headcounts and your split between the two countries.
- 🔴 Loaded cost and one-offs. A logistics workforce is mostly operational roles, so €55-65k fully loaded is more realistic than €75k, unless the 80 are mainly senior indirect roles. One-off costs of €25-50k per head cover transition payment, any social plan, notice buy-outs (Belgian notice for long-tenure staff can be expensive), outplacement and legal. All of this is assumed.
- 🔴 Leakage. In logistics the work usually does not disappear with the person. Agency cover, overtime and service failures eat part of the saving. I used 5-30%.

**Why this matters for the board**

1. **You are comparing a gross run-rate to a P&L number.** €6m can be defended as a gross annualised figure. It cannot be defended as a 2027 saving. The board will see the difference as soon as someone asks about the restructuring charge.
2. **Hitting €6m in 2027 would take a very different plan.** On base assumptions you would need about 217 exits (24% of the workforce) for €6m in-year after leakage, and that is before one-off costs. Alternatively, with 80 exits you would need a loaded cost of roughly €176k per head. Neither is credible.
3. **The restructuring last year raises the risk.** Before you commit, check:
   - How much of the 80 is already counted in the 2027 budget baseline (vacancies, planned attrition, last year's carry-over savings)? Double counting is the most common way these numbers fall apart.
   - What did last year's programme actually deliver against its business case? If it under-delivered, the board will use that as the reference point, and you should show the comparison yourself.
   - Are the easy, low-risk roles already gone? A second round cuts into capacity, and a service failure with a large customer can cost more than the saving.
   - Does the one-off cost belong in FY2026? If the plan is approved and announced before year-end, the provision may have to be booked in 2026 rather than 2027. Check with your auditor.
4. **There are cheaper routes to the same target.** Logistics has high natural turnover. Attrition combined with a hiring freeze, agency and overtime reduction, and non-people cost can close part of the gap with no severance and less service risk. The CEO should see these side by side with redundancies, not only the redundancy case.
