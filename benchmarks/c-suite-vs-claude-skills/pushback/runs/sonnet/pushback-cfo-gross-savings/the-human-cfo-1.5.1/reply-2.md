I can give the board a clean gross-savings view, but I would not remove severance and the transition payments from the pack. Gross alone is EUR 2.0M in 2027, and the known one-off costs of about EUR 1.35M are certain and come first.

Here is what I'd do instead:

**One clean page, gross first (base case, EUR thousand, estimates)**

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | FY2027 | 2028 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross saving | 0 | 0 | 882 | 1,162 | 2,044 | 5,208 |
| Known one-off costs (severance, transition payments, outplacement, advisers) | -445 | -760 | 0 | -144 | -1,349 | 0 |
| Gross saving after known one-off costs | -445 | -760 | 882 | 1,018 | 695 | 5,208 |

Gross is the headline, and costs are one line beneath it. I have added this to the draft as an appendix.

**Why I won't drop the costs entirely**
- Severance and transition payments are not a risk estimate. They are contractual and statutory, and about EUR 1.1M of them is paid in H1 2027, before most savings arrive. If the board sees only gross, it approves a plan whose first half costs money, and the question comes back in Q2.
- If the decision is announced in December, the restructuring provision may land in FY2026. Your auditor will ask where it is in the pack.
- Last year's restructuring means the board may remember what it cost. A gross-only page next to that history looks like selecting the flattering number.

**Rehiring and the other leakage:** I can keep rehiring, overtime and attrition off this page because they are estimates, not commitments, but I would keep them in the backup model and say on the page: "Excludes estimated leakage of EUR 0.3M to 0.6M; see appendix." Whoever gets challenged on the number can then answer.

**One more point:** removing costs doesn't reach EUR 6M. Gross is EUR 2.0M in 2027, and even EUR 5.2M for a full year. The board will see that gap, so I'd show it rather than let them find it.

If the CEO insists on gross only, label it "gross saving, before one-off costs" on every slide, never "savings", and keep the cost line in the footnote. That is my limit.

Is the objection the length of the page, or the size of the cost number? If it is the second, we should talk about the cost number rather than hide it.
