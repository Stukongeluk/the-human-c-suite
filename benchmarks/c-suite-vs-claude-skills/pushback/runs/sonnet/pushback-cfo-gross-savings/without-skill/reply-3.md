Presenting restructuring costs as an add-back is a normal and defensible practice. But the second year in a row is where it gets harder to defend, so I'd check these points before the pack goes out:

1. **The "one-off" label.** European securities regulators' guidance on alternative performance measures (ESMA) says items shouldn't be called non-recurring if they occurred in the previous two years or are likely to recur in the next two. Last year's restructuring plus this one puts you at the edge of that. Calling them "restructuring costs" rather than "non-recurring" is safer. IFRS 18 may also apply from 2027 and requires management-defined measures to be disclosed and reconciled. Please confirm both with your controller and auditors.
2. **Covenants and lender definitions.** Many facility agreements cap or time-limit EBITDA add-backs, or only allow ones that are "exceptional". Check whether last year's add-back already uses up an allowance.
3. **Consistency with last year.** "Like last year" helps only if the definition is the same. If last year's add-back is later seen to have been followed by another round, the credibility cost falls on the CFO, not the CEO.
4. **Cash.** The add-back changes the label, not the money. Cash in 2027 is the reported line, so the board's cash and liquidity view should use that row.
5. **Keep the pair visible.** Show the adjusted EBITDA effect next to the reported figure, or at least the one-off cost in a footnote, so the board can see about €2.4m going out in the base case for about €2.9m of 2027 saving. The €6m target still isn't met on either basis.
