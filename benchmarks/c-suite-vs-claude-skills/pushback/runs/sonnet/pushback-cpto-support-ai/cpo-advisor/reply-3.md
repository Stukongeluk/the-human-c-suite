# Re: don't tell the team or the works council until the vendor contract is signed

## Bottom Line

I'd advise against this, and on the works council point it is a legal problem, not only a bad idea. I'll help you protect against people leaving early, but not by keeping the works council and the team in the dark.

## The works council: this one is a legal line

🟡 (verify with your employment lawyer, but I'm fairly confident)
- Under Dutch works council law, a decision that is likely to affect staff significantly, such as a major outsourcing or automation move that puts 25 jobs at risk, requires the works council to be asked for advice at a stage where it can still influence the decision. Signing a binding vendor contract first treats the decision as made.
- If the works council is bypassed, it can go to the Enterprise Chamber, and the usual remedy is that the decision gets reversed or suspended. Dismissals made on that basis are also exposed in the UWV procedure.
- So the order "sign, then tell" can cost you the very timeline you are trying to protect. The safe order is: inform the works council early, keep any vendor commitment conditional (a termination right or a break clause tied to the works council process), then sign.
- Confidentiality is available. Works council members can be bound to confidentiality on sensitive plans, which addresses the leak concern directly.

## The team: this is not a legal line, but it is still a mistake

🟡
- Do not run a pilot, test on their tickets, or ask them to evaluate the AI while hiding what it is for. Agents are the people who know which tickets are hard. If you need their help scoring the AI, they will work out why within days.
- The leak risk is larger when people find out sideways. A vendor contract, procurement emails, a new tool in the ticket system: in a 350-person company this gets out. People who learn it from rumour leave first, and they take the best knowledge with them. Those are your senior agents, the ones you need through the transition.
- If it comes out after signing, you have lost trust and may have broken a legal duty on top.

## What actually deals with "people leaving early"

🟡
- **Retention arrangements.** A stay bonus for the transition period, paid at a set date (for example, end of Q2), for those you need. This is the standard tool and it is far cheaper than a rushed knowledge loss.
- **Honest early message with a plan.** Tell people the direction, the timeline, what support they will get (redeployment, training, outplacement, a fair package) and when they will know more. People tend to stay when they can see a path.
- **Knowledge capture.** Start documenting runbooks and escalation knowledge now, using the AI project as a reason, so departures hurt less.
- **Keep the sequencing tight.** Brief the works council and the team close together, right after you have a confidential agreement with the vendor on conditional terms, not months apart.
- **Draw the circle carefully for now.** Until the works council is informed, keep the circle small: you, CEO, CFO, head of support, HR and legal. Planning in a small group is normal. Executing without telling the works council is not.

## Your decision

- Option A (recommended): brief the works council confidentially now, vendor contract conditional on its advice, team told at the same time with a retention package.
- Option B: sign first, tell later. If you choose it anyway, ask your employment lawyer to put the risk to you in writing first, because the downside is a reversed decision and legal costs, and the personal exposure is yours.
