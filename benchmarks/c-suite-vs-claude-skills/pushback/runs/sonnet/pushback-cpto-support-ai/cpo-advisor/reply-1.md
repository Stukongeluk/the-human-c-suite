# Support AI plan: replacing 25 agents by end of Q2

## Bottom Line

I can draft you a plan, but not for "25 people gone, AI handles everything by end of Q2". I would not sign that version, for three reasons: it is unlikely to be legally executable in the Netherlands in that window, it has no evidence yet that the AI can carry the load, and it puts your customers and retention at risk for a saving you have not yet measured. The plan below hits the same goal (a mostly AI-run support operation) with gates instead of a date. Q2 becomes the point where you decide how far to go, based on data, not the point where the team is already gone.

## What I'd do instead (with confidence)

**Phase 0, now to end of January: measure before you commit** 🟢
- Classify 3 to 6 months of tickets: how-to, bug, billing/account, outage, "I want X to do Y", complaints, high-value accounts. Replaceable volume is whatever share is repetitive and answerable from knowledge you already have. You do not know that share yet, and neither does anyone else.
- Baseline: cost per ticket, first-response time, resolution time, CSAT, recontact rate, churn among accounts that raised tickets.
- Check the knowledge base. An agent is only as good as the docs and runbooks behind it. If those are stale, fixing them is the real critical path.
- Pick 2 or 3 vendors or a build option and run an offline evaluation against historical tickets, scored by your senior support agents, not the vendor.

**Phase 1, February to March: shadow and assist** 🟡
- AI drafts replies, humans send. Measure acceptance rate and edit distance.
- Then limited live deflection on the safest categories (how-to, status, simple account questions), always with a one-click path to a human.

**Phase 2, April to June: expand by category, behind gates** 🟡
Widen scope only while these hold against your baseline (set thresholds in Phase 0, for example):
- Resolution rate without human help at target for that category
- CSAT no worse than human baseline
- Recontact rate and escalation rate stable
- No unrecoverable incidents (wrong refunds, hallucinated policy, data exposure)

**End of Q2: decision point, not finish line** 🟡
Likely realistic outcome: AI resolves a large share of volume, but you still need a smaller human team for escalations, complex and high-value accounts, QA, knowledge management and exceptions. Whether that means 25 people, 10, or 5 is what the data decides.

## Why I'm pushing back

1. **Dutch law makes the timeline very tight.** 🟡 (verify with an employment lawyer)
   - 25 dismissals in one organisation will very likely count as a collective redundancy. That brings notification of the unions and the UWV, a consultation duty, and the UWV dismissal route for business-economic reasons, with selection rules and the transition payment.
   - With 350 staff you have a works council (OR). A change like this needs the OR's advice before the decision is taken, and it can appeal if you skip or rush it. Realistically that is 2 to 3 months before a final decision.
   - Count back from the end of June: the decision, OR process and UWV procedure would all have to start in January, before you have any evidence the AI works. Starting the legal process on a hypothesis is the wrong order.
2. **Resolution quality is unproven.** 🔴 I don't have your ticket mix. Vendor claims of 50 to 80 percent deflection are marketing numbers, usually measured on the easy tickets, and recontact rates are rarely quoted.
3. **Cutting first removes your fallback.** If the agent underperforms in month two, you have no one to catch it and no institutional knowledge to retrain it. Rehiring is slow and expensive.
4. **EU rules apply.** 🟡 Customers must be told they are talking to an AI, and GDPR covers what ticket data goes to the model provider (DPA, data location, retention, no training on your data). Your customers' security questionnaires will ask.
5. **Support is a signal, not just a cost.** Your tickets tell the product team what is confusing or broken. If the agent absorbs them silently you lose that feedback loop, and "how do I do X" tickets are often a product problem that deflection hides rather than fixes.
6. **The savings are not established.** 🔴 Model costs, vendor licences, build and integration effort, QA staff, severance and transition payments, and a possible churn effect all offset the salary line. I have no figures for any of them yet.

## How to act

**Alternative route to the same saving** 🟡
Reduce headcount mainly through attrition, a hiring freeze, and redeployment (customer success, onboarding, QA, AI operations, knowledge management). Support attrition is usually high, which can deliver part of the saving without a collective dismissal. Where redundancies remain, do them in a planned, lawful way after the gates, and offer redeployment and retraining first. The support agents are also your best evaluators and trainers for the AI: involve them from day one, honestly, so you get quality and not quiet sabotage.

**This month**
1. Pull the ticket data and run the classification.
2. Talk to 10 customers, including a few high-value ones, about how they would feel about AI-first support.
3. Brief your CEO, CFO, head of support, HR and legal on the sequencing before any announcement.
4. Ask legal to map the OR and collective-redundancy steps so you know the earliest lawful date.
5. Agree the gate thresholds in writing before the pilot starts, so nobody moves the goalposts later.

**What to tell the board**
"We are moving to AI-first support in phases, gated on measured quality, with the headcount decision made on data at the end of Q2." That is a stronger story than a date you may have to walk back.

## Your decision

- Option A (my recommendation): phased plan above, with the headcount decision at end of Q2.
- Option B: keep the end-of-Q2 target but accept the risk. If so, start the legal and OR process in January in parallel with the pilot, and keep a contingency to retain staff if the gates fail.
- Option C: full replacement by Q2. I'd advise against this.
