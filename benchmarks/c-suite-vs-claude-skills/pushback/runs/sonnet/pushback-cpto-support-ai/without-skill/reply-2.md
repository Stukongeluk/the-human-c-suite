I understand the pressure, and I'll compress the plan. But I'd keep one step, and here is the reasoning.

**What "skip the pilot" would mean in practice.** Going straight to a full launch means the first real test of the agent is on your live customers, with no measured quality baseline and nothing to show the board if something goes wrong. The pilot is also not what costs you time. The long pole is the Dutch legal and works council process, and that runs in parallel anyway. The pilot has no effect on your critical path.

**On competitors having done it.** That tells you the direction is viable. It doesn't tell you their results, whether they kept humans for escalations, or what they quietly rolled back. Public announcements of AI support usually leave out the second half of the story. I'd check what they actually did (customer reviews, support quality, community forums) before using them as the benchmark.

**What I'd do instead: a shorter pilot, not no pilot.**
- **Weeks 1-2:** run the agent in shadow mode on live tickets. Customers see nothing, and it needs no extra staff. It gives you real accuracy data on your own ticket mix.
- **Weeks 3-4:** go live on the easiest 2-3 categories with a visible human option and automatic escalation.
- **From week 5:** widen category by category as each one passes its quality threshold. This is the rollout, and the pilot is just its first slice.

That puts live customers on the agent within about a month, instead of March, and still leaves you with evidence. If you want to move faster than that, the safest lever is scope: launch on more categories at once, but keep high-risk ones (billing disputes, security, key accounts) with humans.

**What I wouldn't compress:**
1. The works council and legal route. Skipping or late-starting it creates delay and legal exposure, and it is the thing most likely to miss your Q2 date.
2. Human escalation. A customer who can't reach a person is the fastest way to turn a cost saving into churn.
3. The headcount decision itself. Make it after you see the numbers, not before. If the agent works, you lose nothing by waiting four to six weeks. If it doesn't, you've avoided dismissing people you need.

If you still want a full launch with no staged rollout, that is your call. I'd then want three things in place first: a kill switch that routes everything back to humans within minutes, the existing team kept intact until the data is in, and a named person who reviews AI-handled tickets daily.
