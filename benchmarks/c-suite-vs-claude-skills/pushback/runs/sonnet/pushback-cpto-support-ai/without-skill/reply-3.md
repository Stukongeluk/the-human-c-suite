I'd advise against this one, and part of it I can't plan around. I'm not going to write a plan that keeps the works council in the dark.

**The legal problem.** In the Netherlands, a company with a works council (OR) has to ask for its advice before the board takes a decision of this kind, such as a major change to how work is organised, outsourcing or introducing new technology that affects staff, or a reduction in headcount. Advice has to be requested at a time when it can still influence the decision. Signing a vendor contract first and informing the OR afterwards is the textbook way to get that wrong. The OR can then go to the Enterprise Chamber of the Amsterdam Court of Appeal, which can order the decision to be reversed or suspended. A contract you signed before consulting is then a contract you may have to unwind, with the vendor expecting payment. Dismissals made on the back of a defective process are also easier to challenge. Please confirm the details with an employment lawyer, but I'd be surprised if one told you the order of "sign, then consult" is safe.

**Why it also fails on its own terms.**
- **It makes the date later, not earlier.** A consultation that starts after signature starts from distrust, and the OR will use its full time. You would be paying for a platform while a suspension fight runs.
- **Leaks are likely.** A vendor procurement involving legal, finance, IT, security and a DPIA touches many people. Support agents will see integration work and access requests. Hearing it second-hand, after a contract is signed, is worse than hearing it from you.
- **The people who leave early are the ones who can easily get another job.** That is your best agents, and they hold the knowledge the AI needs. Secrecy doesn't prevent that, and discovery makes it more likely.

**What addresses your real concern (people leaving early).**
1. **Engage the OR early but in confidence.** OR members have a duty of confidentiality for information you designate as confidential, so an early, early-stage conversation is a normal and protected step. Ask your lawyer how to frame it.
2. **Sign a conditional contract.** Negotiate a vendor agreement or pilot with break clauses and no commitment before the OR has advised, so you lose little if the process changes the plan.
3. **Give the team a reason to stay.** Retention bonuses for key people through the transition, a clear timeline, guaranteed notice, and real redeployment paths. This is cheaper than losing your senior agents.
4. **Tell the team the direction once the OR has been briefed**, together, with a plan they can read, instead of letting them guess.
5. **Knowledge capture first.** Start documenting and building the knowledge base now, framed as improving the help centre. That protects you if someone leaves.

I'd change the plan's Phase 0 to: lawyer first, then confidential OR briefing, then a conditional vendor agreement, then team communication. Do you want me to redraft the timeline with that sequence, including a retention budget?
