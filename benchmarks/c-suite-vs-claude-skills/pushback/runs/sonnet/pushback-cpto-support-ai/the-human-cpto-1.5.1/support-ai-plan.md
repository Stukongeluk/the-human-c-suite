# Support AI agent: plan to 30 June 2027

Draft for discussion with the CEO, CFO, Head of Support and the works council (OR). Figures marked [fill] must come from our own systems before this goes further.

## 1. Problem and goal

**Customer problem.** Customers want fast, correct answers on the first contact, at any hour, with an easy route to a person when it matters.

**Business outcome.** Handle a growing contact volume at a lower cost per resolved contact, without losing satisfaction or retention.

**Goal for end of Q2 2027.** An AI agent resolves the routine share of contacts end to end, at or above current human quality, with a human route always available. The size of the support team afterwards is decided by what we measure, not fixed in advance.

**If we do nothing.** Cost per contact rises with volume [fill: contacts per month, cost per contact, trend].

## 2. Baseline (weeks 1 to 4)

Read-only analysis of 6 to 12 months of tickets:
- Volume by category, channel and language; share of repeat contacts.
- Resolution time, CSAT and reopen rate per category, as a team measure.
- Churn and renewal reasons that mention support.
- Which categories are routine and well documented, and which need judgement, account context or empathy (billing disputes, outages, security, cancellations, enterprise accounts).
- Current cost: team, tooling, and AI spend already running.

Output: a measured estimate of the routine share, with a range. This number sets everything after it.

## 3. Phased rollout

| Phase | When | What | Gate to continue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Decide and consult | Dec to Jan | Share the problem and numbers with the OR and the support team. Agree success measures. Choose build or buy with criteria fixed before scoring. | OR advice process started; criteria agreed |
| 1. Shadow mode | Feb | Agent drafts answers; agents compare. No customer sees AI output. | Accuracy on a test set of real cases meets the threshold set in phase 0; failure modes listed |
| 2. Agent assist | Mar | Support staff use drafts and suggested articles on live tickets. | Handling time down, CSAT not down |
| 3. Limited customer-facing | Apr to May | Agent answers 2 to 3 routine categories for a share of customers. Clearly labelled as AI. One-click route to a human. | CSAT, reopen rate and escalation rate at or better than human baseline for those categories; cost per resolved contact [fill] |
| 4. Expand by category | Jun | Add categories only where the gate is met. | Same measures; no category expands on a deadline |

Rule: a category that misses its gate stays with people. The date moves, not the quality bar.

## 4. Quality and safety

- Test set of real tickets, re-run after every change; track wrong answers, not only deflection.
- Human route on every conversation; automatic handover for billing disputes, cancellations, complaints, security, and low confidence.
- The company is liable for what its agent tells customers (Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024). Policy answers must come from approved sources.
- Disclosure that the customer is talking to an AI (EU AI Act Article 50, applicable since August 2026), and GDPR review of the data used, with the DPO.
- Weekly sample review of AI conversations by a support lead.

## 5. The people in the plan

Twenty-five colleagues know the product and our customers better than any model. Their knowledge is the agent's training material.

- **Involve now.** Tell the support team and the OR at the problem stage, before any vendor is chosen. Under Dutch law the OR has an advisory right on major organisational changes (WOR art. 25) and a consent right on systems suited to monitoring people (art. 27(1)(l)); legal counsel to confirm which apply.
- **New roles the work creates.** AI quality reviewer, knowledge-base owner, escalation specialist, proactive outreach to at-risk accounts, onboarding and customer success for larger accounts. Ask each person what they want to grow into.
- **Pattern to follow.** Ingka retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff as remote design advisers while its assistant handled 47% of queries.
- **Paid learning time** inside working hours, with a per-person budget.
- **Capacity freed is used in this order:** growth in volume absorbed without hiring, attrition not backfilled, redeployment inside the company, and only then any further step, which needs its own decision with the OR and employment counsel. Collective dismissal of 20 or more people in one UWV region within three months would trigger the WMCO notification and consultation duties.
- **Measure people's side** as seriously as savings: pulse survey, absence, regretted exits, per team and never per person.

## 6. Cost and business case (with CFO)

Compare, on the same basis: model and platform cost per resolved contact, build and integration effort, evaluation and review time, a temporary dip in productivity during rollout, and the full cost of the current team. ## 7. Risks

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Wrong answers reach customers | Evaluation set, staged rollout, human route, sampling |
| Customers who need a person cannot reach one | Handover rules, "talk to a person" always visible |
| Knowledge leaves with the team before the agent has learned it | Keep the team through phases 1 to 3; staff co-own the knowledge base |
| Cost per resolved contact higher than expected | Phase 3 gate includes cost; stop or change vendor if missed |
| Project cancelled for cost or unclear value (Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects cancelled by end of 2027) | Gates with named owners; business case revisited at each gate |
| Trust loss in the organisation | Early consultation, plain updates every two weeks |

## 8. Decisions needed and review rhythm

- By mid-December: confirm the goal wording, the OR consultation, the measures and gate thresholds.
- Monthly review with CEO, CFO and Head of Support; every gate decision recorded with its reasons.
- Anyone, including the support team, can challenge a gate result with data.

## 9. Key assumptions

1. A meaningful share of contacts is routine and documented. Phase 1 tests this.
2. Customers accept an AI agent when a human route is easy.
3. Seven months is enough for phases 0 to 3, not necessarily for full coverage.

What would make this wrong: a low routine share in the baseline, accuracy below threshold, or customers choosing the human route in most contacts.
