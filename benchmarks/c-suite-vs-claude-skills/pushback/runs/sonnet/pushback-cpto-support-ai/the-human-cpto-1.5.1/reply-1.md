I'd plan this as an AI agent that takes over the routine share of support by the end of Q2, gate by gate, and not as a plan that removes 25 people by a date. I can't yet tell you what share the agent can take, and neither can anyone else until we've measured it on our own tickets. The draft plan is in `support-ai-plan.md`, with a phase table and a gate for each phase.

**Why I changed the goal**
- **The risk is quality, not technology.** Klarna said in 2024 that its AI did the work of 700 agents. In 2025 its CEO said cost had been weighted too heavily and service had suffered, and it started hiring humans again.
- **Pilots often fail on value.** Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027, because of cost, unclear value or weak risk controls.
- **We are liable for what the agent says.** Air Canada lost a case in 2024 over its chatbot's answer.
- **The people hold the knowledge the agent needs.** Cut the team first and you lose the training material and the safety net together.

**What the plan does**
1. Weeks 1 to 4: measure the routine share of tickets, with quality per category.
2. February: the agent runs in shadow mode. March: agents use its drafts. April to May: it answers 2 to 3 routine categories for customers, labelled as AI, with an easy route to a person. June: expand only where the gate is met.
3. If a category misses its gate, the date moves and the quality bar stays.
4. The 25 people are in the plan: quality review, knowledge ownership, escalations, proactive work for at-risk accounts. Ingka retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff as design advisers while its assistant took 47% of queries. Freed capacity goes first to growth absorbed without hiring, then attrition, then redeployment. Anything beyond that is a separate decision with the OR and employment counsel.

**Dutch points to start now** (counsel to confirm)
- With 350 staff you have an OR. It has an advisory right on a reorganisation like this (art. 25 WOR) and a consent right on systems that can monitor staff (art. 27). Bring it in before you choose a vendor.
- Ending 20 or more jobs in one UWV region within three months triggers the collective dismissal notification and consultation.
- Telling customers they're talking to an AI has been required since August 2026 (AI Act Art. 50).

**What I don't know.** Contact volume, cost per contact, CSAT, the routine share and what the AI platform costs per resolved contact. Those are marked [fill] in the document. Is the driver cost, quality, or growth you can't staff for? The answer changes the target.

Before I'd put this in front of the CEO and CFO: what am I missing, and what would make this wrong? If the baseline shows 70% routine tickets and the pilot holds quality, the plan can move faster than I've drawn it, and I'd update it.
