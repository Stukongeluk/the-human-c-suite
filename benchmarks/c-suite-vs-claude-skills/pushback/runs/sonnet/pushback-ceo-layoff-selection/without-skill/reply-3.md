I can't give you a lowest-scorers-first formula built on performance ratings and Copilot usage, because it would put the whole plan at risk. Here is why in four lines, and then the selection method that will survive UWV and gets you to 60.

## Why that formula fails

1. **It is not a permitted method.** For a business-economic dismissal, UWV expects the afspiegelingsbeginsel (exchangeable functions, age cohorts, shortest tenure first). Selection on performance scores is a departure from it. UWV will refuse the permits for anyone selected that way, and you lose the timeline. A performance-based selection is only possible by agreement, for example in a union-agreed social plan, and even then it is hard to defend.
2. **Copilot usage does not measure what you need.** It measures tool activity, not output or value. It will systematically favour people in roles that suit the tool, and penalise people who work in ways it does not capture (senior engineers, architects, people in support or sales). Used on a person-by-person basis, it is easy to attack as arbitrary.
3. **It needs the works council's consent.** Using systems that monitor or assess individual employees requires OR consent (art. 27 WOR), and processing usage data for a purpose other than the one it was collected for runs into the AVG (GDPR) purpose-limitation rules. If you use it without consent, the OR can block the selection and employees can claim the data was unlawfully used.
4. **Last year's ratings are weak evidence.** Unless the ratings were consistently applied, calibrated across managers and documented as a basis for dismissal, they will be read as manager discretion. That invites discrimination claims (age, gender, part-time status, sickness) once the list is analysed by cohort, and the list will be analysed.

Using this formula risks the dismissals being refused or reversed, with back pay and wasted months. The method below does the job.

## Selection criteria that will hold

**Step 1: define the roles that go.** For each department, state in writing which positions are redundant and why. Group exchangeable functions (same level, comparable duties, realistically interchangeable). Every group gets a number of cuts. The total is 60.

**Step 2: calculate the cuts per age cohort.** Within each group, count employees per cohort: 15-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55+.

   Cuts for cohort c in group g = (cuts for group g) x (employees in cohort c / employees in group g), rounded so the cohort cuts sum to the group total (largest remainder method).

**Step 3: rank within each cohort by tenure.** Shortest service with the company leaves first. Use the contractual start date, including any period at a group entity or predecessor that counts as continuous employment. Ties: draw lots, documented and witnessed.

**Step 4: apply the key-staff exemption (this is where performance and skills legitimately come in).** You may exempt up to 10% of positions in a group, so the plan can keep people who are essential to the business going forward. Rules:
- Exemption criteria are written down before you look at names: unique or scarce skills the business needs after the cut (for example a certified specialist in a system only one person runs), a customer-critical role with no substitute, or knowledge that cannot be replaced within the notice period.
- Performance can be one supporting factor, but only with evidence that is consistent and documented (calibrated ratings, concrete outcomes). Do not use Copilot usage.
- Each exemption has a one-paragraph written justification. The exempted person is replaced in the cut by the next person in the cohort ranking.
- Note that using the exemption on the maximum (10%) draws the most scrutiny. Use fewer than the cap where you can.

**Step 5: check the list.** Before it goes to the OR: check the outcome by age, gender, part-time status, nationality and sickness or protected status against the group, and fix anything that looks skewed. Remove protected employees from the standard track (your lawyer decides how they are handled) and replace them in the ranking.

## What to do with performance and Copilot data

- Use performance data where it is legal and useful: to inform the key-staff exemption, to decide which roles are truly critical, and later to decide who gets retention offers.
- If the board wants performance to carry more weight, the route is a social plan agreed with the unions and the OR that sets a different selection method. Raise it in the first week of the consultation, not after. This needs lawyer drafting, and I would expect UWV to look closely at it.
- Keep the Copilot data out of individual dismissal decisions altogether. If you want to use it at all, use it at team or role level, for example to understand which workflows have changed, as part of the business case for which roles are redundant.
