# Plan: reducing headcount by 60 (400 to 340) by end of March, Utrecht

Here is a working plan. Three things come first, because they decide whether this plan holds up.

## Three things to settle before anything else

1. **The board decision has to be conditional, not final.** With a works council (OR), you are legally required to ask for its advice (art. 25 WOR) before a final decision is taken. If the board has already "decided" and the OR is only informed afterwards, the OR can challenge it, and a court can undo it. Reword the board resolution to "intention to reduce by up to ~60 FTE, subject to OR advice and legal procedure". If you have no OR, check whether you are required to have one (50+ employees) and whether a personnel representation exists.
2. **End of March is very tight.** Today is 23 November. A collective dismissal (20+ in three months in one UWV region) means a notification to unions and UWV, then a waiting period of at least 30 days before UWV will even start its assessment, plus OR advice (typically 4 to 6 weeks), then UWV's assessment (often 4 to 8 weeks), then notice periods (1 to 4 months, though UWV credits part of it). Realistically, positions can be abolished by March, but many employees will still be on payroll into Q2 unless you settle by agreement. Decide now whether "by end of March" means decisions made, exits completed, or cost run-rate reached. I assume the first two are the board's real aim and plan for the fast route (voluntary and settlement agreements, below).
3. **Check the number before you commit to it.** Revenue down 18% and 60 of 400 jobs (15%) are not obviously linked. Ask finance for one page showing: the cost base, what 60 exits save per month net of severance (transition payments, any social plan, outplacement, lost productivity), and the break-even date. If the saving only arrives in Q3 and the revenue problem is partly cyclical or pipeline-driven, a smaller first wave plus a hiring freeze, contractor reductions and shorter hours may close the gap with less capability loss. Do this alongside the plan, not instead of it, but do not skip it: a 60-person cut is hard to reverse and software companies routinely lose their best people in the process.

I am not a lawyer. Have a Dutch employment lawyer review the legal steps below before you act.

## Legal framework (Netherlands, redundancy for business-economic reasons)

- **Permit route:** dismissal for business-economic reasons needs UWV permission (not the kantonrechter). You must show the redundancy is genuine (revenue drop, financials), that redeployment within the company was not possible, and that selection followed the rules.
- **Collective dismissal notification (WMCO):** 20 or more dismissals within 3 months in one UWV region: notify the unions and UWV, and consult the unions. Employer must report before filing the permit request.
- **Selection: afspiegelingsbeginsel.** Within each group of exchangeable roles (similar function and level), employees are split into age cohorts (15-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55+), and within each cohort the shortest-serving employees go first. This means you largely do not choose individuals. Up to 10% of positions can be exempted as key staff where truly essential, but this needs justification and UWV may challenge it. Consequence: age and tenure, not performance, drive the list.
- **Transition payment (transitievergoeding):** one third of monthly salary (incl. holiday allowance, 13th month, etc.) per year of service, from day one of employment. Budget for it, plus any social plan.
- **Protected categories:** employees who are ill, pregnant, on parental leave, or OR members cannot be dismissed on the standard route (prohibition of dismissal, special procedures). Map these early.
- **Settlement agreements (vaststellingsovereenkomst):** the fast alternative. Employee has a 14-day reflection period. Be careful: settlement offers must not undermine the afspiegeling for the rest.
- **Works council advice and union talks:** a social plan (financial and non-financial compensation) is usually negotiated with unions and OR. Having one makes everything faster and calmer.

## Workstreams and timeline

**Week 0 to 1 (now to early December): prepare**
- Appoint a small steering group: CEO, CFO, HR lead, external employment lawyer. Keep it tight and confidential.
- Finance: the business case (see point 3 above), by department.
- Redefine the board resolution as conditional (point 1).
- HR/legal: build the position inventory: which roles are redundant and why (workload, product, customers), not "which people".
- Map exchangeable role groups, age cohorts, tenure, and protected categories. Do not map to named individuals until the roles are decided.
- Draft the comms plan and Q&A, and line up outplacement and a legal helpline.

**Week 1 to 2: OR and unions**
- Present the intention and business case to the OR (formal request for advice) and notify unions per WMCO. Share the financials openly. Offer to negotiate a social plan.
- OR has time to ask questions, get their own adviser (at company cost), and give advice.

**Week 2 to 6: consultation and social plan**
- Negotiate the social plan (transition payment top-up, outplacement, training budget, early retirement or bridging for older staff, priority rehiring).
- In parallel: voluntary route. Open a window where employees can apply for a voluntary departure package. Warning: your best people are the most employable and the most likely to take it. Cap by role and approve deliberately.
- Freeze external hiring and renew no contractors in affected areas. Redeploy internally where there are real vacancies (UWV will ask).

**Week 6 to 8 (late January): OR advice and final decision**
- Receive OR advice, respond in writing, take the final decision. If advice is negative, wait the required period (one month) before implementing, or risk the decision being challenged.
- Finalise the selection per afspiegelingsbeginsel; document it carefully.

**Week 8 to 12 (February to March): execute**
- Individual conversations (manager plus HR), same day, in person where possible. Hand over the settlement offer. Employees who sign in the 14-day period leave by agreed dates.
- For those who do not agree: file the UWV permit request (after the 30-day wait from the WMCO notification), with full documentation.
- Support for those leaving and for those staying (see below).

**After March:** UWV decisions and notice periods run out in Q2. Budget for that tail.

## Estimating the cost (fill in with actuals)

For each leaver: transition payment (1/3 month per year of service) + notice period salary if applicable + social plan extras + outplacement (typically 5 to 10 thousand euro) + legal costs. Software salaries in Utrecht and long tenures mean 60 exits can easily cost several million euro one-off. Check this against the run-rate saving before the OR meeting.

## Communication

- **Order matters:** steering group, then OR and unions, then managers (briefed with a script and the criteria), then all staff in one meeting, with a written FAQ, and then individual conversations the same day. Do not let rumours run ahead; you will have a few days at most.
- **Say what is true:** revenue is down 18%, the board has concluded costs must come down, here is how roles were selected, here is what support exists. Do not promise "no more cuts" unless you can keep that promise.
- **Managers need training.** Most of the damage in layoffs comes from clumsy conversations.
- **Customers and key partners:** prepare a short line for sales and support so service continuity is not at risk.

## Taking care of those who stay

Survivor effects are real: productivity dips, and your best remaining people start interviewing elsewhere. Within a week of the announcement: CEO open Q&A, clear priorities (what stops, what continues), and a retention conversation with the 15 to 20 people the business can least afford to lose. Watch regretted attrition monthly through Q2.

## Risks to watch

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Final decision taken before OR advice | Conditional board resolution; lawyer sign-off |
| Selection challenged (not afspiegeling-compliant) | Documented role groups, cohorts, tenure; limited key-staff exemptions |
| Best people leave voluntarily | Approve departures per role; retention talks |
| Timeline slips beyond March | Plan fast route (settlements) and tell the board about the tail |
| Cost higher than saving suggests | One-page business case, updated weekly |
| Illness, pregnancy or protected status missed | Early HR mapping; lawyer check |
| Leaks before announcement | Small circle; compressed communication window |
