# Plan: 60 roles, decisions complete by end of March

Understood. The number is fixed and this is the plan for exactly that. I have dropped the alternatives. Two things are not options but plan constraints, because they decide whether the 60 actually land: the legal sequence below, and the fact that you select roles, not individuals.

## What is fixed by Dutch law (this sets the plan)

- 60 dismissals within three months in one work district is a collective redundancy. Order is fixed: works council (OR) advice request, then union notification and consultation, then UWV permit applications. Announcing to staff before the OR has the request is the most common way to get dismissals reversed.
- Individuals are selected by *afspiegelingsbeginsel*: you decide which roles go, then within each group of interchangeable roles, selection follows five age cohorts (15-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55+) and length of service. You cannot pick by performance.
- Realistic outcome: all decisions, notices and filings done by end of March. Last exits fall April to July, because notice periods run after the UWV decision. (Confirm durations with your employment lawyer; UWV timing varies.)

## Selection steps

1. HR lists every role in scope and groups interchangeable roles per department.
2. Within each group, set the number of cuts proportional to group size.
3. Split each group into the five age cohorts; cut proportionally per cohort.
4. Within each cohort, the person with the shortest service goes first.
5. Narrow exceptions: someone with a specific skill the company cannot replace may be kept, but you must document why per person. Keep these to a handful.
6. Lawyer reviews the full model before anything is communicated.

## Announcement day

- Managers briefed the evening before, with a script and the FAQ.
- 09:00 all-hands from you: the reason, the number, the process, the support. No euphemisms.
- Same morning: one-to-one conversations with all affected staff (manager plus HR). Everyone else is told within hours that they are not affected.
- Same day: written FAQ, an HR contact line, outplacement provider available from day one.
- Do not let "at risk" linger for weeks. It is the largest driver of extra attrition among people you want to keep.

## One-off costs to put in the board paper

Transition payments (one-third of monthly salary per year of service, statutory cap), notice pay through the notice period, social plan extras, outplacement, legal fees, and holiday and bonus payouts. Have the CFO model it from the selection output in week 3 so there is no surprise in January.

## After the announcement

- Re-plan the roadmap for the smaller team and say explicitly what stops.
- Handover plans per team before each exit date, so knowledge leaves on notice, not afterwards.
- Retention conversations with critical people within a week of announcement. Plan for extra voluntary attrition among those who stay.
- Report to the board at 30, 60 and 90 days: savings against plan, regretted attrition, delivery and customer metrics.
