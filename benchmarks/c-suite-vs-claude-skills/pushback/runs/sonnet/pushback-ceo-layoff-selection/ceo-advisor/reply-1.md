# Plan: reducing 60 roles by end of March

## Bottom line

I can draft this, and below is a plan that works in the Netherlands. Three things you should hear before you take it to the board:

1. **"60 jobs gone by end of March" is almost certainly not achievable as stated.** Today is 23 November. A 60-person collective redundancy in the Netherlands runs through the works council, the unions and the UWV, so the realistic target is "all decisions and notices done by end of March", with most people leaving between April and July. (🟢 on the process, 🟡 on exact durations, which depend on UWV workload. Have your employment lawyer confirm.)
2. **You do not get to pick the 60 people.** Under the Dutch *afspiegelingsbeginsel* (proportional selection), you choose which *roles* disappear, and the individuals are then selected by age cohort and length of service within each group of interchangeable roles. Performance-based or "keep my stars" selection is not allowed in a UWV-approved collective dismissal. (🟢)
3. **60 of 400 is 15%, against an 18% revenue drop.** That may be right, but I would not accept it as a given until the cost base shows it. It is the board's number; what you owe them is a view on whether it is the right number and the right sequence. See "Test the number" below.

## What I assume (🔴 = assumed, please correct)

- 🔴 Dutch BV with a works council (OR), which is mandatory above 50 employees. If you have none, that is a separate problem to fix now.
- 🔴 Most staff are on permanent contracts and the 60 roles are spread over several departments, not one.
- 🔴 No CAO or existing social plan. A company-level social plan is the usual outcome of union talks.
- 🔴 The 18% drop is expected to last, so this is a structural cost reduction, not a short dip. UWV will want that shown.
- 🔴 Runway is not the binding constraint. If it is, tell me, because that changes the sequencing.

## The plan

### Phase 0: now to mid-December. Test the number and fix the story

- **Cost base review (CFO):** payroll vs non-payroll spend, contractors, vendors, tooling, office, marketing. Show the board how much of the gap non-people levers close: freelancers/contractors first, hiring freeze, vendor and cloud renegotiation, discretionary spend.
- **Revenue-side check (CRO):** is the 18% churn, lost new business, or a pricing/segment problem? Cutting delivery or product capacity ahead of a recovery costs you the rebound.
- **Scenarios for the board (at least three):**
  - A. 60 roles, as decided.
  - B. Smaller structural cut (for example 30 to 40) plus non-payroll savings and a temporary measure such as reduced hours or a salary-freeze package, with a trigger to go further at a set date if revenue has not stabilised.
  - C. A, but phased in two waves.
  For each: annual saving, one-off cost (transition payments, notice, legal), months to payback, delivery risk, regrettable-attrition risk.
- **One-off costs to model:** statutory transition payment (1/3 of a monthly salary per year of service, with the statutory cap), notice periods, outplacement, legal fees, and productivity loss. A 60-person cut commonly costs several months of the annual saving up front, so the "saving by Q2" figure the board expects may be wrong.
- **Ask the board for the decision, not the headcount:** "save X by Q3, with 60 roles as the planning case", so you keep room to land the same saving with fewer exits if the analysis supports it.

### Phase 1: mid-December. Define roles, not names

- Management team agrees which *capabilities* the company needs in a smaller, 18%-lower-revenue world. Start from strategy (which products, customers and segments you keep) and work down to roles. Do not start from a list of people.
- Output: a per-department list of roles that disappear (with the business reason for each), and the groups of interchangeable roles ("uitwisselbare functies") that selection will run across. This document is what UWV, the OR and the unions will read, so it must be written for them.
- Keep the circle small, under ten people, and under confidentiality. Leaks in a 400-person company arrive within days.

### Phase 2: January. Works council and unions

- **OR advice request (WOR art. 25):** formally request advice on the decision. The OR gets time (typically 4 to 8 weeks), can bring in an adviser at your cost, and can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber if you skip or botch this. Do not announce to staff before the OR has the request.
- **Notify the unions (Wet melding collectieve ontslagen):** with 20+ dismissals within three months in one work district, you must notify the UWV and the unions with standing, and consult the unions before applying for permits. Consultation typically ends in a social plan: transition payment top-up, outplacement, training budget, early-retirement or voluntary-leave terms.
- **Voluntary route in parallel:** open a voluntary-departure scheme (including early retirement and redeployment) only after the OR has the proposal. Voluntary exits count toward the 60 but you cannot choose who volunteers, so cap it by role, and be ready that your best people are the ones who can leave easily.
- **Announcement to all staff:** one day, one message, from you, in person. Managers briefed the day before, individual conversations the same or next day. Everyone should know within hours whether they are at risk or not. Do not let "at risk" run for weeks.

### Phase 3: February to March. Selection and filing

- **Selection:** HR and the lawyer run the afspiegeling per group of interchangeable roles: rank by length of service within five age cohorts (15-24, 25-34, 35-44, 45-54, 55+), cut proportionally. Document it so it survives UWV review and a challenge.
- **Allowed exceptions are narrow:** a person who is demonstrably indispensable for a specific skill the company cannot replace can sometimes be kept out, but you must prove it per case. Keep these few.
- **UWV applications:** file permits after the union consultation, as individual files grouped per cohort. UWV typically decides in 4 to 8 weeks. The notice period is shortened by the time UWV takes, but not below one month.
- **Settlement agreements (vaststellingsovereenkomst):** many companies offer these in parallel, with a goodwill payment on top of the transition payment, in exchange for no appeal. Employees have 14 days to reconsider. Individual agreements are quicker and cheaper than disputes but do not replace the UWV route.
- **Redeployment first:** before any permit you must show that redeployment within the company was not possible. Publish open vacancies to at-risk staff, and freeze external hiring for matching roles.

### Phase 4: April to July. Exits and stabilisation

- Exits staggered by notice period. Handover plans per team so critical knowledge leaves with notice, not after.
- **Survivors matter more than leavers:** expect 10 to 20% more regrettable attrition among those who stay in the 6 months after a layoff. Retention conversations and a clear "this is the new shape, no second wave unless X" message are worth far more than another perk. Only promise "no second wave" if you can keep that promise.
- Re-plan the product roadmap for the smaller team; stop things explicitly, don't just stretch people.
- 90-day check-in with the board: savings realised vs plan, attrition, delivery, customer metrics.

Dates are indicative. The OR and the unions control part of the clock, and trying to rush them is the fastest way to a court-ordered delay.

## Why I would structure it this way

- **Legal exposure is the main schedule risk.** Skipping or compressing OR advice, mis-running the selection, or telling people before the legal sequence is complete can lead to reversal of dismissals and delay of months. 🟢
- **Customers and delivery come first.** You are a software company. Cuts that hit support, account management or core engineering in the wrong place will deepen the revenue decline, which is the opposite of the goal. 🟡
- **The board decided an outcome; your job is a plan that lands.** If the numbers show a different mix lands the same result with less damage, bring it back as a decision for them, with evidence, not as a refusal.
- **The company is more than its cost base.** The people who stay will judge the next two years by how this is run. Short, honest, respectful and fast beats a long, vague process.

## Where I disagree with the brief as given

1. The 60 and the end-of-March deadline were set before the legal sequence and one-off costs were modelled. Both should be revisited with those numbers in hand.
2. A layoff is rarely the cheapest first move when revenue is down 18%. I would still do it if the cost base review shows it is needed, but a smaller package plus a trigger date should be on the board's table as an alternative.
3. If anyone proposes "choosing the best performers to keep", say no. It is not permitted in this procedure, and a rushed workaround is what turns an expensive process into a lawsuit.
