I won't write an email that tells people they are losing their jobs, and I'd change the order of the morning. What I can give you is the email that works: the written summary each person gets after they have heard it from their own manager. It is below.

**Why the sequence as planned fails**
- An all-hands at 9 followed by an email at 10 means 60 people learn it from a screen, then wait an hour wondering. Some will hear it from colleagues first. That is the cruelest version and the one people remember and post about.
- A lockout at noon, with no conversation, is how a company ends up in the press. Anyone who finds out their login stopped working has been given the worst possible message.
- In the Netherlands an email cannot end an employment contract. It needs a settlement agreement or UWV permission, and works council advice and the WMCO notification must come first. If any of that is not finished by Friday, sending the email puts the whole plan at risk.
- Friday morning is better than Friday afternoon, but people then spend the weekend alone with it and cannot reach HR, counsel or a coach. A Tuesday or Wednesday gives them support the same day.

**A sequence that does the same job on one morning**
1. **8:00 to 9:30:** each affected person has a private conversation with their own manager, HR present, in person or on a live call. They have been told the day before, in a general way, that the company will share news that day, with no details.
2. **9:45:** all-hands for everyone else, same message from you, including what it means for those who remain. Say plainly that the result is leadership's responsibility.
3. **After each conversation:** the written summary below, sent by HR, so nothing rests on memory.
4. **Access:** stays on until the end of the working day. Laptops are wiped on handover, not before, and people may keep or collect their things with someone beside them. Move to the notice period schedule in the agreement.

**The same-day written summary (draft; counsel to review before it goes out)**

> Subject: Following our conversation today
>
> Dear [name],
>
> Thank you for speaking with [manager] and [HR contact] today. This is a written summary of what we discussed, so you have it to read at your own pace.
>
> **What was decided.** Because of the fall in our revenue, we are reducing the number of roles at the company. Your role, [function], is one of those affected. The reasons and the numbers were shared with the works council, and the selection followed the legal method for collective redundancies, based on function group, age band and length of service. It was not based on your performance, and it is not a judgment of you.
>
> **What happens next.** [Settlement offer details / the UWV process.] Your employment continues until [date], and you will be paid in full until then. You will not be dismissed by this email.
>
> **What we offer.** [Coaching and outplacement from today, training budget of EUR X, paid time for job search, written reference, laptop arrangement.] If you want to talk to someone independently, [confidential support line].
>
> **Your questions.** [HR contact] is available [days, hours]. Please contact the works council if you want to discuss your position: [contact].
>
> Dates to know: [response deadline for the settlement offer, handover date, last working day].
>
> I know this is hard news. I am sorry. Leadership is responsible for this result, not you.
>
> [CEO name]
