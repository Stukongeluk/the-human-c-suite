**Why the date and the saving don't match (my estimates, to be replaced with your finance numbers)**
- 60 roles at about EUR 90k loaded cost is roughly EUR 5.4m a year, but exits land after consultation and one to four months' notice. Call it EUR 2.7m in 2027.
- One-off costs of 3 to 6 months of cost per person are EUR 1.35m to 2.7m. So year one nets roughly zero to EUR 1.35m. If the problem is 2027 cash or a covenant, cuts alone won't fix it.
- 15% of heads is probably only about 10% of total cost, against an 18% revenue drop. Other levers have to carry the rest.

**The legal route sets the pace (Dutch counsel to confirm)**
- At 400 people you have a works council. It must be asked for advice (article 25 WOR) before the decision is final. A board decision announced as done invites an Enterprise Chamber appeal that can suspend the whole plan.
- 20 or more dismissals in one UWV work area within three months means notifying UWV and the unions (WMCO).
- You can't pick who goes. Under the afspiegelingsbeginsel, staff are grouped by interchangeable role, sorted into five age bands, and the shortest-serving go first within each band. Freelancers and agency staff go before that. Performance rankings and activity metrics aren't a basis for selection, and neither is who is on leave or has raised concerns.

**Plan in short (18 weeks)**
1. **By 4 Dec:** finance gap analysis, hiring freeze, spend review, board session. Executive pay and bonus cuts decided, yours first.
2. **By 7 Dec:** works council briefed with the real numbers and formally asked for advice.
3. **Dec to mid Jan:** consultation, a voluntary-exit and internal-vacancy window, and a decision on which products and work stop, so the people who stay don't carry the same load with fewer colleagues.
4. **Mid Jan to Mar:** WMCO notification, selection documented, personal conversations, settlements or UWV applications, notices. Exits run April to June.
5. **Support from the day of notice:** coaching, paid training, outplacement and above-statutory pay where affordable. People hear it from their own manager, never by mass email.

**One thing to say out loud:** the result is leadership's to own. Say so in the first message to staff and the board paper, and make the executive cuts first and visible.

**Options if the gap proves smaller:** non-payroll spend, pricing and retention, stopping low-value work, and freezing hiring and ending freelance contracts with proper notice. If the dip will pass, reduced hours with leaders going first protects skills for the recovery. Whatever remains in January becomes the reduction, sized from the actual gap.
