# 2027 savings: the 80-redundancy proposal and the alternatives, net and by quarter

**Draft for the board pack** · From: CFO · 23 November 2026 · Status: draft for discussion with the CEO before the pack is final

Every figure in this paper is an estimate until it has been replaced with payroll aggregates, ledger data and counsel's advice. The workbook `savings-model-2027.xlsx` holds the same model with live formulas; the yellow cells on its Inputs sheet are where the actuals go. All amounts are in € thousands (€k) unless marked, at 2026 cost levels. Savings are positive and costs are in brackets.

## 1. Summary

1. **80 redundancies do not save €6M in 2027.** The €6M is the full-year cost of 80 roles at an average of €75k fully loaded. With consultation in the Netherlands and Belgium, the first exits fall at the end of May 2027, so the gross saving in 2027 is **€3.2M**. After severance, Belgian notice paid in lieu, outplacement, advisers and the hidden costs, **the 2027 net cash effect is −€1.2M**. If consultation takes three months longer it is −€2.4M.
2. **The cash goes out before the saving comes in.** Cumulative cash bottoms out at −€3.1M at the end of June 2027 and turns positive in May 2028. The lasting net saving from 2029 is about **€4.3M a year**, not €6M, because some work still has to be bought in, some roles come back and some revenue is at risk.
3. **No option delivers €6M net inside 2027.** The option that brings in the most cash in 2027 costs no jobs: renegotiating external spend, repricing contracts and recovering revenue leakage, a hiring freeze with exceptions plus internal moves, and leaders going first. On our estimates it brings in **€2.4M net cash in 2027** (€1.5M in the low case) and €5.0M a year from 2029 (€3.1M in the low case).
4. **Recommendation (Option C).** Start the measures that cost no jobs in December. Add a voluntary exit scheme for 15 roles where the work stops, designed with the works council and the Belgian union delegation. At the end of March 2027, check what has been measured against the target. A targeted reduction follows only if a gap remains, and only in roles where the work stops. In the base case almost no gap remains (€0.2M a year). In the low case the gap is €2.1M a year, which means about 40 roles, decided on measured numbers rather than 80 decided now.
5. **This is our second restructuring in two years.** Its costs must not be labelled one-off or non-recurring. The board should see what last year's round promised and what it delivered before approving another.

## 2. What the €6M is for (to confirm before the board meeting)

The model can't tell the board which option is right until we know what the €6M protects:

- **An EBITDA or margin target for 2027?** Then the 2027 P&L effect is what matters, and Option A makes 2027 worse.
- **Covenant headroom or cash?** Then we need the facility agreement's EBITDA definition, a 13-week cash forecast and an early conversation with the bank. Option A takes cumulative cash down by up to €3.1M (June 2027) before the savings catch up in May 2028.
- **A run-rate the owners expect by the end of 2027?** Then the comparison is run-rate against risk, and the measures that cost no jobs come first because they start sooner.

**Timing behind the table (estimates; counsel to confirm):**
- **December 2026:** the board discusses a proposed decision. Nothing is final.
- **January 2027:** the Dutch works council is asked for its advice and UWV and the unions are notified under the collective dismissal act (WMCO). In Belgium, the information and consultation phase of the collective dismissal procedure (the Renault procedure) opens with the works council.
- **February to March:** the works council gives its advice. In Belgium, consultation and social plan talks run, followed by notification to the regional employment service and a 30-day waiting period.
- **April to May:** Dutch settlement agreements are signed or UWV applications are filed. Belgian notices are given and paid in lieu.
- **May to July:** Dutch employment ends after notice of one to four months, depending on service. 30% of the Dutch roles leave at the end of May, 50% at the end of June and 20% at the end of July. All Belgian roles leave at the end of May.

**What the table means:**
- **One-off restructuring costs: €3,273k**, about €41k per role. Average estimates per person:
  - Netherlands: €18.5k, a social plan at 1.5 times the statutory transition payment, which is about €12.3k at eight years' service.
  - Belgium: €40k of notice paid in lieu (26 weeks) plus €15k of collective dismissal compensation and social plan extras.
  - Everyone: €5k of outplacement and training.
- **The P&L takes the hit in Q1 2027** when the plan is detailed and announced (a provision of €2,973k), if the auditors agree on that timing. If the main features were announced before 31 December 2026, the provision could fall into 2026 instead. That would make 2027 look about €3.0M better on paper without changing a euro of cash, and it would put restructuring charges in two consecutive years. We should not choose the announcement date for that effect.
- **Break-even:** in 2027 there is no saving for lost revenue to wipe out, because the plan is cash-negative before any revenue loss. From 2028 the saving would only disappear if lost revenue reached about €17.8M a year at a 25% contribution margin. The bigger risks are timing, who actually leaves and how many roles come back (section 4).
- **The €75k average needs checking against payroll.** It is well above a typical logistics average, so either the 80 are mostly office, planning and management roles or the €6M is overstated. In the Netherlands, the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) decides who goes within each group of interchangeable jobs. It spreads exits across age bands and, within each band, takes the shortest-serving staff first. The people who leave may therefore cost less than the average for their roles.

How each line is sized (estimates to be validated from the ledger in the next three weeks):
- **External spend:** 3% of an estimated €60M of addressable spend, 2% in the low case. That spend covers subcontracted haulage, fleet and maintenance, energy, IT, insurance, consultants and agency fees, and excludes fuel that is passed through and locked-in leases. The saving builds up over 2027, starting at 10% of the full rate in Q1. We will renegotiate before cancelling, and we won't stretch payment terms for small suppliers.
- **Pricing and revenue leakage:** 1% of an estimated €150M of revenue, 0.5% in the low case. It comes from applying wage and fuel indexation clauses, billing waiting time, extra stops and storage, and repricing or exiting loss-making lanes. 10% of the gain is assumed lost to customers who leave.
- **Hiring freeze with exceptions:** 30 vacancies not refilled in 2027, 20 in the low case, out of an estimated 90 people who leave each year. Driver, warehouse, safety and customer-critical roles are still refilled. People whose work stops move into the vacancies, with training.
- **Leaders first:** a 10% pay cut for the executive team in 2027, and no 2027 bonus for executives and senior managers. This is temporary and excluded from the run-rate. The bonus saving shows in 2027 P&L but in March 2028 cash, when the bonus would have been paid.
- **Voluntary scheme:** 15 people in roles where the work stops, leaving at the end of June 2027. They receive a €25k premium on top of support. 15% of the roles are assumed to be refilled, because good people take packages.

## 6. Recommendation and decision gate

**Option C, with a gate at the end of March 2027.**

| When | What | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| December 2026 | Start the spend programme, contract repricing and the hiring freeze with exceptions. The executive team announces its own 2027 pay cut and bonus decision. | CFO, COO, Commercial Director, CEO |
| December 2026 to January 2027 | Share the problem, these numbers and the options with the Dutch works council and the Belgian works council and union delegation, while the plan can still change | CEO, HR Director |
| February to March 2027 | Agree the voluntary scheme with employee representatives. Counsel confirms the WMCO and Renault thresholds | HR Director, counsel |
| April to May 2027 | Voluntary window open. Exits at the end of June | HR Director |
| End of March 2027 (gate) | Compare the measured run-rate with the target. If a gap remains, propose a targeted reduction only where the work stops, sized on the measured gap. Each 10 roles adds about €0.5M a year from 2029, costs €0.4M up front and is about −€0.3M in 2027 | CEO, CFO, board |

**The case for the 80, stated fairly.** Payroll savings are contractual once people have left, while procurement and pricing estimates are uncertain. If the work behind these roles has gone for good, through a lost contract, automation or lower volumes, keeping the roles only postpones the problem. One decisive round is kinder than several small ones. A clear plan also reassures owners and the bank. Our answer:
- The gate tests the uncertainty within one quarter, and waiting costs little in 2027 because Option A is cash-negative in 2027 anyway.
- If the work really has gone, the targeted round covers it, sized to the work that stopped rather than to a number chosen first.
- If any involuntary round is needed, there should be one round, and we should say it is the last only if that is true.

**What would change this recommendation:**
- The €6M protects a covenant test or a cash low point in the first half of 2027. Then the bank conversation and a 13-week cash forecast come first.
- Payroll shows that the work in these roles has already stopped.
- The ledger shows spend and pricing savings far below the low case.
- Last year's restructuring shows the hidden costs here are understated.
- If the problem is a volume dip that will pass, reduced hours bridge it at far lower cost than exits. In Belgium that means temporary unemployment for economic reasons, with current conditions to be checked. The Dutch short-time work scheme currently covers only calamities; a broader crisis scheme was before parliament in 2026, so its status needs checking.

## 7. Leaders first

Before any job goes, the executive team, the CEO and CFO included, takes a 10% pay cut for 2027 and forgoes its 2027 bonus. Senior managers forgo theirs. That is about €0.5M (estimate), and it is temporary, so none of it is in the run-rate. No bonus in 2027 or later should reward headcount reduction as such. Restructuring costs stay inside the profit measure that bonuses are paid on.

## 8. Law and process (not legal advice; counsel in both countries to confirm)

**Netherlands**
- The works council has an advisory right on the reorganisation (Works Councils Act, article 25). Its advice must be asked while it can still change the decision. If the final decision departs from the advice, implementation waits one month.
- Planning to dismiss 20 or more people in one UWV work area within three months triggers notification to UWV and the unions and consultation with them (WMCO). Settlement agreements the employer initiates count towards the 20, including those under a voluntary scheme.
- Dismissal for business reasons needs UWV permission or a settlement agreement.
- Selection follows the reflection principle within groups of interchangeable jobs, unless a collective agreement sets its own order.
- The statutory transition payment is one third of a month's pay per year of service. Notice runs one to four months, minus the time the UWV procedure took, with at least one month remaining.
- If last year's social plan is still in force, it probably sets the severance terms.

**Belgium**
- The planned reduction will almost certainly trigger the collective dismissal rules. They apply from 10 dismissals in 60 days for 20 to 99 employees, 10% of the workforce for 100 to 299, and 30 for 300 or more.
- Information and consultation with the works council or union delegation must come before the decision. After it, the employer notifies the regional employment service (VDAB, Forem or Actiris) and the federal employment ministry. A 30-day waiting period follows, which can be extended to 60 days if the unions object.
- An employment cell and outplacement duties apply.
- Notice runs by service, for example 18 weeks at five years, 27 at eight and 33 at ten, and pre-2014 service can lengthen it.
- Protected employees (works council and health and safety committee members) need prior approval.

**Both countries**
- Use objective, documented selection criteria, never protected characteristics, leave status or who raised concerns. People are told by their own manager, in person, before any wider announcement.
- The auditors confirm when the provision is recognised.
- Where the two countries' rules differ, running the stricter process in both is usually simpler and fairer.

## 9. What last year's restructuring tells us

**Before the board decides, it should see:**
- what last year's round cost, against plan;
- the savings promised against the savings delivered, line by line;
- how many roles or contractors came back within 12 months;
- voluntary and regretted turnover among the people who stayed;
- the state of the relationship with the works council and the unions;
- whether the social plan is still in force.

These figures replace the hidden-cost estimates in this model: 10% of roles refilled, 2 points of extra turnover, and 15% of the work still bought in during 2027. They are the best evidence we have.

**Honest numbers.** Two restructuring charges in two years are a pattern, not a one-off.
- ESMA's guidelines on alternative performance measures say that items that "affected past periods and will affect future periods will rarely be considered as non-recurring, infrequent or unusual (such as restructuring costs…)" (ESMA/2015/1415, paragraph 25). These bind listed issuers, and we should treat them as good practice either way.
- Any adjusted EBITDA in the board pack or in bank reporting sits next to the reported figure, with the restructuring charge shown and not described as one-off.
- If the facility agreement allows restructuring costs to be added back to covenant EBITDA, we need to check the cap and whether a second year still qualifies.

**People.** A second round in two years tells the people who stay that a third may follow.
- One study found that a 1% workforce reduction was followed by a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). The model assumes far less (+2 points). Last year's figures will show which is closer.
- Replacing a leaver costs half to twice their annual salary (Gallup). The model uses the low end.

## 11. Indicators and review rhythm

- **Monthly to the executive team, quarterly to the board, with the gate at the end of March 2027:**
  - Money: net savings delivered per measure against plan, each with an owner; cash against forecast; EBITDA margin.
  - People: voluntary and regretted turnover, absence, overtime and agency hours, safety incidents.
  - Customers: on-time delivery, damage and claims, complaints, contract renewals.
- **A year after any reduction:** an honest review of what was saved, what it cost (including hidden costs), and what in our planning made a second round necessary.
