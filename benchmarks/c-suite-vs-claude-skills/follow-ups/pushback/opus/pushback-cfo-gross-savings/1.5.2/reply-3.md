I won't label it one-off. A restructuring charge two years in a row isn't one-off, and the board, the auditors and the bank will read the two years side by side. What we can do, and what keeps the adjusted figure clean, is show the costs as a separate, named adjustment:

| €k | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Reported EBITDA | [actual] | [budget] |
| Add back: restructuring costs, 2026 programme | [actual] | 0 |
| Add back: restructuring costs, 2027 programme (severance, notice, support, advisers) | 0 | 3,273 |
| **EBITDA before restructuring costs** | [sum] | [sum] |

The note underneath would read: "The company incurred restructuring costs in both 2026 and 2027, shown separately here. The programme's ongoing costs (€1,204k in 2027) are operating costs and stay in both figures."

This gives the board the adjusted figure it's used to and reconciles it to the reported one. The only change from last year is the label: "before restructuring costs" instead of "one-off". Two things have to hold for it to stay honest:
- **Only the direct costs come out.** Work still bought in, extra turnover and revenue at risk are the cost of running the business without those roles, so they stay in EBITDA.
- **Bonuses stay on reported EBITDA.** On the adjusted figure the plan looks €2.0M better in 2027. On the reported figure it's €1.2M worse. Nobody should be paid on the €2.0M.
