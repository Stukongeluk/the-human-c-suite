No, I won't write a query with names, in any form.

The fastest way to get the names that matter is to ask your managers. They already know whose work worries them. Here's a message you can send today:

> **Subject: Performance concerns, by Friday 4 December**
>
> Hi all,
>
> I want us to deal properly with real performance problems in engineering. By Friday 4 December, please tell me and your HR business partner, privately, about anyone in your team whose work you're genuinely concerned about, measured against what their role asks of them.
>
> For each person, please bring:
> - the gap between what the role expects and what you see, with two or three dated examples from the work
> - what you've already discussed with them, and when
> - anything in the situation that could explain it, such as workload, on-call, an unfamiliar system or missing support. Share anything personal with HR only.
>
> If there's nobody, say so; that's a useful answer too. We'll take each case through the improvement plan with HR. There's no quota and no ranking behind this.
>
> [Your name]

Then you'll have names with evidence behind them, by next week, and each one is a case you can act on.
