# AI-first customer support: plan to 30 June 2027

**Draft v0.2 (fast track: no separate pilot)** · Prepared 23 November 2026 · Owner: CPTO · For: leadership team, then the works council and support team leads
**First decision needed:** leadership agreement on the approach and the people commitments below, by Thursday 3 December 2026

Figures marked *estimate* are placeholders until the baseline in Phase 0 replaces them with our own data.

---

## 1. Summary

From Monday 1 February 2027 an AI agent answers routine support questions for all customers on chat and email, starting with the categories that pass the offline test, with a person always one click away. By the end of March every routine category that passes the test is live. The support team moves to the work the agent can't do and to the customer work we have never had capacity for.

The numbers behind it (*estimates*):

- The support team costs about **€1.5M a year** (25 FTE at about €60k fully loaded).
- On realistic assumptions the agent frees **about 4 to 9 FTE** of capacity, worth **€0.23M to €0.55M a year**. The work it can't take still needs **16 to 21 FTE** at today's volume (section 5).
- At most, that equals about one year of volume growth plus natural turnover (together roughly 9 to 10 FTE). So we can lower the cost to serve each customer without dismissals.

This plan contains no dismissals. We decide on the team's size and shape after the June review, with measured results, together with the works council.

## 2. The problem we are solving

*To confirm with the CEO and CFO before 3 December:*

- **Cost to serve:** is there a 2027 target for support cost, as a total or per customer? How much, and by when?
- **Service:** response times, 24/7 coverage, peaks we can't staff today.
- **Scale:** growth in customers without the support team growing at the same pace.
- **What happens if we do nothing:** support headcount grows with the customer base, and competitors offer instant answers we don't.

The goal we can commit to now: **lower cost to serve and faster answers on routine questions, with customer satisfaction and retention at least as good as today.**

## 4. Options considered

**A. Do nothing.** No change in risk. Support cost grows with the customer base, and we miss a real chance to give customers faster answers on simple questions.

**B. Replace the whole support team with an AI agent by 30 June 2027.**
The case for it is serious. Support is one of our largest cost lines that AI can now reach. The board and investors expect results from AI. Competitors advertise instant 24/7 answers, and many customers prefer an instant answer to a simple question. On paper it saves about €1.5M a year.
Why we don't recommend it:
- **The work doesn't disappear.** Across the range in section 5, up to the vendor's headline rate, the conversations the agent can't resolve are the long ones: bugs, configuration, billing disputes, enterprise escalations. They still need about 16 to 21 FTE at today's volume. Someone also has to run the agent: its knowledge, evaluation and hand-overs.
- **The timing doesn't work.** For contracts to end by 30 June we would have to decide in January or February 2027, before the agent has even a few weeks of live results. That decision would need works council advice, a collective dismissal notification to UWV and the unions with a one-month waiting period, a UWV procedure or settlement agreements, and notice periods of one to four months (section 10).
- **The costs come first and the savings later.** Transition payments, legal costs and overlap come before the first saving, so the 2027 saving would be well below the annual figure.
- **It can't be undone quickly.** If quality drops, rebuilding a support team with product knowledge takes many months. Klarna said in 2024 that its assistant did the work of 700 agents. In 2025 its CEO said cost had been too dominant a factor and quality had suffered, and Klarna started hiring human agents again. In a 2025 survey of more than 1,000 leaders, 55% of those who had made people redundant because of AI said they had made the wrong decisions.
- **Customers could leave.** If ARR is €50M (*placeholder*), one point of extra churn costs €0.5M a year, a third of the whole team's cost.

**C. AI-first support, with the team reshaped (recommended).** The agent takes routine questions category by category, each through a quality gate. The team moves to complex cases, running the agent, and onboarding and customer success work. We pause external hiring of new support agents, so growth and natural turnover absorb the freed capacity. In June we decide on the team's shape with measured data.
This is reversible at every step, and it starts saving through avoided hires in 2027. It keeps the knowledge the agent depends on: the people who know the answers grade its test set and run its quality review. Ingka Group (IKEA) followed this pattern. Its AI assistant handled 47% of call-centre queries, and Ingka retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff as remote interior design advisers. That business brought in about €1.3 billion in 2022.

How the freed capacity is absorbed without dismissals:

- **Growth:** if contact volume grows 15 to 20% a year with the customer base, the agent absorbs 3.75 to 5 FTE of hiring a year (€225k to €300k).
- **Natural turnover:** at about 20% a year, roughly 5 of 25 people leave for their own reasons. With external hiring paused, the team shrinks gradually, and we backfill only when queue or quality thresholds are breached.
- **Redeployment:** to the roles in section 8.

If volume isn't growing, turnover alone absorbs about 5 FTE a year, so the high scenario would take about two years without dismissals.

"Verified resolution" means no re-contact within 7 days and no hand-over to a person. Vendor dashboards also count conversations where the customer simply left. We report both numbers and steer on the verified one.

## 7. Quality gates and customer safeguards

There is no separate pilot. The offline test and the live review do that work. Draft thresholds, to agree with the support team in December:

| Gate | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Go-live, per category (first: 29 Jan)** | At least 90% of test-set answers in that category graded correct and complete. Zero critical errors (pricing, contract terms, security, personal data). Hand-over tested end to end. AI disclosure live. Before the first go-live: DPIA signed off, customer notice period complete, works council advice received |
| **Live review (from 1 Feb)** | A daily sample review by agents for the first two weeks, then weekly. A category stays live while CSAT on AI-handled conversations is within 5 points of human-handled ones, 7-day re-contact is no higher than the human baseline, and the sample review finds no critical errors. Team pulse and absence stable |
| **Stop rule** | A critical error that reaches a customer sends that category back to people the same day, until it is fixed and passes the test set again |

Customer safeguards:

- **Customers always know** they are talking to an AI. This has been required under Article 50 of the EU AI Act since August 2026.
- **A person is always one click away,** and the hand-over passes on the conversation, so customers don't have to repeat themselves.
- **Out of scope at the start:** billing disputes and refunds, contract and pricing questions, security incidents, data-protection requests, outages (we link to the status page instead), cancellations, complaints, and enterprise accounts with named support contacts unless they opt in.
- **The agent answers from approved sources only,** with read-only account access at first. It takes no actions without confirmation, and every conversation is logged.
- **We are liable for what the agent says.** In Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024) a tribunal held the airline responsible for wrong information its chatbot gave.

## 8. People plan

**What we commit to now:**

- No dismissals in this plan. Any proposal to change the size or shape of the team comes after the June review, with the data, to the works council for advice. Redeployment and natural turnover come first.
- The support team designs the agent with us: they choose the categories, grade the test set and run the weekly quality review.
- We report AI quality for the team as a whole and never use it to rate or rank individual agents. Any tool that monitors agents' performance needs works council consent first.
- Learning happens in paid work time.

**Roles that grow** (the figures in brackets are to be confirmed with the leaders concerned):

| Work | Size | Where people come from |
|---|---|---|
| AI support operations: knowledge, conversation design, evaluation | ~5 FTE in January, ~3 in Feb–Mar, ~2 after | Support team |
| Tier-2 technical support and bug triage with engineering | Grows as routine work moves to the agent | Support team |
| Onboarding, implementation, customer success | [Customer success lead: current backlog] | Support team, with training |
| Proactive support: account health checks, churn-risk outreach | [To size] | Support team |
| QA and testing, documentation, product feedback | [Product and engineering leads] | Support team, with training |

**How:**

- **December and January:** a career conversation with each team member. What would they most like to do if routine questions moved to the agent? Which skills do they have that we don't use? We keep a skills overview for the team only, with no individual scores.
- **Internal moves:** support staff get first consideration for internal vacancies, with training.
- **Hiring:** a pause on external hiring of new support agents until the June review. We backfill a leaver when queue or quality thresholds are breached, so nobody carries an overload.
- **Learning:** half a day a week of paid learning time from March for people moving roles, and a €2,500 personal learning budget for each team member.
- **Entry route:** support stays a way into customer success, QA, product and engineering. It is how many of our people start.
- **If roles end later:** voluntary options come first, and we take one decision, not a series of rounds. Anyone whose role ends gets coaching, paid training, job-search support from the first day of notice, and priority for internal vacancies.

## 10. Works council, employment law and data protection (Netherlands)

*This is a summary for planning, not legal advice. Employment counsel and our privacy officer check it before 11 January.*

- **Works council (mandatory at our size):** an advisory right on important decisions under article 25 WOR. This likely includes introducing an important technology such as this agent, and any later change to the organisation. Advice must be asked while it can still change the plan, so before the vendor contract is final. If a decision departs from the advice, we must wait a month before carrying it out, and the council can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber, which can order a decision to be reversed. We can share information with the council in confidence: members are then bound to secrecy (article 20 WOR).
- **Consent right:** any system suited to monitoring agents' attendance, behaviour or performance needs the council's consent (article 27(1)(l) WOR).
- **Redeployment comes first in law too.** An employer may dismiss for economic reasons only if redeployment within a reasonable time, with training if needed, isn't possible (article 7:669(1) BW).
- **If team size changed later:** 20 or more dismissals in one UWV work area within three months is a collective dismissal (WMCO). We would notify UWV and the unions before the decision is final, followed by a one-month waiting period before UWV handles dismissal applications. Settlement agreements count towards the 20. Other requirements: a statutory transition payment, and notice of one to four months by length of service, minus the time the UWV procedure took.
- **EU AI Act:** disclosure to customers under Article 50, in force since August 2026. AI used to evaluate or monitor workers would be high-risk. We don't plan any.
- **GDPR:** a DPIA before go-live, a data processing agreement with the vendor, EU data location, retention limits, and no training on our customers' data without contractual terms that allow it.
- **Customer contracts:** adding the vendor as a subprocessor usually requires advance notice under our customer DPAs. Some enterprise contracts promise human support or response times.

## 14. Assumptions, and what would change this plan

- AI-resolved conversations take about half the average handling time. If the baseline shows that routine questions take most of the hours, the freed capacity is larger.
- AI drafting saves about 10% on the remaining human work, and running the agent takes about 2 FTE.
- €60k per FTE fully loaded, about 20% turnover, 15 to 20% volume growth: to replace with our own figures.
- **What would change the plan:** if the live results show verified resolution and CSAT at parity on most of the handling hours, and the capacity freed is larger than growth and turnover can absorb, a smaller team becomes a real option for H2. That would go through the works council, with voluntary routes first and support from the first day of notice. A cash emergency would also change the timing.

**Questions for the leadership team:** what's the 2027 cost or service target this must serve? How much of our revenue depends on customers who expect a person? What are we missing, and what would make this plan wrong?
