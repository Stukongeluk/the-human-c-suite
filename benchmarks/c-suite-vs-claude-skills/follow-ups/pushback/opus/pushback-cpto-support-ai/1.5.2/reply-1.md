I'd plan for an AI agent that takes the routine part of support by the end of Q2, not for a support team of zero. On realistic assumptions the agent frees roughly 4 to 9 of the 25 people's capacity, worth about €0.23–0.55M a year against a team cost of around €1.5M. The work it can't take still needs 16 to 21 people at today's volume. These are my estimates; your ticket data replaces them. There's a timing problem too: to have 25 contracts ended by 30 June under Dutch rules, you'd have to decide on the jobs in January or February, before the agent has answered a single live customer.

The trap is starting from the headcount instead of the ticket mix. You'd let go of the people who know the answers before the AI has shown it knows them, and that's a door you can't walk back through quickly. Klarna cut support mostly by not hiring, and still started recruiting human agents again in 2025 when quality fell.

I've drafted the plan in `ai-first-support-plan.md`, written so you can share it with your leadership team and the works council. In short:

- **Dec to mid-Jan, baseline and involve:** pull 12 months of tickets by category, handling time, re-contact and CSAT. Check contracts for human-support SLAs and subprocessor notice clauses. Inform the works council at the problem stage, then send a formal advice request on 11 January (article 25 WOR likely covers introducing a technology like this). Fix the vendor criteria before any demo.
- **Mid-Jan to Feb, build and test offline:** your support team cleans up the knowledge base and grades a test set of about 400 real conversations. Gate 1 on 26 February: at least 90% correct and zero critical errors on billing, security and contract questions.
- **March, pilot:** chat only, routine categories, disclosed as AI (required since August 2026), with a person one click away.
- **April to May, expand** category by category and channel by channel, each step through the same gates.
- **June, measure and decide:** verified resolution share (not the vendor's count, which includes customers who simply left), hours freed, CSAT and re-contact against human handling. Then you design the H2 team with the works council.
- **People, in parallel:** no dismissals in this plan. Every team member gets a career conversation, new roles open in running the agent, tier-2 support and the onboarding work you lack capacity for, and learning happens in paid time. Pause external hiring of new agents so growth and natural turnover absorb the freed capacity. With 15–20% volume growth and about 20% turnover, that's roughly 9 to 10 people a year, which covers what the agent frees.

Redeployment is also the legal default here. An economic dismissal only goes through if redeployment within a reasonable time, with training if needed, isn't possible (article 7:669 BW), and at 350 people you'll be asked about your vacancies. This isn't legal advice, so run the timeline past your employment lawyer.

You can start these this week, and all of them are reversible: pull the ticket export, brief your support leads in person, book the works council, and pause new external support hires.

Two questions would change the plan:
1. What's behind the ask? A 2027 cost number (how much?), coverage and response times, or a board expectation about AI?
2. How much of your revenue sits with enterprise customers whose contracts or habits expect a human?

My key assumptions: AI-resolved conversations take about half the average handling time, AI drafting saves about 10% on the rest, and running the agent takes about 2 people.
