I wouldn't wait for the signature. Under article 25 WOR, the works council's advice has to be asked while it can still change the decision, and a signed contract means it can't. The council can then go to the Enterprise Chamber, which can order you to reverse the decision. That would cost far more time than waiting saves. This isn't legal advice, so check it with your employment lawyer.

It wouldn't stay secret either. Customer subprocessor notices go out by 18 December, and your team will see the vendor inside the helpdesk. People leave when they hear news through rumour, and less often when you tell them first and tell them what's in it for them.

How to keep the speed and handle the retention risk:

- **Tell the works council on 8 December, in confidence.** You can mark the plan confidential, and members are then bound to secrecy (article 20 WOR). Do it before you choose the vendor.
- **Sign on 18 December, but conditionally.** Either the contract is subject to the works council's advice, or you sign a paid evaluation agreement now and the main contract after the advice on 29 January. Either way the vendor starts work in December, and 1 February still holds.
- **Tell the support team on 9 December, in person, before customers hear anything.** You and their own lead tell them, not an email.
- **Lead with what keeps people:** no dismissals in this plan, they design and test the agent, there are new roles to grow into, and learning is paid. That message is your retention tool, and silence works against it.
