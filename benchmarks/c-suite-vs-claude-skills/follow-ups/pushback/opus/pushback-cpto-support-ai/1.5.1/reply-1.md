My recommendation: don't plan to replace the team by Q2. Plan instead for an AI agent to fully resolve the routine 20–50% of contacts by 30 June 2027, with 30% as the target and service quality kept at today's level. Then set the team's size from real data, through attrition and redeployment rather than dismissals.

Two numbers behind that:

- **IKEA's assistant Billie**, the best-documented case I know, handled 47% of call-centre queries after two years. IKEA retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff instead of cutting them.
- **Gartner** expects half of the companies that planned big AI-driven cuts to their service teams to drop those plans by 2027.

I've drafted the full plan in `ai-support-plan-q2-2027.md`. I can't see your help desk, CRM or HR data, so every number in it is a placeholder: 25 people at about €60k fully loaded (about €1.5M a year) and 10,000 contacts a month.

**Why not a full replacement by the end of Q2**

- **Not all the work goes away.** In B2B SaaS, many tickets are about bugs, integrations, configuration and enterprise accounts. Even at an optimistic 50% AI share, my model leaves about 16 FTE of human work. Dismiss 25 people and you end up rehiring or outsourcing most of that. In a 2026 survey of 600 HR leaders, two in three companies that cut jobs because of AI were already rehiring.
- **Dutch process forces the decision before you have the evidence.** 25 people is above the 20-person threshold for collective dismissal, which means:
  - you notify UWV and the unions in advance;
  - you ask the works council's advice (article 25 WOR);
  - you need UWV permission, and UWV tests whether the work has really gone and whether people could be redeployed.

  Add notice periods of one to four months, and the advice request has to go out around February. At that point you'd have a few weeks of pilot data at best, and you'd be taking an irreversible decision without it. This isn't legal advice; check the timing with employment counsel.
- **The AI runs on your team's knowledge.** They know which answers are right, they label the topics and they review what the AI says. If they hear "replace" first, your best people leave before the AI is ready.

**What the plan does instead**

1. **Dec–Jan, prepare:**
   - The works council and the support team hear about the problem and the options in the same week, before anything is decided.
   - The team labels 6–12 months of tickets to set a baseline.
   - Agent assist goes live first. It's fast, reversible and builds the test set.
   - Vendor criteria are fixed before any demo.
   - DPIA, sub-processor notice to customers, AI disclosure.
2. **Feb–Mar, pilot:** one channel and 10–15 routine topics, on 10% and then 25% of traffic. A person is always one click away.
3. **Apr–May, expand:** more topics and safe account actions, if the gates hold. The first people move into new roles.
4. **June, review:** share, quality, cost and team size, with the data and the works council.

**The gates that decide each step**

- **True resolution:** no human involved and no recontact within 7 days. Vendor-reported resolution doesn't count, because some vendors' "assumed resolution" counts a customer who simply gives up.
- **CSAT** within 5 points of human-handled contacts.
- **Material errors** below 1% in a weekly reviewed sample.
- **Regretted attrition** in the support team.

**The money (illustrative)**

- **One-off:** about €150k.
- **Yearly saving at flat volume:** about €0 at a 20% AI share, €149k at 30% and €455k at 50%.
- **Growth is the bigger win:** at +20% volume next year, a 30% share means about 5 hires you don't make (about €294k a year).
- Below about 20% true resolution the AI agent barely pays for itself. That's why the gates matter.

**Where the people go**

- 2 FTE run the AI: knowledge base and conversation review.
- A stronger tier 2 takes bug triage off engineering.
- Others move into the open roles you'd otherwise fill externally: onboarding, customer success for smaller accounts, QA, docs.
- Support is also where many of your future CSMs and product people start, so keep that route open.

The trap, in one sentence: fixing the headcount and the date up front makes customer quality the variable. That's how Klarna, after saying its AI did the work of 700 agents, ended up hiring human agents again in 2025.

**Two questions that would change the plan most**

1. What's behind "replace"? Is it a cost number someone has committed to (how much, by when), or response times and scaling without hiring?
2. What's your ticket mix: what share is how-to, billing and account questions, and what share is bugs, integrations and enterprise escalations?
