I wouldn't label this year's restructuring cost "one-off" in adjusted EBITDA: you booked one last year too, so it is now recurring, and a recurring cost labelled one-off overstates profit and makes the cut look free. The version I can give you is to show the cost below the line but honestly named, with a reconciliation to reported EBITDA.

The test is simple: a similar charge in the last two years means "non-recurring" is the wrong label. The US SEC rule on non-GAAP measures says exactly that for US-listed companies (C&DI 102.03). If you are not US-listed it isn't binding, but your auditor and your bank will apply the same logic, and I'd treat it as good practice.

Here is a layout that keeps the adjustment you want and stays defensible:

| EUR thousand (estimates) | 2025 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Reported EBITDA | [actual] | [forecast] |
| Restructuring costs, 2025 programme | [actual] | 0 |
| Restructuring costs, 2027 programme (severance, transition support, advisers) | 0 | 2,660 |
| **Adjusted EBITDA** | | |

Under the table, state: "Restructuring costs were incurred in 2025 and are planned for 2027; they are not described as non-recurring. Adjusted EBITDA excludes them to show underlying trading." Show both years side by side, so readers see two programmes in three years. Then show a second line, "Adjusted EBITDA after restructuring costs", so the board sees both.

Two checks before the board pack goes out:
- Check your credit agreement. Many define covenant EBITDA with a cap on add-backs, and a repeated restructuring add-back may be capped or refused.
- Ask the auditor when the provision lands. If you announce before year-end, part of the EUR 2.0m of severance and outplacement could fall in the 2026 accounts, not 2027.

One more point: moving costs below the line changes presentation, not cash. Q2 2027 still pays out about EUR 1.5m, and the CFO still has to fund it.
