I'd keep "80 redundancies = EUR 6m" out of the board pack: on realistic timing it delivers about EUR 2.8m gross in 2027, and after severance and the costs that follow it is roughly cash-neutral in 2027 (base case -EUR 0.4m, range -EUR 2.1m to +EUR 1.0m). It reaches about EUR 4.5m a year from 2028. I've built the model and a draft board paper so you can show the board what it really does.

**Why EUR 6m doesn't work**
- 80 roles at an assumed EUR 63k loaded cost is EUR 5.0m a year, even in a full year. EUR 6m needs EUR 75k per role, and the cut still has to go through consultation first.
- In the Netherlands the works council has an advice right before you decide (art. 25 WOR). Dismissing 20 or more people in one UWV area within three months triggers the collective dismissal notification. Belgium has its own collective dismissal procedure and costlier notice for long-serving staff. On my estimates, Dutch exits start around 1 June and Belgian around 1 July.
- Severance and outplacement are paid when people leave, so Q2 and Q3 2027 are negative. Cumulative cash turns positive in Q1 2028 (Q4 2027 in the fast case, Q3 2028 in the slow one).
- The saving comes back in part: overtime and agency work, about 10 roles refilled within a year, and extra leavers among the 820 who stay. After a second round in two years I'd expect the high end of that.
- In the Netherlands the reflection principle decides who goes, not us, so cost per head depends on the affected job groups.

**What I'd put to the board instead**
1. A EUR 6m run-rate by the end of 2027, not a EUR 6m 2027 result. Nothing closes EUR 6m inside 2027, and it's better you hear that from me than from the auditor.
2. Levers that cost no jobs first (estimates, same basis, counted once):
   - Not refilling 60 of the roughly 126 leavers a year: EUR 1.7m in 2027, EUR 3.4m a year after.
   - Zero-based non-payroll spend: EUR 0.3-0.8m, rising to EUR 0.8-1.8m.
   - Pricing and revenue leakage: EUR 0.2-0.5m, rising to EUR 0.5-1.2m.
   - Leadership bonuses paused and a pay cut, which is temporary and so not in the run-rate: EUR 0.3-0.6m.
   - Together EUR 2.5-3.6m in 2027 and EUR 4.7-6.4m a year from 2028, with no one dismissed.
   - Working capital could add EUR 0.7-1.7m of one-off cash, which is not a saving.
3. A gate in Q2 2027: whatever those levers haven't delivered then goes to a redundancy decision, sized with real data, done once, with support from the day of notice and the work reduced along with the roles.

**Last year's restructuring**
A second restructuring charge means it is no longer one-off. Show both years inside your adjusted figure, or reconcile adjusted to reported line by line. I'd also check last year's plan against actual savings and how many of those roles have been refilled before the board trusts this year's assumptions. Whether the provision lands in the 2026 accounts if you announce this year is a question for your auditor.

The people side: 820 colleagues carry the work of 80 more, so I'd track regretted attrition, absence and safety incidents monthly.

**Two questions that would change the numbers**
1. What is the EUR 6m for (covenant, margin, cash) and by when? If the real deadline is year-end 2027, a run-rate target works. If it is a 2027 P&L number, the CEO should hear now that no option meets it.
2. What did last year's restructuring save against plan, and how many of those roles are back?

**Caveat:** I had no payroll, HR or ledger data, so every figure is an estimate on assumptions I labelled: a 60/20 NL/BE split, average loaded cost, severance per head, exit dates, and the revenue and non-payroll bases behind the lever ranges. Replace the yellow cells in the workbook and it recalculates. Employment law, works council and Belgian procedure, and the provision timing need counsel, HR and your auditor; this isn't legal or accounting advice.
