I wouldn't do that. Signing before you tell the works council is the most likely way to stall this project, and it is more likely to make people leave early than to prevent it.

**Legal risk (counsel to confirm).** The OR's advisory right on a major change (WOR art. 25) means asking for its advice while it can still influence the decision. A signed contract is a decision already taken. If the OR challenges that, the Enterprise Chamber can order the decision suspended or reversed, and that could land in the middle of your Q2 deadline. Any tool that can monitor staff also needs the OR's consent (art. 27) before it is introduced.

**The practical risk.** 25 people usually hear about something like this from a vendor, a LinkedIn post or an IT ticket before an announcement. Then they learn that leadership knew and chose to say nothing. The best people, who have the easiest time finding work, leave first, and they take the knowledge the agent needs. Secrecy produces the exits you are afraid of.

**Your worry is fair, though.** Early leavers are a real risk, so address it directly:
1. **Tell the OR first, in confidence.** A short, private conversation with the OR chair, with the real numbers, before anything is signed. Ask for its advice in the formal sense later.
2. **Tell the team the same week, from their managers, with a plan.** What is decided, what is not, what it means for roles, and what you are doing for them. Do it live, not by email.
3. **Give people a reason to stay.** Name the new roles (AI quality review, knowledge ownership, escalations, proactive account work), fund paid training, and offer a retention bonus through the rollout period. Say plainly that no headcount decision comes before the measured results.
4. **Sign in a way that doesn't lock you in.** Make the contract conditional on the OR process, or start with a short, low-commitment term with exit rights. Then nothing is final until the people affected have been heard.

I'd also bring the CEO and CFO into this decision now. Hiding a change from the OR is a company-level risk, and it shouldn't rest on you alone.
