I can make it faster, but I wouldn't skip the pilot. What I can cut is the calendar, by running it as a four-week gated launch instead of the staged plan I drew. Skipping it entirely means our customers become the test set.

**Why a competitor doing it doesn't change this.** It tells us customers may expect it. It doesn't tell us their agent works. Klarna had the same head start in 2024 and by 2025 was hiring humans back because service had suffered. Air Canada was held liable for its chatbot's wrong answer, and the same would apply to us. A pilot is the cheapest way to find out about that before a customer does.

**Faster, without skipping the test**
1. **Week 1:** read the last 12 months of tickets and pick the 2 or 3 most routine, best-documented categories. This also replaces the guess about what the agent can take with a number.
2. **Weeks 2 to 3:** run the agent in shadow mode on live tickets. Nothing reaches customers. Check accuracy against a test set of real cases.
3. **Week 4:** go live on those categories only, labelled as AI, with a visible route to a person. Review a sample of conversations daily.
4. **After that:** add categories as each one clears the same quality bar. A category that misses stays with people.

This could put real customers on the agent by mid-January, not June. By end of Q2 you can have broad coverage of everything that passed its gate. That's more than a pilot-free launch would give you, because you won't be rolling back.

**What stays fixed, whatever the date**
- A human route on every conversation, and AI disclosure (AI Act Art. 50).
- The works council comes in now. It has an advisory right on a reorganisation (art. 25 WOR) and a consent right on monitoring systems (art. 27), so skipping it can stall the launch for longer than the pilot would take.
- No headcount decision until the measured numbers exist. Cutting 25 people before the agent has proven itself removes both the safety net and the knowledge it learns from.

**What would change my mind:** evidence that competitors' agents are holding quality, such as their CSAT or churn, or a baseline showing most of our tickets are simple. Do you have either? If so, the gates could be looser than I've set them.
