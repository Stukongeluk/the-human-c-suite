# Support AI agent: plan to 30 June 2027

Draft for discussion with the CEO, CFO, Head of Support and the works council (OR). Numbers marked [pull] must come from our own ticket and finance data before the plan is final.

## 1. Goal

By 30 June 2027 an AI agent resolves a measured share of customer contacts at quality equal to or better than today, and the support team is working on the contacts and customers the agent should not handle. The share is set from the baseline in January, not guessed now.

Why this goal and not "replace the 25-person team": the aim is cost to serve and response time at the same or better customer satisfaction. Headcount is an outcome we learn from the gates, not an input we fix in advance.

## 2. What happens if we do nothing

[pull] Cost per contact, first-response time, CSAT, backlog and support cost as a share of revenue today. If these are fine, the urgency is lower than the deadline suggests and we can go slower.

## 3. Baseline (December to mid-January)

- Classify the last 6 to 12 months of contacts [pull]: volume by topic, share that is repeat or how-to, share needing account access, bugs, billing disputes, churn-risk or contract-sensitive accounts.
- Per category: current resolution time, reopen rate, CSAT.
- Cost per contact today, and the target cost per contact including model, infrastructure, evaluation and human oversight.
- Ask the agents which contacts are easy and which are hard. They know where the quality risks are.

## 4. Phases and gates

| Phase | When | What | Gate to continue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Baseline and consultation | Dec to mid-Jan | Baseline above; brief the OR on the problem and options; name the owner for each affected group | Baseline agreed; OR advice process started |
| 1. Shadow mode | mid-Jan to Feb | Agent drafts answers on real contacts, agents rate them; nothing goes to customers | Accuracy and policy-error rate meet thresholds set from the baseline on a test set of real cases |
| 2. Assist | Mar | Agent suggests, humans send. Measure time saved and error catches | Agents say it helps; quality unchanged |
| 3. Limited live | Apr | Agent answers the safest categories directly for a small share of customers or design partners, with a one-click route to a person | CSAT, reopen rate and escalation rate within agreed margin of human baseline; cost per contact within target |
| 4. Expand | May to Jun | Widen categories and traffic in steps, one at a time | Same gates at each step; stop or roll back if any is missed |
| 5. Review | End of June | Decide, with the OR, how support work and team look next, based on measured results | See section 6 |

Thresholds are set in phase 0, before we see results, and written in the decision record.

## 5. Quality and safety

- Customers are told they are talking to an AI (AI Act Article 50, in force since August 2026) and can always reach a person. This is good practice beyond the legal minimum.
- The company is liable for what its agent says (Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024). Refunds, contract terms, security and data requests go to a person.
- Data protection review (DPIA) before phase 3, with counsel. Not legal advice.
- Monitor by category and team. No ranking of individual agents on AI-related metrics.
- Evaluation continues after launch: sampled review by humans every week.

## 6. People

The plan does not assume jobs disappear. Reasons: measured quality comes before any staffing decision, and the evidence that cutting support for AI too early backfires is strong (Klarna reversed in 2025 and went back to hiring people; vendor surveys find many AI layoffs regretted).

Order of options as the freed capacity becomes real:

1. Redeploy to work customers value and we lack capacity for: onboarding and proactive customer success, complex or high-value accounts, quality reviewers and trainers of the agent, knowledge base owners, product feedback (Ingka retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff as design advisers rather than cutting).
2. Natural attrition and a pause on backfills.
3. Paid retraining, from the first day, for roles that change.
4. Only if sustained, measured results show the above isn't enough: any further step goes through the OR and legal process (below), once, with support from the first day of notice.

Agents are in the design from phase 0: they review the agent's answers, own the escalation rules and help decide what the freed time is used for.

## 7. Works council and law (check with counsel)

- With 350 employees we have an OR. Bring it in at the problem stage in December, not at the decision. A change in how support is organised falls under its advisory right (art. 25 WOR); an AI tool that can monitor agents' behaviour or performance may need its consent (art. 27(1)(l) WOR). Design the tool so it doesn't.
- If it ever came to ending 20 or more jobs in one UWV area within three months, collective dismissal rules (WMCO) apply, and consultation comes before the decision, with notice periods and transition payments. The saving in year one is well below the annual figure.
- Employer-initiated settlement agreements count towards the 20.

## 9. Indicators, reviewed monthly

- Customer: CSAT, reopen rate, escalation to human, first-response and resolution time, complaints, churn reasons that mention support.
- Cost: cost per resolved contact, total support cost.
- People: agent pulse, absence, attrition, regretted exits, internal moves.

## 10. Risks and what would change the plan

- Quality fails a gate: stay in the previous phase; no deadline pressure overrides it.
- Contacts are mostly complex: the agent takes a smaller share; the team shifts toward assist.
- Cost per contact doesn't beat humans once oversight is included: stop and reconsider.
- Customers dislike it: more human routes.
