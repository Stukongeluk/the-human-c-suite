I wouldn't sign first: telling the works council after the contract is the one sequencing choice that can stop the whole project, and it doesn't protect you from people leaving. (Not legal advice; confirm with your employment counsel.)

**Why the order matters**

- The works council's advisory right (art. 25 WOR) requires that advice is requested while it can still influence the decision. A signed contract looks like a decision already taken. If the council argues that, it can go to the Enterprise Chamber, which can order the decision suspended or reversed. That could cost you weeks you don't have.
- Support staff will hear anyway: from the vendor, from IT access requests, from a procurement thread. Hearing it second-hand, after signing, is what makes people leave, and the best ones leave first.

**What gets you the speed and the confidentiality**

1. **Brief the council under confidentiality now.** Council members have a statutory duty of secrecy for information you designate confidential (art. 20 WOR). Share the problem, the numbers, and the vendor shortlist. Ask for advice on the principle and the approach, not a finished contract.
2. **Sign a contract that can't hurt you.** Pilot-sized scope, a termination right if the council's advice or the quality gates say stop, no volume commitment before week 8, and no exclusivity. Procurement can finish in parallel with the consultation.
3. **Tell the support team the same week the council is briefed, in person, from their own manager.** Say what is decided (we're testing an AI agent), what isn't (team size: no decision before two months of live numbers), and what you're doing for them (retraining, redeployment first).
4. **Reduce flight risk on purpose:** a retention conversation with each of the 25 in the first two weeks, a named owner for questions, and training time that is paid.

**The draft message to the team (one paragraph)**
"We're starting a test of an AI agent on routine contacts. No decision on the team's size will be made before we have two months of real results, and you'll help design it and grade its answers. Here is what we know, what we don't, and when you'll hear next."
