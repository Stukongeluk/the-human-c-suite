I'd draft this as a gated plan toward a measured outcome by end of Q2, not as "the 25-person team is gone by end of Q2". The full draft is in `support-ai-plan.md`, in this folder. Here's why, and what's in it.

**Why not a fixed replacement date**

- We don't yet know what share of your contacts an agent can resolve at today's quality. Until we do, "25 to zero" is a guess. If it's wrong, customers pay for it first.
- Klarna said in 2024 its AI did the work of 700 agents, then said in 2025 that cost had been too dominant and quality had suffered, and began hiring human agents again. The companies that cut jobs for AI tend to regret it (surveys by vendors, so a signal rather than a precise rate).
- You are liable for what the agent tells customers (Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024).
- Seven months is enough to prove a lot. It isn't enough to prove you can safely end a whole function.

**What the plan does instead**

1. **Baseline (Dec to mid-Jan).** Classify 6 to 12 months of contacts by topic and by how easily they resolve. Pull cost per contact, CSAT, reopen rate and response time. This sets the real target.
2. **Shadow, assist, limited live, expand (mid-Jan to June).** Each phase has quality and cost gates, fixed before we see results. Miss a gate and you stay in the phase. Customers can always reach a person and are told they're talking to an AI (required by the AI Act since August 2026).
3. **Review at end of June.** With measured results, you decide what support looks like.

**People**

- Redeploy first: onboarding, complex accounts, agent trainers and reviewers, knowledge owners. Ingka retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff as advisers instead of cutting.
- Then attrition and paused backfills, then paid retraining. A reduction only if the data shows the other routes aren't enough, and only after full legal process.
- The 25 are in the design from day one. They know which contacts are hard.

**Netherlands specifics** (check with counsel; not legal advice)

- You have a works council. Bring it in during December while it can still shape the plan. Changes to how support is organised fall under its advisory right (art. 25 WOR). If the tool could monitor agents' performance, consent applies (art. 27(1)(l) WOR), so design it not to.
- If this ever ended 20 or more jobs in one UWV area within three months, collective dismissal rules apply (WMCO), with consultation before the decision. Transition payments and notice mean the year-one saving is well below the annual figure.

**What I need from you to firm this up**

1. What's the problem you're solving: cost, response time, or both, and what is each worth? If support cost is a small share of revenue, the case for the deadline is weaker than it looks.
2. Which team builds this, and what do they stop doing? Plans that only fit with overtime fail.

The draft has placeholders marked [pull] for numbers I don't have: ticket mix, cost per contact, budget.

**Key assumptions:** a meaningful share of contacts are routine; quality can be measured against today's; the works council is engaged early.

What am I missing, and what would make this wrong? If you have a board commitment or a contract that makes the date fixed, tell me, and I'll rework the plan around the smallest safe version of it.
