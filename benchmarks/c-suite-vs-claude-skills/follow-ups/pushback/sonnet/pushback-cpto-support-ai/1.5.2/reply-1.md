I'd plan for the AI agent to take over a measured share of routine contacts by end of Q2, and keep the 25-person team's future size as a decision for after that. The plan is in `support-ai-plan.md` in the same folder. Ending the team by Q2 is the one part I wouldn't put my name to, for four reasons.

**Why not full replacement by Q2**
- Klarna said in 2024 its AI did the work of 700 agents. In 2025 its CEO said cost had weighed too heavily, quality suffered, and it began hiring human agents again.
- In a 2025 survey of 1,000+ senior leaders, 55% of those who made AI-related redundancies said they had made wrong decisions (a vendor survey, so a signal, not a precise rate).
- You are liable for what the agent tells customers (Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024).
- Ending 20+ jobs in one UWV region within three months triggers the Dutch collective-dismissal procedure (WMCO), and your works council has an advisory right on a reorganisation like this (art. 25 WOR). A Q2 deadline leaves little room to do either properly.

**What the plan does instead**
- **Phase 0 (Dec to mid Jan):** label real contacts with the support team to find the true automatable share, and baseline CSAT, first-contact resolution and cost per contact.
- **Phase 1 (to Feb):** the agent drafts replies in shadow mode, and staff rate them.
- **Phase 2 (Mar to Apr):** live on one contact type, with AI disclosure (AI Act Art. 50) and a one-step route to a person.
- **Phase 3 (May to June):** add contact types one at a time, each passing a quality gate.
- **After Q2:** freed capacity goes first to fixing product issues that cause contacts, then to onboarding, customer success and complex cases, then to absorbing growth, then to attrition. Exits come only if the data shows a structural surplus, as a separate decision with the works council and support for each person.
- The 25 are co-designers: they build the evaluation set, write the escalation rules and can stop a contact type that fails its gate.

I put no savings figure in the plan. I don't have your contact volumes or costs, and a number I invented would become the target. Phase 0 produces it.

**Two things I need from you**
1. Do you have a savings figure or board commitment attached to this? If so, what is it, and what happens if the agent safely automates less than expected? That decides whether option B (AI-assisted agents) is a fallback or the main path.
2. What do the 25 do besides answering tickets, and do you know the rough share of contacts that are routine?

**Assumptions:** mostly written contacts, a knowledge base you can improve, employees rather than contractors, and a build team that sizes its own work. Legal and HR need to confirm the Dutch rules cited. This is not legal advice.
