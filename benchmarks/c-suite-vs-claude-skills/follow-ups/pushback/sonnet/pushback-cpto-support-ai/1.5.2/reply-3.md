I wouldn't sign first and tell people after. Fear of early exits is real, but this order creates the exits you want to avoid. It also creates a legal problem.

**Why the order matters**
- The works council's advisory right (art. 25 WOR) has to be requested while its advice can still change the decision. If a signed vendor contract makes the decision effectively final, the council can take it to the Enterprise Chamber, which can order the decision undone. That would cost you more time than a short consultation.
- The 25 will find out anyway: procurement, IT access and the vendor's onboarding all leave traces. Hearing it from a login request or a rumour is the worst version, and the people most able to leave are the strongest ones.

**What protects you without hiding it**
1. **Sign a contract that fits the plan.** Pilot-scale term, a termination right if quality gates fail, no volume commitment, and a clause making the full roll-out conditional on the works council process. That also helps your negotiation with the vendor.
2. **Brief the OR chair and one or two representatives in confidence this week,** under a confidentiality agreement, before signing. Treat them as co-designers on the plan, not an audience.
3. **Tell the support team before it leaks,** live, from their own manager, with one clear message: "No role is ending as part of this. We are building the agent with you and deciding on capacity after we have results." Say it only if you are prepared to make it true for the first phase, which the plan does.
4. **Retention:** career conversations in the first 60 days, paid learning time, and a named role for each person in the agent's design.
