Back the goal, decline the design. Tell Operations you'll work with them on the 8%, but you won't ship a live leaderboard or a weekly bottom-10% list to subcontractors. A bottom 10% is 240 drivers every week, about 160 of them not your employees, and it always fills, however good everyone gets.

**Why it won't deliver what they expect**
- **It ranks routes, not people.** Stops per hour depends on density, flats without lifts, parcel size, traffic and depot loading, so a raw ranking flags rural routes, hard districts and new drivers.
- **It pays for speed.** A live ranking rewards rushing and skipped breaks. The cost returns as incidents, damage, failed deliveries and drivers leaving, which the 8% probably doesn't net off.
- **A flag to a subcontractor is a decision about someone's job,** made with your data, where the driver can't see the numbers, object or get a person to look. The responsibility stays with you. Your Dutch works council has a consent right on performance-monitoring systems (art. 27(1)(l) WOR), Belgian CAO 39 requires consulting staff representatives on new technology, and per-driver data sent to another employer needs a GDPR impact assessment. France's CNIL fined Amazon €32 million in 2024 for over-intrusive productivity tracking (I couldn't check its appeal today). Have counsel confirm; this isn't legal advice.

**What to do next**
1. **Talk to the head of Operations privately this week**, before a wider meeting. A line you can use: *"I want the 8% too. I won't build something that names 240 drivers a week, mostly for having the hardest routes, and sends the names to employers who aren't us. Give me eight weeks to find where stops per hour are really lost, and we'll measure it together."*
2. **Ask where the 8% comes from** and whether it's for absorbing growth or removing routes. At today's volume it means about 180 fewer driver-equivalents, which is a different conversation.
3. **Offer the alternative**, mostly in your hands:
   - stops per hour by route, depot and subcontractor, adjusted for route difficulty;
   - cut what the app and depots cost per stop: loading waits, bad addresses, sequencing, scan and photo steps, failed first attempts;
   - drivers see their own numbers privately, against their route's expected pace;
   - subcontractors judged on team outcomes, including safety and working-time compliance.
4. **Pilot in three or four depots against controls for eight to twelve weeks**, tracking stops per hour, first-attempt success, damage, incidents and driver turnover. Design it with drivers, your works councils, the subcontractors and your DPO before building.

The dashboard and pilot are reversible and can start now. The weekly list isn't: once a subcontractor drops a driver, you can't undo it.
