Back the goal, not the design: work with Operations on the 8%, but don't build a live leaderboard or a weekly bottom-10% list for subcontractors. That list names about 240 drivers every week however well everyone drives, and it mostly measures their routes.

**Why this design won't deliver the 8%**
- Stops per hour is mostly the route: density, flats versus houses, parcels per stop, parking, how late the van left the depot. The bottom 10% will be hard routes and new drivers. Drivers flagged after a bad week will improve the next week with nothing changed (regression to the mean), so the tool will seem to work.
- The quickest way up the board is worse service: "not home" cards without ringing, skipped breaks, speeding. And a live ranking invites glances at the screen while driving.
- Rankings often backfire. In a three-year randomised trial, salespeople sold more once their private ranking was hidden, mostly because those ranked lower than they expected disengaged ([Barankay, Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/why-its-sometimes-better-not-to-tell-employees-where-they-stand/)).
- Legal floor (check with counsel): Dutch works council consent for performance monitoring (art. 27(1)(l) WOR) and a mandatory DPIA. For ~1,600 subcontractor drivers you'd score people you don't employ for their employer to act on, and under the CJEU's SCHUFA ruling a score someone relies on heavily can itself be an automated decision (Article 22 GDPR). Belgium's Parcel Delivery Act presumes you liable when a direct subcontractor breaks its driver rules, such as the 9-hour day.

**What to offer instead**
1. Find where the time goes, in aggregate: stop time by route type and depot, late departures, failed addresses, and the app's own taps and waits per stop, which you can fix yourself.
2. Route-adjusted dashboards per depot and subcontractor, compared with their own past, with first-attempt success, damage, complaints and safety beside stops per hour. Subcontractors manage their people; you manage the contract on outcomes.
3. Test it after the December peak: a few depots against matched control depots for six to eight weeks.
4. Drivers and employee representatives in both countries in the design from the start, not at sign-off.

**First step:** talk to the head of Operations privately before this reaches the exec team. A line you can use: "I want the 8% too. I won't ship a tool that flags 240 drivers a week no matter how they drive, but give me the depot data and six weeks after peak, and we'll find where the time actually goes."
