## The decision in one sentence

We ask the board to approve a runway plan that gets the company to break-even, or to cash into mid-2028, under a downside case. The plan starts this week with measures that cost no jobs. Any workforce reduction is sized from the gap that remains and decided once, after union negotiation, instead of fixing a 30% cut this month.

## Where we stand

- **Runway:** nine months at today's burn and growth, to the end of August 2027.
- **Series B:** the earliest close is now summer 2027. On today's path we would run the process with zero to two months of cash, after one slip already. European climate-tech growth capital is scarce: World Fund estimates a Series B shortfall of $13.5bn over 2020–2024.
- **Growth:** revenue grows about 4% a month, about 60% a year. With flat costs, break-even takes ln(costs ÷ gross profit) ÷ ln(1.04) months. For us that is [n] months, against nine months of cash.
- **Responsibility:** we hired and spent on the assumption of a Series B by [original date]. That was management's plan, approved by this board, and management owns the result. The leadership team takes the first cut (option 7).

## Target and decision rule

- **Target:** break-even before cash runs out (what Paul Graham calls "default alive"), or cash into mid-2028 counting only committed money, in the **downside case**: growth at 3% a month and no Series B before [Q1 2028].
- **Order:** we use the measures that do the least harm to people first, and move to the next only when the earlier ones can't close the gap in time.
- **One round:** any reduction is sized so that no second round is needed in the downside case.

## What a reduction saves, and when

Swedish rules set the timing. Employment counsel will confirm them before we rely on them.

- **Negotiation first:** the Co-determination Act (MBL) requires negotiating with the unions before deciding major changes, including redundancies.
- **Notice to Arbetsförmedlingen:** for 5 to 25 people in one county, at least two months before the first person's employment ends. It is a notice of risk, not a decision. It becomes public, so staff hear it from us first.
- **Notice periods:** one to six months, by length of service (Employment Protection Act, LAS).
- **Selection:** LAS sets the order by length of service within each operational unit and collective agreement area. We may exempt three people; any other list needs agreement with the union. People dismissed for lack of work who have more than 12 months' employment in the past three years have priority for re-employment for nine months.
- **What that means for savings:** a decision in December saves nothing before February or March 2027. The saving within our runway is roughly the monthly saving times the months remaining, minus the package, which is well below the annual figure.
- **Hidden costs:** in Trevor and Nyberg's study, a 1% downsizing was associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the following year. The newest hires, who go first under LAS, are often the commercial staff the Series B depends on.
- **Short-time work:** the public scheme covers temporary, serious difficulties caused by external events that could not be foreseen or avoided, and is granted only in special cases (Skatteverket). A delayed funding round is unlikely to qualify.
- **Board duty:** if there is reason to assume that equity has fallen below half the registered share capital, the board must immediately prepare a control balance sheet (Companies Act, chapter 25, section 13). We will ask counsel to tell us when that test comes close.

## Recommendation

1. **This week, at no cost in jobs:** options 2–4, 6 and 7; a private conversation with each investor about a bridge (option 9); grant applications started; employment counsel engaged.
2. **By 10 December:** finance completes the base and downside scenarios. We share the position and the options with all staff and with the unions, open the voluntary options (8) and decide what work stops (5).
3. **Only if a gap remains in the downside case after committed measures:** one reduction (option 11), sized to close it. We negotiate it under MBL in December and decide only once that negotiation is complete, with individual conversations in the week of 11 January 2027. It isn't 30% by default: the model sets the size, which could be smaller, similar or larger.

## Package, if a reduction happens

- Paid notice under LAS, plus [two to three] months' extra pay. For comparison, Airbnb in 2020 gave every departing employee at least 14 weeks' pay, dropped the one-year equity cliff and had its recruiters help people find their next job.
- Transition support from the day of notice: the job security council where a collective agreement applies, otherwise Kammarkollegiet's basic transition support, plus our own budget of SEK [ ] per person for coaching and training.
- Equity: the most generous treatment our plan and the tax rules allow, confirmed by counsel before anything is promised.
- Laptops kept (wiped), written references, introductions across our investors' portfolio companies, and an alumni list.
- Early, individual help for people on work permits.
- Each person hears it from their own manager, in person with HR present. Access changes after the conversation, and a written summary follows the same day.

## Measures, reviewed monthly

- **Money:** cash and runway in months, net burn, gross profit as a share of costs, revenue growth (three-month average), weighted pipeline, debtor days.
- **People:** regretted leavers, sickness absence, a short pulse survey on trust and workload, overtime. Reported at team level only.
- **Financing:** bridge and Series B commitments in hand, with dates.

## Assumptions, and what would change our mind

- Gross profit covers [ ]% of costs. Below about 30%, cost measures alone can't reach break-even within the runway; without a bridge, a deeper reduction and stopping a whole line of work would be needed.
- Growth holds at 3% a month or more after the changes. If it slows, the plan must protect commercial capacity.
- The Series B slipped because of [the market / investors' feedback on growth]. If it is growth, deep cuts make the round harder to raise.
- Insiders will bridge on a credible plan. If they won't, the downside case becomes the base case.
