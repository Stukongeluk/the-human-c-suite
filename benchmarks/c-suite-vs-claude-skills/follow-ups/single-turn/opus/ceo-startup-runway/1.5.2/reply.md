Don't commit to cutting 30% this month, but don't wait either. Agree a runway target with your board, start the moves that cost no jobs this week, and decide by mid-December, on numbers, whether a cut is still needed and how big. Your cash runs out around August 2027, so a round "next summer at the earliest" leaves no margin. Under Swedish rules a cut decided today saves cash only from about March or April. By my estimate, 30% of the team buys 1.5 to 4 extra months; the moves that cost no jobs buy 1.5 to 2.5.

**The problem is a date, not a headcount.** Raising a B with weeks of cash left means bad terms or no deal. So any plan, the investors' included, has to clear this bar: cash into at least Q2 2028 even if the round slips again, or "default alive", meaning revenue covers costs before the money runs out. A percentage picked before that target exists tends to cut whatever is easiest to count.

**Rough scenarios.** These are estimates. They assume people are about 70% of costs, growth stays at 4% a month, and leavers get two months' severance on top of paid notice.

| Option | Jobs lost | Revenue covers 20% of costs | Revenue covers 35% |
|---|---|---|---|
| As is | 0 | Cash out Aug 2027 | Aug 2027 |
| No-job-loss moves (below) | 0 | Oct 2027 | Nov 2027 |
| 30% cut alone | 14 | Oct–Nov 2027 | Nov 2027–Jan 2028 |
| No-job-loss moves + bridge of 3 months' burn | 0 | Feb 2028 | Break-even ~Sep 2028, thin margin |
| No-job-loss moves + 30% cut | 14 | Jan 2028 | Break-even ~Mar 2028 |
| No-job-loss moves + bridge + 15% cut | 7 | May 2028 | Break-even ~Jun 2028, ~3 months' cash spare |

No single move gets you safely past a summer round, and the bridge does the most work. If no bridge is on offer and revenue covers little of your costs, a cut near 30% may well be right, as one part of a plan.

**This week (reversible, nobody loses their job):**
- Freeze hiring, including hires already in the nine-month plan, and don't replace people who leave.
- You and the leadership team take the first and largest pay cut until the B closes. Tell the board and the team plainly that the gap is leadership's result, because the plan bet on a round that slipped.
- Cut spend that isn't people: office, cloud, tools, consultants, and pilots or hardware that don't lead to revenue. Contractors you let go get the same notice and honesty as staff.
- Pull cash forward: discounted annual prepayment, faster collections, customer-paid pilots.
- Start the money conversations: an insider bridge, venture debt against your growing revenue, a strategic or corporate investor, and Energimyndigheten, Vinnova or EIC Accelerator funding. Grants are slow, so apply now.
- Show the team the runway scenarios and open MBL talks with the union(s) now, while they can still change the plan. Offer voluntary reduced hours, without pressure.
- Agree with the board to review runway, pipeline and regretted departures every month.

**Go back to the two investors with a deal, not a no.** They're right that delay is expensive: in this model, each month a needed cut waits costs half a month to a month of runway. They're also right that one decisive cut beats three small ones, and that running out of cash protects nobody. Ask what runway they're really targeting, and whether they'll bridge if you reach it with cost action sized to that target.

**If a gap remains in mid-December, cut once, sized to the gap:**
- Decide what work stops (a product line, a market, a pilot), not only who goes.
- The Swedish floor, which is not legal advice, so check it with employment counsel:
  - MBL negotiation before the decision.
  - Varsel to Arbetsförmedlingen at least two months before dismissals of 5 to 25 people.
  - One to six months' notice, by length of service.
  - LAS selection by length of service, with three exemptions, unless the union agrees a different list.
- You can't simply choose who stays. The newest hires go first, and they are often the commercial people behind the 4% growth that is your B story.
- Go beyond the minimum: severance on top of paid notice, TRR support from the day of notice (or paid outplacement if you have no collective agreement), longer option exercise windows, references and real rehiring priority. Everyone hears it from their own manager, in person.
