**Short answer:** three reversible moves you can start this month. Realistic by Q2: 10–25% fewer hours on the targeted admin work (my estimate), not 40% firm-wide.

**The three moves**

- **1. Aim AI at the paperwork.** Pick 3–4 high-volume workflows (policy checking, renewal and submission prep, file notes, claims intake), baseline hours per team for two weeks, then deploy.
- **2. Let the people who do the work build it.** A champion per team, paid training time, works council in before tools are chosen, and a promise: nobody loses their job over this.
- **3. Decide now where the freed hours go.** Earlier renewals, cross-sell, more advice, growth without extra hires. Saved hours only count once they become revenue.

**Guardrails**

- A qualified person signs off anything a client relies on; the error stays yours ([Air Canada was held liable for its chatbot](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-february/bc-tribunal-confirms-companies-remain-liable-information-provided-ai-chatbot/), 2024).
- Enterprise tools only: client data stays out of public chatbots.
- Measure teams, not individuals: hours per policy, errors, complaints, retention.
- Headcount decisions wait for 2–3 months of real data: two in three firms that cut jobs for AI were already rehiring ([Careerminds](https://careerminds.com/resources/cost-of-ai-layoffs), Feb 2026).

**Why not 40%:** chasing a competitor's number with no baseline by Q2 usually ends in cutting what's easiest to count. In a large field study, AI lifted support agents' output 14%, and 34% for newcomers ([Brynjolfsson et al., NBER 2023](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)).

**Two questions**

- 40% of what: revenue per head, policies per handler, or cost? And what happens if you miss Q2?
- Do you know where your handlers' hours go today?
