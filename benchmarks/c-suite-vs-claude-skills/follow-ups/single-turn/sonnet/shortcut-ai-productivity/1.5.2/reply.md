Fastest honest path: measure first, then put AI on the highest-volume admin work, with the people who do it. Treat 40% as a hypothesis to test, not a promise. Competitor claims are usually self-reported, and in a controlled trial (METR, 2025) experienced developers using AI were 19% slower while believing they were faster.

- **Move 1: Baseline two or three workflows in 30 days.** Pick the high-volume ones (policy renewals, quote intake, certificate and document handling, claims admin). Record time per case, error rate, rework and customer wait. Without this, any "40%" is a feeling.
- **Move 2: Automate the toil with the people doing it.** Run 8-week pilots on those workflows: document extraction, quote comparison, email drafts, call notes. Brokers and service staff design it, because they know the edge cases. Pay for the learning time. Keep a human sign-off on advice and compliance.
- **Move 3: Turn freed hours into growth, not exits.** Redirect capacity to renewals, cross-sell and the backlog of clients nobody had time to call. Measure hours freed, retention and new premium. Say openly that nobody loses their job because of this; roles will change. Layoffs for AI are often regretted (one 2025 survey: 55% of leaders who made them).

Realistic Q2 outcome: a measured gain in the piloted workflows, with a plan to scale. Company-wide 40% by Q2 is unlikely, and chasing it usually ends in cutting what's easiest to count.

Two questions that would change this: what exactly are competitors measuring (hours, revenue per head, cost)? And what is the real goal behind 40%: margin, growth, or a board promise?
