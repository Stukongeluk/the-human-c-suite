Aim for a measured 10-20% capacity gain by Q2 in the work AI handles well, not a company-wide 40%. That is my estimate, not a benchmark. Treat the competitors' 40% as unverified: ask what they measured (hours, output, revenue per head?) and over what period. Many AI pilots still show no measurable P&L impact.

**Three moves, fastest first:**

- **Find where the hours go (2-3 weeks).** Map tasks, not jobs, in servicing, renewals and placement. Ask the people doing the work where the repetitive effort sits: submission intake, re-keying data, certificates, policy checking, quote comparison, routine client emails. Pick the 3-5 highest-volume tasks.
- **Put AI on those tasks, with people in the loop.** Brokers, account managers and ops staff co-design it and review the output. Measure time saved per task, error rates, turnaround and client satisfaction. Keep compliance and E&O exposure in the review loop, and involve your works council or employee reps early if you have one.
- **Point the freed hours at revenue, not headcount cuts.** Use them for renewals, cross-sell and the client backlog you never had time for. If you can say "roles will change, nobody loses a job because of this", people will show you where the real waste is.

**The trap:** a target number without a diagnosis usually ends with cutting whatever is easiest to count. Productivity that is claimed rather than measured tends to reverse later.

**Two questions that would change this:**
- What is at stake behind the 40% by Q2: margin, growth or a board promise?
- Which two or three workflows eat the most hours today?
