Don't ship it as specced. Say yes to the goal (productivity, 8% is worth chasing) and no to a public stops-per-hour ranking with an automatic bottom-10% flag. Offer a 10-week pilot with quality measures and a comparison group instead, and ask operations where the 8% comes from.

**Why the leaderboard as specced will not deliver 8%**

- **Stops per hour mostly measures the route, not the driver.** Dense city centre versus rural Belgium, apartment blocks, parcels per stop, access problems, failed first attempts, how the depot loaded the van, traffic. Rank drivers on it and you rank territories. The bottom 10% will be the people with the hardest routes, and the flags will be unfair in a way drivers see at once.
- **The bottom 10% never goes away.** Even if everyone improves, 10% of 2,400 is 240 drivers flagged every week, roughly 12,500 flags a year. Most of that is noise, and it teaches drivers that the number is arbitrary.
- **People optimise the number.** Skipped or deferred hard stops, parcels left with neighbours or marked delivered early, rushed handovers, missed breaks. Stops per hour rises while first-attempt success, damage and complaints worsen, and those cost more than the minutes gained. It is also a road-safety issue when a live leaderboard sits on the screen of someone who is driving.
- **The weekly flag lands on people with no voice.** About 1,600 of your drivers work for subcontractors. A flag from you to their employer works as a dismissal signal, and a driver cannot see the data or challenge it. Expect turnover and subcontractors who walk away, which hits capacity you cannot replace quickly.

**Legal and governance exposure (not legal advice, check with counsel)**

- Monitoring and ranking individuals is a works council matter in the Netherlands (consent right on systems suited to monitoring behaviour or performance, art. 27 WOR) wherever a council exists, and for subcontractor employees that is the subcontractor's council, not yours. Belgium has its own rules on monitoring employees; get a local opinion.
- GDPR: individual performance data on people you don't employ raises joint-controller, purpose and proportionality questions, and probably a DPIA.
- Steering subcontractor drivers directly through your app and pushing them out by weekly ranking is the kind of thing that raises worker-classification and chain-liability questions. Ask counsel before it ships, not after.
- If any automation allocates work or evaluates drivers, check the EU AI Act high-risk rules for worker management (the information duty applies from December 2027, so it isn't live yet).

**Steelman for operations**

Variance between drivers is real, margins are thin, and some drivers do need help. They are right that the app should be doing more for productivity. The disagreement is about where the lost minutes actually are, and what to do with someone who is slow.

**What I would offer instead**

1. **Find the minutes first (2-3 weeks).** From existing app and scan data, aggregated by depot, route and time of day: where do stops lose time? I'd expect the big items to be route planning and stop sequencing, depot loading order, failed deliveries and redeliveries, address and access data quality, app friction per stop. That is product-and-tech work I can own and commit to.
2. **Show drivers their own data, privately.** Their stops per hour, first-attempt rate and a route-adjusted comparison, plus a way to report obstacles (blocked access, bad address). No public leaderboard. Pair every speed number with a quality number (first-attempt success, damage, complaints).
3. **Flag routes and depots, not drivers.** The weekly report to subcontractors becomes the bottom 10% of routes and depots by route-adjusted time lost, with the causes. Individual conversations remain the subcontractor's call, based on what they see, not an automatic list from us.
4. **Test the 8% claim.** Pilot in a few comparable depots against matched control depots for 10 weeks. Success is agreed in advance: productivity up, first-attempt success, damage, complaints, safety incidents and subcontractor driver turnover not worse. Involve subcontractors and driver representatives in designing it, before choosing the metrics.

**Two questions that would change my answer**

- Where does the 8% come from: a pilot, a competitor, a model? If operations have data from a comparable setup that included quality and turnover, I want to see it.
- What happens to a flagged driver? If the answer is "the subcontractor drops them", the real cost belongs in the business case, including recruiting and onboarding replacements.

**A line to use with operations**

"I want the productivity gain, and I'd rather get it where the time is lost than rank drivers on a number that mostly reflects their route. Give me ten weeks, a control group and quality guardrails, and if a ranking beats my alternative on those terms we ship it."

I'd have this conversation privately with the head of operations first, then bring the CEO and CFO in on turnover and subcontractor capacity costs.
