Don't ship it as specced. Say yes to the goal (more productivity), no to a live individual leaderboard and a weekly bottom-10% flag, and offer Operations a route-adjusted, depot-level alternative you can test in eight weeks. The 8% is a claim, not a measurement, so ask where it comes from before anyone builds anything.

**Why the spec fails**

1. **Stops per hour mostly measures the route, not the driver.** Parcel density, apartment blocks versus detached houses, Amsterdam centre versus rural Limburg or West Flanders, time windows, loading delays at the depot, and failed attempts all swing it. A raw ranking will mostly rank routes, and it will flag the same people on hard rounds every week.
2. **A "bottom 10%" always exists.** Of 2,400 drivers, that is about 240 flagged every week, even if everyone improves or the fleet is already excellent. It is a rank-order rule, not a performance standard.
3. **People optimise the number.** Expect skipped breaks, rushed hand-overs, "delivered" scans that aren't, parcels left in unsafe places, and speed on the road. You'd see stops per hour rise while failed deliveries, damage, complaints and accidents rise too. That cost lands on customers, your brand and road safety. A live leaderboard that drivers glance at in the cab adds a distraction risk.
4. **The flag passes the problem on, not the responsibility.** About 1,600 drivers work for subcontractors. A weekly list sent to their employer is an invitation to drop or cut hours of the people on it, with no check on whether the number was fair. You would have built the tool and set the rule, so it is also yours. Many of those drivers have the thinnest buffer and the least voice.
5. **The legal and relationship floor is real.** This is not legal advice, so have counsel check it. In the Netherlands, systems suited to monitoring employees' performance need works council consent (art. 27(1)(l) WOR), which applies at your own entity and potentially at each employer using the data. The data is personal data, so a DPIA and a clear controller/processor split with the subcontractors are needed. Under the AI Act, AI that monitors or evaluates workers is high-risk, with information duties from December 2027 (the date moved in 2026). Ranking people by a metric inside your own app and sending it to another employer is also the kind of thing that can blur the line between subcontractor and your own workforce. Ask counsel about that too.

**What I'd offer Operations instead**

- **Route-adjusted, team-level view.** Compare each depot or route cluster with its own past, adjusted for density, parcel mix and address type. Pair stops per hour with first-attempt delivery rate, damage, complaints and safety incidents, so nobody can win one number by breaking another.
- **Drivers see their own data, privately.** Their own trend and what affects it, not a public ranking. Let them flag why a round was hard.
- **Fix the system first.** Look at what drags the slow rounds: depot loading and sequencing, bad addresses, access problems, route design, app friction. That is where an 8% would actually come from.
- **If an individual concern remains,** the subcontractor's manager or yours talks to the driver with the route context in hand, and a person decides. A number alone doesn't trigger action.
- **Design it with the people affected.** Take it to your works council(s), the subcontractors and a few driver representatives before it is built, not after.

**Test it, don't argue about it.** Run 6 to 8 weeks in a handful of matched depots against control depots, with the team-level view and the process fixes. Agree up front what you'd measure (stops per hour, first-attempt success, damage, complaints, incidents, driver turnover and subcontractor churn) and what result would make you roll it out or stop. If the pilot shows nothing, Operations has its answer from data and not from you. This is reversible and starts now; contract changes with subcontractors and any ranking should wait for the results.

**What I'd ask Operations before the meeting**
- Where does the 8% come from: another market, a pilot, a vendor? Did the comparison hold quality and safety constant?
- What share of the variation in stops per hour is explained by route and depot? Your data team can answer this in days.
- What happens to a flagged driver at the subcontractor today, and what do your contracts say about it?

**Assumptions and what would change my mind.** I assumed you have works councils or can consult driver representatives, and that the subcontractors are separate employers. If the route-adjusted data shows large driver-level variation that persists after adjustment and without a quality cost, a private coaching conversation for the lowest group would be reasonable, still with human review and without a public board.
