**Bottom Line:** Don't bring the board a "no". Bring a staged discipline plan with a pre-agreed trigger for cuts. Layoffs become the fallback, not the opening move.

**What (confidence)**
- Cutting 15% (~38 people) saves roughly €3-4M a year at ~€90k loaded cost. [medium: my assumption, check against your payroll]
- In the Netherlands, a collective dismissal means works council advice, UWV/union steps and transition payments. Year-one savings will be far smaller than the headline. [medium]
- After the €12M loss you hold about €28M. Whether that reaches 2028 depends on your burn, which I don't have. [assumed]

**Why**
- The board wants visible discipline. Discipline is the proof, not the headcount. Cutting 38 people from teams that must ship two 2028 titles risks the only assets that can recover the loss.
- Games talent is hard to rehire; cutting now and re-hiring in 2027 costs more than it saves.

**How to act (Thursday)**
1. **Cost plan, same savings target (~€3-4M):** hiring freeze, no replacement of natural attrition (typically 8-12% a year), cut contractors and external marketing/publishing spend, review leases and tools.
2. **Stage-gate both projects:** release funding in tranches against milestones (vertical slice, playtest data, publisher or platform interest). The board gets control, not just a promise.
3. **Cash floor trigger:** the board agrees now that if cash drops below a set level (for example €20M) or a milestone is missed, you run a targeted restructuring. This answers "what if you're wrong?"
4. **Post-mortem:** present a short, honest account of why the launch failed and what has changed in greenlighting.
5. **Redeploy:** move people from the flopped title's live team onto the 2028 projects before looking outside.
6. **Later option:** a co-development or publishing deal to extend runway.

**Before you go in**
Get your CFO's monthly burn and a 24-month cash forecast. Without it the board will pick the number you can't defend. Call key directors before Thursday.

**Your decision:** back this plan with the cash-floor trigger. If the board still insists on cuts, propose a smaller, voluntary-first reduction (under 5%) tied to the same trigger.
