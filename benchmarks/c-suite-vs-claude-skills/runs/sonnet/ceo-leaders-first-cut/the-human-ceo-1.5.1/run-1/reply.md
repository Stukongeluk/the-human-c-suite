**Recommendation: don't cut 15% now. Offer the board a phased discipline plan with a cash floor and dated checkpoints.** Cutting about 38 people saves roughly €2.5-3m a year (my estimate, at €70-80k loaded cost each), minus severance and lost knowledge. You hold €40m against a loss that is largely a one-off launch result, and your 2028 projects depend on the people you'd cut.

**What leadership should do**
1. **Own it first.** Say plainly that the flop is leadership's result. Executives and board take the first and largest pay and bonus cut, as a signal of trust, not a savings plan.
2. **Spend and focus.** Audit marketing, licences, vendors and office. Pause anything not feeding the two 2028 projects.
3. **Freeze hiring** and use attrition. Studios often lose 10-15% a year, so 25-35 people may leave in 12 months without a layoff (my estimate; check your turnover).
4. **Redeploy** launch-team people to the 2028 projects, with paid reskilling.
5. **Bridge revenue:** live-ops on the shipped game, co-development, or publisher or milestone funding for one project.
6. **Cash floor:** agree a minimum with the board (say €20m) and checkpoints in Q2 and Q4. Below it, you revisit staffing with evidence.

**Why not cut now:** 38 redundancies trigger collective-redundancy rules (union and works council consultation, UWV), take months, and push your best people to leave just before 2028 production ramps up. Not legal advice; involve counsel and your works council now. If cuts ever become unavoidable, do them once, decently, with notice and paid job-search support.

**Where I could be wrong:** if burn is far above €12m a year or a milestone slips, runway shrinks. That is what the triggers are for.
