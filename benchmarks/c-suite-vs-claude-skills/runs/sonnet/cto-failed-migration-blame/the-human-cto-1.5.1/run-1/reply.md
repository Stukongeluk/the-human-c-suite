Don't fire the platform team lead now. Take ownership yourself, give the board accountability with names and dates, and let a blameless review establish the cause. You don't yet know this was one person's fault, and a €2 million, 9-hour outage rarely is.

**Why not fire now**
- A migration this size passes through approvals: who signed off the plan, whether rollback was rehearsed, whether the window was realistic, how much testing was funded. If those were weak, the cause is the setup, and the lead just held it. The same failure returns under a new lead.
- Firing before the facts are in tells engineers that raising risk is dangerous. Your next migration gets fewer warnings.
- Legal risk: in the Netherlands a dismissal needs a defensible ground and a proper process, and "the board wanted someone" is not one. Speak to an employment lawyer first. This is not legal advice.

**This week**
1. **Own it, privately first.** Call the chair before any board meeting. The result is yours, and you say so before discussing anyone else's part.
2. **Start a blameless review** with a deadline of about 10 working days, led by someone outside the migration team. Cover the timeline, who approved what, what was tested, why rollback took 9 hours, and whether the lead raised risks and what happened to them. Include your own decisions.
3. **Give the board real accountability.** Every action gets an owner, a deadline and a measure, such as a timed rollback rehearsal and a go/no-go checklist you sign. Report monthly. If the review shows someone ignored explicit rules or hid risks, individual consequences can follow, on facts and through proper process.
4. **Tell the lead yourself** that a review is happening and they will be heard. Their account is your best input.
5. **Keep written records factual.** Dates and sources, no guesses about motives.

**A line for the chair:** "I own this outage. Firing someone before we know the cause fixes nothing and makes the next failure likelier. In two weeks you'll get the review, owners and dates for every fix, and a clear answer on whether anyone broke our rules."
