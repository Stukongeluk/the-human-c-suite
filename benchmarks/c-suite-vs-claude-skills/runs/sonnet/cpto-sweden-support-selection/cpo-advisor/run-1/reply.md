**Bottom Line:** Don't use performance ranking as the selection method. In Sweden, a redundancy (arbetsbrist) selection is governed by LAS seniority rules, not by merit. Your real design decision is what the 21-person team needs to do, not who scores best.

**What (confidence)**

1. **Selection is rule-bound, not discretionary.** (Medium-high; confirm with Swedish employment counsel.)
   - Under LAS, layoffs for redundancy follow a *turordning* (last in, first out) within each "turordningskrets": the same collective-agreement area and the same employer operating unit (driftsenhet), here Malmö, grouped by category of work.
   - Only employees whose work can be covered by the remaining staff count as comparable, so you need to define the roles properly.
   - Collective agreements can change the rules, so check yours.
   - A limited exception exists (up to 3 employees you may exempt, with union agreement where required for larger employers). That is a narrow tool for key skills, not a way to apply a performance filter to nine people.
   - Selecting on tickets resolved and CSAT risks unfair-dismissal claims and damages. (High that this is a risk.)

2. **The process has fixed steps.** (Medium-high; confirm timings with counsel.)
   - Union negotiation (MBL) comes before you decide.
   - Notify Arbetsförmedlingen (varsel) before notices go out. For 5-25 affected people this is normally at least 2 months before, so build that into the timeline.
   - Check whether redeployment to other roles is possible (omplacering) before dismissing anyone.
   - Notice periods depend on length of service and the collective agreement.

3. **The metrics are weak even where you may use them.** (Medium.)
   - Tickets resolved and CSAT now come from a mix of work that AI has changed. Remaining agents handle harder, slower, lower-CSAT tickets, so the numbers punish people on the toughest queues.
   - CSAT is also noisy at per-person level, and it measures individuals while support quality is a team outcome.

**Why:** You are redesigning what the human team does, so the 21 roles should come from that design. Starting from a ranking of people gets the order of decisions backwards, and in Sweden the law decides the order anyway.

**How to Act**

1. Define the 21-person target: which skills and roles you need (AI-escalation handling, complex or enterprise cases, quality review and AI training/tuning, language coverage). Base this on real ticket data after the AI change, not on who scores well today. (This week)
2. Look first at alternatives to dismissal: voluntary departures with severance, redeployment into other teams, and natural attrition and open roles. Even a few reduce the number of forced exits. (Before the union meeting)
3. Engage Swedish employment counsel and the union early. Get the turordning list, the exemption options and the exact varsel dates. (Now)
4. If selection is needed, apply the legally required order. Use performance data only where it is legally relevant, for example to support a documented, agreed exemption for skills you cannot lose. Document the criteria before anyone sees names.
5. Redeploy and invest in the remaining 21: retrain them for escalation and AI-quality work, and agree on how you will measure the new team (team-level resolution quality, not individual ticket counts).
6. Communicate honestly and on time, with support for those leaving (outplacement, references, and so on).

**Your Decision:** Do you want to (a) run the process as a lawful, seniority-based reduction with a defined skills exemption, or (b) first explore voluntary exits and redeployment to cut the forced-exit number? I recommend (b) first, then (a) for any remainder. Please have counsel validate before HR sends anything to staff.

*Caveat: I am not a Swedish employment lawyer. The LAS and MBL points above are the general framework and need local legal confirmation.*
