I wouldn't use tickets resolved and CSAT, and in Sweden you likely can't. For redundancies, the Employment Protection Act (LAS) sets the order, normally last in, first out, per unit and agreement area. Performance isn't a criterion. This isn't legal advice; confirm with HR and employment counsel.

**Selection approach (draft)**

1. **Fewer exits first.** Model what AI removes in tickets and hours, not heads. Then use a hiring freeze and attrition, voluntary exits, and redeployment into growing work (second-line, AI quality review, knowledge base, escalations). Nine may shrink.
2. **If cuts remain, the law sets the order.** Apply LAS per unit and agreement area, or the union-agreed list. You may exempt three people; use that for documented business needs like language or specialist skills, not a performance ranking.
3. **Negotiate before deciding.** The Co-determination Act (MBL) requires negotiating with the unions first, with the real numbers and alternatives, while they can still change the plan.
4. **Notify and time it.** Five or more redundancies must be notified to the Public Employment Service at least two months ahead. Check notice periods, re-employment priority and collective agreement terms.
5. **Support from day one.** Involve the job security council (TRR for private-sector white-collar staff) when notice is given, with coaching and paid training.
6. **Keep ticket and CSAT data off individuals.** They reflect queue mix and customer type, and AI now takes the easy tickets. Use them for team-level workload planning only.
