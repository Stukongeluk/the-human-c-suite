Don't absorb this quietly and don't fight it publicly. Take the CEO a decision to make, in a private 1:1 this week: the three features are a real commitment, so the question is what moves to make room, not whether you'll try. The relationship usually suffers more from a silent Q1 miss in March than from an honest trade-off in November.

**1. Do the homework first (2-3 days)**
- Ask the customer-facing owner exactly what was promised: scope, wording, "end of Q1" in writing or just said?
- Get a rough estimate from the engineers who'd build each feature. Ranges and confidence levels are fine, but not your gut.
- Pull your current Q1 roadmap with the revenue, churn or customer problem behind each item. Also note what is already committed to other customers.
- Ask what the customer actually needs. Often it is the problem behind the features, and a smaller version or a configuration may cover it.

**2. Open the 1:1 as an ally**
Start with the shared goal: keeping this customer. Then show the arithmetic. "I want to deliver this. The team is at 100%, and these three need about X weeks. Here are the options:"
- **A. Swap:** build all three; name the roadmap items that slip, with what each slip costs.
- **B. Cut scope:** the one or two features that matter most to them by end of Q1, the rest in Q2 with a date you can stand behind.
- **C. Pilot:** a thin version with the customer as design partner by end of Q1, full version later.
- **D. Add capacity:** a contractor or borrowed team, with the real cost and ramp-up time.

"On top of everything" is not on the list, because it only works through overtime and quality loss, and it will end in a missed date anyway.

**3. A line you can use**
"I don't want to say no to this customer, and I don't want to promise a date the team can't hit. Can we decide together which of these gives them the most by March?"

**4. Protect the future**
Ask for one habit, framed as protecting him: before any customer commitment, ask product and engineering for a quick estimate. Sales and executives can promise "we'll come back with a date in two weeks", which keeps deals alive without locking you in.

**Questions that would change my advice:** Is the promise written down? Did the customer's renewal depend on it? Is your CEO the type who responds to numbers, or to being asked about the customer? Tell me and I'll tailor the conversation.
