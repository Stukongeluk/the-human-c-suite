I'd take a different plan to the board than the three moves the leadership team agreed. Fix the customer problems first, make the rebrand small and gated, do one designed restructure, and review contractors and juniors instead of ending them all. A buyer checks churn and NPS in due diligence, and your NPS has fallen 25 points. Profit gained by cutting capability while customers leave will be priced back out of the sale.

The full board paper is in `board-paper.md`. Everything in it marked [FILL] needs your own numbers. I don't have your data, so my figures are labelled illustrations.

**Why not the three moves as agreed**

- **They are solutions to a problem nobody has named yet.** None of them is tied to why customers are leaving. A new name does not fix an unhappy customer.
- **A fourth restructure in two years.** Each one brings lost knowledge, new hand-offs and departures. Ask what the last three failed to solve and why this one will. If the answer is vague, the structure is not the problem.
- **Ending all junior and contractor roles** removes your cheapest capacity and your future seniors in one step, and contractors often hold knowledge of systems inherited from four companies.
- **Illustration, with my assumptions.** On €100m recurring revenue, 3 extra points of churn is €3m a year. Over 18 months that is about €4.5m, or about €13.5m of sale value at an assumed 3x revenue. Ending 60 juniors at €55k saves about €3.3m a year gross, before severance and rehiring. Replace these with your actuals.

**What I'd propose instead**

1. **Customers first.** In 30 days, rank the top five customer problems with evidence, and give each at-risk large account an executive owner and a save plan.
2. **Rebrand, small and gated.** Decide the name in Q1 and tell staff first. Do only the must-haves: name, legal entities, domains, contracts. Launch to customers when the save plans exist and the top problems are improving.
3. **One restructure, designed.** Run a layers-and-spans analysis per company first. Use freezes, attrition and redeployment for duplicated roles. Involve works councils before deciding, then hold stability for 12 months.
4. **Contractors and juniors.** Review every contractor by role, rate and knowledge held. End what is genuinely not needed, with proper notice. Keep a planned junior intake.
5. **Profit before people.** Four acquisitions mean duplicate vendors, licences, cloud, offices and tools. Add pricing, cross-sell and stopping low-value projects. Finance sizes each by day 30.
6. **Leaders first.** The leadership team's variable pay takes the first reduction, and bonuses are tied to retention, NPS and quality as well as EBITDA. The result is the leadership team's to own, and the paper says so.

**What the board gets in return:** a quarterly EBITDA bridge by lever, churn and NPS by company, and a sale-readiness story that holds up in due diligence.

**Germany, to confirm with employment counsel (not legal advice)**

- Find out which entities have a Betriebsrat and whether there is a group works council. For a major change in a company with more than 20 employees, expect a reconciliation of interests and social plan negotiation (§§111-112 BetrVG).
- Collective dismissals above the thresholds need advance notice to the employment agency (§17 KSchG), and operational dismissals need a social selection (Sozialauswahl).
- Check false self-employment rules before ending or converting contractor arrangements.
- Supervisory board worker representation can apply from 500 employees.
