# Board paper: Protecting enterprise value ahead of the sale

**Prepared by:** CEO · **For decision at:** next board meeting · **Status:** proposal, with the numbers still to be filled from our own systems (marked [FILL])

## 1. Decision requested

The leadership team proposed three moves: one new brand in Q1, a fourth restructure to remove management layers, and ending all contractor and junior roles. This paper recommends a different sequence that serves the same goal, higher profit and a strong sale in about 18 months:

1. Fix the customer problems that drive NPS and churn first. This is the largest and most certain profit lever we have.
2. Reduce the rebrand to a small, gated first step, and launch it to customers when two readiness conditions are met.
3. Replace the fourth restructure with one designed restructure, based on a layers-and-spans analysis, done once and consulted with works councils before it is decided.
4. Replace the blanket ending of contractor and junior roles with a review of contractor spend and a protected junior intake, and take cost out of vendors, duplicated systems and pricing first.
5. Leadership takes the first reduction in variable pay, and bonuses are linked to retention, NPS and quality as well as profit.

## 2. The problem in one sentence

Our customers are leaving faster and rating us lower (NPS +31 to +6 this year, churn rising), and a buyer will check exactly those numbers in due diligence. Profit that we gain by cutting capability while churn grows will be priced back out of the sale.

Responsibility for this sits with the leadership team, me included. This paper is our plan to fix it, not an explanation of whose fault it was.

## 4. Why the three moves, as agreed, put the sale at risk

- **The order is wrong.** Rebrand, restructure and ending contracts are solutions. Nobody has yet shown which customer problem each one fixes. A new name does not repair an unhappy customer, and three of the four companies' customers will meet it while their top problems are still open.
- **A fourth restructure in two years.** Each reorganisation brings new hand-offs, a productivity dip and departures of the people who know how things work. Before doing another, we should name what the last three failed to solve and why this one will. If the answer is not specific, the structure is not the problem.
- **Ending all junior and contractor roles at once** removes the cheapest capacity and the future talent pipeline in a single step. AWS's CEO called replacing junior staff "one of the dumbest things I've ever heard" (August 2025). Contractors often hold specific knowledge of the systems inherited from four companies, and it leaves with them.
- **What a buyer pays for.** A buyer pays for retained customers, recurring revenue and capable teams, and checks churn, customer satisfaction and key-person risk in due diligence. Research on private equity buyouts finds very different employment and productivity effects by deal (Davis et al., NBER 2019, revised 2024). That study does not examine cost cuts timed just before a sale, so we should not claim it supports either side. The buyer's checklist is the relevant evidence.

## 6. Recommendation: the plan

### 6.1 Customers first (starts now, owner: [FILL, a named executive])

- Within 30 days: rank the top five customer problems, each with its own evidence (NPS comments, churn reasons, support tickets, revenue at risk).
- Within 30 days: personal outreach by an executive to the [FILL] largest at-risk accounts, with a named owner and a save plan for each.
- One accountable customer owner per major account across the four companies, so customers stop dealing with four organisations.
- Targets (to be set when baselines are known, by day 45): churn, NPS, time to resolve, renewals. Reported to the board each quarter.

### 6.2 Rebrand: small, phased, gated

- Q1: decide the name and the story; tell all staff first (their own manager first), then customers through their account managers. Must-have scope only: name, legal entities, domains and redirects, contract templates.
- Everything else (signage, product UI, collateral) follows the product and customer roadmap, funded within a fixed budget [FILL].
- Customer-facing launch when two conditions are met: save plans exist for the top at-risk accounts, and the top customer problems show measurable improvement. If these are not met by the planned date, we move the date, not the conditions.
- Owner: [FILL]. Cost cap: [FILL].

### 6.3 Structure: one restructure, designed, then stability

- Weeks 0-6: layers and spans analysis per company, with the duplicated functions (finance, HR, IT, support) mapped across the four.
- Decide on the evidence, then do it once. Where roles are duplicated, use hiring freezes, attrition, redeployment and paid reskilling first.
- Works councils involved before any decision (see section 8), with the real numbers.
- No further restructure for at least 12 months after it.

### 6.4 Contractors and juniors: review, don't blanket-end

- Contractors: list every contractor by role, rate, tenure and the knowledge held. End contracts where the work is no longer needed or is duplicated, with proper notice and honesty. Where a contractor has de facto worked as staff for a long time, convert them to employees if that is cheaper and safer, after legal advice.
- Juniors: protect a planned annual junior intake and training budget. They are the least expensive staff, the most active with new tools, and the source of the seniors we will need.
- Any AI-driven claim that a role is no longer needed has to be measured in our own work first.

### 6.5 Profit levers ahead of people

Waste and spend after four acquisitions (duplicate vendors, licences, cloud, offices, insurance, tooling), pricing and packaging, cross-sell to the combined customer base, and stopping low-value projects. Finance to size each lever by day 30 [FILL].

### 6.6 Leaders go first

The leadership team's variable pay for the year is reduced first, with a defined share [FILL, for the board to set] deferred or waived. Executive bonuses are linked to retention, NPS and quality as well as EBITDA, so that nobody is paid for profit that is borrowed from the sale price.

## 8. German legal and consultation steps (to confirm with employment counsel; this is not legal advice)

- Ask whether each entity has a Betriebsrat, and whether there is a Gesamt- or Konzernbetriebsrat. Involve them at the problem stage.
- For a major operational change in a company with more than 20 employees, the employer must negotiate a reconciliation of interests and a social plan with the works council (§§111-112 BetrVG).
- Collective dismissals above the thresholds (for example 10% or more than 25 people in an establishment of 60 to 499 within 30 days) must be notified to the employment agency in advance (§17 KSchG), and employer-initiated termination agreements count towards the threshold.
- Dismissals for operational reasons need a social selection (Sozialauswahl) by length of service, age, maintenance obligations and severe disability (§1(3) KSchG). Performance rankings are not a selection method.
- Co-determination applies to systems suited to monitoring employee behaviour or performance (§87(1) no. 6 BetrVG). That covers many analytics and AI tools used in the restructure.
- Statutory notice periods run up to seven months after 20 years of service (§622 BGB).
- Supervisory board worker representation starts at 500 employees; counsel should confirm how this applies to the group structure.
- Contractor arrangements: check false self-employment and agency-labour rules before ending or converting them.
- Where a temporary dip in demand is the issue, Kurzarbeit needs the works council's agreement; the maximum duration falls from 24 to 12 months on 1 January 2027 unless extended. This is not our situation today and is mentioned only as a bridge, never as a structural answer.

## 10. Measures (money and people, reviewed quarterly)

- Money: EBITDA bridge by lever, gross and net of one-off costs; covenant headroom; recurring revenue, gross and net retention.
- Customers: churn by company and cohort, NPS and its comments, resolution times, at-risk accounts.
- People: regretted attrition, pulse survey, absence, junior intake and progression, contractor spend versus plan.
- Not used: individual productivity metrics of any kind. Reports are for teams, never ranked individuals.

## 11. Assumptions, and what would change my mind

- Assumption: churn and NPS fall for fixable reasons (service, integration, product, account coverage). If the diagnosis shows the decline is mostly one segment or one company, the fix gets narrower and cheaper.
- Assumption: the owners value a clean due diligence story. If the owners will sell on a fixed EBITDA multiple regardless of churn, the case for B weakens and we should discuss that openly.
- Would change my mind on structure: layers-and-spans data showing a layer that adds nothing, with a plan that works councils help design.
- Would change my mind on contractors and juniors: costs and knowledge risk measured and found to favour ending specific groups.
- Would make the plan wrong: a covenant or liquidity problem the paper has not seen. If one exists, we should say so now and build a different plan around it, with leadership pay first.
