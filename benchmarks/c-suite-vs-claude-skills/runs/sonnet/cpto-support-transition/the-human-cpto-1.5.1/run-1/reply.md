I'd plan this as a gated move from 18 to 6, not a fixed 12-person cut, and I'd keep involuntary exits as the last step. I've drafted the full people plan here: `people-plan.md`. The reason for the gates is that "AI resolves most tickets" is not yet the same as "6 people can serve our customers well". Klarna said in 2024 that its AI did the work of 700 agents, then said in 2025 that quality had suffered and began hiring human agents again.

**The plan in short**

- **Months 0-2, understand.** Measure what the AI really resolves (customer-confirmed, no reopen within 7 days, not just "deflected"). Compare CSAT and reopen rates for AI and human handling, by language. Calculate what 6 people must cover: escalation volume x handling time, plus hours, holidays and Swedish, Finnish and English cover. Bring employee representatives and unions in now, with the real numbers.
- **Months 2-5, absorb.** Hiring freeze, attrition not replaced, voluntary options opened, leaders take the first and largest cut. Headcount about 16 to 14.
- **Months 5-8, redeploy.** Move people into second-line, AI quality and knowledge work, customer success and onboarding, and internal vacancies, with paid learning time. This is the pattern IKEA's Ingka followed, retraining about 8,500 call-centre staff instead of cutting them. About 12 to 10.
- **Months 8-12, settle.** The last step to 6 only if the quality, workload and people indicators have been green for two months.
- Each step is released by a gate: customer metrics at least as good as before, escalation workload per person inside an agreed limit, pulse and absence not worse. If a gate fails, we pause and tell people.

**If involuntary reductions are still needed**, do it once and by the local rules, with a documented case that every earlier step was not enough.

- **Sweden:** you negotiate with the unions first (MBL). Selection is by length of service (LAS), not manager choice, with three exemptions per employer. Arbetsförmedlingen must be notified at least two months ahead for 5 to 25 people. Job security council support (TRR, if your agreement covers it) starts at notice.
- **Finland:** co-operation negotiations apply (the full act from 50 employees). Change-security training worth at least one month's salary is owed after five years' service, and a four-month rehire duty applies at 50 or more employees.
- **Both:** apply the stricter process at both sites. The 12 should come from the capacity model and legal order, not from which city is cheaper to cut.

Consultation, notice and packages mean the first-year saving will be well below the annual figure, because the costs come first. Customers must be told when they talk to an AI (AI Act Article 50), with a human route kept. The plan also covers the package, communication (each person hears it from their own manager, representatives first) and the monthly indicators.

This is not legal advice; Swedish and Finnish counsel and HR need to check it.

**What I assumed, and what would change it:** I have no data from you yet. If the AI's quality is weaker in one language, that team stays larger. If attrition is low, the timeline stretches. Three questions would sharpen it: which collective agreements apply, how many people you employ in each country in total, and your real annual attrition.
