## Why I would not just announce "18 to 6"

- **Resolution rate is not the same as a smaller workload.** When AI removes the easy tickets, the average human ticket gets harder, longer and more emotional. Six people may handle fewer tickets each but need more skill and more recovery time. Plan on handle time per human ticket rising, and measure it.
- **The people who know why customers get stuck are the ones you are cutting.** That knowledge is what the AI is trained and corrected with. If they leave before the knowledge base and escalation rules are mature, quality drops and nobody is left who can tell.
- **Support is a primary source of product insight.** Support tickets are the cheapest continuous research you have. Cut the team and you cut your product team's ear to the customer unless you deliberately keep that loop.
- **A sudden cut in headcount shows up in how remaining staff behave.** Six people who have watched twelve colleagues leave will be careful, risk-averse and quietly looking for other jobs. You need your best people to stay, so the way you treat leavers is also a retention plan for the six.
- **Doing this "over the next year" gives you a real advantage.** A year allows attrition, internal moves and retraining, which are the gentlest tools and often get a large share of the way there on their own.

If after reading this you still want 6 by month 12, the plan below can do it. The gates just become stricter and the severance and redeployment spend goes up.

## The plan

### Phase 0: Prove it (weeks 1 to 6, before anyone is told a number)

- Measure true resolution: tickets closed with no reopen and no repeat contact within 7 days, plus CSAT on AI-handled versus human-handled tickets. Sample at least 200 AI-closed tickets by hand.
- Break the ticket mix down by category, language (Swedish, Finnish, English, others) and customer tier. Check AI quality per language. Finnish is a morphologically hard language and models are often weaker there than in Swedish or English, so verify this and do not assume it.
- Define the post-AI job map: what work remains, how many hours a week, and which skills it needs.
- Set the **gate metrics** and write them down: AI true-resolution rate, CSAT delta, reopen rate, escalation time, and human handle time. Each headcount step is released only when these hold for 8 consecutive weeks.
- Bring in employment counsel in both countries and, separately, HR and finance. Decide who is in the planning circle. Keep it small and keep it confidential, and expect it to leak anyway, so prepare the message early.

### Phase 1: Redesign the work (months 1 to 3)

Define the roles that remain. A realistic target structure for 6:

| Role | Count | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Senior escalation specialists | 3 | Complex, emotional and high-value customers. Own the hardest cases end to end. |
| AI support quality lead | 1 | Reviews failed conversations, owns the knowledge base and escalation rules, reports on AI quality weekly. |
| Support operations and insights | 1 | Tooling, staffing, reporting, and the feedback loop into product. |
| Team lead | 1 | People, coverage, customer relationships. Could be one of the above if the team is small. |

Be careful with coverage. Six people across two cities and several languages, with holidays and sickness, will not cover extended hours. Decide explicitly which hours and languages are covered by humans, and what the AI says outside those hours. If that coverage is not acceptable, the real number is 7 or 8, and it is better to know that now.

### Phase 2: Reduce without layoffs first (months 2 to 9)

Use these in this order, and be honest with people about which ones you are using:

1. **Hiring freeze and no backfill** on any leaver. Natural attrition in a support team is often 15 to 25% a year, which could be 3 to 4 of your 18 people. (🟡 depends on your actual rate)
2. **Internal moves with real training**, not a token offer. Candidates for the 6 roles above should be chosen on a transparent, written skills basis. Other good destinations are customer success, onboarding, QA and AI evaluation, technical writing for the knowledge base, sales support, and product operations. Pay for the training and protect the time to do it.
3. **Voluntary exits** with a better-than-statutory package, offered first. Volunteers are cheaper, faster and kinder than forced exits, and they keep goodwill with the people who stay.
4. **Reduced hours or role-sharing** where people want it.
5. **Redundancy as the last step**, only for what remains after steps 1 to 4, and only after the gate metrics support it.

Expected effect, as a planning assumption only: attrition 3 to 4, internal moves 3 to 4, voluntary exits 2 to 3, leaving roughly 2 to 4 compulsory redundancies to reach 6. If your numbers come out very differently, the plan changes.

### Phase 3: Selection and legal process (run per country, with counsel)

This is where the two cities differ and where mistakes are expensive. These are the points to confirm with local counsel. I am flagging what to check, not giving legal advice.

**Sweden (Stockholm, 10 people)**
- Redundancy (arbetsbrist) in Sweden is normally subject to the Employment Protection Act (LAS) order of priority, which in practice means last in, first out within each "turnover unit" and collective agreement area. This is a significant constraint: it can force you to keep the people who joined most recently away from the roles you design, unless you reach an agreement with the union. Check whether you are covered by a collective agreement and whether it changes the rules. (🟡 verify)
- If employees are unionised, there are negotiation duties with the union before decisions on significant changes, and notice duties to the Swedish Public Employment Service (Arbetsförmedlingen) above certain numbers. Notice periods are tied to length of service. Check the thresholds. (🟡 verify)
- The most common route that works in Sweden is a negotiated agreement with the union on selection and on a package, rather than a purely unilateral process. Plan time for it.

**Finland (Helsinki, 8 people)**
- Finnish dismissals for economic and production reasons require a genuine, substantial and permanent reduction in the work, and a duty to offer other suitable work and, in many cases, retraining, first. "AI now handles it" can qualify, but you must document that the work has actually gone. If you re-hire for similar work shortly afterwards, you risk liability. (🟡 verify)
- The Finnish co-operation procedure (change negotiations) applies to employers above a size threshold and has a minimum notice and negotiation period. Check whether your Finnish entity is above or below the threshold, because the process differs. Smaller employers still have duties to consult. (🔴 I am not sure which side of the threshold you are on)
- Finland has change security (muutosturva) arrangements and employer duties around outplacement support in some cases. Check what applies. (🟡 verify)

**Both countries**
- If you employ through a single entity or hire through a vendor or EU-wide agreement, the structure changes who the employer is. Confirm.
- Selection criteria must be objective, written and applied consistently. Do not use anything that correlates with age, gender, parental leave, sickness, ethnicity or union activity. Do not let anyone's manager pick the names informally.
- Employees on parental leave, sick leave, or with union or safety representative roles have extra protection in both countries.
- AI-driven decisions about who stays cannot be a black box. If you use any data to score people, a human has to be able to explain and defend every score.

### Phase 4: Support for those who leave (build this in from the start)

- Notice periods paid in full and not shortened, with garden leave where appropriate.
- Severance above statutory, scaled by tenure. Set the budget with finance now (see decisions below).
- Paid outplacement and a CV and interview service. External career coaching is worth more than a generous cash offer to many people.
- Time off to interview during notice, and a clear reference policy.
- Training budget they can spend after they leave.
- An honest written explanation of why the decision was made, delivered by their own manager in person, with HR present.
- Keep the door open: a re-hire list for 12 months.

### Phase 5: Make the six strong (continuous)

- Retention bonuses for the 6 for the transition period, paid at gates, so people stay until the work has actually moved.
- Real growth: pay increases and new titles for the new roles, so the remaining team is not just "the survivors".
- Rotation across escalation, quality and ops so nobody burns out on only hard tickets.
- Weekly AI quality review and a monthly customer insight report that goes to product and engineering.
- A written rule that headcount can go back up if the gate metrics slip. That is what keeps the six honest about quality.
