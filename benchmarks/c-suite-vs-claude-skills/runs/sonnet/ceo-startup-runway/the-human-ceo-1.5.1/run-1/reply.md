**Don't cut 30% this month, and don't ignore the investors either. Use this month to close the gap with the options that cost no jobs. Set a dated decision point, and if a reduction is still needed, do it once, sized to the number.** Nine months of runway against a Series B that is "next summer at the earliest" means zero margin: if the round slips by even a quarter, you run out of cash first. So the investors are right that something has to change. "30%" is a solution, though, not a problem, and the real problem is getting to roughly 15 or more months of runway.

**What a cut this month actually buys.** In Sweden you can't cut on Monday and save on Tuesday. You negotiate with the unions first (MBL), notify Arbetsförmedlingen at least two months ahead for 5 to 25 people, and pay notice of one to six months. Selection follows seniority (LAS), with only three exemptions, so you can't choose who stays. Cutting 14 of 48 would likely remove your most recent hires, which may be the people building the next product. The first real saving arrives around month 3 or 4, which is why a short, deliberate decision period costs less than it feels like. Please check all of this with employment counsel; I'm not giving legal advice.

**What I'd do**
1. **This week:** take the first and largest pay cut yourself and have the leadership team follow. Say plainly that the runway is leadership's responsibility, not the team's. Stop hiring, cut non-people spend, and look at pricing and prepaid annual contracts.
2. **Put the numbers in front of the team now.** Share the runway scenarios monthly. Nobody should learn about a 30% cut from a surprise meeting. Check whether you have union representatives or a collective agreement, and bring them in at the problem stage.
3. **Go back to the two investors with a trade, not a no.** If they want 30%, ask what runway they are targeting and whether they will fund a bridge. Offer a written tripwire: if by a set date (say mid-February) no bridge is committed or runway is under, say, 12 months, you carry out one reduction, sized to the gap, tied to which work you stop.
4. **If the tripwire trips,** cut the work as well as the roles, do it once, and go well beyond statutory terms: paid notice, job-security-council or outplacement support from day one, and rehiring priority. Sweden's job security councils (TRR for white-collar staff) exist for exactly this where a collective agreement applies.
5. **Show the Series B investors the plan,** not just the cuts. A startup that reaches default alive, or close to it, negotiates from strength.

**What would change my mind:** no bridge appetite from any investor; revenue growth falling below about 2%; or your real non-people costs being so small that the leaders-first steps buy under a month. Then the deeper cut comes sooner, though still once and still decently.
