NPS falling from +28 to +4 with those complaints open for months is lost revenue that hasn't reached the slide yet. A new name won't fix an unhappy customer.

I'm assuming you can't overrule the CEO, so here is how I'd take it to them.

**1. Start with a private conversation with the CEO.** Don't argue against the rebrand. After a merger, one name and one story can be right. Argue about size and order. A line you can use:

"I'm not asking to stop the rebrand. I want to size it, so the must-have part lands in Q1 and the customer fixes aren't starved. Can I bring you numbers in a week?"

On "any decision beats no decision": agree for choices that are cheap to undo. The full rebrand scope is hard to undo, so it deserves a week of evidence.

**2. Gather evidence for a week, with the teams.**
- Rank the three complaints by revenue at risk, churn reasons and ticket volume.
- Ask each team to estimate the rebrand pieces and the three fixes themselves, not under pressure.
- Compute capacity per team: headcount minus leave, unplanned work, on-call and meetings. Show a range.

**3. Propose a Q1 shape.**
- **Rebrand must-have:** the new name in the app, legal entities, the domain with redirects. Phase the full design system and marketing site into Q2, highest-traffic surfaces first.
- **Customer fixes:** each gets a named owner, a measure (invoice time, integration error rate, reports shipped) and a date. Start with the most revenue at risk.
- **Slack:** leave room for incidents and debt. A plan at 100% means overtime, and that returns later as incidents and attrition.
- Write down three to five outcomes and a short "waits" list.

**4. Make the trade-off visible.** Give leadership one page: what's in, what's out, what each choice costs in customers and dates. If they still choose the full rebrand, ask them to record the decision with its reasons and a review date ("if NPS is still below +4 by [date], we revisit"). Don't absorb the gap through overtime.

**5. Tell the teams the real plan,** with the reasons and what is still open. "Be flexible" is not a plan.

**What would change my mind:** churn concentrated somewhere other than these three complaints, or a contractual, legal or domain deadline that makes more of the rebrand unavoidable in Q1.
