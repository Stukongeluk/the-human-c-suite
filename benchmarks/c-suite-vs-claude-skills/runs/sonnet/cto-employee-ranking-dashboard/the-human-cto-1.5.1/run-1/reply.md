You can disagree without being difficult: ask the VP for a private 20 minutes, bring a better way to answer what they actually want, and raise the works council question as a process point, not an objection.

**Why you're right (the short version)**
- Acceptance rate measures tool usage, not value. It can rise while quality falls, and a careful engineer who rejects bad suggestions ranks "worse".
- PRs merged rewards volume, and AI makes volume cheap. It punishes the people who review, mentor, fix incidents and delete code.
- Once a number decides who gets a plan, people optimise the number. Expect small PRs and blind accepts within a month.

**What to do**
1. **Start with their goal.** Ask: "What problem are you trying to solve: speed, quality, or confidence that Copilot is paying off?" Then offer to help with that.
2. **Bring an alternative.** Team-level delivery and health: lead time, review wait time, change failure rate, unplanned work, plus a short developer survey. For Copilot, compare team outcomes before and after, with a comparison group. Offer to set it up yourself.
3. **Separate the real concern.** If a specific person worries someone, that's a manager conversation with clear expectations, feedback and support, based on their actual work and peer input, not a dashboard.
4. **Ask the process questions.** In the Netherlands, a company of 300 must have a works council (ondernemingsraad). It has a consent right over systems suited to monitoring employee behaviour or performance (art. 27(1)(l) WOR), and a weekly per-person ranking very likely qualifies. Also ask whether the privacy officer has looked at it under the GDPR. Asking "has the OR seen this?" is a normal question, not defiance.
5. **Ask about the plans.** Improvement plans normally need documented, fair expectations. Ranking the bottom 10% by definition makes someone "bottom" every week, however good the team is.
6. **Find allies.** Other senior engineers, your manager, and OR members, before any group meeting.

**A line you can use**
"I want this to work. I'm worried these two numbers will reward the wrong behaviour and cost us people we want to keep. Can I show you a team-level version that answers the same question, and check with the works council before we roll anything out?"

**Be honest about the risk.** If the VP insists anyway, you're not obliged to fight it alone. Document your concerns neutrally and let the works council or HR take it from there. This isn't legal advice; check your own contract and employment situation if it escalates.
