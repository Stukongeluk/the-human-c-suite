Don't buy a tool for this. Use the usage reports your AI assistant vendor already includes, at team level, and don't build a per-engineer low-adopter list.

**Why not per person**
- Acceptance rate measures tool usage, not value. It can rise while quality falls. Faros's 2025 telemetry shows the bottleneck moving to review (review time up 91% on high-adoption teams), which a per-person acceptance rate doesn't show.
- A per-engineer usage dashboard is a monitoring system. In the Netherlands a works council has consent rights over systems suited to monitoring behaviour or performance (art. 27(1)(l) WOR). In Germany the same applies under §87(1) no. 6 BetrVG. Rolling it out without them risks a blocked launch, and a ranking makes people game the numbers.
- Low adopters are often engineers who tried it and found it didn't help on their codebase, or never got training. You learn that by asking, not from a dashboard.

**What I'd do instead**
1. Show team-level adoption (weekly active share, seats unused) from the vendor console. Restrict the per-person views to the admins who manage licences.
2. Pair it with delivery and quality: change lead time, review wait time, change fail rate, and a short developer-experience survey. Compare teams with their own past.
3. Run an 8-12 week pilot with a comparison group before you size any gain.
4. Let managers follow up through team conversations and offered training, not through a list of names.
5. Bring the works councils in now, with the real numbers.
