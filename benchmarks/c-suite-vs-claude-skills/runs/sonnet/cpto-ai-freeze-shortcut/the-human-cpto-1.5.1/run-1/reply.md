Not as framed. Staff the AI assistant now, but with two or three teams, not all eight, and cap tech debt work rather than freeze it.

- **Launch is a signal, not a deadline.** We don't yet know what our customers want from an assistant, or how much revenue or how many deals are at risk.
- **Eight teams for six months** stops most customer commitments and adds coordination cost faster than speed.
- **A blanket debt freeze** tends to come back as incidents and slower delivery, just when the assistant needs a stable base.

**Instead (estimates, to check with the teams):**
1. Two to three weeks of discovery: 15 to 20 customers and lost deals, plus tickets and usage. Pick the one or two jobs it should do.
2. Start with the two or three teams closest to those jobs. Use design partners, a test set, a cost-per-use figure and a human fallback. Add teams only when a gate shows it speeds things up.
3. Cap debt work at 10 to 15% of capacity, limited to what blocks the assistant or causes incidents.

Doing nothing is also an option, but a poor one if you're already losing deals.

Two questions would change my answer: how much revenue is at risk from this launch, and what have the teams promised customers for the next two quarters?
