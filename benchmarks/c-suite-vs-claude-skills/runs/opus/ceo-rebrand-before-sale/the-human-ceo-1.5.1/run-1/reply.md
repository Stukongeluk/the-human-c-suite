My recommendation is to take the board a different plan from the three agreed moves. Make winning back customers the first profit lever and take profit from integration savings and pricing. Keep junior roles, review contractors one by one, and decide the organisation once, in March, with the works councils.

The number behind it is NPS: it fell 25 points this year (+31 to +6) while churn rose, and you're 18 months from a sale. That trend is what a buyer will price. The three moves as agreed would probably make it worse just as due diligence starts. Meanwhile German consultation and notice periods mean most of their savings wouldn't arrive before the second half of 2027.

I've drafted the board paper here: `/home/claude/c-suite-workspace/round-13/opus/ceo-rebrand-before-sale/ours/run-1/outputs/board-paper.md`. It keeps the leadership team's goal of higher, lasting profit before the sale, and changes the route.

**What changes from the three agreed moves**
- **Rebrand:**
  - Decide the name, brand architecture and legal structure in Q1.
  - Switch the customer-facing brands once NPS and churn improve. The target is September 2027, outside the logistics peak.
  - A new name on four unhappy customer bases gives them a reason to re-tender.
- **Fourth restructure:**
  - No decision next week.
  - First, take an honest look at what the last three were meant to fix and what happened to NPS and attrition after each. Add a review of management layers and spans.
  - Duplication is likely after four acquisitions. If it's there, consolidate once, starting at the top, then commit to stability until the sale.
  - Waiting until March delays this saving by about three months.
- **Contractors:** review them contract by contract. End with honest notice, convert to employment, or keep.
- **Juniors:**
  - Keep them, and pair them with the people who alone maintain critical systems. That reduces the key-person risk a buyer will check.
  - Ending "all junior roles" also carries age-discrimination risk, and apprentices past probation can only be dismissed for good cause.

**Where the profit comes from instead**
- Duplicated systems, licences, cloud and vendors across four companies.
- Contract indexation and unbilled work.
- Services utilisation.
- A hiring freeze with selective backfill. As an illustration: at 8–12% attrition, not replacing one leaver in three saves €1.9–2.9m a year by the end of 2027 without a single dismissal. That figure assumes €80k average fully loaded cost; replace it with your HR data.
- Every point of churn you avoid.

**Leaders first.** The paper says plainly that customer satisfaction fell on the leadership team's watch and that you own it now. The executive team, starting with you, takes the first cut on bonus and perks. 2027 executive incentives weigh customer retention as heavily as EBITDA. The size of the cut is yours to agree with the chair, so I've left it in brackets.

**Human impact in brief**
- No job losses are decided next week.
- The hiring freeze means fewer new colleagues, so it needs a stop list, or the same work lands on fewer people.
- Some contractors will lose their contracts, with early notice and time to hand over.
- Managers in duplicated layers may be affected from Q3 2027. They get internal roles first, then voluntary options, a social plan and support from the day of notice.

**Legal floor** (check with employment counsel; this isn't legal advice)
- Confirm which of the four companies and sites have works councils, and whether there is a group one. Involve them now, while they can still shape the plan.
- Removing layers is likely an operational change under §§111–112 BetrVG. That means a reconciliation of interests and a social plan.
- Expect §17 KSchG notifications and social selection under §1(3) KSchG.
- Merging the four legal entities into one can bring one-third employee representation on the supervisory board.

**Before it goes out this week**
1. **Fill the brackets:** EBITDA, cash and covenant headroom, revenue retention, churn reasons, contractor and junior numbers, management layers. Your CFO and HR lead can pull most of it in days.
2. **Talk first, privately:** walk the chair (or the fund's lead partner) and your leadership team through it before it circulates. It changes what the leadership agreed, and nobody should first see that in the board pack.
3. **Check the NPS method.** If the survey or sample changed when the companies were combined, part of the drop may be measurement. Rising churn says it isn't all measurement.

The trap: when three solutions are agreed before anyone has named the customer problem, the group ends up on its fourth restructure.
