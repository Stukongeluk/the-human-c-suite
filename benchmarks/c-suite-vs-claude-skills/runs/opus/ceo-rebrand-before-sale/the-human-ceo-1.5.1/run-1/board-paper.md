# Plan to the sale: higher profit through customers and integration

**Board meeting:** [date, December 2026] · **Decision owner:** CEO · **Prepared:** 23 November 2026 · **Status:** draft for board discussion

## 1. Summary

We can and should raise profit before the sale. But our cost base is not the biggest risk to the sale price. Our customers are: NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year and churn is rising. A buyer will see both trends in due diligence, next to four acquisitions and three restructures since the group was formed.

The leadership team agreed three moves: a rebrand in Q1, a fourth restructure to remove management layers, and ending all contractor and junior roles. I propose we keep the goal behind them, higher and lasting profit before the sale, and change how we get there:

1. **Win customers back first**, because retained recurring revenue is what a buyer pays for.
2. **Take profit from integration and pricing**: duplicated systems and vendors across four companies, unbilled work, services utilisation, a hiring freeze with selective backfill.
3. **Leaders go first** on pay and on organisational change.
4. **Decide the organisation once**, on evidence and with the works councils, at the March 2027 board, then hold it stable until the sale.
5. **Keep junior roles and review contractors one by one** instead of ending both groups.
6. **Move to one name in two steps**: decide it in Q1, switch customer-facing brands once customers are recovering.

### Decisions requested today

1. Make customer retention the first priority of the plan to exit, with the programme in section 6.1 and a 2027 budget of €[x].
2. Ask the CFO to bring a sized profit bridge (section 7) to the February 2027 board.
3. Approve a hiring freeze from 1 January 2027 with selective backfill. Customer-facing roles and roles on critical systems are exempt.
4. Approve the leadership's first cut and the re-weighted 2027 executive incentives (section 6.3).
5. Approve the new name in two steps. Name, brand architecture and legal structure are decided by 31 March 2027. The customer-facing switch waits for customer recovery, with a target of September 2027.
6. Take no decision today on a fourth restructure or on ending all contractor and junior roles. Instead, approve a review to finish by the end of February 2027, with the works councils involved from the start, and one organisation decision at the March 2027 board.

## 2. The problem, and who owns it

**In one sentence:** we have about 18 months to raise profit before a sale, and if customer satisfaction and churn keep moving the way they did this year, a buyer will price the trend: through a lower multiple, an earn-out or an escrow, or by walking away.

Customer satisfaction fell on the leadership team's watch, during a period of acquisitions and repeated restructuring. That is our responsibility, not our teams'. From now on it is mine, and this plan starts there.

We do not yet know how much of the profit gap is cost, how much is price and how much is revenue we are losing. The numbers in section 3 will tell us. Until then, the safe moves are the ones that are fast and reversible, and the moves that can't be undone wait for evidence.

The rebrand idea is right in part: a buyer pays more for one integrated group than for four companies held side by side. The restructure may also be right in part: four acquisitions usually leave duplicated management. The plan below keeps both of those parts and changes the order and the scope.

**Seen from each seat (option D)**
- **Customers:** the same named contacts, their top problems fixed, no forced product migrations, and one name change explained once.
- **Finance:** a smaller headline saving in Q1 than option B. But savings that hold up in a buyer's quality-of-earnings review, fewer one-off costs, and retention that supports the multiple. Covenant headroom still needs checking (section 3).
- **Operations:** the stop list frees capacity, and no organisational change lands during the Q4 peak season.
- **Technology:** tool consolidation is sequenced so it doesn't disrupt customers, and juniors are paired on critical systems to reduce key-person risk.
- **People:** one change, decided once with the works councils, no job losses decided today, and stability after March.

## 6. The recommended plan

### 6.1 Customers first
**Owner:** [COO / Chief Customer Officer] · **Starts:** December 2026

- **By 31 January 2027:** rank the top five customer problems across all four companies, each with its own evidence: NPS comments, churn and downgrade reasons, support tickets, SLA breaches and revenue at risk. Confirm the NPS method is comparable across companies and years.
- **Within 30 days:** an executive sponsor calls each of the top [50] accounts, and every account that renews in the next 12 months and scored as a detractor.
- **From February:** each top problem gets an owner, a measure and a date. Roadmap items and internal projects that don't serve those problems stop or wait, and we publish the list.
- **Continuity:** key customers keep their named contacts through 2027 unless they ask for a change. Nothing customer-facing changes during the Q4 peak season.
- **Close the loop:** detractors hear what changed because of what they told us.
- **Targets:** owners and dates are set now. The numeric targets come to the February board once the baseline is in. The ambition is to bring NPS and gross revenue retention back to [last year's] level in the 12 months before the data room opens.

### 6.2 Profit from integration and pricing
**Owner:** CFO · **Sized bridge:** February 2027 board

- **Non-people spend:** duplicated software across four companies (CRM, ticketing, finance, HR, collaboration), unused licences, cloud rightsizing and idle environments, vendor consolidation on group volume, and offices at their lease breaks.
- **Pricing and billing:**
  - Apply indexation clauses where contracts allow it and it hasn't been done.
  - Bill out-of-scope work and change requests.
  - Harmonise services rate cards and discount rules.
  - None of this on at-risk accounts until their problems are fixed.
- **Services:** utilisation and realisation (billed against worked hours), and write-offs.
- **Focus:** stop low-value internal projects and custom one-offs. Freeze new features on overlapping products, but force no customer migrations before the sale, since a forced migration invites a customer to re-tender.
- **Hiring freeze with selective backfill from 1 January 2027:**
  - Customer-facing roles and roles on critical systems are exempt.
  - The CFO and the responsible executive review requests weekly.
  - The freeze comes with the stop list, so teams aren't simply given more work with fewer people.

**Illustrative sizing of the hiring freeze** (assumptions to replace with HR and finance data: annual voluntary attrition of 8–12%, one in three leavers not replaced, an average fully loaded cost of €80k a year):

### 6.3 Leaders go first
**Owner:** CEO, with the chair

- **The executive team, starting with the CEO:** [no 2026 executive bonus / a [x]% reduction], and no executive pay rises or new perks in 2027.
- **2027 executive incentives:** [40]% EBITDA, [40]% customer retention (gross revenue retention and NPS), [20]% people (regretted attrition and key-person coverage), so that leaders earn their bonus by building what a buyer pays for.
- **The top layers first:** the organisation review (6.4) starts with the top two layers, including the executive team.
- **What it is for:** this is a signal of responsibility, not the way we close the gap, and we will say so.

### 6.4 One organisation, decided once
**Owners:** CEO and CHRO · **Decision:** March 2027 board

- **Learn from the last three restructures:** what each set out to fix, what it cost (severance, advisers, lost staff), and what happened to NPS, churn and regretted attrition afterwards. If we can't say why the last three didn't solve the problem, a fourth won't either.
- **Spans and layers by company** (aggregated data only): layers from CEO to front line, managers with very few direct reports, and duplicated roles across the four companies.
- **The works councils from the problem stage:** they get the real numbers while they can still change the design. People accept decisions that go against them far better when the process was fair (Kim and Mauborgne, 1997).
- **If layers go:**
  - Start at the top.
  - People in affected roles get first call on open roles (customer success, key accounts, project and technical leads), with paid retraining.
  - Voluntary options come before compulsory ones.
  - A social plan, plus coaching and job-search support from the day notice is given, not after the last day.
- **Then stability:** no further restructuring before the sale. We announce that only once the board has agreed it, so that it is true.
- **Timing:** a change agreed in March would typically take effect in Q3 to Q4 2027, after consultation, notifications and notice periods. Starting in December would bring this part of the savings forward by about three months. The price would be deciding before we know what the last three restructures missed.

### 6.5 Contractors and junior roles
**Owners:** CHRO and CTO · **By:** end of February 2027

- **Contractors:** review each contract for the work done, tenure, criticality, cost against an employee doing the same work, knowledge held and status risk. There are three outcomes:
  - **End** the contract, with honest early notice and time for handover.
  - **Offer employment** where the work is ongoing and core.
  - **Keep** the contract where the need is specialist or temporary.

  Ending a contract still ends someone's income, so contractors get the same notice and honesty as employees. [n] contracts, €[x]m spend.
- **Junior roles: keep them.**
  - Juniors are among our cheapest people per head, and they will be the seniors who maintain our systems after the sale.
  - They are also the cheapest way to reduce the key-person risk a buyer will examine: pair them with the [n] people who alone maintain critical systems.
  - Keep a deliberate share of entry-level roles in the workforce plan, and track time to productivity. The 2027 apprenticeship intake stays [as planned].

### 6.6 One name, in two steps
**Owner:** CMO · **Name and legal structure by:** 31 March 2027

- **Q1 2027:**
  - Choose the name and clear the trademark in Germany and the EU.
  - Agree the brand architecture, for example "[Company], a [NewName] company" during the transition.
  - Plan the legal structure with counsel, covering contracts, employment transfer and co-determination. A single German company with more than 500 employees has employee representatives on one third of its supervisory board, so merging the four entities can change board composition.
- **The customer-facing switch:** once NPS and churn have improved for [two consecutive quarters]. The target is September 2027, outside the Q4 peak, with January 2028 as the fallback, so a buyer still sees one group.
- **The message to customers** leads with what does not change: their contract, product, support and named contacts.

## 8. People and the legal floor

| Who | What changes | Support |
|---|---|---|
| Customer-facing and support teams | More focus on the top problems; continuity with key customers | Stop list and paid time to fix; workload review per team |
| All staff | Hiring freeze; fewer new colleagues | Stop list; quarterly pulse; plans explained by their own manager first |
| Contractors ([n]) | Some contracts end, some convert to employment | Early notice, handover time, offers of employment where the work continues |
| Managers in duplicated layers, if the review confirms it | Role change or exit, from Q3 2027 | Internal roles first, paid retraining, voluntary options, social plan, outplacement from the day of notice |
| Juniors and apprentices | Kept, and paired on critical systems | Mentoring and structured learning time |
| Executive team | First cut in pay and perks; incentives re-weighted | — |

We will check who would be hit disproportionately: long-serving managers in the acquired companies, part-timers, people on leave, and any single site.

**Legal and consultation steps.** These are to be confirmed with employment counsel; this paper is not legal advice.
- **Works councils:** confirm which companies and sites have one, and whether there is a company or group works council. Involve them before decisions are made, not after.
- **Removing layers or ending roles across sites:** this is likely to be an operational change. That means negotiating a reconciliation of interests and a social plan with the works council (§§111–112 BetrVG).
- **Collective dismissals above the thresholds:** these must be notified to the Federal Employment Agency in advance (§17 KSchG). Termination agreements the employer initiates count towards the threshold, and errors in the notification can make dismissals void.
- **Social selection:** where the Dismissal Protection Act applies, selection runs among comparable employees on length of service, age, maintenance obligations and severe disability (§1(3) KSchG). "Junior" status and performance rankings are not selection methods.
- **Ending all junior roles:** this targets an age-correlated group and risks indirect age discrimination claims (AGG). Apprentices past their probation period can only be dismissed for good cause (§22 BBiG).
- **Long-standing contractors who work inside our teams:** check their status (status procedure, §7a SGB IV). If any are in fact employees, ending their contract is a dismissal, and back social security contributions may be due.
- **Notice periods:** statutory periods run from four weeks to seven months, depending on service (§622 BGB).
- **Merging legal entities:** check the co-determination effects (DrittelbG) and the transfer of employment before choosing a structure.

## 11. The case for the three moves, and what would change this plan

**The case for them.** Cost savings are certain and quick, and at exit every euro of lasting EBITDA is multiplied. Customer recovery is uncertain and takes time to show. Both points are fair. But in Germany the people-related savings land late, because consultation, notifications and notice periods come first, while their one-off costs land in 2027. A buyer's quality-of-earnings review tests whether savings will last, and its commercial due diligence tests retention. Falling NPS, rising churn, a fourth restructure and no juniors would read as a business being run for the sale rather than for its customers.

**What would change this plan:**
- **Covenant headroom at risk within six to nine months:** faster and deeper cost action, still starting with non-people spend and leadership pay, and an earlier organisation decision.
- **Churn driven mainly by price, product gaps or market change rather than service:** the recovery programme shifts to product and pricing.
- **Heavy duplication in the spans-and-layers review:** a management consolidation in Q2 2027, done once.
- **An NPS drop that turns out to be mostly a change of method:** we re-baseline NPS and use churn as the main measure.
- **Likely buyers who value one brand earlier:** we bring the customer-facing switch forward.

**Questions for the board:** what are we missing, and what would make this plan wrong?
