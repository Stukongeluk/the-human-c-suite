## 1. Summary

**The story in three sentences.** We are four acquired companies that do not yet feel like one company to our customers: NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year while we restructured three times. Over the next 18 months a buyer will decide whether we are an integrated logistics software group with recovering retention and rising margins, or a roll-up with a churn problem. This plan raises EBITDA through integration savings that customers will not feel. It turns the customer curve first, and it changes the order and scope of the three agreed moves so that they help the sale instead of hurting it.

## 2. Where we stand

- **The facts:** 900 people. Four companies acquired over two years. Three restructures already. NPS down from +31 to +6 this year. Customer churn is rising. The owners plan a sale in about 18 months, around Q2 2028. **[Verified]**
- **NPS moves before churn does.** In B2B software with multi-year contracts, dissatisfaction shows up at the next renewal. A 25-point fall this year points to more churn in 2027, and 2027 is exactly the period a buyer will examine. **[Medium]** If our products are embedded systems that are costly to replace, rising churn means customers are leaving something that is hard to leave, which makes the signal more serious. **[Assumed]**
- **The window is short.** Buyers will judge us on FY2027 and the first quarter of 2028. Vendor due diligence is likely to start around Q4 2027. **[Assumed]** That gives us about three quarters to show the customer curve turning.
- **What we do not know yet:** churn and NPS by company, product and segment. Why customers are leaving. The split between recurring and services revenue. What the last three restructures actually saved, and what happened to NPS after each one. Closing these gaps is the first job of the diagnostic (Appendix).

## 3. What a buyer will pay for

**Exit value = EBITDA × multiple − net debt.** Every move has to be judged against both factors, not just EBITDA.

- **Buyers test whether the EBITDA is real.** A quality-of-earnings review adds back cuts the business cannot sustain. That covers capacity that will have to be rehired and service levels that customers are already leaving over. Savings like that do not get paid for. **[Medium]**
- **Buyers price software largely on retention.** Gross and net revenue retention and growth drive the multiple, and a commercial due diligence will interview our customers. An NPS of +6 will be found. **[Medium]**
- **One turn of multiple equals a full year of EBITDA.** At [€E] EBITDA, the difference between [M]× and [M−1]× is [€E] of value. **[Verified: arithmetic]**
- **Churn versus cuts.** Each €1M of subscription revenue that churns removes roughly €0.7–0.8M of EBITDA **[Assumed: 70–80% gross margin on subscriptions]**. That is about the fully loaded cost of a dozen junior employees **[Assumed: about €65k each]**. Losing one mid-sized customer can wipe out the savings from a junior cut.
- **The integration premium is earned, not named.** A buyer pays more for one integrated group than for four companies with a shared owner. That case rests on shared customers, shared processes and platforms, and stable retention, not on a shared name. **[Medium]**

## 7. The three agreed moves, re-scoped

### 7.1 Rebrand

- **Q1 2027:** finish the naming work: trademark clearance, domains and brand architecture. If an agency is already contracted, that work continues.
- **Q2 2027:** launch at group level: legal holding name, corporate website, recruiting, investor materials and email signatures. Products carry an endorsement: "Product X, a NewName company".
- **Customer-facing switch:** only when NPS has been at or above [+20] for two consecutive quarters and gross revenue retention is back at [prior-year level], and customer research shows that the new name does not hurt. Given the timeline, the switch may well be the buyer's decision. We hand them a ready-to-run plan.
- **Before any legal-entity merger or contract re-papering:** the General Counsel checks the contracts. Re-papering gives unhappy customers an opening to renegotiate. **[Medium]**
- **Cost:** CMO to cost the full rebrand against the group-level launch: [€ full] versus [€ group-level].

### 7.2 Management layers

- **Data before design (Dec–Jan):** count the layers from CEO to front line and the spans per manager, across all four companies. As a benchmark, aim for spans of 5–7 and no more than 5–6 layers. **[Medium: design benchmark]**
- **Scope:** remove layers and duplicate leadership roles where the data shows them: mainly in the functions duplicated across the four companies, not in customer-facing teams.
- **Method:** attrition, voluntary agreements and redeployment into customer-facing roles first. Dismissals are the last resort.
- **Timing:** design in Q1 and consult the works councils from Q1, so that implementation is complete by Q3 2027.
- **Commitment to employees:** this is the one structural change in 2027. We say it and we keep it.

### 7.3 Contractors and junior roles

**Contractors.** Within two weeks we build a full list covering name or vendor, function, annual cost, tenure, whether the work is tied to revenue, whether it is critical for customers, and status risk. Each contractor then goes into one of three groups:
- **End:** not tied to revenue and not critical. End at contractual notice.
- **Convert:** long-tenured, critical and embedded in our teams. Offer employment. This usually costs less than the contractor premium, and it removes the risk that a freelancer is reclassified as an employee (false self-employment, *Scheinselbstständigkeit*), which a buyer's due diligence will check. **[Medium]**
- **Keep:** short-term specialists, and contractors tied to revenue at a healthy margin.

**Juniors.**
- We keep existing juniors, apprentices and dual students.
- We slow junior hiring to match demand.
- We redeploy juniors to customer-recovery work: the support backlog, onboarding, data clean-up and test automation.
- In services, juniors carry the billable leverage. Removing them raises our cost per delivered hour and makes us more dependent on seniors, who are the people most likely to leave. **[Medium]**

### 7.4 German labour-law constraints on any headcount reduction

Labour counsel will confirm these for each entity and each establishment before any announcement.

| Rule | What it means for us | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Works councils must be informed and consulted on planned operational changes (*Betriebsänderung*, §111 BetrVG). Fundamental changes to organisation, which delayering can be, count | Works councils are informed at the planning stage. We must attempt a reconciliation of interests (*Interessenausgleich*) before implementing | Medium |
| Implementing before that attempt is complete | Affected employees can claim compensation (*Nachteilsausgleich*, §113 BetrVG) | Medium |
| A social plan (*Sozialplan*) can be enforced for pure headcount reductions. Thresholds per establishment: 10% (at least 60) at 500+ employees; 15% or 60 at 250–499; 20% or 37 at 60–249 (§112a BetrVG) | Thresholds apply per establishment, not per group. With four companies we have several establishments, each with its own threshold | Verified (statute) |
| Mass-dismissal notification to the Federal Employment Agency (§17 KSchG): 30 or more dismissals within 30 calendar days at establishments of 500+; 10% or more than 25 at establishments of 60–499. Employer-initiated termination agreements count | Must be filed after works council consultation and before dismissals. Errors can invalidate dismissals | Verified (thresholds); Medium (consequences) |
| Social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, §1(3) KSchG) by tenure, age, dependants and severe disability, among comparable employees | We cannot select "all juniors" or "all managers in layer X" as a category | Medium |
| Apprentices: after probation, the training company can end the contract only for important cause (§22 BBiG) | "All junior roles" cannot include apprentices | Medium |
| Age discrimination (AGG): a cut defined by "junior" falls mostly on younger employees | Risk of indirect-discrimination claims | Medium |
| Severance: the statutory benchmark is half a month's pay per year of service (§1a KSchG). Negotiated social plans often pay more. Statutory notice periods for long-tenured staff run up to seven months (§622 BGB) | One-off costs fall in FY2027. Run-rate savings arrive later than the plan assumes | Medium |

## 11. Risks, and what would make this plan wrong

| Risk | Trigger | Response |
|---|---|---|
| The cause of churn is product (an ageing platform or a stronger competitor), not service | The diagnostic shows detractors cite missing functions or a competitor | Targeted product fixes for the top accounts. Honest discussion of exit timing at Gate 2 |
| Savings arrive later than the owners need | January bridge shows run-rate EBITDA below plan | Pull forward the non-people levers. Escalate at Gate 1 with the customer effect visible |
| Key people leave during the sale period | Regrettable attrition above 10% in critical roles | Retention pool. Stable leadership for customer-facing teams |
| The works council process takes longer | No reconciliation of interests by end of Q2 | Start information in December. Design for attrition and voluntary routes |
| Data across the four companies is inconsistent | Different churn or ARR figures for the same period | Common definitions from Q1. Finance owns the single source |

**Critic's view of this plan.**
- **Weakest point:** it assumes churn can be turned within about three quarters through service and account work. If the cause is product, it will not be.
- **Missing perspective:** nobody has yet shown what the last three restructures saved and what they did to NPS, and nobody has asked the customers who left.
- **If it is wrong:** we deliver less EBITDA in H1 2027 than Path A shows on paper, and we reach the sale with both weaker margins and weak retention. That is why the gates and the January diagnostic are part of the decision, not an afterthought.

## 12. Questions the board is likely to ask

1. **"The leadership team agreed three moves. Why change them?"** We keep the goal and most of the savings intent. We change the order and the scope. As agreed, the moves cut capacity in the year our customers decide whether to renew. [The leadership team has reviewed this paper.]
2. **"How much EBITDA, and when?"** A range at the January session, after the diagnostic. Non-people and contractor savings come from Q1, back-office consolidation from H2 2027.
3. **"Isn't the rebrand central to the equity story?"** The integration story is central, and the name launches at group level in Q2. What a buyer tests is whether customers stay. Switching product names in the middle of a retention problem gives customers a reason to re-tender.
4. **"Contractors and juniors are the easiest cut. Why not?"** We are cutting contractors, role by role. Juniors are our cheapest capacity, they are legally constrained (apprentices, social selection, age discrimination) and they are the pipeline a buyer will want.
5. **"Why not sell sooner, before churn shows more?"** Commercial due diligence will find the NPS decline through customer interviews. Selling into a falling curve brings a discount or an earn-out, and our disclosure obligations apply either way. We get more for a curve that is turning.
6. **"What if churn does not respond?"** Gate 1 and Gate 2 exist for exactly that, with decisions defined in advance.
7. **"What does this cost?"** Customer recovery [€X], retention pool [€Y], one-off consolidation costs [€Z]. The CFO's bridge shows these against the savings.
8. **"Where are the works councils in this?"** They are informed at the planning stage and consulted from Q1. The relationship also matters for the sale itself.
