CEO ADVISOR: Board plan for raising exit value before the sale

**BOTTOM LINE**
I wouldn't take the three moves to the board as agreed. With NPS down 25 points, they would raise EBITDA on paper and lower what a buyer pays for it. Take a plan that puts customer retention first. It keeps the profit goal and changes the order and scope of the three moves. Confidence: high on the direction. The numbers still need your CFO.

**WHAT**
- **Your customers are the biggest risk to exit value.** NPS falling from +31 to +6 usually turns into churn at the next renewals. With a sale around Q2 2028, those renewals land in the period a buyer's due diligence looks at. [Verified: your figures. Medium: the link from NPS to churn]
- **A buyer pays EBITDA × multiple and tests both.** Cutting people who deliver or protect revenue doesn't hold up. Either the buyer's quality-of-earnings review adds the cost back, or the cut shows up as churn. Each €1M of subscription revenue lost to churn costs roughly as much EBITDA as cutting a dozen junior roles saves. [Assumed: 70–80% gross margin; about €65k fully loaded per junior]
- **Rebrand:** choose the name now and launch it at group level in Q2. Switch the customer-facing brands only once NPS and retention recover. Forcing a name change in Q1 gives unhappy customers a natural moment to re-tender. [Medium]
- **Fourth restructure:** replace it with one targeted consolidation of the functions duplicated across the four companies. Base it on data about spans and layers, and deliver it mostly through attrition. Under German labour law, any restructure means works council consultation and possibly a social plan (Sozialplan). Larger reductions also require a mass-dismissal notification. So savings land in H2 2027 at the earliest. [Verified: §17 KSchG and §112a BetrVG thresholds. Medium: timing]
- **Contractors and juniors:** review contractors one by one and decide whether to end, convert or keep each role. Converting long-term contractors also removes a false self-employment risk that buyers check for. Keep the juniors. They are your cheapest capacity, and apprentices can't be let go after probation. A cut defined by "junior" also carries age-discrimination risk. [Medium]

STILL TO VERIFY BEFORE THE PAPER GOES OUT: CFO for every € figure, labour counsel for the German process and timing, CMO for the rebrand cost, General Counsel for the contract clauses.

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
One turn of exit multiple is worth a full year of EBITDA. If retention is still falling when buyers look in late 2027, you'll pay for it in the multiple, through an earn-out or by losing the deal. Buyers will also add back cuts that look like deferred cost. So in 2027 the customer curve has to turn, while EBITDA rises from savings customers don't feel.

**HOW TO ACT**
1. Reopen the three moves with the leadership team in person, using the customer data, not a memo. Get them to co-own the revised plan. → You → this week, before the pre-read goes out
2. Brief the private equity deal lead one-to-one so the change isn't a surprise in the meeting. → You → 2–3 days before the board
3. Fill in the bracketed figures. At minimum: churn and NPS by company, recurring vs services revenue, and what the last three restructures actually saved. → CFO + customer lead → before the paper goes out
4. Get labour counsel's view on timing and cost for each company, and inform the works councils while plans are still being made, as German law requires. → CHRO + labour counsel → before the board
5. Ask the board for decisions, not sign-off on numbers: the direction, a 6-week diagnostic, budgets for customer recovery and key-people retention, and the gates. → You → at the board meeting. Full business case by end of January 2027

**RISKS**
- **The cause may be the product, not the service.** If customers are leaving because of an ageing platform or a stronger competitor, churn won't turn in three quarters. The January diagnostic has to answer this. If it doesn't turn, the Q3 2027 gate puts exit timing on the table.
- **The owners may prefer the agreed plan because it looks faster.** The comparison of the three paths in section 4 of the paper is built for that conversation. Path B can escalate to deeper cuts at the first gate. Path A can't undo cuts once churn speeds up.
- **Dismissing people before the works council process is complete brings compensation claims** (§113 BetrVG). It also leaves you with a hostile works council just as the sale approaches.

**YOUR DECISION**
- **Option A: the three moves as agreed.** Biggest saving on paper. Highest risk to retention, the multiple and the legal timeline, and the hardest to reverse.
- **Option B: retention first.** Customer recovery comes first. Profit comes from duplicated functions and non-people costs. The three moves are re-scoped and gated. Smaller savings in H1 2027, but EBITDA a buyer will pay full price for.
- **Option C: customer recovery only, no structural moves.** Lowest risk to customers, but it doesn't meet the owners' goal and won't be approved.

**Recommendation: B.** It's the only option where the profit you add survives due diligence, and it keeps the harder cuts available if the gates are missed.
