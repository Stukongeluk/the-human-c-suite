# Board paper: a 15% lower cost base by Q3 2027

**For:** Board meeting, Monday 30 November 2026 · **From:** CEO · **Prepared:** 23 November 2026
**Status:** Proposal for decision. Measures that affect jobs are proposed decisions until the works council has given its advice.

## 1. Summary

I propose that we reach the board's target of €7.2M lower annual opex (15% of €48M) at run-rate within Q3 2027 in this order: our €17M of non-people spend first, then a hiring freeze, a leadership pay cut and a voluntary programme, and forced redundancies only for whatever gap remains, decided once at the February board on verified numbers. On current estimates that means about 16 forced redundancies (range 0 to 38), against the 60 we discussed.

- **60 forced redundancies on their own save about €4.4M a year** at run-rate (60 × €73.8k average cost per person). That is 61% of the target. After works council advice, the collective dismissal procedure, notice periods and severance, they save about €1.5M in 2027.
- **The proposed plan reaches €7.2M** in the planning case and saves an estimated €4.1M in 2027 after one-off costs. That takes 2027 EBITDA to about break-even (estimate) instead of −€4M, and to about +€3.2M a year from Q3 2027 if revenue holds.
- **Headcount still falls**, by about 50 people by the end of Q3 2027 in the planning case (about 64 in the worst case of our estimates), mostly through roles not refilled and people leaving by their own choice.

The non-people figures are industry-based estimates, not yet our verified numbers. Verifying them is the first six weeks of the plan, and we size the forced part only after that.

## 2. The problem, and who owns it

Our costs (€48M) are higher than our revenue (about €44M, estimate), so we lose about €4M a year and have 22 months of cash. If nothing changes we run out of cash around September 2028, and any funding round before then is raised from weakness.

Our cost base grew faster than our revenue. That was the leadership team's call, and mine first. So the plan starts with us: [CEO −30% of base salary, executive team −20%, no leadership bonuses for 2027], until EBITDA has been at least break-even for two consecutive quarters. It saves an estimated €0.2–0.4M. Its purpose is to show who carries the cost first, not to close the gap.

**Assumptions behind the 2027 figures (estimates):** marketing savings from January; AWS commitment savings from February and engineering savings from June; SaaS savings at renewal, on average mid-year; facilities from July; hiring-freeze savings building up from December; voluntary leavers off the payroll from April; forced leavers from about May in B and June in E, after works council advice, the collective dismissal procedure and notice of one to four months. Forced exits cost about €20k per person (1.5 times the statutory transition payment at an assumed four years' service, a €4k transition budget and one month of notice not worked), voluntary exits about €23k (2 times the transition payment), plus €0.15–0.25M for legal and process costs.

**Considered and not proposed:** short-time work (werktijdverkorting). The Dutch scheme currently covers only calamities outside normal business risk, and our gap is structural rather than a temporary dip.

**Not counted in the €7.2M, but part of the fix:** pricing and retention. Each 1% of net price on about €44M of revenue is worth about €0.44M a year, and the cheapest EBITDA is a customer we keep. The proposed plan keeps sales and customer success capacity intact; cutting 60 roles, as in options B and D, makes that much harder to promise.

## 6. Work we stop

A freeze or any exit without stopping work leaves the people who stay with the same work and fewer colleagues. By 15 January 2027, each department proposes, with its teams, what stops or pauses:

- products, features or markets with low usage or negative contribution
- internal projects that don't serve the 2027 plan
- reports, approval steps and meetings nobody uses

The executive team decides the stop list by 29 January. It sets where the voluntary programme is offered and, if a forced reduction is needed, which function groups it affects.

## 7. If forced redundancies are needed: how we will do it

This section is not legal advice. Employment counsel will confirm every step before we act; rules were checked in November 2026.

**Principles.** One round, sized to the verified gap. We say it is the only round only if that is true. We cut work together with roles.

**Dutch legal steps:**

- **Works council.** It advises on the plan's people measures (article 25 WOR) and must be asked while its advice can still change the decision. Until then, every decision is a proposed decision, in all communication. If the final decision departs from its advice, implementation waits one month. A decision taken first and "consulted" afterwards can be challenged at the Enterprise Chamber.
- **Collective dismissal (WMCO).** Twenty or more dismissals in one UWV work area within three months require notifying UWV and the unions before the decision is final, and consulting the unions. Settlement agreements we initiate count towards the 20, so the voluntary programme counts too. A one-month waiting period applies to settlement agreements unless the unions confirm in writing that they were consulted and agree. We expect to cross 20 and will notify before the voluntary window opens.
- **Route.** Settlement agreements drafted so people keep their unemployment benefit rights, or a UWV permit on business-economic grounds. Suitable internal vacancies are offered first to people in affected roles, with training where reasonable.
- **Selection.** Where a whole team or a unique role disappears with its work, those roles end. Where interchangeable roles shrink, the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) decides who goes, per group of interchangeable jobs per location: staff are sorted into five age bands and, within each band, the shortest-serving go first. Agency workers, secondees and freelancers in those groups go before it applies; they get the same notice and honesty. Selection is never based on performance ratings, activity data, leave, health, pregnancy or having raised concerns. People on sick leave are protected by a dismissal ban.
- **Statutory payments and notice.** The transition payment (transitievergoeding) is one third of a month's salary per year of service, up to €102,000 in 2026 or one annual salary if that is higher. Notice is one to four months depending on length of service, less the time a UWV procedure took, with at least one month remaining.

**Package (proposal, to agree with the works council in a social plan):**

- Severance at 1.5 times the statutory transition payment; 2 times for the voluntary programme.
- A €4,000 transition budget per person from the day notice is given, for coaching, training and job search, plus paid time for job search during notice.
- People keep their laptop (wiped), get a written reference and a reference call, priority for rehiring for 12 months and an alumni network.
- Confidential psychological and financial support.
- Extra care where losing the job costs more: people whose residence permit depends on their job (highly skilled migrants generally have three months to find a new employer), people close to retirement and people on leave.

**The day itself:**

- Each person hears it from their own manager or a senior leader, with HR present, in person or on a live call. No mass email. Access changes happen after the conversation, not before.
- A written summary the same day: what happens next, the package, deadlines, contacts.
- People can say goodbye to colleagues if they want to.
- Managers are briefed and supported first. Not on a Friday, not just before a holiday.

**The people who stay:**

- The same day: what happened, why, what it means for them, and whether the round is over.
- Workloads re-planned with the teams, using the stop list; 1:1s within two weeks.
- Engagement, absence and regretted exits watched for 12 months. In Trevor and Nyberg's study, a 1% workforce reduction was associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the following year.

|---|---|
| Who is affected? | About 16 people by forced redundancy (0–38), in function groups set by the stop list; about 15 voluntary leavers; about 19 roles not refilled; the leadership team's pay; everyone through the freeze and the uncertainty from December to February |

| What legal and consultation steps apply? | Works council advice (article 25 WOR), collective dismissal notification (WMCO), settlement agreements or UWV permits, the reflection principle |
| How will we know it is going badly, and what will we do? | Regretted attrition above last year's monthly rate, a falling pulse score, rising churn or incidents. The executive team reviews within a week and adjusts: re-plan work, pause reductions in the affected area |

## 12. Pre-mortem: why this could fail

| It is a year from now and the plan failed because... | Early warning | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud and tool savings came in low; engineers were pulled back to the roadmap | Verified savings below €2.3M by 15 January; cost team hours diverted | Protect the cost team; size the forced part on verified numbers, once |
| People we wanted to keep left | Regretted attrition above last year's monthly rate; voluntary applications from critical roles | Offer the programme only where work stops; retention conversations; say early whether a forced round will follow |
| The same work fell on fewer people | Workload item in the pulse falls; absence rises | Executive team enforces the stop list; no new project without stopping one |
| Revenue fell | Pipeline coverage, churn, support response times | Marketing cuts based on attribution; protect support and customer success; pricing work |
| A legal or procedural error delayed or voided exits | Counsel flags; the works council objects to the timing | Counsel from day one; proposed-decision language; early WMCO notification |

## 13. Assumptions, and what would change our mind

- **Non-people savings of €2.3–4.2M.** If verified savings come in below €2.3M, the forced part moves towards the worst case (38 roles); if they come in higher, it shrinks to zero.
- **Voluntary attrition of 10–14% a year.** If it is lower, the freeze saves less.
- **Revenue holds at about €44M.** If revenue is falling, the problem is bigger than cost and we will bring a proposal on which product or market to stop. If it grows strongly, part of the gap closes on its own.
- **"By Q3" means run-rate within Q3 2027.** If the board means €7.2M saved within 2027, none of these options reaches it: even 60 forced redundancies save about €1.5M in 2027.
- **What the target is for.** Break-even at current revenue needs €4.0M; €7.2M is break-even plus a €3.2M buffer. If the aim is break-even, we would stop before any forced redundancy.
