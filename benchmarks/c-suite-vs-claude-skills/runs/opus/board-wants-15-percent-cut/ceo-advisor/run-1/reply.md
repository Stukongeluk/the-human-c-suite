CEO ADVISOR: Opex down 15% by Q3, and the 60-role reduction

BOTTOM LINE
The 60-role plan you asked for is drafted in the attached pack. I would not take "60 layoffs" to the board on Monday as the whole answer, for three reasons. Cutting 60 roles saves about €4.4M, which is 9% of opex, not 15%. It costs €1.1–2.2M to carry out. And under Dutch law it can only go to the board as a proposed decision until the works council has given its advice. Take a €7.2M plan instead: start with the savings outside payroll, then size the role reduction to the gap that remains. My estimate is about 30 roles (15–45), done in a single round. Keep 60 in the pack as the alternative. Confidence: medium on the direction, low on the exact euro figures until your CFO replaces my assumptions with actuals.

WHAT
• **60 roles doesn't reach the target.** 15% of €48M is €7.2M. Your average fully loaded cost is €31M ÷ 420 = €73.8K, so 60 roles comes to €4.4M a year (9.2% of opex), or €3.6–5.7M depending on which roles go. Reaching 15% through headcount alone would take about 98 roles, 23% of the company. [VERIFIED: your figures; cost of the affected roles ASSUMED] Confidence: high
• **The cost comes first, the saving later.** The one-off cost is €1.1–2.2M. That covers the transitievergoeding (statutory severance of ⅓ month's pay per year of service, capped at €102K in 2026), a social-plan top-up, outplacement, advisers and 1–2 months of paid notice. That is 15–30% of the roughly €7.3M cash your 22-month runway implies. Realistically people leave at the end of April or May 2027, or later if their case goes through UWV, so the net cash benefit in 2027 is only €1.4–2.1M. [ASSUMED: 3.5-year average tenure, social plan at 1–2× statutory] Confidence: low
• **There is €2.6–4.5M outside payroll.** AWS costs €7.5M, about 17% of your implied ~€44M revenue, which is high for SaaS. Commitments and rightsizing could cut 15–25% (€1.1–1.9M). SaaS tools cost €7.6K per employee; rationalising saves €0.6–1.0M. Office, marketing programmes and "other" add €0.8–1.6M. A hiring freeze where you don't backfill every leaver adds €1.2–1.7M: about 16–23 roles that disappear without anyone being dismissed. [ASSUMED: benchmarks; I have no contract or bill data] Confidence: low to medium
• **Dutch law sets the order and the pace.** Dismissing 20 or more people in one region triggers the WMCO (collective dismissal act). You must notify UWV and the unions, then wait one month unless the unions agree in writing. Your works council (OR) has a legal right to advise before the decision becomes final (art. 25 WOR). Any dismissal that goes through UWV must follow the afspiegelingsbeginsel ("reflection principle"): selection within groups of interchangeable roles, by age band, last-in-first-out, with freelancers and temps going first. You cannot hand-pick 60 names. [VERIFIED: UWV and law-firm guidance; your employment lawyer must confirm] Confidence: medium to high
• **60 roles plus the other levers comes to €9.4M (19.5%).** That is more than the board asked for. Either way, the number 60 wasn't worked out from the target; the role count should be whatever gap the other savings leave. Confidence: high (arithmetic)

WHY THIS MATTERS
Suppose you present 60 and a director does the division in the room. You will be asked for a second round to close the remaining €2.8M, and two rounds of layoffs damage trust far more than one. Revenue is the other risk. Each 1% of revenue lost to churn from thinner customer-facing teams costs €0.44M, so losing 3% wipes out about a third of the saving from 60 roles.

HOW TO ACT
1. Call your chair before the pre-read goes out and ask what the board actually needs: 15% off opex, EBITDA break-even, or a runway number. → You → Wednesday
2. Replace my assumptions with actual figures. The list (cash, monthly burn, costs and tenure by function, freelancers inside the €31M, the AWS bill, SaaS renewal dates, lease terms) is in section 7 of the pack. → CFO + CHRO → Thursday
3. Have a Dutch employment lawyer check the legal route, the timeline and how the social plan is set up. → CHRO → engaged by Wednesday
4. Send the board pack with the plan framed as a voorgenomen besluit (proposed decision). Ask the board to approve the target, the lever mix, a cap on the number of roles and a restructuring budget, and to authorise starting the works council process. → You → Friday pre-read
5. Start the levers you can reverse the day after the board meets: hiring freeze with exceptions, AWS commitment analysis, a hold on SaaS renewals, and a ranking of marketing programmes by return. → CFO / CTO / CMO → Tuesday after the board

RISKS
• **A leak before the works council has the advice request.** It invites a legal challenge and costs you the staff's trust. Keep the circle small, and minute Monday's decision as "proposed".
• **Cuts in customer success, support or sales coverage.** This drives churn, at €0.44M per 1% of revenue. Map every affected role to accounts and pipeline before you fix the number.
• **The people who stay carry the same work with fewer hands.** This leads to regrettable departures. Publish a "stop doing" list on the day you announce.
• **Non-payroll savings arrive on renewal dates you don't control.** Some may miss Q3. Get the AWS and SaaS savings signed before you fix the role number, so there is only one round.

YOUR DECISION
Option A: 60 roles as the main lever. It saves €4.4M (9%) at a one-off cost of €1.1–2.2M, so it misses 15% unless you add the non-payroll levers anyway. It is the easiest to explain, can't be undone, and carries the highest risk to customers and morale.
Option B: a blended €7.2M plan. Non-payroll levers (€2.6–4.5M) and the freeze (€1.2–1.7M) come first, then about 30 role reductions (15–45), done in one round. The number is fixed in mid-December once the AWS and SaaS savings are signed. The one-off cost is €0.8–1.7M, and modelled cash at the end of 2027 is €2.5–3M higher than under A. The downside is more workstreams to run, and some savings depend on renewal dates.
Option C: 15% through headcount alone. About 98 roles (23%) at a one-off cost of €1.7–3.4M. It hits the number on paper, with the highest risk to revenue and delivery.
Recommendation: B. It meets the board's number, roughly halves the dismissals, cuts the restructuring cost by about a third, and keeps the cut to one round.

CRITIC CHECK (needed because the restructuring cost can exceed 20% of remaining cash)

Missing perspective: which revenue the cut puts at risk. Nobody has mapped roles to accounts and pipeline yet.
If wrong, the cost is: a second round in the second half of 2027, or €1–2M of revenue lost to churn.
Proceed: after addressing that point. Fix the role number only once the AWS and SaaS savings are signed, by mid-December.
