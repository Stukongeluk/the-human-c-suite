## 4. The reduction plan: 60 roles as requested, resizable

This is written for 60 roles, as you asked. The process is the same at 30 or 45; only the number in 4.2 and the costs in 4.6 change.

### 4.1 Rationale (draft text for the proposed decision)

> We spend €48M a year and lose €4M. At this rate we run out of cash in 22 months. To become self-funding, and to decide our own future rather than have it decided by the next funding round, we will reduce our annual cost base by €7.2M by Q3 2027. We start with what we buy (cloud, software, office, programmes) and with not replacing every leaver. That is not enough on its own. We therefore propose to reduce [60] roles in [functions]. We will stop [named work] so that the people who stay are not left doing the same work with fewer hands. Everyone affected will be treated under a social plan agreed with the works council and the unions.

UWV will expect the business-economic reasons to be supported with financial data. The CFO should prepare the figures in the form UWV asks for.

### 4.2 Scope: choosing roles, not people

The principles, in this order:
1. **Start from the work you will stop.** That means products or features to sunset, segments to deprioritise, internal projects to cancel, and management layers where teams are below 5 people. A role reduction with no matching stop on work just shifts the workload onto others.
2. **Cancel open vacancies first.** They reduce the number of people you have to dismiss.
3. **End freelancer and agency work first** in every affected category. It costs no severance, and under the reflection principle these workers go first anyway (group 1, section 4.3).
4. **Protect:** quota-carrying sales with live pipeline, customer success for your largest accounts, security and on-call reliability, and anyone who is the only holder of critical knowledge.
5. **Check customer exposure.** Map every affected role to accounts and pipeline before the number is fixed.

### 4.3 Legal route (Netherlands). Employment counsel must confirm every step

1. **Proposed decision.** Monday's board approves an envelope, not a final decision on roles. Minute it as "proposed".
2. **Works council advice (art. 25 WOR).** The advice request sets out the reasons, the consequences for staff, how those consequences will be handled (the social plan) and the timeline. At least one consultation meeting must be held. The law sets no deadline for the advice; in practice it takes weeks [ASSUMED: plan for about 6]. If your final decision departs from the advice, you must wait one month before carrying it out, and the works council can appeal to the Ondernemingskamer (the Enterprise Chamber of the Amsterdam Court of Appeal) during that month. Do not make the decision final before the advice is in.
3. **Unions.** Notify the unions with members in the company, invite them to consult, and negotiate the social plan with them. Their written statement that they were consulted and agree removes the WMCO waiting month.
4. **WMCO notification to UWV.** This applies because 20 or more people are dismissed in one region (Utrecht falls under Flevoland/Utrecht) within three months. Every route counts, including mutual-consent agreements. The notification covers the reasons, numbers by function, age and gender, total headcount, planned dates, the selection method, the severance arrangements and the termination route. A one-month wait runs from the date UWV confirms the notification is complete, unless the unions agree. Applications for a UWV permit can be processed straight away.
5. **How the employment ends.**
   - (a) A mutual termination agreement (vaststellingsovereenkomst, VSO) under the social plan. This is the usual route. The employee has a 14-day cooling-off period after signing.
   - (b) A UWV dismissal permit for anyone who doesn't sign. Here the reflection principle and the duty to redeploy are tested strictly. The notice period may be shortened by the length of the UWV procedure, with a minimum of one month left.
   - (c) The cantonal court, rarely used.
   - Statutory notice runs by tenure: 1 month under 5 years, 2 months for 5–10, 3 for 10–15, 4 for 15 or more. [Standard Dutch law; counsel to confirm.]
6. **Selection: the afspiegelingsbeginsel (reflection principle).** It applies wherever the UWV route is used.
   - It works per category of interchangeable roles, across the company or site.
   - Order within a category:
     - Group 1, first: external workers (freelancers, temps, secondees).
     - Group 2: employees past state pension (AOW) age.
     - Group 3: zero-hours contracts.
     - Group 4: fixed-term contracts that end within 26 weeks.
     - Group 5: permanent staff.
   - The permanent group is spread across five age bands (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55+), with last-in-first-out inside each band.
   - Exceptions are narrow. One is a documented case for "indispensable" knowledge.
   - **What this means:** you cannot select on performance. If you offer VSOs to particular people, the offer has to be genuinely voluntary, and the UWV fallback still has to work on the reflection principle. Counsel needs to design this before any list exists.
7. **Duty to redeploy.** Before dismissing anyone, check whether a suitable vacancy exists. The hiring freeze helps here, but list the remaining vacancies honestly.
8. **Dismissal protections.** People who are sick, pregnant or on maternity leave, and works council members, have specific protection. Counsel should review each case.
9. **Staff outside the Netherlands** follow local rules. Confirm where all 420 people are employed.

### 4.4 Social plan outline (to negotiate with the unions and works council)

- **Severance:** the statutory transitievergoeding as the floor, plus a top-up factor of [1.0–2.0×] to be decided. Budget assumption: up to 2× statutory.
- **Notice:** statutory notice, paid, with release from work where it makes sense (garden leave).
- **Outplacement or mobility budget:** €2.5–5K per person, plus access to a career coach from the day of the conversation.
- **Quick-decision bonus (snelbeslissersbonus):** optional, for signing within [x] weeks.
- **Hardship clause** for individual cases, positive references, the option to keep laptop and phone, and clear treatment of equity or options under the plan's leaver rules.
- **Optional voluntary departure window** within the affected categories before compulsory selection. Applications need company approval, so you don't lose people you need.

A decent social plan is not only the right way to treat people. It also speeds up signing, lowers the legal risk, and is what the Utrecht tech market will remember when you hire again.

### 4.7 Communication sequence

**Order on announcement day:**
1. The board (already informed).
2. The works council in the morning, with the advice request.
3. The unions.
4. An all-hands meeting the same morning, led by you, live.
5. A written message and FAQ to everyone within the hour.
6. A toolkit and briefing for managers.
7. Key customers and partners, contacted through their account owners.
8. An external statement, ready in case it's needed.

**What you say**
- **Why:** the numbers, plainly.
- **What:** which areas are affected, and how many roles.
- **How:** the process and the timeline, the role of the works council and unions, and the social plan.
- **What it means for each person:** when they will know, by a specific date.
- **What you are cutting first:** spend outside payroll, and vacancies.
- **What you will stop doing.**
- Say "no second round is planned" only if it is true and sized that way.

**What to avoid**
- No names before the works council has advised.
- Don't call it "final" while it is still a proposed decision.
- No reassurances you can't keep.
- No announcements on a Friday or just before the holidays.
- Don't let people find out from a locked laptop.

**Individual conversations**
- Held by the person's own manager with HR, in private, from a prepared script.
- Written confirmation the same day.
- Outplacement and confidential support available immediately.
- Access handled respectfully.

### 4.8 Customers and the people who stay

**Customers**
- Every affected role has a named handover for each account it touches.
- Track support and customer success SLAs weekly.
- The account owner contacts your top 20 accounts whenever their contact changes.

**The people who stay**
- Publish the "stop doing" list together with the announcement.
- Re-plan the roadmap to the capacity you will actually have.
- Offer retention arrangements to a small number of critical people [ASSUMED budget €0.1–0.2M, inside the restructuring budget].
- Train managers for the conversations that follow.
- Run a pulse survey at 30, 60 and 90 days.

## 6. Likely board questions and draft answers

1. **"Why not just 60, or more?"** Sixty roles saves €4.4M, 9% of opex, and still leaves €2.8M to find. Doing all 15% through headcount means 98 people, 23% of the company, with the highest risk to revenue. Option B reaches 15% with about half the dismissals.
2. **"How sure are you about the savings outside payroll?"** Ranges are shown. The role number will be fixed only after the AWS and SaaS savings are signed, by 18 December. If they land at the low end, the role number moves towards 45, still in one round.
3. **"What is the revenue risk?"** Each 1% costs €0.44M. Every affected role is mapped to accounts and pipeline, quota-carrying and key-account roles are protected, and SLAs are tracked weekly.
4. **"What happens to runway?"** If revenue holds, cash bottoms out at about €5.5–6.0M in spring 2027 under B and recovers to about €7.3–7.8M by year-end. The company becomes self-funding.
5. **"Why can't this be faster?"** Dutch law requires the works council's advice, the WMCO procedure and statutory notice. The earliest realistic full run-rate is June 2027, still ahead of Q3. Skipping steps invites challenges that cost more time.
6. **"Will there be a second round?"** None is planned, because the number is sized against the full target. The triggers that would reopen it are revenue falling below [x] or churn rising above [y].
7. **"What does it cost?"** €0.8–1.7M under B, against €1.1–2.2M under A. Payback is within about six months.
8. **"Are we cutting into growth?"** Protected: [list]. Stopped: [list].
9. **"Legal and reputational risk?"** Counsel is engaged, a social plan is agreed with the works council and the unions, and communication is sequenced. Section 4.3.
10. **"What do you need from us?"** The four asks in section 1.
