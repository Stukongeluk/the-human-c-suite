# Support in 2027: people plan for AI-first support

**Draft for consultation** · Prepared 23 November 2026 · Owner: [executive sponsor] · Next decision: gate review, end of April 2027

> **Status:** this is a proposal. No decision on reductions has been taken. In Sweden and Finland, changes like these are negotiated with employee representatives before any decision is made. This draft is meant to be the starting point for those talks, and it will change as a result of them.

## 1. Summary

- Our AI assistant now resolves most first-line tickets. The support team's work is changing, and the team's size and shape should follow the work that remains.
- On current assumptions, the work left after AI needs **about 12 people (range 8 to 14)**, against 18 today. A team of 6 works only if the AI takes over **about 72% of the team's current hours** and creates no more than one person's worth of new work. We will measure that before deciding.
- We reach the size the work needs in this order: a hiring freeze and natural attrition, then moves into new roles, then voluntary options. Only if the data at the end of April 2027 shows it's needed do we hold **one** negotiated round of reductions per country. The final structure is in place by December 2027.
- Everyone affected gets the same package in both countries, using whichever country's rule is more generous.
- Success is judged on customer quality, service levels and how people land. We review it monthly, and every quarter together with employee representatives.

## 2. The problem in one sentence

AI now handles much of the routine first-line work, so we need a support team sized and skilled for the work that remains (escalations, complex cases, keeping the AI accurate), without lowering service for customers and without leaving the people affected to carry the change alone.

## 4. How big does the team need to be?

Simple tickets are the quickest to handle, so the share of tickets the AI resolves is larger than the share of hours it saves. The cases left over are harder, and running the AI creates new work: reviewing its answers, keeping the knowledge base right, testing changes before they go live and handling its hand-overs.

| | Cautious | Central | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share of team hours spent on first-line work today | 60% | 65% | 80% |
| Share of first-line work the AI truly resolves | 60% | 70% | 80% |
| Extra time per remaining first-line case (harder mix) | +20% | +15% | +5% |
| Change in escalations and other work | +5% | +5% | 0% |
| Share of today's hours the AI removes, after the harder mix | 29% | 41% | 63% |
| New work: AI review, knowledge base, testing (people) | 1.5 | 1.5 | 1.0 |
| **People needed for the work** | **14** | **12** | **8** |

Formula: 18 × (first-line share × (1 − AI share) × (1 + harder mix) + other work × (1 + change)) + new work. The results are 14.2, 12.2 and 7.6, rounded. Every input is an assumption until the December measurement replaces it.

**Coverage sets a floor.** With two languages and, as a minimum, two people available per language during opening hours (one on the live queue, one on escalations and covering breaks), we need 180 staffed hours a week. After holidays, sickness and training, a full-time person is available for about 32 of their 40 hours, so the floor is about **6 people most of the year**. In July, when availability falls to about 60%, it is **about 8**. In Sweden, staff can take four consecutive weeks of holiday between June and August (Semesterlagen 12 §), and in Finland the summer holiday falls between May and September.

**What 6 would require:** the AI removing about 72% of today's hours after the harder mix, with only one person's worth of new work. That happens, for example, if first-line work is 85% of the team's hours and the AI truly resolves 85% of it with no harder mix. It would also mean accepting a thin summer, or deciding to merge the language queues or shorten opening hours. Those are customer decisions, and they should be made on their own merits, not to fit a headcount.

**How the money was estimated.** At an illustrative fully loaded cost of €4,000 per person-month (replace with payroll), each role costs €48k a year.

| | A. Go to 6 now | C. Recommended, central case |
|---|---|---|
| Roles leaving the support budget | 12 | 6: 3 through attrition, 3 moves |

| Total over 2027–2028 | €672k | €444k |

Notes:
- With no package beyond notice, option A's 2027 saving would be about €240k.
- If the moves don't fill real openings, option C saves €72k in 2027 and €144k a year after that.
- The AI's own running cost is the same in both options and isn't included here. The business case must include it, since fees per resolution grow with volume.
- Not priced: rehiring or outsourcing if the remaining work exceeds the team, lost customers if quality drops, and the extra turnover that follows cuts.

**The strongest case for option A:** if the work really fits in 6, A saves more from 2028. But if the April data shows it does, option C reaches the same size by December 2027 with the evidence in hand. The cost of waiting is about two months on the roles still affected, roughly €48k if all six further reductions happen.

## 6. Recommendation: option C

1. Freeze hiring for support now. That move is reversible and costs nothing.
2. Measure what the AI really resolves and how many hours of work remain, against criteria we fix with representatives **before** we look at results (section 13).
3. Move people into the new roles in support and into real openings elsewhere, with paid training.
4. Offer voluntary options only if the measured work points below the team that remains.
5. At the end of April 2027, decide once. If quality holds and the work needs fewer people, run one negotiated round per country, with notices served by early June, so the new structure is in place by December 2027. If the data isn't clear by April, move the gate to September and accept a final structure in Q1 2028. A slower single round beats two rounds.

## 7. Our commitments

- **No surprises.** Representatives and the team see the data and this draft while they can still change it.
- **No dismissals before the April gate,** and the gate criteria are agreed before anyone sees the results.
- **One round at most.** If reductions are needed, they happen once.
- **Work goes with the roles.** If the team shrinks, we stop or simplify work, so the people who stay don't carry the same load with fewer colleagues.
- **Teams, not individuals.** Measurement stays at team level. Nobody is ranked by tickets or handle time, and nobody is selected because they are on leave or raised concerns.
- **A person is always reachable.** Customers can reach a human when they ask, and the assistant tells them it is an AI. The AI Act has required that disclosure since August 2026 (Article 50).
- **Leaders carry it too.** No bonus or target is tied to headcount reduction. The sponsor's goals include customer quality and how the affected people land, and the sponsor owns the decision and its result in public.
- **The more generous rule applies.** Where Swedish and Finnish rules differ, everyone gets the better of the two.

## 9. The new team, and where people can go

**In support (central case, about 12 people):**
- **Escalation and complex-case specialists** in each language: the cases the AI hands over, complaints, and customers who ask for a person.
- **AI quality and knowledge specialists** (1.5 to 2 people): review a weekly sample of AI conversations, keep the knowledge base current, run a test set before every change to the assistant, and tune when it hands over to a person. The people who know where the AI fails today are on this team.

The roles are designed around skills current team members can reach with reasonable training. They aren't written to fit chosen individuals, which would also get around the Swedish selection rules and be open to challenge (section 10).

**Outside support (real, funded openings only):** customer success and onboarding; product operations, turning top contact reasons into product fixes; QA and testing; implementation; account management and renewals; documentation. When Ingka (IKEA) let an AI assistant handle 47% of call-centre queries, it retrained about 8,500 call-centre staff as remote interior design advisers, and that service brought in about €1.3 billion in 2022. We should ask the same question: what do customers want that nobody had time to do?

Every move comes with paid training in work time, a mentor and a check-in after three months. If a move doesn't work out, we look for another option together before anything else happens.

## 10. Country process (legal floor; confirm with counsel)

This is not legal advice. Rules depend on company size and collective agreements, and both countries changed theirs in 2025 and 2026.

**Sweden (Stockholm, 10 people)**
- Negotiate with the unions before deciding on major changes, including the new support model and any redundancies (MBL).
- Before any dismissal for shortage of work, offer any vacant role in the company that the person has sufficient qualifications for (LAS 7 §).
- If roles are cut, who goes follows length of service, per operational unit and collective agreement area. The employer may exempt up to three people, and the union can agree a different list (LAS 22 §). The people who stay must have sufficient qualifications for the remaining work, which is why the new roles must be reachable with training.
- Notice runs from one month (under two years' service) to six months (ten years or more) (LAS 11 §).
- Five or more dismissals must be notified to the Public Employment Service (Arbetsförmedlingen) at least two months in advance.
- People dismissed for shortage of work with more than twelve months' employment in the last three years have nine months' priority for re-employment (LAS 25 §).
- Transition support: TRR where the white-collar collective agreement applies, or otherwise the public basic transition support through Kammarkollegiet (Act 2022:850). Transition study support from CSN pays about 80% of salary, up to a ceiling, for up to about a year of full-time study, for people with a long work history.

**Finland (Helsinki, 8 people)**
- Dismissal for financial or production reasons requires the work to have decreased substantially and permanently. Before dismissing, the employer must offer other suitable work and reasonable training (Employment Contracts Act, chapter 7).
- Co-operation Act, as amended in July 2025: it applies in full from 50 employees. Companies with 20 to 49 employees negotiate only when the measures affect at least 20 people within 90 days. The minimum negotiation period is three weeks, or seven days when fewer than ten employees are affected or the employer has fewer than 50. A proposal to dismiss ten or more people starts a 30-day assessment period with the employment authorities.
- Notice runs from 14 days (up to one year's service) to six months (more than twelve years).
- Employers with 30 or more employees must offer training or coaching worth at least one month's salary to people dismissed for economic reasons after five years' service, and more to people aged 55 or over.
- Since 1 June 2026, employers with 50 or more employees must offer work to dismissed employees if they need staff for similar work within four months, or six months after twelve years' service.
- Check whether the applicable collective agreement sets an order for who is dismissed last.

**Both countries**
- Any tool that measures agents' work in the new model is agreed with representatives before it's introduced, and reports on teams only.
- Check with counsel how voluntary exit agreements count towards notification and negotiation thresholds.

## 11. Support for every person affected

**Voluntary options** (offered only when the measured work is below the remaining team):
- Reduced hours, for example 80%, with a pay and pension arrangement agreed with representatives.
- Study leave, with a company top-up for people who use Swedish transition study support, and a company-funded study grant of the same value in Finland.
- A voluntary exit package on the same terms as below. We can decline a request that would leave a language uncovered, and we will explain why.

**If a role ends**, in both countries:
- Paid notice, with time off for job search and training from the first day.
- Severance on top of notice: a proposed starting point of three months' pay, more for long service, to be negotiated and costed.
- A training and coaching budget worth at least one month's salary for everyone, regardless of tenure. This extends Finland's legal minimum to all, on top of TRR, Kammarkollegiet or Finnish change security.
- Outplacement coaching, plus a CV and LinkedIn review.
- Nine months' priority for re-employment for everyone (the Swedish rule, extended to Finland), and an alumni network.
- Keeping the laptop after company data is wiped, a written reference and a promise of a good reference call.
- Confidential psychological and financial counselling.
- Extra care for people on parental or sick leave, who are told in a way that suits them and are never selected because of the leave, for people close to retirement, and for anyone whose residence permit depends on the job.

**For the people who stay:** they hear the same day what happened and why, and that the process is over, if that is true. The workload is re-planned with them, with work stopped rather than redistributed. They have 1:1s within two weeks, and if a role grows in scope, its pay is reviewed. We watch pulse, absence and regretted leavers closely for twelve months. One study found that each 1% of workforce reduction was associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the following year.

## 12. How we communicate

- [ ] Representatives in both countries involved before anything is final, with the real numbers and time to respond
- [ ] Managers briefed first, with a script, answers to likely questions and support for themselves
- [ ] Team members told in person by the sponsor and their own manager, never by email, never by a login that stops working
- [ ] The why explained with numbers: what's decided (the freeze, no dismissals before the April gate), what's open, and the next dates
- [ ] Written summary the same day: what changes, what support is available, who to ask
- [ ] The rest of the company told the same day, honestly
- [ ] No announcements in the weeks before Christmas or the summer holidays, and none on a Friday afternoon
- [ ] Follow-ups scheduled: open Q&A in each city, and check-ins after two and six weeks
- [ ] A named owner and a place to ask questions in each city

## 13. How we'll know it's working

**Gate criteria** (proposed; to be agreed with representatives in January and fixed before the results are in):

| Criterion | Proposed threshold |
|---|---|
| Truly resolved: no repeat contact on the same issue within 7 days, no escalation | Stable for three consecutive months |
| Satisfaction, AI-resolved vs human-resolved | Within 5 points |
| Wrong or harmful AI answers in the weekly sample | Below 2%, and none left open on money, contracts or personal data |
| Customers who ask for a person reach one | Within the service target |
| Human queue at the measured staffing | Response targets met for eight weeks, including a peak |
| Customers who contacted support | No rise in churn or complaints |

**Indicators reviewed monthly, and jointly every quarter:**
- **Customers:** truly resolved rate, satisfaction gap, repeat contact, time to reach a person, complaints, churn among customers who contacted us.
- **Service:** human backlog and response times, escalation resolution time, how current the knowledge base is.
- **People:** team pulse, sick absence, regretted leavers, the share of affected people in a new role or new job after six and twelve months, and training time used.

## 14. Pre-mortem: it's December 2027 and this went wrong. Why?

| What went wrong | Early warning | What we do now |
|---|---|---|
| Quality slipped after the team shrank. Klarna said its assistant did the work of 700 agents, later said quality had suffered, and began hiring people again | Repeat contacts and the satisfaction gap rise | Gate criteria, a human route, monthly review |
| The best people left early because of uncertainty | Regretted leavers | Clear dates, no dismissals before the gate, honest answers |
| The new roles weren't real, and people were moved only to be cut later | Moves without a funded role | Only funded openings, a mentor, a three-month check-in |
| A legal process error: deciding before negotiating, or selecting outside the rules | Counsel review | Representatives involved from January; counsel checks each step |
| Summer coverage collapsed in July | Holiday plan shows gaps | Summer plan before any notice |
| The remaining team burned out on the hardest cases | Absence, backlog | Re-plan the work and stop low-value tasks |
| AI costs rose with volume or a price change | Monthly AI bill per resolution | Net the AI bill in every business case |

Companies that cut jobs for AI often regret it. In a 2025 Orgvue survey, 55% of leaders who made AI-related redundancies said they had made the wrong decisions. In a 2026 Careerminds survey, two in three were already rehiring, and for 30.9% the rehiring cost more than the savings. Both are vendor surveys, so read them as a signal, not as precise rates.

## 15. Assumptions, and what would change this plan

**Assumptions:**
- The workload inputs in section 4.
- 10–20% yearly attrition, and 2–4 suitable openings elsewhere in 2027.
- Two languages, weekday opening hours from 08:00 to 17:00, and two people available per language.
- €4,000 per person-month fully loaded, and an average notice period of three months.

**What would change the plan:**
- **Lower:** the measured data shows the AI removing 70% or more of the hours with the gate criteria met. We then go toward 6 to 8 in one round.
- **Higher:** quality gaps, harder hand-overs or growth in customer volume.
- **Faster:** a real cash deadline. The pace would change, but not the fairness or the legal process.
- **More moves:** more openings elsewhere.
- **A lower coverage floor:** a separate customer decision to merge language queues or shorten hours.
