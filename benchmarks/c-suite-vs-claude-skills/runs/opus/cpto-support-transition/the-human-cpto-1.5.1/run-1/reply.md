I'd plan for a team of about 12 by the end of 2027, reached without dismissals, and let measured data decide whether to go further toward 6. On reasonable assumptions, the work left after AI needs 8 to 14 people. Six only fits if the AI takes over about 72% of the team's current hours and creates almost no new work.

I've drafted the people plan on that basis in `people-plan.md`. It's written as a proposal for consultation, and it can still reach 6 if the numbers support it. Nothing irreversible happens before they do.

**Why I wouldn't commit to 6 now**

- **Tickets aren't hours.** The AI takes the quickest tickets. What's left is escalations, complaints and the cases it hands over. On top of that comes new work: checking its answers, keeping the knowledge base right and testing changes. My central estimate is 12 people.
- **Coverage sets a floor.** I'm assuming two locations and two languages. Keeping two people available per language during opening hours takes about 6 people most of the year and 8 in July, when Swedish and Finnish staff take their summer holiday. Six leaves nothing for peaks or sickness.
- **The law sets the pace and who stays.**
  - In Sweden you negotiate with the unions before deciding (MBL). The people who stay are chosen by length of service unless the union agrees a different list (LAS), so you won't get to pick the six. Notice runs up to six months.
  - In Finland the work must have decreased substantially and permanently, and you must offer other work and training first.
  - Announcing "6" before those talks would be a legal problem as well as a trust problem.
- **The money is closer than it looks.** I used an illustrative €4,000 per person-month; replace it with payroll.
  - At full run rate, going to 6 saves €576k a year from 2028, against €288k for a team of 12.
  - After notice periods and a decent package, though, the 2027 saving is about €96k for the fast route and €156k for mine. That assumes the people who move fill roles you'd otherwise hire for.
  - If the April data shows 6 works, my route gets there by December 2027 anyway. Waiting costs about €48k.

The trap: a headcount target set before the work is measured usually leaves the people who stay carrying the hardest cases with too few colleagues. Klarna shrank through attrition after its AI launch, then started hiring people again because quality suffered. In a 2026 survey of HR leaders, two in three companies that cut jobs for AI were already rehiring. It's a vendor survey, so read it as a signal, not a precise rate.

**What the plan does**

1. **Now:** freeze hiring for support. Define "resolved" properly: no repeat contact within 7 days, satisfaction close to human-handled cases, and a low error rate in sampled conversations. Fix the gate criteria before anyone sees results.
2. **January:** union and employee representatives see the data first. Then both teams hear it in person from you and their manager: what's decided (the freeze, and no dismissals before the end of April), what's still open, and the dates.
3. **February to April:**
   - Career conversations with all 18.
   - New roles designed with the team: escalation specialists, and AI quality and knowledge specialists.
   - Openings elsewhere offered to support staff first, with paid training in work time.
4. **Spring:** voluntary options (reduced hours, study leave, an exit package), but only if the data points below 12.
5. **End of April:** the gate. If quality holds and the measured work needs fewer people than remain, there's one negotiated round per country, decided once. Notices go out by early June, so the new structure is in place by December 2027.
6. **One package for both countries,** using whichever country's rule is more generous:
   - support from the first day of notice
   - a training budget of at least one month's salary for everyone
   - nine months' priority for rehiring
   - paid time off to look for work.

**Two questions that would change the plan**

- What does "resolves" mean in your data: closed by the bot, or no repeat contact and satisfaction on par with human cases? And over how many months has it held?
- Is there a cash deadline behind the number 6, or is the goal a lower cost to serve? A real gap with a date would change the pace, but not the fairness.

HR and counsel also need to confirm three things:
- the collective agreements in each country
- the Finnish company's headcount, which decides whether the Co-operation Act applies
- each person's length of service, which sets the notice periods.

None of this is legal advice.

My key assumptions are in the plan:
- first-line work is about 65% of the team's hours, and the AI truly resolves about 70% of it
- yearly attrition is 10–20%
- there are 2–4 openings elsewhere that support staff could move into.
