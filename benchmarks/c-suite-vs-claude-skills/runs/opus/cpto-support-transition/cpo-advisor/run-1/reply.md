**WHAT**
- **"Resolves" carries the whole plan, and nobody has verified it yet.** Most dashboards can't tell deflection from resolution. Deflection means the customer gave up or came back another way. First-line tickets are also the quick ones. If AI handles 70% of tickets but only 35 to 40% of handle time, the workload points to about 11 to 12 people, not 6. [ASSUMED: illustrative ratio, not your data] Confidence: Low.
- **In Sweden, "6" is something you propose to the unions, not something you decide.** The Co-determination Act (MBL §11) requires you to negotiate with the unions *before* you decide. Who stays follows length of service under the Employment Protection Act (LAS). You can exempt up to three people, or agree a different list with the union. Five or more redundancies in one county also means notifying the public employment service (Arbetsförmedlingen) at least two months before the first exit. [VERIFIED: Livsmedelsföretagen LAS/MBL checklist Jul 2026; Arbetsförmedlingen] Confidence: High.
- **In Finland, the process depends on how many people the Finnish entity employs, and the rules changed in 2025.** Since 1 July 2025, the Co-operation Act applies from 50 employees, with at least 7 days of negotiation when fewer than 10 people are affected. An entity with 20 to 49 employees only has to negotiate if 20 or more people are affected. At any size, the work must have shrunk substantially and permanently, other work must be offered first, and people on family leave can't be dismissed unless operations stop entirely. [VERIFIED: Työmarkkinatori; Yrittäjät; Eurofound] Confidence: High. [ASSUMED: your Finnish headcount]
- **The cautionary tale is from Stockholm.** Klarna cut support staff in favour of AI. Its CEO later said cost had been "a too predominant evaluation factor" and that "what you end up having is lower quality." Gartner (Feb 2026) predicts that half of the companies that cut service staff because of AI will rehire by 2027. [VERIFIED: CX Today/Bloomberg; Gartner] Confidence: High.
- **The tickets the AI now resolves are really a product backlog.** If most of them are "how do I do X" questions, that's a usability red flag the AI is now hiding from you. Two of the six roles should own turning contact drivers into product fixes, so the number to track is contacts per customer as well as cost per contact. **WHY THIS MATTERS**
If the number is wrong, you lose the people who know your customers best, and the churn shows up two to three quarters later. Rehiring also isn't a clean reset: the people you let go have priority for re-employment (9 months in Sweden). If the process is wrong, you add union damages in Sweden and compensation claims in Finland to the reputational hit in two small hiring markets.

**HOW TO ACT**
1. Confirm the facts: Finnish entity headcount, collective agreements, tenure list, and who is on family leave or is a union rep, shop steward or safety rep. Engage employment counsel in Sweden and Finland. → HR lead → Fri 4 Dec 2026
2. Freeze backfills in first-line support (no dismissals) and draft the six role profiles: 1 lead, 3 escalation specialists, 2 AI-quality and knowledge owners. → Head of Support → Fri 18 Dec 2026
3. Run the evidence check. Measure 7-day re-contact rate, a weekly audit of 100 AI conversations, satisfaction scores for AI vs human, time to reach a human, and a July-sized workload model. → Head of Support + AI owner → Fri 29 Jan 2027
4. Decide the end state (6, 7 or 8) against the gates and sign off the cost model. → You + CFO + CCO → Fri 5 Feb 2027
5. Open the process in both countries on the same day. Announce it in person at both sites, then open the redeployment and voluntary window. → You + HR + counsel → week of 8 Feb 2027

**RISKS**
- **Your strongest people leave first** once you announce, and you end up with the wrong six. Mitigation: retention agreements, the exemptions or an agreed list, and honest role profiles.
- **Six people can't cover everything.** With three per site, July holidays plus one sick day leaves a site with one person, and the team can't absorb an AI outage. Mitigation: two people per language in July, a cross-site rota, and an overflow contract for six months.
- **Notice periods delay the savings.** Notice runs up to 6 months by Swedish law, and many white-collar collective agreements add 6 more for people aged 55+ with 10 years' service. That pushes savings into the second half of 2027 and some into 2028.
- **Don't announce in December or present anything as decided before negotiations.** Either damages both the legal position and trust.

**YOUR DECISION**
- **Option A: Announce 18 to 6 now.** Fastest savings, but you would be cutting on numbers that may count deflection as resolution, and you risk getting the legal sequence wrong.
- **Option B: Evidence first, then one process to the number the data supports (6 to 8).** Redeployment and voluntary departures come before dismissals, and exits are staggered. About 2 months slower, but it's one round of change and you can defend it in both countries.
- **Option C: Attrition and redeployment only.** Lowest risk to the people involved, but you probably end 2027 at 10 to 13 people. [ASSUMED]
- **Recommendation: B.** You reach the same place with one round of pain instead of two, and the data you collect is also the legal ground you need in Finland.
