Plan Q1 around your customers' top three problems, with a small, dated rebrand alongside, and take that to your CEO as a costed option rather than an objection. NPS has fallen 24 points, from +28 to +4, and a quarter with everyone on the rebrand keeps those complaints open until at least April.

The trap: a new name doesn't fix an unhappy customer, and "any decision beats no decision" suits choices that are cheap to undo, not leaving customers waiting another quarter.

**1. Talk to the CEO privately first, this week.** Ask for the reasons, not a reversal. A line you can use:

> "I'll deliver the rebrand. Before we commit the whole organisation, I'd like you to see what it costs: our top three complaints stay open another quarter with NPS at +4. Can I bring you two options with team estimates within two weeks, and you decide?"

That respects "leadership decides" and makes the trade-off visible instead of leaving your teams to absorb it quietly.

**2. Rank the complaints by evidence:** revenue renewing in the next two quarters from accounts that raised them, churn reasons in the CRM, ticket volume, NPS comments.

**3. Get the teams' own estimates for both,** and plan to those, with slack for incidents and no overtime.

**4. Split the rebrand.** Must-have: the name in the app, the legal entity on invoices and contracts, the new domain with redirects. Can be phased: the full design system and the marketing site (often an agency job). Watch the domain: it touches the accounting integration's OAuth callbacks and webhooks, and invoice emails from a new domain need SPF, DKIM and DMARC or they land in spam. Rushed, the rebrand can make two of your three complaints worse.

**5. Put two options on one page:**
- **A, as decided:** everyone on the rebrand; complaints wait; revenue at risk from step 2.
- **B, recommended:** a small squad ships the rebrand must-haves by the date that matters; everyone else fixes the three complaints, rebuilding the invoicing and report screens in the new design system, so customers see the new brand where they work. Each fix gets an owner and a measure: invoice run time, integration sync failures, renewals among those accounts.

The CEO has a point: after a merger, one name and one story matter for sales, staff and investors. If there is a hard external date, option B protects it.

The question that would most change my answer: what fixes the Q1 date (a legal entity change, a contract, an event)?
