Recommend yes to demand-based scheduling and no to all 140 stores by March: run a 12-week controlled pilot in about ten stores once the central works council has consented, and decide on rollout by early summer. The numbers behind it: 12% of hours equals about 1,080 of your 9,000 people, and three days' notice is below the four days Dutch law sets by default for on-call staff.

**Why not March**

- **Much of the 12% may not exist here.** Shifts cancelled inside the call-in period are still paid, on-call staff on under 15 hours get at least three hours' pay per call, and 15-year-olds may work at most two hours on a school day, until 19:00. Same-day cuts and 15-minute slices save far less under these rules.
- **The works council must consent.** Roster rules and systems that can monitor attendance or performance fall under article 27 WOR; without consent it can invoke nullity, and staff needn't follow the schedules. A DPIA is mandatory, and allocating shifts on individual scores brings AI Act high-risk duties from 2 December 2027.
- **The timing is the worst of the year:** eight to ten stores a week through December and into the run-up to Easter (28 March), while the supermarket CAO talks are suspended.
- **Fewer hours can cost sales.** In a 28-store Gap trial, more stable schedules raised median sales 7% and productivity 5%. Unstable schedules predict quitting.

**What to propose instead**

1. Now: take the problem and the vendor's case to the central works council, start the DPIA, have the vendor backtest its forecast on 12 months of your till data, and sign nothing beyond a pilot with exit terms.
2. Pilot about ten stores against matched control stores, with safeguards built in: schedules two weeks ahead, minimum shift lengths, same-day changes only by choice, youth and CAO rules hard-coded, store managers approving every schedule, no individual scoring.
3. Fix targets before go-live (hours, sales, availability, queues, turnover, sickness, student earnings), and agree that saved hours come from not replacing leavers, not from cutting contracts.

The trap is letting the vendor's deadline and headline number set the plan before you've measured your own over- and understaffing.

**A line for the COO, one-to-one before any exec meeting:** "I want the saving too, and I want a number we can bank. Give me ten stores and twelve weeks, with the works council on board, and you'll have one."
