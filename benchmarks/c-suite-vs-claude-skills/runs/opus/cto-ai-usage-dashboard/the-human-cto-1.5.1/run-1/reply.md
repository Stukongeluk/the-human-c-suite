**I wouldn't buy a tool for per-engineer tracking, and you probably don't need to buy anything.** The assistant's own admin console already reports adoption and acceptance (e.g. [GitHub Copilot's metrics](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/billing-and-usage/copilot-usage-metrics/copilot-metrics), generally available since February 2026). Report it by team, and keep per-person data with the licence admins.

Why not per engineer:

- **Acceptance rate measures tool use, not value.** In [Faros AI's 2025 data](https://www.faros.ai/blog/ai-productivity-paradox-report-2025) (10,000+ developers; Faros sells analytics), high-adoption teams merged 98% more pull requests, but review time rose 91%.
- **Chasing low adopters makes usage the target.** People learn to accept suggestions, and the real reasons for low use stay hidden: code where the tool helps little, no training, seniors busy reviewing.
- **It's a monitoring system in both countries.** Dutch works councils have a consent right over it (art. 27(1)(l) WOR); German ones, where elected, have co-determination (§87(1) no. 6 BetrVG). GDPR applies too. Not legal advice.

What I'd do instead:

1. Take the rollout to both works councils now: purpose, data, who sees what.
2. Compare seats paid with weekly active users per team (teams of 5+ only) to right-size licences.
3. Ask teams what gets in the way, then fix training, IDE support or review rules.
4. Baseline change lead time, review wait time and change fail rate; compare after 8–12 weeks.
5. Give low-adoption teams paid learning time and pairing, not follow-up calls.

Two questions that could change this: what should the follow-up achieve (lower licence cost, faster delivery, something else)? And does each site have a works council?
