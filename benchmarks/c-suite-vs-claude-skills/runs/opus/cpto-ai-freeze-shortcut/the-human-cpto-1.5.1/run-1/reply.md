**No, not in that shape.** Yes to building an AI assistant fast; no to all eight teams, and no to freezing tech debt.

Why:
- Eight teams on one new product mostly adds coordination, not speed, and current customers get nothing new for six months.
- Frozen debt keeps growing. In Stripe's developer survey, engineers estimated it already takes about a third of their week (13.5 of 41 hours).
- Base rate: about 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact (MIT NANDA, 2025; contested, but directionally right).

Instead:
- **Weeks 1–2:** ask 10–15 customers and sales what they want from the competitor's assistant; check lost deals for it.
- **Then:** one or two teams owning the closest product area build it, with a test set of real cases, cost per conversation, and design partners before beta. In the EU, check AI Act transparency and data protection with counsel.
- **Around week 8 (estimate):** add teams only if usage and quality prove demand. The rest keep shipping for current customers.
- **Keep each team's debt budget;** pay first the debt on the assistant's path (data access, APIs, auth).
- No date to the board until the building teams have estimated it.

A competitor's launch is a signal to understand, not a deadline; matching it with everyone bets two quarters on a guess.

Two questions that would change my answer:
1. Are we actually losing deals or customers to their assistant?
2. How much of each team's time goes to unplanned work today?
