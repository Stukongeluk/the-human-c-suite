**AI agents for everyone: yes, staged. Dropping review for small changes: no.** Review is where AI gains get stuck, so fix it, don't remove it. On high-adoption teams Faros AI (2025, 10,000+ developers) saw 98% more pull requests merged but review time up 91%. Veracode (2025) found AI-generated code introduced OWASP Top 10 vulnerabilities in 45% of test tasks.

The trap: skipping the check makes you look faster and moves the cost to incidents, where it's dearer and harder to see.

What I'd do instead:

- January: offer agents to all engineers, with training and paid learning time. No usage targets, no per-person AI stats.
- First, baseline lead time, review wait, change failure rate and escaped defects.
- Pilot the full agent workflow with 2–3 teams for 8–12 weeks against a comparison group; scale what holds.
- Make review faster, not optional: small PRs, a same-day first-review norm, AI first-pass review, tests and security scans in CI; a human still approves.
- Want a fast lane? Define it by risk (docs, copy, test-only changes), not size. An agent can split a risky change into ten "small" ones.
- SOC 2, ISO 27001 and many customer contracts usually expect peer review on changes.
- Review is also how juniors learn; keep it.
- If you have a works council, bring them in before you pick the tool.

Two questions:

1. Where do changes wait today: coding, review or deploy? If it's review, the queue is the fix.
2. What is the speed-up for: roadmap, debt, or headcount?
