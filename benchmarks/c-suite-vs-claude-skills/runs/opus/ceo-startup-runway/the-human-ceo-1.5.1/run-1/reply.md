Don't agree to a 30% cut this month. Agree this month on a plan for the real problem: nine months of cash against a Series B that closes in seven to nine months at the earliest means raising on an empty tank. Start the moves that cost no jobs this week, and let the numbers decide within about three weeks whether a reduction is needed and how big. If it is, do it once, in January, properly.

**Your investors are right about the problem.** Nine months takes you to the end of August 2027. The B has already slipped once, and European climate-tech growth money is scarce: [World Fund](https://www.worldfund.vc/knowledge/series-b-funding-gap-in-european-climate-tech) puts the Series B shortfall at $13.5bn over 2020–2024. Doing nothing isn't an option.

**Where I'd push back is the instrument.** A round number by a near date usually ends with cutting what's easiest to count.

- **Growth won't close the gap alone.** 4% a month is about 60% a year, but at flat costs break-even still takes 18 months if gross profit covers half your costs, and 28 if it covers a third.
- **"This month" isn't possible in Sweden.** MBL requires negotiating with the unions before you decide. [Arbetsförmedlingen](https://arbetsformedlingen.se/for-arbetsgivare/anmal-varsel-och-permittering) must be notified at least two months before the first person leaves (5–25 people), and notice runs one to six months by length of service. Savings start in February or March at the earliest.
- **You don't choose who goes.** LAS sets the order by length of service, with three exemptions, unless you agree a list with the union. A 30% cut would mostly take your newest hires, often the commercial people you hired for the B.

**What 30% buys depends on one number you have and I don't: the share of monthly costs your gross profit covers.** An illustration, on my assumptions rather than your numbers (SEK 85k fully loaded per person, payroll 73% of costs, 14 people leaving from March with two months' extra pay):

| Gross profit covers | 30% cut on its own | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| 20–30% of costs | Cash lasts to October–November 2027 | Buys about two months; you still need a bridge |
| About 40% | Into early 2028; only to November 2027 if growth slips to 3% | Tipping zone: protecting growth matters as much as cost |
| 50% or more | Break-even before cash runs out | A smaller combination probably gets there too |

In the same illustration, the no-job moves below buy roughly 1.5 to 3.5 months. So the honest answer could be less than 30%, 30% plus a bridge, or about 30% once.

**This week: fast, reversible, no jobs**

1. You and the leadership team take the first and largest pay cut (say 20–30%, bonuses suspended), and say plainly that the gap is a leadership result: you and the board planned around a B that didn't come.
2. Freeze hiring; backfill only critical roles; honour signed offers.
3. Cut spend: cloud, tools, consultants, office, events, pilots that don't sell. Check you claim the [R&D deduction](https://www.skatteverket.se/foretag/arbetsgivare/arbetsgivaravgifterochskatteavdrag/forskningsavdrag.4.8dcbbe4142d38302d7cb4.htm) on employer contributions (20% of pay for staff spending at least half their time on R&D) for everyone eligible.
4. Pull cash forward: annual prepayment on renewals, faster collections, renewal pricing.
5. Ask every insider, the two included, whether they'd fund a bridge tied to this plan. Apply for grants, but keep them out of the base case.

**Within three weeks**

- Model three scenarios (B in summer 2027, B late or never, growth at 3%) against one target: break-even, or cash into mid-2028.
- Tell the team the numbers and the options while they can still shape them, and bring the unions in early. Offer reduced hours, leave and voluntary exits, knowing exits can take people you'd want to keep.
- Decide which work stops before deciding about roles.

**If a gap remains,** make one reduction, sized for the downside case so there's no second round. Notify Arbetsförmedlingen early: it's a notice of risk, not a decision, but it's public, so tell your team first. Hold the individual conversations in early January, not the week before Christmas; that costs a few weeks of those salaries. Go beyond the minimum: extra pay on top of paid notice, transition support from day one (your job security council, or Kammarkollegiet without a collective agreement), and extra care for anyone on a work permit, who has [three months](https://www.migrationsverket.se/en/you-have-a-permit-in-sweden/work/if-you-change-employer-or-profession-or-stop-working.html) to find a new job.

**With the two investors,** see each one privately before the board meeting. Agree on the problem, then ask what runway they need to see and whether they'd back a bridge if the plan gets there. Their answer tells you whether this is about cash or about showing discipline to B investors.

Two questions would change my answer most:

1. What share of your monthly costs does gross profit cover, and does "nine months" include planned hires?
2. Why did the B slip: the market, or what investors said about your growth? If it's growth, a deep cut that slows growth makes the B harder.
