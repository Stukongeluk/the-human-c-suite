# Runway, the 30% cut and the slipped Series B: detail

As of 23 November 2026. All company figures below are illustrative (🔴 assumed) until your CFO swaps in the real numbers. The Swedish rules and market data are sourced at the end (🟢).

---

## 1. The number that decides the size: revenue as a share of monthly costs

**Target used:** cash lasts through May 2028 (about 18 months) with at least two months of today's net burn still in the bank, or the company breaks even before that. That gives you room for a B that starts next summer and slips by a quarter.

**People to cut (of 48) to reach the target, assuming you also take 15% off non-people costs:**

| Revenue as % of monthly costs | No new money | + insider bridge of 2 months' burn | + insider bridge of 4 months' burn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | more than half the team | more than half the team | 18 (38%) |
| 25% | more than half the team | 22 (46%) | 14 (29%) |
| 30% | more than half the team | 18 (38%) | 10 (21%) |
| 35% | 20 (42%) | 13 (27%) | 6 (12%) |
| 40% | 15 (31%) | 9 (19%) | 2 (4%) |
| 45% | 11 (23%) | 4 (8%) | 0 |
| 50% | 6 (12%) | 0 | 0 |
| 60% | 0 | 0 | 0 |

How to read it: 30% (14 people) hits the target in only a few cells: revenue around 40% of costs with no new money, or around 25% with a 4-month bridge. With no new money and revenue below 40%, 30% is too little. From 45% up, it is more than you need, and it would cost you growth you are about to sell to B investors.

**What a 30% cut on its own buys (no other changes):**

| Revenue as % of costs | Cash runs out, no action | Cash runs out, 30% cut |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | Sep 2027 | Nov 2027 (two more months) |
| 35% | Oct 2027 | Feb 2028 |
| 50% | Dec 2027 | Never: break-even comes first (around Dec 2027) |

**If the cut slows growth:** if growth drops from 4% to 3% a month after the cut, break-even moves 4 to 7 months later (at 35% revenue/cost: Sep 2028 becomes Apr 2029).

**Model assumptions (🔴 replace with actuals):**
- "Nine months" = cash divided by today's net burn with revenue held flat. If your nine months already counts the 4% growth, you have less room than these tables show.
- People are 70% of total costs. If you are hardware-heavy and people are nearer 55%, you need a bigger cut for the same saving. Example: at 35% revenue/cost you would need 23 people with no new money, 14 with a 2-month bridge and 5 with a 4-month bridge.
- The people who leave cost the company the average. Savings start in March 2027. Notice pay and settlement packages add two months' cost per person, paid on top of the notice period.
- Costs stay flat apart from the cuts, and revenue keeps growing 4% a month.
- The bridge arrives in February 2027.

**The efficiency number B investors will look at.** Burn multiple = monthly net burn ÷ new annualised revenue added that month. With 4% monthly growth it is roughly (1 − r) ÷ (0.48 × r), where r is revenue as a share of costs. A common benchmark is under 2x. This only applies if your revenue is recurring; for project or hardware revenue, B investors will weigh deployment and technical milestones more.

| Revenue as % of costs | Today | Jul 2027, no action | Jul 2027, 30% cut | Jul 2027, 30% cut + 15% non-people |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20% | 8.3x | 6.1x | 4.4x | 4.0x |
| 35% | 3.9x | 2.5x | 1.6x | 1.4x |
| 50% | 2.1x | 1.1x | 0.5x | 0.3x |

At around 20%, no cut makes you look efficient by summer. Your B story then has to rest on technology or deployment milestones, and the cut is mainly about surviving long enough to reach them.

## 2. The Swedish process, and why savings start in March, not December

| When | Step | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Now | Engage Swedish employment counsel. Check whether you are bound by a collective agreement (kollektivavtal), what it says on notice, and who is a union member. | Without a collective agreement you still negotiate with every union that has members among the affected staff, and you must ask employees whether they are members. |
| By 11 Dec | Call MBL negotiations (the co-determination law's duty to negotiate before major changes). File varsel (written notice of redundancies) with Arbetsförmedlingen. Tell the whole team the same day. | Varsel is required for 5 or more people in the same county, at least 2 months before the first affected person's last day (5 to 25 people). |
| Within about 2 weeks | Union meets. Name your three exemptions at the latest at this negotiation. Look at redeployment (omplacering) before any dismissal. | You must not decide, and no one may be given notice, until negotiations are finished, unless there are exceptional reasons. |
| Late Dec to early Jan | Negotiations close, then notices go out. | Notices may be given during the varsel period. |
| Feb to Mar 2027 | First last days, then payroll falls. | Statutory notice is 1 month (under 2 years' service), 2 (2 to 4 years), 3 (4 to 6), 4 (6 to 8), 5 (8 to 10) and 6 months (10+). A collective agreement can change this. |
| Until about 9 months after they leave | Re-employment priority. | Anyone employed more than 12 months in the last 3 years has priority if you rehire for their work, during notice and for 9 months after. If the B closes in autumn 2027, your first rehires may have to be the people you let go. Often that is good news. |

**Who goes.** You decide which work is no longer needed. Among the people affected, the order is by length of service within each operating unit (driftsenhet): last in, first out. You can exempt at most three people who are especially important to the business. Ask counsel how far you can depart from that order through a collective agreement with the union, or through voluntary packages. A straight 30% by seniority tends to take your newest hires. Check whether that means the commercial and deployment people behind the 4% growth.

## 3. The paths compared

| Path | Upside | Downside | Reversibility | Second-order effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. 30% now, as asked** | Fast signal of discipline; two investors satisfied | A round number that is wrong in most of the table above; seniority rules may hit growth; cannot legally be "this month" anyway | Low. Rehiring is slow, and departing people take customer and technical knowledge with them | Risk of a second cut in spring; remaining team assumes more cuts are coming |
| **B. One cut sized to the target, decided by 9 Dec, started before Christmas** | Size tied to survival and to the B story; done once | Two weeks slower to decide, yet savings land at the same time (March) because of the process; investors must accept a number that isn't theirs | Low (it is still a cut), but sized with margin, so it is less likely to need repeating | Credible "no second round" message to the team; clean story for B diligence |
| **C. Smaller cut plus insider bridge** | Keeps capacity for growth and B milestones | Depends on investors putting money in; a bridge with no milestones attached just delays the problem | Medium: you keep people, and the bridge terms are the cost | Bridge terms shape the B; insiders get more say |
| **D. Strategic track in parallel** (corporate partner or strategic investor in your sector) | Money from someone who values your technology, not just your burn multiple | Slow, distracting, can limit later options | Medium | Worth opening in Q1 if revenue is under about 30% of costs, where no cut works on its own |

Recommended: **B, with C as the counter-offer**, and D started in Q1 if your numbers land in the bottom rows of the table.

## 4. The conversation with the two investors

Hold it one-to-one first (by 4 Dec), then take the decision to the full board (9 Dec). It is a board decision, not a side agreement. Check your shareholders' agreement for any investor consent rights over budget or headcount.

Points to make:
1. "I agree on urgency. The process starts before Christmas. Under Swedish law, savings land in March whatever we decide this week."
2. "The size comes from a target, not a percentage: cash through mid-2028 with a reserve, or break-even first. At our real numbers that means X people."
3. "If you want the cut smaller than X, the difference is a bridge of Y months' burn. Will you commit, on what terms, and by when?"
4. "What reserves do you hold for us?" Climate-specialist VC funds raised nearly 40% less in 2025 than in 2024. The pressure to cut may partly reflect thin follow-on budgets. Better to know now.
5. "What milestones do you need to see to lead or join the B?" Their answer and the B leads' answers set what the cut must protect.

Treat any bridge as nonexistent until it is signed.

---

## 5. If you cut: once, and done well

- **One cut, with margin.** Tell the team it is the only one planned, and what would have to happen to change that. Then make that true.
- **Same story everywhere.** Team, board and B investors hear the same runway, the same milestones and the same reasons.
- **Leadership first.** Founders and leadership take a visible pay cut before or together with the cut. It is small money, but it buys credibility.
- **For the people leaving:** notice pay plus a fair package, garden leave where it makes sense, proper references, active introductions to your investors' portfolio companies and Stockholm climate-tech peers, and the transition support they are entitled to (through TRR or your agreement's equivalent if you have a collective agreement). Ask counsel what you can offer on vested options or warrants.
- **Protect:** customer delivery, the 2 or 3 B milestones, and the revenue engine. Rank the work: keep the lights on, then protect the core, then grow the core, and only then new bets. Cut from the bottom of that list, so bets, side projects and nice-to-have work go first.
- **Watch afterwards:** regrettable attrition in the next 90 days is the early warning that the cut damaged the B story.

---

## 6. Cash levers to pull regardless (from 1 Dec)

- Hiring freeze, and no backfills without your sign-off.
- About 15% off non-people costs: contractors, tools, travel, office, events.
- Ask your largest customers for annual upfront billing; a small discount in return is cheap money.
- Supplier payment terms: renegotiate where you can.
- Grants and soft loans (Swedish Energy Agency, Vinnova, EU programmes): pursue them, but count them as upside. They are too slow to plan around before summer.
