📊 CEO — Nine months of runway, a slipped Series B, and the 30% cut

**BOTTOM LINE**
Don't cut 30% just because two investors picked 30%, but don't wait either. By 9 December, size a single cut against a hard target: cash through mid-2028 with a reserve, or break-even before then. Start the Swedish process before Christmas, and make the two investors choose between that cut and putting in bridge money. — Confidence: 🟡 Medium. The logic holds, but your real numbers will set the size.

**WHAT**
- **You have less time than "nine months" suggests.** Your cash runs out somewhere between August and December 2027, depending on whether your nine months already counts revenue growth. A B that starts "next summer at the earliest" means you pitch with a few months of cash at best, and with none if it slips. That is the weakest position to negotiate from. 🟡 for your timeline. The market won't rescue you either: only 14.7% of European climate-tech seed companies reach a Series B, and climate VC funds raised almost 40% less money in 2025 than in 2024. 🟢
- **The right size depends on a number you have and I don't: revenue as a share of total monthly costs.** In a rough model (assumptions in the detail doc):
  - Up to about 30%, no cut on its own gets you to mid-2028; you also need insider money.
  - Between 35% and 45%, you need a cut of roughly 25–40% with no new money, or about 25% or less with a bridge worth 2–4 months of burn.
  - At 50% or more, a 30% cut is overkill and costs you the growth you're selling.

  30% is only right in a narrow band. 🔴
- **A cut "this month" isn't possible in Sweden, and it saves less than your investors think.**
  - You can't decide on it until the MBL negotiations with the union are finished. MBL is the co-determination law that requires you to negotiate before major changes.
  - You must notify Arbetsförmedlingen (varsel) at least 2 months before the first person leaves.
  - Notice periods run 1–6 months, depending on length of service.

  In practice, payroll drops from about March 2027, and until then the cut costs you money in notice pay and packages. 🟢
- **Who goes matters as much as how many.** Swedish law sets the order by length of service (last in, first out) within each unit, and you can exempt only three people. A straight 30% cut could take your newest hires, who may be the commercial people behind your 4% growth. In the model, if growth slips from 4% to 3% after the cut, break-even moves 4–7 months later. Cut bets and side projects, not the revenue engine, and get employment counsel to design the cut. 🟡
- **What the two investors are making is an opening bid, not a board decision, and their money is your other lever.** Ask what reserves they hold for you. With climate funds raising less, part of the push to cut may be that they can't fund a follow-on. 🟡

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
This decision sets whether you go into the B with six-plus months of cash and a credible efficiency story, or with two months and no leverage. If you size it wrong, you either break the growth you're selling, or you face a second cut in the spring, in front of the same B investors and a team that won't believe "this is the only one."

**HOW TO ACT**
1. Rebuild the runway model with real numbers: revenue as a share of costs, each person's length of service and notice period, package costs, and whether you're bound by a collective agreement. → CFO → Fri 27 Nov
2. Start the steps that make sense either way: hiring freeze, about 15% off non-people costs, and a request to your largest customers for annual prepayment. Engage Swedish employment counsel. → CFO + HR → Tue 1 Dec
3. Meet the two investors one-to-one: agree on the urgency, ask about their reserves, and ask whether they'd bridge. Ask three target B leads what they need to see, and by when. Those 2–3 milestones are what the cut has to protect. → You → Fri 4 Dec
4. Bring two options to the full board: the cut the target requires with no new money, and the smaller cut a committed bridge would allow. Then decide. → You + board → Wed 9 Dec
5. If you're cutting: call the MBL negotiations, file the varsel, and tell the whole team the same day, with the numbers and the plan. Present it as negotiations starting, not a done deal. → You + HR → Fri 11 Dec

**⚠️ RISKS**
- **The cut is too small.** You'd need a second one during B due diligence, and your best people would leave first.
- **You decide or announce before the MBL negotiations close.** That invites damages claims and a dispute with the union.
- **You count on a bridge that isn't signed.** Until it is in writing, plan as if no new money arrives before the B.
- **Revenue turns out to be under ~30% of costs.** Then no cut saves the company on its own, and new money becomes existential. In that case, open talks with a strategic investor or industry partner in Q1, in parallel with the B.

**🔑 YOUR DECISION**
- **Option A: cut 30% now, as asked.** It's the fastest option and it satisfies two investors. But it's a round number that may be too small (low revenue, no bridge) or too big (revenue at 45% of costs or more). The seniority rules may also take out your growth engine.
- **Option B: one cut sized to the target, decided by 9 Dec, with the process started before Christmas.** The decision takes two weeks longer, but the savings land at the same time (March) because of the Swedish process. The investors have to accept a number that isn't theirs.
- **Option C: a smaller cut plus an insider bridge, meaning a loan or note from existing investors that converts at the B.** It protects growth, but only works if they put money in, tied to B milestones.

**Recommendation:** Option B, with C as your counter-offer: "This is the cut the numbers need. If you want it smaller, the difference is a bridge."
