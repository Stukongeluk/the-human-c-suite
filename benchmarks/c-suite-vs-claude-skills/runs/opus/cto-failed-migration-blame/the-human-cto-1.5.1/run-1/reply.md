Don't let the platform lead go. Give the board accountability that lowers the risk of another 9-hour, €2 million outage: you own the result, an independent review finds the causes, and you report fixes with owners and dates.

The board is right that €2 million needs an owner and proof it won't happen again. Firing the lead gives them neither.

**Why firing is the wrong answer**
- The migration ran in your organisation, so the result is yours first. Say so before anyone's name comes up.
- A 9-hour outage is rarely one person's error. It usually takes several gaps at once: no tested rollback, no go/no-go point, too few people, time pressure. Firing the lead leaves all of them in place.
- You lose the person who knows most about what went wrong, and every engineer learns to hide risks, which makes the next incident bigger.
- In most European countries, dismissal over one failure in work the company approved is also hard to defend. Involve HR and counsel first; this isn't legal advice.

One exception: if the review shows the lead skipped agreed safeguards or ignored clear warnings, handle that separately and fairly, not as the answer to the board.

**What to do this week**
1. **Talk privately with your CEO and the board chair before the board meets again.** A line you can use: "This happened under me, and I'm accountable. Removing the person who ran it won't close the gaps behind a 9-hour outage. Give me three weeks for an independent review and a plan with owners, dates and measures."
2. **Start a blameless review led by someone outside the platform team.** Why did recovery take 9 hours? Was rollback tested? Who signed off the plan and the window, and what pressure was the team under? Include how the €2 million was calculated.
3. **Talk to the lead and the team yourself.** Tell them you won't offer up a person as the answer, and ask them to help find the causes. Make time off after that weekend normal.
4. **Report actions the board can check:** a timed rollback and restore rehearsal before every major migration, a go/no-go gate with a maximum outage window, sign-off for high-risk changes. Each with an owner, a date and a measure.
5. **If the board wants a visible consequence, offer it at your level,** such as part of your bonus tied to delivering those fixes.
