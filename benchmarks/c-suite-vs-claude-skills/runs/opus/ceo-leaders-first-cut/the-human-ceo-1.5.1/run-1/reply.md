**Don't cut 15% now. "Show discipline" is a signal, not a cash gap; give the board a better one: discipline aimed at what failed, leaders paying first, and a trigger if things worsen.**

**Why not the cut (estimates; check with finance)**
- €40m covers about three years at a €12m loss, past your 2028 launches, if the loss is roughly cash burn.
- 15% is about 37 people, saving roughly €2.5–3m a year at €70–80k loaded cost, less in year one. A quarter of the loss, and it removes people building the 2028 games. Survivors leave too: 1% downsizing went with 31% more voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008).

**The alternative for Thursday**
1. **Own it.** Say plainly the launch was leadership's call. Executives take a 20–30% pay cut and no 2026 bonuses. A signal, not savings: Iwata halved his salary after the 3DS flop and kept Nintendo's teams together.
2. **Cut non-payroll spend:** vendors, outsourcing, licences, cloud, office, uncommitted marketing, sized line by line.
3. **Freeze non-critical backfills.** At an assumed 8–10% turnover, €1.5–2m a year without dismissals.
4. **Fix the cause.** Independent post-mortem on the flop, then stage gates with kill criteria, external playtests and milestone funding for both 2028 projects.
5. **Revenue:** price and platform moves on the launched game; co-development or publisher co-funding.
6. **Trigger:** if cash falls below an agreed floor or a project misses a gate, you return with options, voluntary first.

Involve the works council now, at the problem stage (article 25 WOR); a 37-person cut would also require notifying UWV and the unions. Get employment counsel first; this isn't legal advice.

**Two questions**
- How much of the €12m is cash, and what do the 2028 projects still need?
- Who owns you, and what must "discipline" prove to them?
