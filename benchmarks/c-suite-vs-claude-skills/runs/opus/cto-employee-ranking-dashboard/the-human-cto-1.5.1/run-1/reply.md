You can push back without being "difficult". Raise it privately with the VP as help with their goal, and bring a better measure plus one fact they'll want early: in the Netherlands, a per-person performance ranking like this very likely needs works council consent before it goes live. Article 27(1)(l) WOR covers systems that can monitor performance.

**Why your instinct is right**
- Acceptance rate shows how often someone accepts a suggestion, not whether the code was good. Uplevel's 2024 study of about 800 developers found no significant change in cycle time with Copilot, and 41% more bugs.
- PRs merged rewards splitting work. It punishes the people who review, mentor, fix incidents and delete code.
- A fixed bottom 10% puts someone on a plan even in a strong team, and people can game both numbers within a week.

**What to do**
1. **Ask before you argue, in a 1:1.** A new VP often reaches for numbers they can see quickly, so assume pressure, not malice. Ask what problem this should solve: slow delivery, proving Copilot pays off, or specific people they're worried about. Each has a better answer than a ranking.
2. **Offer to build the alternative yourself.** That makes you the helpful one. At team level, track lead time, review wait time, PR size and change fail rate, and compare each team with its own past. For Copilot, measure delivery and quality for 6–8 weeks against a comparison team that starts later. A concern about one person goes through their manager: clear expectations, feedback and a fair chance.
3. **Mention the works council as protection, not a threat.** "Before rollout, can we check with HR whether this needs OR consent? Better to know now than have it stopped later." The privacy officer should see it too, since it's personal performance data. This isn't legal advice; HR or counsel can confirm.
4. **If it goes ahead anyway,** put your questions in writing, neutrally. Talk to other tech leads and engineering managers (you're probably not alone), then the works council if needed. Keep a short record of the facts. How far you take it is your call: speaking up doesn't mean you have to risk your job.
