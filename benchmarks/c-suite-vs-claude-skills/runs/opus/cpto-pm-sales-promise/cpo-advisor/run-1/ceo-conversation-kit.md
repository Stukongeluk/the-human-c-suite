# CEO conversation kit: three promised features for Q1

A working sheet for the five steps in my reply. Fill it in before you meet the CEO.

## 1. Get the facts first (step 1)

Ask the CEO or the account owner. Frame it as "help me deliver this", never as "why did you promise this".

- What exactly did you tell them? Is there an email, a slide or meeting notes?
- Is it in a contract, order form or renewal, or was it said in conversation?
- What problem does each feature solve for them? What happens on their side if it's missing?
- What does "done" mean to them: live for all their users, a beta, a pilot team?
- What's behind end of Q1: their renewal date, their own launch, a budget cycle?
- Which of the three matters most to them? If nobody knows, can you and the account owner ask them directly?

## 2. Trade-off table (steps 2–3)

Fill this in with your engineering lead. Size each item S / M / L and note how confident engineering is.

| Item | Size | Confidence | Option A | Option B | Option C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Promised feature 1 | | | Full, Q1 | Full, Q1 | Slim, Q1 |
| Promised feature 2 | | | Full, Q1 | Full, Q1 | Slim, Q1 |
| Promised feature 3 | | | Full, Q1 | Early Q2 | Slim, Q1 |
| Roadmap item (name it) | | | Moves to Q2 | Stays | Stays |
| Roadmap item (name it) | | | Moves to Q2 | Stays | Moves to Q2 |
| **Who is affected by what moves** | | | | | |

The option columns are examples. Your sizing decides the real mix.

- Name the items that move and who they affect, for example "Project X moves to Q2, which affects Customer Y and the sales team". "Some roadmap items" isn't good enough.
- Don't plan the team at 100%. A plan with no slack slips on the first surprise. A common rule of thumb is to plan around 80% and keep the rest for bugs, support and unknowns.
- Leave overtime out of every option. If a plan only works with extra hours for four months, it doesn't work.

## 3. The one-to-one with the CEO (step 4)

**Opening:**
> "I want [Customer] to get what you promised them, and I want us to hit the date. I'd rather have a hard conversation with you now than a bad one with them in March. Here's what it takes."

**Then, in this order:**
1. "Here's how big the three features are." (sizing)
2. "Here's what's already committed for Q1, and why." (current roadmap and who it serves)
3. "Here are three ways to do it, and what each one costs." (the table)
4. "I recommend [option] because [one reason]."
5. "Which do you want? And will you back the deferrals when people ask me why their work moved?"

**Phrases to avoid:**

| Avoid | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "You shouldn't have promised this." | "Let's make sure we can deliver it." |
| "We can't do this." | "We can do this if we move X." |
| "We'll try." / "We'll see." | "Here's what I can commit to with confidence." |
| "The team is too busy." | "These are the specific items it would replace." |
| "Engineering says no." | "Here's what engineering and I worked out together." |

**If he says "just do all of it":**
> "We'll give it everything. I need to be straight with you, though: with the people we have, I expect [item] to slip by about [time]. If that's not acceptable, which matters more to you?"

Then put that in writing (section 4), so the risk is on record and it's his call.

## 4. Follow-up email (same day)

> **Subject:** [Customer] Q1 features: what we agreed
>
> Hi [CEO],
>
> Thanks for making time today. To confirm what we agreed:
>
> - **We'll deliver:** [features and scope] by [date].
> - **To make room, these move:** [items] to [new timing]. I'll tell [people affected] by [date].
> - **Main risks:** [one or two]. I'll flag these early if they start to happen.
> - **Updates:** a short progress note every two weeks, starting [date].
>
> Let me know if I've got anything wrong.
>
> [Your name]

## 5. Later: preventing the next one

Don't raise this in the first meeting. Wait a few weeks until he can see progress, then frame it as protecting him:

> "This is going well. Next time you want to commit a date to a customer, give me 48 hours. I'll come back with what's realistic, so you can promise with confidence and we never have to renegotiate with the customer."

It also helps to keep a simple, shared view of what's committed each quarter that the CEO and sales can see. That way the next promise starts from what the team actually has room for.
