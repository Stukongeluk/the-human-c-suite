Don't absorb it quietly and don't refuse it. Within a week, take the CEO a private, one-page view of what the three features cost, what slips if you build them, and two or three ways to keep his promise. Then let him choose the trade-off. That protects the customer, his credibility and your team.

The trap is saying yes and hoping extra hours close the gap. A missed date in March hurts him with his biggest customer far more than an honest conversation now.

**Before you talk to him (two or three days)**
- Ask your engineers for a rough estimate of each feature, as a range.
- Using Jira and your roadmap, list what would slip and which customers that affects.
- Find out what the customer is trying to achieve: a renewal, a rollout, a deadline of their own.
- Tell your team you're handling it and won't commit them to anything they haven't estimated.

**The conversation (one-to-one, before any wider meeting)**
- Start from his goal, not your capacity. He made the promise to protect an important account, and that's a fair reason.
- Show the options, for example:
  1. All three by end of Q1; these named roadmap items move to Q2.
  2. The smallest version of each that solves the customer's problem, or two by Q1 and the third with a firm date.
  3. Part of the need met another way: configuration, an integration or a workaround.
- Be clear about what isn't an option: overtime, or skipping testing or security review. That comes back as incidents and people leaving.

**Afterwards**
- Send him a short summary of what he chose and what moves, and agree together how the leadership team hears it. The trade-off is then visible instead of carried silently by your team.
- Offer to join him when he updates the customer. Many customers prefer a reliable date for less over three rushed features.
- Propose one rule for next time: nothing is promised to a customer until the team building it has estimated it. Until then, the customer gets a date for an answer.

**A line you can use**
"I want your promise to [customer] to land, and I don't want either of us to find out in February that it won't. Here's what the three features cost, what would move, and three ways we could do it. Which trade-off do you prefer?"

Two things would change this advice: whether the date and scope are in a contract, and whether the account is up for renewal before then.
