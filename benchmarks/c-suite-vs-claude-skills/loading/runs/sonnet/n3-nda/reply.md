**Bottom Line: Negotiate. Do not sign as written.**

Yes, this clause is one-sided. It is also unusually aggressive, even for a one-way NDA.

**The Risks**

1. **It runs in one direction only.** Only the Recipient indemnifies. The Discloser gives nothing back, for example for sharing information it had no right to share or for inaccurate information. If the NDA is meant to be mutual, this clause breaks that.
2. **It is strict liability.** "Any loss arising from any disclosure, howsoever caused" does not depend on fault. As written, the Recipient pays even if:
   - the Discloser's own systems or staff leaked the information,
   - a third party stole it despite reasonable security,
   - the Recipient was legally compelled to disclose it, or
   - the disclosure was authorised or harmless.
   It reaches beyond the Recipient's own breach. A normal confidentiality obligation makes you liable for breaching your duty. This clause makes you liable for outcomes.
3. **It is uncapped and unbounded.** There is no liability cap, and "any loss" has no limit. It does not exclude indirect or consequential loss, lost profits or reputational harm. It also does not require the loss to be foreseeable or proven. A leak of low-value information could create unlimited exposure.

There is a further problem. The clause covers "any disclosure", not "any unauthorised disclosure". It also does not mention the standard carve-outs: public domain, rightfully received from a third party, independently developed, and required by law. A disclosure that the NDA itself permits could still trigger indemnity.

**Counter-Proposals**

Suggested replacement, to be adapted by counsel:

> "The Recipient shall be liable to the Discloser for direct damages actually suffered by the Discloser to the extent caused by the Recipient's breach of this Agreement. Neither party shall be liable for indirect, consequential or punitive damages or lost profits. Each party's aggregate liability under this Agreement shall not exceed [a fixed amount / 12 months of fees]."

If the other side insists on an indemnity, at minimum:
- limit it to unauthorised disclosure caused by the Recipient's breach, or by its representatives acting under its control,
- make it mutual,
- exclude information that falls within the standard carve-outs (public domain, rightfully received, independently developed, legally compelled with notice),
- add a cap and an exclusion of indirect loss, and
- add a duty to notify and mitigate, with the Recipient able to control the defence of any claim.

Also check that the NDA contains the usual carve-outs (public domain, rightfully received, independent development, required by law). In practice most NDAs rely on a breach-of-contract remedy plus injunctive relief and do not use an indemnity at all. So you can fairly ask for the indemnity to be deleted.

**Outside Counsel Action Items**

- Check which law governs the NDA. Indemnity and liability-limit rules vary by jurisdiction, and some limits on liability cannot be waived.
- Have counsel confirm the full definition of "Confidential Information" and the permitted-disclosure carve-outs.
- Ask whether this is a one-way or mutual NDA and who the Recipient is. The risk differs a lot if you are the Recipient, which is likely given the clause.
- Check the clause against any liability cap or limitation-of-liability clause elsewhere in the document.

**Your Decision**

Whether the deal is worth pushing back on, or walking away from, if the other side will not move off an uncapped, no-fault indemnity. Only you can weigh how much you need the relationship against the exposure. If you are the Recipient, I would not sign this clause as drafted.

This is general information, not legal advice. I have only seen this one sentence, so please have a licensed attorney review the full NDA before you sign.
