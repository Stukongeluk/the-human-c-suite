**Recommendation:** pay the plan on adjusted EBITDA, but make it 40% of the bonus, not 100%. Define "adjusted" so it can't be flattered, and balance the rest with cash, customers, people and quality. Pay about a third of leaders' awards over three years. I don't know your company yet, so every threshold, weight and percentage below is a labelled starting estimate to calibrate against your own budget and history.

**The trap:** a bonus on EBITDA alone pays managers to cut training, maintenance, security and people this year and leave the bill for next year. It also pays for relabelling recurring costs as "one-off". The design below closes both doors.

## 1. Define "adjusted EBITDA" first

- **Start from reported EBITDA** and reconcile every adjustment line by line to it. The audit committee (or owners, in a smaller company) approves the adjustment list before the year starts, not after.
- **Allowed add-backs:** truly one-off items such as a disposal gain or loss, or an acquisition's transaction costs.
- **Not allowed:** restructuring or severance that has occurred in the last two years or is likely in the next two. That is a cost of running the business, so it stays in.
- **Spend floors:** training, maintenance and security spend must stay at or above the budgeted level, or the shortfall is deducted from the bonus profit measure. Capitalised development may not rise above last year's share of spend without committee approval.
- **No bonus for headcount reduction as such.** A cut that costs more than it saves (severance, rehiring, lost revenue) is counted at its full cost.

## 2. Scorecard (estimates, to calibrate)

| Measure | Weight | Threshold (pays 50%) | Target (100%) | Stretch (cap 150%) | Guard against gaming |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adjusted EBITDA | 40% | 90% of budget | budget | 110% of budget | approved adjustment list, spend floors, recurring charges stay in |
| Cash conversion (operating cash flow / EBITDA) | 15% | 70% | 80% | 90% | no squeezing small suppliers; payment terms not stretched beyond contract |
| Customer retention / satisfaction | 15% | last year minus 2 points | last year | plus 2 points | measured on the same base, not on a reselected customer set |
| People: regretted attrition, engagement, safety | 15% | last year plus 2 points attrition | last year | minus 2 points | an exit shown as "non-regretted" needs HR and works-council or employee-representative review |
| Quality or risk (incidents, audit findings, security) | 15% | no major breach | agreed target | agreed stretch | owned by a function outside the bonus group |

Weights add to 100%. Straight-line interpolation between points, so there is no cliff that invites last-quarter games. The 150% cap on each line limits the upside from any one number.

**Gate:** if adjusted EBITDA is below threshold, the financial lines pay zero and the non-financial lines pay at most 50% of target. Nobody is paid full bonus for a bad year just because the soft measures looked good.

## 3. Who gets what (estimates)

| Group | Target bonus, % of salary | Paid in year | Deferred |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive team | 40-60% | 2/3 | 1/3, vesting in equal parts over 3 years |
| Senior managers | 20-30% | 80% | 20% over 2 years |
| Other managers | 10-15% | 100% | none |

Cost check on a worked example: a 150-person management group with an average salary of 90,000 and a blended 20% target pays out about 2.7M at target (150 x 90,000 x 20%), up to about 4.05M at the 150% cap. Run the real figures against budget so the pool is affordable at target and doesn't push you under covenant at stretch.

## 4. Multi-year protection

- **Malus and clawback:** deferred amounts can be cut, and paid amounts recovered for up to 3 years, if results prove misstated or were bought at the long term's expense (for example, a deferred maintenance bill or a security incident traced to the cut).
- **Two-year view:** the committee reviews the plan against the following year's results before the deferred tranches vest.

## 5. Leaders first

State in the plan, in advance: when results disappoint, executive and senior-manager bonuses fall first and furthest, and they are cut before any frontline pay, hours or jobs. Leaders' targets are on the company result, not only their own unit. Frontline and non-management colleagues should have a share in a good year (a profit share or a flat one-off payment on the same gate), so "the company did well" is not felt only by managers.

## 6. Compliance and governance

- Pay design, works council or employee-representative rights, and tax treatment vary by country. Have employment counsel and your tax adviser check the final text. This is not legal or tax advice.
- If a lender's covenant is built on EBITDA, use the same definition so the bonus and the bank see one number.
- Whoever sets the adjustment list shouldn't be paid from it: the committee decides, not the executives it rewards.
