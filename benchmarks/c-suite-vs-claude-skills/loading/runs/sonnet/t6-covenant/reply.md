Go to the bank now, before the breach, with a funded plan and a request for a waiver or covenant reset. In parallel, close the gap with cash, spend and pricing levers first. Cutting jobs is the last option, and for a next-quarter deadline it is usually too slow to help anyway.

**Why not cut people**
Notice periods and consultation in most of Europe push savings back by months, and severance is paid up front. A headcount cut therefore often makes the covenant quarter worse. Reported EBITDA takes the one-off charge, and cash falls before any saving arrives.

**Step 1: size the gap (this week)**
Leverage is net debt divided by covenant EBITDA, so you can close the gap from either side. Check the definitions in the loan agreement first, because add-backs, cure rights and the test date change the answer.

**Step 2: build the 13-week cash forecast and two cases**
Make a base case and a downside case, each with its covenant headroom at the test date. The bank will ask for both. Label every figure as actual or estimate.

**Step 3: pull levers, least harmful first**
1. **Cash and working capital:** collect overdue receivables, invoice on time, resolve disputes, time capex and inventory. Don't stretch small suppliers to fix your own ratio.
2. **Waste and spend:** renegotiate or cancel vendors, cloud, licences, consultants and office costs.
3. **Revenue and pricing:** fix unbilled work, unapproved discounts and churn, and raise prices where the value supports it.
4. **Financing:** an equity cure or owner contribution, a bridge from existing investors, or a waiver or amendment. A waiver fee is often far cheaper than the alternatives.
5. **Owners and leaders first:** dividends, distributions and leadership bonuses stop before anyone's job is touched.
6. **Hiring freeze and attrition:** pause non-critical hiring.
7. **Hours, not exits, for a temporary dip:** short-time work schemes where they exist.

Cost each lever on the same basis, net of its own costs and dated by quarter. Include "do nothing" in the comparison. A lever you cannot size yet does not count toward closing the gap.

**Step 4: talk to the bank early**
Bring the forecast, the base and downside headroom, the actions each case triggers, and what owners and leaders are contributing. Ask for a waiver or a reset. Banks are generally more flexible with early, credible, numerate borrowers than with surprises.

**Two cautions**
- Don't relabel recurring costs as one-off to lift covenant EBITDA. Every adjusted figure must reconcile to the reported one, and the bank's own definitions rule.
- Don't hit the ratio by cutting training, maintenance or security. That moves the problem to next year.

This is not legal or financial advice. Have counsel read the covenant, cure and cross-default clauses, and tell your auditor early, because a breach can affect going-concern and debt-classification assessments.
