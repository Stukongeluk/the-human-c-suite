## This week

- **Recompute the ratio exactly as your agreement defines it.** That covers the EBITDA definition and allowed addbacks, the cash-netting rules, the test and certificate dates, any equity cure, and whether the test only applies above a certain revolver drawing. Use the definitions as agreed, and don't relabel recurring costs as one-off.
- **Map what a breach triggers:** a freeze on the revolver, acceleration, and cross-defaults into other loans, leases and hedges. A breach only becomes a cash problem when lenders freeze or call the money, which is why you go early.
- **Mind the balance-sheet date.** Under IFRS (IAS 1), a loan in breach at the reporting date is shown as current unless the waiver was signed before that date. Tell your auditors, because this feeds their going-concern view.
- **Stop money flowing to owners and leaders:** dividends, management fees, buybacks and this year's leadership bonus.

## Why not redundancies

Forty roles at €75k fully loaded save €3.0m a year. With consultation and notice periods, though, people leave after the test date, so none of the saving reaches LTM EBITDA in time. Meanwhile the charge, about €1.2m, usually hits the P&L at announcement. That takes the ratio to 4.05x unless the agreement adds the charge back.

Some agreements let you add back expected run-rate savings, and then a layoff looks like a fix on paper. But that addback covers the spending savings too, it is usually capped, and lenders tend to discount savings announced in the test quarter. If the decline proves structural, the reset buys time to resize through attrition and by stopping low-value work.
