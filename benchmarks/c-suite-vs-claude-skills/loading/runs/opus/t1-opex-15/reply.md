**My recommendation: commit to the board now on about 9% of structural opex savings, run-rate by Q3, from spend and hiring flows. Decide how to close the last ~6 points at a checkpoint at the end of February, once those savings are validated, and not with a headcount number today.** On my illustrative numbers, cutting about 10% of staff to close that gap saves 6 points of run-rate but only about 0.8 points of 2027 opex after severance, and that disappears if the cut pushes roughly 2% more of those who stay to leave.

I don't have your ledger, so every figure below is an estimate. Opex is indexed so that today's annual run-rate = 100, and I've assumed fully loaded payroll is about 60% of it. If payroll is a bigger share, the non-payroll savings shrink.

The board isn't wrong to want a leaner cost base, and a hard target forces decisions that otherwise drift. The trap: a percentage and a date with no diagnosis usually lands on payroll, the biggest and easiest line to count.

## The two things that change this plan most

1. **What the 15% is for and how it's measured.** Cash runway, a loan covenant, margin or a valuation story? Q3 run-rate or full-year 2027, against today's run-rate or the 2027 budget? If the budget already contains planned hires, deferring them may close several points by itself. If the goal is margin, pricing counts too. If the company is profitable and has cash, tell the board so plainly rather than cutting people to reach a round number.
2. **Where the money actually sits.** Opex by vendor and activity from the general ledger, plus cloud, SaaS and contractor spend, turns my ranges into your numbers within two to three weeks.

Pin both down this month: the first with the board, the second from finance.

That leaves a gap to 15% of about **6 points in the mid case**, somewhere between 2.3 and 9.5.

- **Stop low-value work outright instead of shaving 15% off everything.** Ending two or three initiatives frees the contractor and cloud spend counted above, and frees people to fill the roles the freeze leaves open, which is what makes the freeze safe.
- Ending contractor and agency work still ends someone's income: give proper notice and the real reason. Don't pay small suppliers late to ease your own cash.
- **Protect** customer-facing capacity, security, maintenance and training. If any of them has to be cut, write its future cost next to the saving.
- These are Q3 run-rate figures; the full-year 2027 effect is smaller because savings ramp up.

## Closing the last ~6 points

| Option | Extra Q3 run-rate | 2027 net, after one-off costs | Reversible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop at ~9% and agree a later date for the rest | 0 | 0 | Yes |
| Go for margin instead of opex: price rises, unbilled work, unapproved discounts, churn | Not opex | Depends on revenue | Mostly |
| Temporary measures: cut the leadership bonus pool first, then voluntary reduced hours or unpaid leave | 0 (temporary) | 0.5–1.1 | Yes |
| Voluntary exits: ~10% of staff, 6-month package | 6.0 | −0.2 | No |
| Compulsory redundancies: ~10% of staff, ~4 months' cost per person, exits from 1 July | 6.0 | +0.8 | No |

Severance, paid notice and support are paid when people leave, so they land in the same year as only half a year of savings. In much of Europe, consultation and notice make July the earliest realistic exit date even if you decide in March. The ~2% break-even assumes replacing a leaver costs 0.75 times their annual cost (Gallup's range is one-half to two times salary), and one study found a 1% downsizing went with 31% more voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). Only about 10% of cost programmes showed sustained results three years later (cited by McKinsey), so the plan has to change what work gets done, not just budgets.

## How I'd run it

- **This month:** leadership tells the board and staff plainly that the cost base is its responsibility, and puts its own bonus pool on the table first. Start the hiring freeze with an exceptions process and tighten discretionary spend. Brief the works council or staff representatives now, with the real numbers, while they can still improve the plan; they usually know where the waste is.
- **December to February:** renegotiate before cancelling, stop the chosen initiatives, move people into open critical roles. Every saving gets an owner, a date and a measure.
- **End of February checkpoint:** report validated run-rate savings to the board, and only then decide whether a gap remains and which option closes it. If redundancies prove unavoidable, do them once, with legal advice on the procedure, a fair package and support from the day notice is given.
- **Every month, to the board:** opex run-rate and cash, next to regretted attrition, customer churn and delivery or incident measures, so a saving that quietly costs customers or people shows up early.
