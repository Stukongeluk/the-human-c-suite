**Recommendation:** make adjusted EBITDA the biggest single measure in the 2027 management bonus (40%), but not the only one. Cash conversion, customer retention, people, and quality and risk make up the other 60%. Write down what "adjusted" means before 1 January, and defer 40% of every award for three years, with malus and clawback. On illustrative numbers (12 participants, €2.0m total base salary, €15m budget EBITDA), that gives a €600k target pool, or 4.0% of budget EBITDA, capped at €900k.

**The problem with EBITDA alone:** it pays managers to cut training, maintenance and people this year and to relabel recurring costs as "one-off", so the number rises while the company weakens. Of 401 senior financial executives surveyed, 78% said they would give up economic value for smoother earnings, and many would cut R&D, advertising or maintenance to hit a target (Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal, 2005). EBITDA's strengths are real (simple, what owners and lenders watch), so it stays the largest measure and the gate: nothing pays unless the profit shows up.

## 1. Scorecard (one for the whole team)

| Measure | Weight | Threshold (pays 50%) → target (100%) → stretch (150%) | How it could be gamed, and the guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bonus EBITDA (defined in section 2) | 40% | 95% → 100% → 110% of budget | Cutting protected spend or relabelling costs: neutralised by the definition |
| Cash conversion: operating cash flow ÷ bonus EBITDA | 15% | last year → budget → budget + 5 pts | Squeezing suppliers or rushing collections at year-end: measured as the average of four quarter-ends, with small suppliers paid on agreed terms |
| Customers: gross revenue retention (or repeat revenue / on-time delivery if you don't sell recurring contracts) | 15% | last year → budget → set with the targets | Buying renewals with discounts: renewals below the margin floor don't count, and the measure is gross, so upsell can't hide churn |
| People: regretted attrition and engagement (plus lost-time injuries where the work is physical) | 15% | last year → agreed improvement → set with the targets | Pushing people out or pressuring survey answers: team-level data only, an independently run survey, and "regretted" recorded by HR at exit |
| Quality and risk: severity-1 incidents, defects or complaints, overdue audit findings, maintenance backlog | 15% | last year → agreed improvement → set with the targets | Under-reporting: incidents classified by an independent function, and a hidden incident triggers malus |

- Payout is linear between the points, zero below threshold, and capped at 150% of target bonus.
- **Gate:** nothing pays on any measure if bonus EBITDA is below 90% of budget, or if a loan covenant is breached or needs a waiver.
- No individual multipliers (they turn the plan into a negotiation); business-unit heads are measured 50% group, 50% own unit. The committee can reduce an individual award to zero for misconduct, risk breaches or gaming, but never raise one.

## 2. What "adjusted" means

Bonus EBITDA starts from reported EBITDA in the audited accounts. Each adjustment is named in a bridge from the reported figure, and the audit committee or auditor signs that bridge off before anything is paid.

- **Allowed, in both directions (gains come out as well as costs):** deal costs of acquisitions and disposals the board approved, with targets restated pro rata; gains and losses on selling assets; and a truly one-off item. "Truly one-off" means nothing similar in the prior two years and nothing similar reasonably likely in the next two. That is the SEC's test before a US-listed company may call a charge non-recurring in a non-GAAP measure (Regulation S-K Item 10(e), C&DI 102.03). It is good practice everywhere.
- **Stays in:** recurring restructuring and redundancy costs, share-based pay, the bonus itself, recruiting, normal litigation, and "transformation" programmes. No measure rewards cutting headcount. A saving from job cuts counts only after subtracting severance, notice pay, contractors and rehiring.
- **Neutralised:** if training, maintenance, security, quality or R&D spend falls below 95% of budget, the underspend is deducted from bonus EBITDA. Changes to capitalisation, provisioning or lease accounting policy made during the year are reversed for the bonus calculation. If total adjustments exceed 10% of reported EBITDA, the committee reviews each one and records its reasons.

## 3. Payment, deferral, malus and clawback

- **60% in cash** after the 2027 accounts are signed, so in spring 2028. Accrue it monthly during 2027 so the cost sits inside the EBITDA being measured.
- **40% deferred** into shares, or into phantom shares or deferred cash in a private company, vesting three years after the award. It vests only if customer and people measures stay at or above threshold in the following two years and the 2027 results haven't reversed (revenue pulled forward, maintenance backlog coming due, restatement).
- **Malus** applies to unvested amounts. **Clawback** applies to paid amounts for three years. Either is triggered by a restatement, misconduct, or results later found to be wrong or achieved at the long term's expense.

## 4. Leaders first, written into the rules

If during 2027 the company freezes or cuts staff pay, cuts the staff bonus or profit share, or cuts jobs for financial reasons, no management bonus is paid for that year. The management bonus is the first cost to go when results disappoint, and the plan says so in advance. If you have a staff profit share, run it on the same bonus EBITDA so everyone works towards one number.

## 5. What it pays (illustrative; replace with your figures)

Assumptions: 12 participants, €2.0m total base salary, 30% average target bonus, €15m budget bonus EBITDA.

| Scenario | Payout (% of target) | Pool | Cash in 2028 | Deferred |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. EBITDA 88% of budget (below the gate) | 0% | €0 | €0 | €0 |
| B. EBITDA 96%, customers at threshold, rest on target | 76% | €459k | €275k | €184k |
| C. Everything on target | 100% | €600k | €360k | €240k |
| D. Adjusted EBITDA 108%, but 5 points come from cutting training and maintenance (103% after the definition); attrition worse than threshold; quality at threshold | 84% | €501k | €301k | €200k, at risk of lapsing |
| E. A strong year done honestly: EBITDA 110%, everything at stretch | 150% | €900k | €540k | €360k |

An EBITDA-only plan would pay D 140% (€840k) against 100% for C: cutting would beat delivering. Here D pays less than an on-plan year, with its deferral at risk. The stretch pays for itself: target to stretch adds €1.5m of EBITDA and at most €300k of bonus (20%).

## 6. Governance and law

- The board or remuneration committee approves rules, definitions and targets before 1 January; no mid-year resets except under the acquisition rule. Finance calculates, the auditor or audit committee checks the bridge, HR supplies people measures in aggregate only (no names or individual pay). The board reviews the scorecard quarterly.
- Employment counsel should write deferral, malus and clawback into contracts before the year starts (enforceability varies by country, and a works council may have a say on pay schemes); a tax adviser should review deferred or phantom shares. A listed company also needs its shareholder-approved remuneration policy and, if US-listed, the clawback policy required under SEC Rule 10D-1. This is not legal or tax advice.

**Assumptions:** a calendar-year plan, a board-approved budget to set targets from, and reliable data for each measure (billing or CRM, the HR system plus an independent survey, incident and audit logs). If a measure has no reliable baseline yet, move its weight to cash conversion for 2027 and build the baseline during the year.
