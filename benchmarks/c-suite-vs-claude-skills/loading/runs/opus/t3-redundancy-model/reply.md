**Put the net figure in the board pack, not 40 × salary.** On placeholder inputs, 40 redundancies take about €3.0m a year out of payroll at full run-rate, but net of their costs they save only about **€0.1m in FY2027** and **€2.3m in FY2028**. I'd recommend a hiring freeze plus targeted voluntary exits instead: about **€0.5m in 2027 and €2.0m in 2028**, with no compulsory exits.

I've drafted the board paper (`board-paper-draft.md`) and the model behind it (`savings_model.py`, saved alongside this reply; change the inputs at the top and every table reprints). Every figure is an estimate until payroll, counsel and account owners replace the placeholders: average base salary €60k, 25% on-costs (€75k loaded per role), consultation in Jan–Feb 2027, exits at the end of May and July 2027, calendar financial year.

One trap worth naming: a headcount number chosen before the problem it solves usually reaches the board at its gross value, and here the first year sees about 4% of the €3.0m.

- FY2027 gross is half the run-rate because salaries keep running through consultation and notice.

- The P&L takes a restructuring provision of about €0.8m in Q1 2027, when the plan is communicated and before any saving arrives. Cash bottoms out at −€735k in July 2027 and only pays back in December.

### What breaks it

- About 4 more leavers than assumed, or €123k more lost margin, wipes out the FY2027 saving.
- Exits 3 months later: FY2027 becomes −€527k. Severance of 6 months instead of 3: −€477k. Exits in Jan–Feb 2027 (only where no consultation duty or long notice applies): +€1.24m.
- FY2028 holds up better: it takes about €2.3m of lost gross margin a year to erase it.

Option B freezes hiring from January, backfilling only critical roles (about 20 left unfilled, assuming roughly 500 staff and 10% turnover) and offering those vacancies first to people whose work is stopping, plus 12 targeted voluntary exits on 6 months' base pay. Any remaining gap of about 8 roles is decided at a Q3 2027 review. It carries its own scheme and review costs (€60k), reaches its full €2.4m run-rate only in January 2028, and a freeze can bite where work can't stop. Either way, management must name the work that stops.

Three measures aren't credited to either option, because both can use them:
- a spend review (est. €0.38–0.75m a year)
- faster collections (est. €0.5–1.0m one-off cash)
- deferring the FY2027 leadership bonus (est. €0.32–0.48m, one year only)

If results below plan are behind this, leadership pay should move before any role does.

### Before it goes to the board

- In most of Europe, 40 exits in one round is a collective redundancy (EU directive 98/59/EC; in the Netherlands from 20 in three months in one UWV work area, in the UK 20–99 at one establishment means at least 30 days' consultation first). So the board should approve a **proposal to consult**, not a final decision. US federal WARN may not apply at 40, but some states are stricter. Counsel must confirm; this isn't legal advice. No names or individual pay in the pack.
- Call the charge "one-off" only if there was no similar one in the last two years and none is likely in the next two. Reconcile any adjusted figure to the reported one.
