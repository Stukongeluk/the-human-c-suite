I'd take the board a real AI case rather than a case for cutting 30% of the team in 2027, because on my first estimates that version doesn't survive Swedish law or the full cost. Cutting 27 roles in mid-2027 shows up on a slide as SEK 22m a year, but net of everything it costs, it is **about SEK -30m in 2027 and -6m over 2027–29**.

The better route lets measured AI capacity lead and headcount follow, through attrition, a hiring freeze and redeployment. That route nets **-14m in 2027 and +5m over three years**. It reaches **about 24 fewer FTE (27%) by end-2029** and the **same run-rate saving of about SEK 15m a year**, with no compulsory redundancies.

The board paper and the model are attached. Every figure is an estimate until we load our ledger, payroll aggregates and HR data.

**Why "30% next year" doesn't hold up**
- **The AI won't be ready in time.** Even straightforward finance uses such as data extraction, AP automation and report creation typically take [nine to ten months to reach their expected value](https://www.cfodive.com/news/cfos-need-realistic-ai-time-value-expectations-gartner/831315/) (Gartner). On my ramp, net capacity is under 1 FTE in the first half of 2027 and about 14 FTE by Q4.
- **Swedish law sets the timing.** We need MBL negotiation first. Arbetsförmedlingen must be notified four months ahead for 26–100 people, and notice runs one to six months.
- **Swedish law also decides who leaves.** LAS selection order goes by length of service, so the leavers are mostly recent hires, not the people whose tasks the AI took. Anyone we rehire for the same work within nine months must first be offered to them.
- **The work doesn't vanish on exit day.** Contractors and overtime to cover the gap cost about SEK 16m over 2027–28. Packages add about SEK 11m, plus retention, extra resignations and legal fees.
- **Every route ends at about 24 FTE.** The limit is what the AI can do, and redundancies only add one-off cost and a bet we can't undo.

**The options** (SEK m, against doing nothing, estimates)

| | 2027 | 3-year net | Run-rate a year from 2030 | FTE fewer end-2029 | Compulsory dismissals |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1. Board's ask: 27 redundancies, mid-2027 | -29.7 | -6.2 | 15.4 | 24.4 | 27 |
| 2. One round of 24 in Jan 2028, after the AI is proven | -27.4 | -5.3 | 15.1 | 24.0 | 24 |
| **3. Capacity-led (recommended)** | **-14.4** | **+4.9** | **15.4** | **24.4** | **0** |

Option 2 is the board's idea at its strongest.

**If the AI underdelivers,** option 1 suffers most. It only breaks even over three years at 113% of the capacity I've assumed. At 60%, it loses about SEK 27m and we rehire 12 people; option 3 simply stops at 15 fewer FTE and loses 3.6m.

**My plan's weak spot:** if attrition is only 6% *and* there are no internal vacancies to move people into, option 3 needs 12 voluntary exits in 2028. It then ends up about SEK 1–2m behind the redundancy routes over three years. I'd call that a fair price for not betting jobs on an unproven tool, but it's the board's call.

**What I'd ask the board to approve**
- **About SEK 16m for 2027, in two tranches:** 8.5m now and 7.9m at the mid-year gate. Every half-year we stop or scale on measured results: touchless invoice rate, coding accuracy, auto-match rates, days to close and control tests.
- **A hiring freeze with exceptions from January,** keeping a small entry-level intake. People whose work the AI takes over move into funded vacancies in claims, risk, data or DORA work. About 30 are reskilled, and all 90 get the AI-literacy training that Article 4 of the AI Act already expects.
- **A plain commitment:** no compulsory redundancies are planned in this programme. If measured capacity outruns attrition, voluntary terms come first: reduced hours, partial retirement or packages.
- **Controls stay with people.** We keep human sign-off on payments, journals and everything we report, and run a DORA third-party review. If any part counts as outsourcing a critical or important function, we notify Finansinspektionen in advance.
- **No bonus tied to headcount, mine included.** The scorecard is cost per invoice, close speed, error rates, audit findings and regretted attrition.

One Nordic practice worth using is to bring the unions into MBL now, with this model, while the numbers can still change. The OECD finds that [AI adoption goes better for workers when their representatives are consulted](https://read.oecd.org/10.1787/08785bba-en), and it is far cheaper than a dispute over selection order. (Counsel to confirm the legal steps; not legal advice.)

The trap: a case built to prove a number the board has already picked is the first one auditors, unions and FI will take apart.

**Two questions that would change the numbers most**
1. **What is the board actually solving?** An expense-ratio gap to peers, a SEK target with a deadline, or an AI story for owners? The team costs about SEK 75m a year (estimate), and each SEK 10m saved is 0.1 points of expense ratio per SEK 10bn of premium. If the expense ratio is the target, bigger levers may sit outside finance: procurement, IT run costs and claims leakage.
2. **What do our systems say?** Three inputs move the model most: hours by process, attrition and tenure profile, and contractor spend.

**Key assumptions (all estimates)**
- Salary SEK 47k a month, loaded ×1.47
- AI handles 45% of AP hours, 30% of reconciliations and 15% of reporting
- +4 FTE from simplifying processes, -3 FTE of new oversight work
- 10% attrition a year
- Programme cost SEK 16–18m in 2027 and 4.8m a year after
- Exit packages of six months' pay
