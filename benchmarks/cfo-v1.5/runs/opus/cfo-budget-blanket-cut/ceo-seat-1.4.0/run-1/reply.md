Take the board one company-wide target with different targets per cost centre, not an 8% cut in every cost centre. With payroll at 68% of opex, a uniform 8% cut is in practice about 8% of your people. That is around 48 roles, or about 68 if "8% lower" means below 2026 actuals and you also have to absorb next year's wage increases. In a services firm, a billable person whose time you sell brings in roughly twice what they cost, so the simple version can lower opex and profit at the same time.

I've drafted the board paper in `board-paper-budget-2027.md`, with placeholders for your actuals. The essentials:

**What the board needs to hear first**
- **Doing nothing doesn't keep costs flat.** CPB expects wage costs per employee in the business sector to rise 4.4% in 2027, with inflation at 2.7% ([CPB MEV 2027 via AWVN, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.awvn.nl/nieuws/mev-2027-wat-werkgevers-ervan-moeten-weten/)). On your cost mix, that is +3.9% opex. On a thin margin, that alone can wipe out 2027 unless prices rise. That is the real problem behind the 8%.
- **"8% lower" needs a baseline.** 8% below 2026 actuals is 11.4% below the 2027 run-rate. No approach gets that into the 2027 P&L, the uniform one included. Dutch redundancies take effect from mid-2027 at best: works council advice, a one-month wait after notifying UWV, then notice. Transition payments are due before the savings arrive ([business.gov.nl](https://business.gov.nl/regulations/collective-redundancies/), [Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/ontslag/vraag-en-antwoord/hoe-hoog-is-de-transitievergoeding-als-ik-word-ontslagen)).
- **This is a profitable company with a margin problem, not a cash emergency.** Say so plainly.

**The approach: one target, one set of rules, different percentages**
- **Delivery teams** get a contribution-margin target per service line, met through price, utilisation, realisation and staffing to sold demand. They don't get an opex percentage.
- **Enabling functions** each bring options at −5%, −10% and −15%, with the effect on clients and colleagues. The management team (MT) then sets their targets, from 5% to 15% or more.
- **Third-party spend** gets one category review across all cost centres.
- **Protected lines:** training, junior intake where demand supports it, security and compliance, and client service levels. Every vacancy that isn't refilled comes with a decision on what work stops.

An 8% cut below 2026 is worth 11.9 points against the 2027 run-rate. This plan matches that in EBIT terms in the upper half of its range, with no redundancies in the base plan. The uniform rule gets 3.3–6.9 points into 2027, before lost revenue, and needs 13–43 redundancies on top of attrition to reach its run-rate. In BCG's December 2025 survey, only about 20% of cost programmes succeeded, and blunt workforce cuts were rated the most damaging lever for engagement ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/quadrupling-odds-cost-transformation)).

Add a checkpoint at the end of Q1. If the forecast falls short, voluntary measures come next. Targeted reductions follow only where demand has fallen for good, by a separate board decision after works council advice.

**Leadership goes first, in writing**
The paper says the thin margin is the management team's result, yours and the CEO's included. MT bonuses are suspended and MT base pay is cut before anyone's job is touched. That saves only 0.3–0.6 points, but staff will judge the rest of the plan by it.

**Works council**
Bring the works council (OR) in now, at the problem stage, with the real numbers. They are entitled to the budget (art. 31a WOR), and their advice right (art. 25 WOR) covers any reorganisation that follows. Employment counsel should confirm which measures need advice or consent; this isn't legal advice.

**Your next three weeks**
1. **This week, talk privately with your CEO** before anything goes to the board. Keep what's right about "simple" (one number, one set of rules, one deadline, starting at the top) and drop the single percentage. A line you can use: "Let's keep it simple: one target, one set of rules. But 8% off a billable team cuts the revenue we need for the margin the board wants."

3. **Week 2:** collect cost-centre options, run a pricing review with commercial leads, ask staff where the waste is, and talk early with the OR chair.
4. **Week 3:** consolidate, run a pre-mortem with the MT, get the CEO's sign-off and send a pre-read to the board chair.

Ask the board to decide the target and the approach, not a headcount number.

**Two questions that would change this**
- What is the 8% protecting: a margin target, a bank covenant, cash or a shareholder expectation? And is it measured against 2026 actuals or the 2027 run-rate?
- Are your delivery teams full, or is the bench growing? If they're full, cutting capacity cuts revenue. If some service lines have lost demand for good, a targeted reduction there may become the honest answer.
