# Budget 2027: delivering the 8% target without weakening the business

**For decision** · Board meeting of [date] December 2026 · Prepared by: CFO, on behalf of the management team · Draft of 20 November 2026

All figures are in points, where 1 point = 1% of 2026 opex = €[x]m. Ranges are planning assumptions until they are replaced with ledger, HR and time-and-billing data in the first week of December. External facts carry their source and date.

## 1. Recommendation

We recommend one company-wide target for 2027 with different targets per cost centre, instead of the same 8% cut in every cost centre.

- On planning ranges, the plan lowers the cost base by **7.1–10.6 points by the end of 2027**, and price, indexation and realisation add **1.5–3.1 points of margin**. That is an EBIT improvement of **8.6–13.7 points**, against the 11.9 points that an 8% cut below 2026 opex would be worth if it cost no revenue.
- The base plan has no redundancies.
- A uniform 8% cut would mean about 48–68 fewer roles, depending on the baseline, including billable staff whose work we sell. It would deliver no more in the 2027 P&L (3.3–6.9 points before lost revenue) and would put revenue at risk.

We ask the board to decide the target and the approach, not a headcount number. A checkpoint at the end of Q1 2027 decides whether further measures are needed.

## 2. The problem

Our margin is thin but positive: [x]% in the 2026 forecast. This is not a cash emergency. It is a margin that will not survive 2027 unchanged. The CPB expects wage costs per employee in the business sector to rise 4.4% in 2027 and inflation to be 2.7% (CPB, Macro Economische Verkenning 2027, 18 September 2026). With payroll at 68% of our opex, doing nothing raises costs by about 3.9 points. Unless our prices rise by as much, the margin shrinks towards zero.

The management team is responsible for this result. The cost base, the pricing and the shape of the organisation are the result of our decisions. We are therefore taking the first measures ourselves (section 8). We are not asking employees to carry the first part of the correction.

## 4. What "8% lower" means

The target needs a baseline, because 2027 costs rise before we do anything.

| | Index (2026 opex = 100) |
|---|---|
| 2026 opex | 100.0 |
| Wage cost increase on payroll (68 × 4.4%) | +3.0 |
| Inflation on other costs (32 × 2.7%) | +0.9 |
| **2027 "do nothing" budget** | **103.9** |

| Reading of the target | 2027 opex target | Reduction needed against the 2027 do-nothing budget |
|---|---|---|
| A. 8% below 2026 actual opex | 92.0 | 11.9 points (11.4%) |
| B. 8% below the 2027 do-nothing budget | 95.6 | 8.3 points (8.0%) |

Under reading A, no approach, including a uniform cut, gets the full reduction into the 2027 P&L. Savings from vacancies arrive as people leave during the year. Savings from redundancies start in mid-2027 at the earliest and come after one-off costs (section 10). We propose to express the target as an **EBIT improvement of €[x]m by the end of 2027**, with the cost base as its main component, so that measures on the revenue side count too.

**Why a uniform cut does more harm than it looks.**

- **It is mostly a cut in people.** Because people are 68% of our costs, 8% from every cost centre is in practice about 8% of our people.
- **It cuts revenue.** If 75–85% of staff are billable, a billable person whose time we sell brings in roughly 1.8–2 times their payroll cost, because their revenue also covers overhead and margin. Each 1% of sold billable capacity we remove saves about 0.5–0.6 points of payroll and loses about 1 point of revenue. So a uniform cut only improves margin where it removes capacity we cannot sell.
- **It treats every cost centre the same.** Lean teams are cut as hard as padded ones, and teams that bring in revenue as hard as teams that don't. Next year it rewards whoever padded their budget.
- **We can't choose who leaves.** Dutch dismissal rules select by the reflection principle (by age group, most recent hires first) within each type of job, not by where capacity is surplus. Recent hires, often our juniors, are hit first.
- **People who stay leave too.** In a widely cited study, downsizing a workforce by 1% was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor and Nyberg, 2008).
- **Blunt programmes rarely last.** In a December 2025 survey of 2,080 executives whose companies had run a cost programme, BCG found that about 20% succeeded. About one in three programmes used across-the-board cuts. Blunt workforce reductions were rated the most disruptive lever for engagement. Half of the companies ran a new cost programme every one to two years. It is a consultancy survey, so read it as a signal, not a precise rate.

A uniform cut has real advantages: it is simple to explain, it feels fair, it is quick to allocate and it avoids arguments about exemptions. The targeted plan keeps those advantages with one target, one set of rules and one deadline for everyone, but it does not use one percentage.

The effect in the 2027 P&L assumes 70–85% of the third-party savings land within the year, as contracts come up for renewal; vacancy savings land at half their annual value, because leavers are spread over the year; and price effects land at 65–80%, depending on contract anniversaries. Under reading A, the cost measures close 60–90% of the 11.9-point gap, and price and realisation are expected to close the rest. If they don't, the Q1 checkpoint decides on option 4.

## 7. How cost-centre targets are set

- **One company target**, cascaded according to what each cost centre is for.
- **Delivery teams** get a contribution margin target per service line, not an opex percentage. Staffing follows sold demand, and vacancies are refilled where utilisation is above [x]%.
- **Enabling functions** (finance, HR, IT, facilities, marketing, management) present options at −5%, −10% and −15%, each with its effect on customers, colleagues and risk. The MT then sets targets between 5% and 15% or more, depending on what each function is for.
- **Third-party spend** is managed by category across all cost centres, so we negotiate once with each supplier.
- **Protected lines:** training and learning budgets, junior and trainee intake where delivery demand supports it, information security and compliance, and the service levels we promised clients.
- **No vacancy without a decision on the work.** Every role not refilled comes with an explicit decision on what work stops or changes, so the workload doesn't simply move to the colleagues who stay.

## 8. Management first

The management team accepts responsibility for the margin. For 2027 we propose:

- suspending 2027 bonuses for the MT and directors
- a [10]% base pay reduction for the MT, including the CEO and CFO, by individual agreement
- a review of management perks.

The board may wish to consider the same for its own fees. These measures save little, 0.3–0.6 points. Their purpose is to show where responsibility lies before we ask anything of anyone else.

## 10. Employee participation and legal steps

This section is not legal advice. Employment counsel will confirm each step before we act on it.

- **Works council (OR).**
  - We share the problem and the 2027 numbers now, at the problem stage, and ask for the council's ideas. The council is entitled to the budget and financial information (art. 31a WOR), and we will discuss the plan in the consultation meeting (art. 24 WOR).
  - We request the council's advice on any reorganisation or significant reduction of activities (art. 25 WOR), early enough for the advice to change the plan.
  - We request consent where a staff regulation changes, such as hours, leave, or pay and bonus schemes (art. 27 WOR).
- **Collective dismissal**, relevant only to options 2, 4 and 5. Dismissing 20 or more people in one UWV region within three months requires:
  - notifying UWV and consulting the unions
  - a one-month waiting period after notification
  - selection with external staff first, then by the reflection principle within each age group
  - counting the settlement agreements we initiate towards the threshold (business.gov.nl).
- **Transition payment and notice.**
  - Dismissed employees receive a statutory transition payment of one third of a monthly salary per year of service, up to €102,000 in 2026 or one annual salary if that is higher (Rijksoverheid).
  - Notice periods are one to four months. The UWV procedure time can be deducted, but at least one month must remain (KHN).
  - In practice, redundancies decided in December take effect from mid-2027, and the one-off costs come first.
- **External workers:** we respect their contract terms and give notice and an honest reason.

## 12. Indicators and reporting rhythm

Reviewed monthly by the MT and quarterly with the board and the OR. People data is reported by team and in aggregate, never by individual.

- **Money:** opex run-rate by category against plan; EBIT margin; price realisation on indexation; utilisation and realisation by service line; revenue per billable FTE.
- **People:** voluntary attrition, including leavers we wanted to keep; sickness absence rate and frequency; a workload pulse; the share of vacancies filled internally; overtime. Absence matters financially as well: Dutch employers pay at least 70% of wages for up to two years of illness (UWV).
- **Clients:** service levels, complaints, renewals and churn.
- **Reversal rule:** if a team's sickness absence rises by more than [1] point, its regretted attrition exceeds [x], or its service levels slip, its budget is reopened.

## 13. Assumptions, and what would change our mind

- **About 12% voluntary attrition (about 70 leavers).** If the real figure is below 8%, vacancy savings fall by about a third.
- **75–85% of staff are billable, and delivery teams are largely sold.** If some service lines carry bench that demand won't fill, a targeted capacity reduction there (option 5) becomes the honest answer for those teams.
- **10–15% of third-party spend is reducible.** The first-week spend review will confirm or correct this.
- **Clients accept indexation.** If they don't, the cost base has to do more of the work and the Q1 checkpoint comes into play.
- **The 8% is not tied to a bank covenant or a fixed date.** If it is, the timing changes and we will bring a revised plan.
