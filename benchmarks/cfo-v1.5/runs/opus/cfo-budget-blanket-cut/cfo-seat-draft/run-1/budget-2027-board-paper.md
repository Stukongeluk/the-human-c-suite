# Budget 2027: close the gap with a ranked plan, not 8% per cost centre

**Board paper, draft for CEO review** · From: CFO · For: board meeting of [date]

Tables are in € thousands; the text rounds to € millions. **Every figure in this draft is an illustrative estimate**, scaled to what we know for certain (600 people, payroll 68% of opex, a thin positive margin). Each estimate is listed in Annex A and will be replaced with ledger, payroll and billing data before the meeting.

## 1. Recommendation

We recommend closing the gap with a ranked plan that starts with spend, pricing and leadership pay and manages headcount through natural attrition, instead of cutting every cost centre by 8%.

- **The plan:** about **€4.8M** better operating result in 2027 (€3.8M lower opex and €1.0M better margin), 82% of the 8% target of €5.8M. It runs above the target rate from Q3 and delivers about €6.1M a year from 2028. **No redundancies.**
- **8% per cost centre:** about **€0.2M** better operating result in 2027 after its own costs. Because payroll is 68% of opex, it means about 48 redundancies, about 36 of them in client delivery. The savings start around 1 July, and about €1.5M of revenue is at risk in 2027 (est.).
- **We ask the board to:**
  1. confirm what the 8% is meant to achieve, and against which base (section 2);
  2. approve the plan, including leaders first (sections 5 and 11);
  3. agree the review points and contingencies if the plan falls short (section 14).

Two things follow.

- **The base matters by about €2.0M.** Payroll rises about 3.5% next year from CAO rises and increments (est.) before we decide anything. "8% below this year" means 10.8% below the do-nothing budget; "8% below the budget" means 8.0%.
- **8% of opex is about twice our operating result.** In a services firm, opex is nearly all of our cost, so an 8% cut is really a margin target: from 3.9% to about 11.6%. If margin is the goal, pricing and revenue leakage count towards it as much as cost does. We ask the board to tell us the margin, cash or covenant number it needs, and by when. This paper treats the target as **€5.8M better operating result in 2027 (B)** and also shows the gap to (A).

## 3. Why we don't recommend 8% per cost centre

The case for it is real: one rule is simple to explain, everyone contributes, and nobody gets to lobby for an exemption. In our cost base it also does the following.

- **It lands on people.** 8% of payroll is about €3.9M, about 48 roles at €82k fully loaded (est.).
- **It cuts revenue, not just cost.** About 36 of those roles are in client delivery, where a person costs about €82k and brings in about €167k (est.). Every delivery role we remove that had client work makes the margin worse.
- **It lands late, and the costs come first.** Works council advice, the collective dismissal notification, UWV permits or settlement agreements, and notice periods put the first exits around 1 July (est.). Severance, social plan and advisory costs of about €1.6M are booked mostly in Q2, when the plan is announced, and cash dips in Q3 (section 7).
- **It cuts efficient and bloated teams alike,** including about €98k of training.
- **It may not work as designed.** Dutch selection rules for redundancy (the reflection principle, *afspiegelingsbeginsel*) apply across interchangeable roles in a business location, by age group and length of service, not by cost centre. Managers can't choose "their 8%".
- **Cuts like this rarely stick, and the people who stay leave too.** A 1% downsizing was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). Replacing an employee can cost half to twice their annual salary (Gallup, a US estimate). Research cited by McKinsey found that only 10% of cost-reduction programmes still showed results three years later.
- **To be fair:** from 2028 the blanket cut removes more cost than the plan (€5.5M against €4.8M). It comes out ahead only if fewer than about 1 in 8 of the 36 delivery roles it removes had client work (section 7). If our delivery capacity is that idle, we have a demand problem, and the answer is a targeted capacity decision in the teams with bench, not 8% everywhere.

We keep what is good about the proposal: **one rule for every cost-centre owner** (section 5), and the executive team contributes first.

## 5. How we close it

**How every cost-centre owner builds their 2027 budget:**

1. Payroll at current headcount, minus the roles that won't be refilled, plus approved exceptions.
2. Every non-payroll line justified from zero, by vendor and activity: keep, cut or renegotiate.
3. Every saving has an owner, a date and a measure.
4. What stops or waits is written down (section 9).

The levers are ranked from least to most harm to people. We use the next lever only when the earlier ones can't close the gap in time.

## 8. What we protect, and why

- **Training and development (€1,230k).** Skills are what we sell, and today's juniors are the seniors of 2032. Cutting 8% would save about €98k and cost more in rates, quality and retention.
- **Client-delivery capacity** in teams at or above target utilisation: every role there earns about twice its cost (est.).
- **Sales capacity**, and the marketing that sources pipeline.
- **Security, compliance** and the systems clients rely on.
- **Payment terms for small suppliers:** we don't fix our margin with their cash.

## 9. What stops or waits

- Internal projects without an owner or a clear return (list to be agreed by the executive team; about €328k).
- Consultants and advisory work without a business case and an owner.
- Internal events, non-client travel, and office space beyond hybrid occupancy.
- New support roles, and refills of support roles, unless the executive team approves an exception.
- Executive bonuses for 2027.

## 10. Headcount through flows

- We expect about 66 people to leave in 2027 (11% voluntary attrition, est.). We don't refill about 18 of those roles: about 12 in support functions and about 6 in delivery teams with bench. Headcount goes from 600 to about 582, with no redundancies.
- Delivery roles in teams at or above target utilisation are refilled. Internal candidates are considered first for every open role.
- Exceptions go to a weekly executive review. Teams that don't refill a role get a check on workload and absence after one and three months.
- Contractors whose work moves to our own people end at their contract end date, with the agreed notice.

## 11. Leaders first

- Executive bonuses are suspended for 2027, including the CEO's and the CFO's. The senior managers' bonus pool is halved, and there is no executive pay rise. Estimated saving €500k, to be replaced with the actual provisions. The board decides on its own fees.
- The 2028 management bonus is reset on a balanced scorecard: operating margin with restructuring costs included, client retention, regretted attrition and delivery quality, with part deferred over three years. No bonus is ever paid for reducing headcount as such.
- Contractual bonuses need the individual's agreement, and changes to a bonus scheme may need works council consent (section 15).

## 12. Cash

- Collecting 10 days faster (invoicing on time, resolving disputes, enforcing terms) releases about €2.1M once (est.). It is not counted in the opex numbers.
- If we have bank covenants, we share the plan and its downside case with the bank before the next test date, not after.

## 13. Risks and sensitivities

| If... | Effect on 2027 | Plan 2027 becomes |
|---|---:|---:|
| No lease break in 2027 (housing saving moves to 2028) | -308 | +4,457 |
| Non-payroll levers deliver 25% less | -635 | +4,130 |
| Revenue levers deliver half | -494 | +4,271 |
| Only 12 instead of 18 roles can stay unfilled | -246 | +4,519 |
| All four together | -1,683 | +3,082 |

- **Bench:** levers 1 and 9 both rely on spare capacity in delivery teams. If the utilisation data shows little bench, those two levers shrink, but so does the case for any cut in delivery.
- **Lease terms:** the housing saving depends on a lease event in 2027.
- **Clients:** price changes at renewal carry some churn risk, so account directors own them account by account.
- Even with all four downsides together, the plan delivers about €3.1M in 2027, more than the blanket cut does in 2027 in any case modelled.

We review monthly. These steps come in order, each with its trigger:

| 3 | Targeted capacity decision only where bench is structural and no redeployment exists | Utilisation data shows lasting overcapacity in a team | not modelled | sized on data | One-way door: works council advice, collective dismissal rules; lands in 2028 |

With both contingencies, 2027 comes within about €150k of (B), and the plan reaches (A) from 2028 at 2027 prices, but not in 2027. If the board means (A) for 2027 itself, no plan without job losses gets there. We would show the board what the extra €3.1M would cost in jobs and revenue, and ask it to choose between the target and the timeline.

## 15. Works council, law and governance

- **Works council.** We share this approach and the real numbers at the next consultation meeting, before the budget is final, while the council can still change the plan. A significant reduction in jobs or a reorganisation needs its advice before the decision is final (art. 25 WOR). If we decide against that advice, we must wait a month, during which the council can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (art. 26 WOR). Changes to remuneration schemes, such as bonus or lease schemes, may need its consent (art. 27 WOR) where a collective agreement doesn't settle them.
- **Collective dismissal.** Twenty or more dismissals in one UWV work area within three months require notifying UWV and the unions, and consulting the unions, before the decision is final (WMCO). Settlement agreements we initiate count towards the 20.
- **Costs and timing of any dismissal.** A statutory transition payment (art. 7:673 BW); employer notice of one to four months by length of service (art. 7:672 BW), less the time a UWV procedure took; selection by the reflection principle (Ontslagregeling).
- **Honest accounts.** Any restructuring cost stays in reported opex. Any adjusted figure is reconciled to the reported one, and we agree the timing of any provision with the auditor.
- This paper is not legal advice. Employment counsel reviews this section, and any people measure, before it is decided.
