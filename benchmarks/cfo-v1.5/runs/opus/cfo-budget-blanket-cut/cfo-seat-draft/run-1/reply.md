My recommendation: don't take 8% off every cost centre. Take the board a ranked plan that starts with spend, then pricing, hiring through attrition and leaders' pay, and ask the board what the 8% is meant to buy. On my illustrative numbers, that plan improves the 2027 operating result by about **€4.8M** against an 8% target of **€5.8M**, reaches the full target rate from Q3, and needs no redundancies. Cutting 8% per cost centre improves it by about **€0.2M** in 2027 once its own costs are counted.

The draft board paper is in `budget-2027-board-paper.md`. I didn't have your ledger, so every figure is an estimate scaled to what you told me: I assumed €70M opex and €73M revenue. Swap in the real numbers; the conclusions hold as long as the proportions roughly do.

**Why 8% everywhere fails on its own terms**

A uniform percentage lands where the money is, and in a services firm that's also where the revenue comes from.

- 8% of payroll is about €3.9M, or about 48 roles, roughly 36 of them billable. A delivery person costs about €82k and brings in about €167k (est.). So each one you remove who had client work makes the margin worse.
- In the Netherlands the exits land around 1 July at best (works council advice, the collective dismissal notification, UWV or settlement agreements, notice). About €1.6M of severance and social plan costs come first.
- Redundancy selection follows the reflection principle across interchangeable roles in a location, not by cost centre, so managers can't pick "their 8%" anyway.
- It cuts training and well-run teams as hard as bloated ones.

To be fair to it: from 2028 it removes more cost than my plan (€5.5M against €4.8M). But it only comes out ahead if fewer than about 1 in 8 of the billable roles it removes had client work. If that's true, you have a demand problem that needs a targeted answer, not 8% everywhere.

It protects training (€1.2M), delivery capacity in fully utilised teams, sales, security and small suppliers' payment terms. Collecting 10 days faster adds ~€2.1M of cash, once. If the plan falls behind, contingencies follow in order, each with a trigger: sell more of our own capacity, then voluntary measures in support functions, and a targeted capacity decision only where the data shows lasting bench.

**Two questions that change the numbers**

1. **What is the 8% for?** 8% of opex is about twice this year's operating result, so it's really a margin target (from about 4% to about 11.6%). If the board needs a margin, cash or covenant number, pricing counts as much as cost, and the right target may not be €5.8M at all.
2. **8% below what?** With CAO rises of ~3.5% (est.), 8% below this year's actual opex is about 10.8% below next year's do-nothing budget, a €7.8M gap. No plan without job losses closes that in 2027, so the board would have to choose between the target and the timeline.

**Take it to the CEO first, privately**

The CEO's rule has real virtues: it's simple, everyone contributes, nobody lobbies for exemptions. Keep those. Something like: "I want to keep what's good about your rule: one rule for everyone, no exemptions. But in our cost base 8% everywhere means 48 jobs, 36 of them billable, and it delivers almost nothing in 2027. This plan gets more than four-fifths of the target this year and the full rate from Q3, and it starts with our own bonuses." Ideally the paper goes to the board from the two of you.
