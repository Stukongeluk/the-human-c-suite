# 2027 cost budget: approach for the board

*Working draft for CEO alignment. This becomes the board paper. All figures are illustrative, based on the stated shape of the business (600 people, payroll 68% of opex, thin positive margin) and the assumptions in Appendix D. Replace them with actuals in week 1. The structure and the logic stay the same.*

---

## Decisions requested from the board

1. **Target definition.** Reduce the underlying cost base by 8% against the 2027 do-nothing baseline. Measure it as the exit run-rate in Q4 2027, with a separate, committed 2027 in-year figure. (If the board means 8% below 2026 actual, the actions needed rise from about €5.8M to about €8.3M. See section 1.)
2. **Framework.** Set one company target and allocate it by cost type through five rules (section 3), not as a uniform percentage per cost centre.
3. **One-off budget.** Approve up to €0.3M of restructuring costs for Option B, reported separately from the underlying result.
4. **Process.** Note that structural changes involving job losses go to the works council as proposed decisions under art. 25 WOR. The board approves the target and framework now, not individual role eliminations.
5. **Checkpoint.** Hold a Q1 review, with triggers for a second tranche agreed now (section 6).

## 1. What "8% lower" means in euros

| | €M | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| 2026 operating cost, run-rate | 70.0 | payroll 47.6 (68%), non-payroll 22.4 |
| Labour cost drift 2027, +4.4% | +2.1 | CPB MEV 2027: labour cost per employee, market sector (contract wages +3.8%). Replace with own CAO/merit plan |
| Non-payroll indexation, +2.7% | +0.6 | CPB 2027 CPI. Replace with actual contract indexation |
| **2027 do-nothing baseline** | **72.7** | same headcount, same contracts |
| Target if "8% below 2026" | 64.4 | **8.3 of actions** (11.4% of baseline) |
| Target if "8% below 2027 baseline" | 66.9 | **5.8 of actions** (8.0% of baseline) |

Settle three more definitions before the meeting:

- **In-year vs exit run-rate.** In the Netherlands, people-related savings land around mid-year at the earliest (section 5). An in-year 8% target forces one of two things. Either you cut deeper than the run-rate needs, or you lean on one-off deferrals that come back in 2028. Proposal: target the Q4 2027 exit run-rate and commit to a separate 2027 in-year number.
- **Scope.** Total operating cost, including cost of sales and capitalised labour, under constant accounting policies. A saving counts only when a run-rate line disappears (contract ended, role closed). Deferrals are reported as deferrals. Nothing is reclassified between opex, cost of sales and capex.
- **Vacancies.** Removing budgeted-but-unfilled roles saves money against budget, not against the actual run-rate. It counts only if the baseline includes those roles.

**Recommendation:** use the second definition (8% below baseline) for cost, combined with full indexation on the revenue side. If the board's real objective is margin, price matters as much as cost. Each 1% of extra price realisation adds about €0.7M of EBIT, roughly the same as removing 8–9 roles.

## 2. Options

- **A: Uniform 8% per cost centre.** Simple to announce: every budget holder takes 8% off their 2027 baseline.
- **B: Targeted 8% (recommended).** Same total, allocated by cost type through five rules. Billable capacity is managed on utilisation, not on cost.
- **C: B plus a second tranche.** Reaches the "8% below 2026" definition by adding deeper support-function redesign, a housing step-change and a moderated salary round.

| Illustrative, €M | A: uniform 8% | B: targeted 8% | C: B + tranche 2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Run-rate cost reduction** | **5.8** | **5.8** | **8.3** |
| of which non-payroll | 1.8 | 3.4 | 4.1 |
| of which support payroll | 1.0 | 1.2 | 2.5 |
| of which billable payroll | 2.9 | 1.2 | 1.2 |
| of which salary round below drift | – | – | 0.5 |
| Roles removed | ~48 | ~25 | ~40 |
| of which likely forced exits | up to ~48 | 7 or fewer | ~22 |
| Collective-dismissal rules (WMCO) apply | likely | no | likely |
| Revenue capacity at risk, run-rate | 2.6–5.2 | ~0 | ~0 (retention risk from salary round) |
| One-off costs | 1.2–1.7 | ~0.25 | ~0.7 |
| 2027 in-year saving | ~3.5 | ~3.7 | ~4.9 |
| 2027 EBIT effect (after one-offs and lost revenue) | −0.8 to +1.0 | ~+3.4 | ~+4.2 |
| Run-rate EBIT effect | +0.6 to +3.2 | ~+5.8 | ~+8.3 |

*Sub-lines may not add exactly because of rounding.*

**Why A underdelivers here**

- **Billable people are the revenue engine.** In 2027 a billable employee costs about €81k and carries about €145k of revenue capacity. 8% of billable payroll is about 36 people and €5.2M of capacity. How much revenue you actually lose depends on whether those people were busy. Under afspiegeling, redundancy selection runs by age group and length of service within interchangeable functions, so you cannot pick the people on the bench.
- **Attrition doesn't rescue it.** Doing A through attrition instead of dismissals means leaving two-thirds of all leavers unreplaced, including billable roles with client demand, so the revenue effect is the same. Attrition also doesn't fall evenly across cost centres. Uniform targets would still force dismissals in some teams while others keep surplus capacity.
- **It rewards the wrong budgets and hides the trade-offs.** Cost centres that padded their budgets are rewarded and lean ones are penalised. The board sees no trade-offs.
- **It gives no legal rationale.** Every eliminated role needs a business-economic rationale that holds up at UWV or in a settlement negotiation. A uniform percentage is not one.

**What C adds (tranche 2, ~€2.5M run-rate)**

- Deeper support-function redesign, about 15 more roles: €1.3M run-rate, about €0.5M in 2027, €0.45M one-off.
- Housing step-change at the next lease break: €0.5M run-rate, mostly from 2028.
- A salary round 1 point below market drift, only where no CAO applies: €0.5M. Retention risk is real in a market where contract wages are forecast to rise 3.8%.
- A faster lease-car and mobility policy change, plus IT consolidation: €0.2M.

**The margin alternative to C:** full pass-through of contractual indexation to clients. At about €0.7M of EBIT per 1% of price realisation, closing even part of an indexation gap competes with tranche 2 and costs no jobs.

## 3. Framework: five rules

| # | Rule | Applies to | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One company target, owned by the whole ExCo; progress reported as one number | everything | keeps the message simple |
| 2 | Uniform −12% per cost centre on discretionary spend; budget holders choose what to stop | marketing and events, external advisors, travel, other discretionary (~€4.4M) | uniform works where spend is genuinely discretionary and quick to change |
| 3 | Central contracts managed centrally, each with a named owner | contractors, IT and software, housing, lease cars, recruitment (~€16.5M) | these are contract decisions, not cost-centre decisions |
| 4 | Hiring freeze with a weekly exception panel (CFO, COO, CHRO; 30 minutes). Backfill only (a) billable roles with demand evidence in a practice at or above utilisation target, or (b) critical control, security or compliance roles | all hiring | makes natural turnover the main payroll lever, without dismissals |
| 5 | Billable capacity managed on utilisation and pyramid, not on cost %. Each delivery unit gets a utilisation target and a headcount envelope tied to its pipeline | delivery units | protects revenue |

**Protect list (no cuts without an ExCo decision):**

- Training and certification (€0.7M). In a services firm, skills are the product.
- Delivery quality and client-critical roles.
- Information security and compliance.
- Sales capacity backed by pipeline.
- Retention of a named key-talent group.

## 5. People and process

### 5.1 Attrition first

- Assumed attrition is 12%, about 72 leavers a year (about 54 billable, about 18 support). Option B needs 18 of them not replaced (25%). The 7 redesigned support roles are covered by redeployment where possible before anyone exits.
- If attrition runs at 8% instead of 12%, the non-backfill levers deliver about €0.5M less run-rate. Track it monthly.
- Watch regretted attrition. A hiring freeze plus uncertainty is when good people leave first. Keep a named key-talent list and a small retention budget within the exception panel's remit.

### 5.2 Structural changes in support functions

- **Design roles out, not people.** Start from the work that stops, merges or gets automated.
- **Redeploy before any exit.** Redeploy first, including into billable roles where skills fit, and offer voluntary options. Only then select by afspiegeling within interchangeable functions per location.
- **Cost per exit: about €25–35k all-in (illustrative).** This covers:
  - the statutory transition payment: 1/3 of a monthly salary per year of service, capped at €102,000 in 2026
  - a likely uplift under a social plan
  - outplacement and legal costs

  Notice periods of 1–4 months by tenure delay the saving; they don't add to the cost.

### 5.3 Works council and legal timeline (from the board meeting)

| Step | Indicative timing |
|---|---|
| Share the budget framework with the OR. The OR is entitled to budget information (arts. 31–31a WOR) but has no advice right on the budget itself | weeks 0–2 |
| Design specific structural changes; employment counsel review; draft social plan if needed | weeks 1–5 |
| Request advice on the proposed decision (art. 25 WOR), at a stage where the advice can still influence it | ~week 6 |
| OR advice (in practice 4–8 weeks) | ~weeks 10–14 |
| Decision. If it departs from the advice, a one-month suspension follows (art. 25(6) WOR), during which the OR can appeal | ~weeks 14–18 |
| Exits via settlement agreements or a UWV permit; notice of 1–4 months | ~weeks 16–30 |

As a result, support-function exits land around mid-2027 at best. That is why Option B relies on non-payroll and attrition for the in-year number.

**Collective dismissal (relevant for A and C):** 20 or more dismissals in one UWV work area within three months, settlement agreements included, trigger the collective-dismissal rules. You must notify UWV and the unions and consult the unions, and a one-month waiting period applies unless the unions agree in writing.

**Two smaller points:**

- Changes to lease cars and other benefits for existing staff touch individual employment terms. Apply them at renewal and for new hires unless counsel advises otherwise.
- Reviewing long-running contractor (ZZP) assignments also reduces exposure to false self-employment claims, which the Belastingdienst has been enforcing again since 2025.

### 5.4 Communication

- Brief the OR chair informally before budget-holder instructions go out. Rumours start the day they do.
- Send an internal message the same week. Cover the target, the approach (discretionary spend, contracts and natural turnover first), how hiring works now, and that any role changes will go to the works council first.
- Don't promise "no redundancies" unless you can keep that promise.

## 6. Risks, indicators and triggers

| Risk | Effect (illustrative) | Early indicator | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attrition at 8% instead of 12% | −€0.5M run-rate from non-backfill | monthly leavers vs plan | Q1 trigger; tranche 2 |
| Demand softens | bench grows, utilisation falls, revenue below plan | pipeline coverage, utilisation | tighten backfill; practice-level review (its own OR process) |
| Regretted attrition rises under freeze and uncertainty | delivery and revenue risk; recruitment costs return | regretted leavers, engagement pulse | key-talent list, retention budget, clear comms |
| Wage drift above 4.4% | each +1 pt ≈ +€0.5M on the baseline | CAO settlements, market offers | pass through via indexation |
| Contractor insourcing fails on skills | savings slip; client delivery risk | contractor spend run-rate | phase by skill set; keep critical contractors |
| Savings by deferral, not removal | rebound in 2028 | savings register evidence | count run-rate only |
| OR advice delays or opposes the redesign | support-payroll savings slip into 2028 | OR timeline | involve early; solid rationale; social plan |

**Q1 checkpoint.** If the secured run-rate at the end of March is below 70% of plan (about €4.1M), the ExCo prepares tranche 2 elements (Option C) for the OR. That still leaves time to land them in H2.

## 7. Tracking

- **Savings register per lever:** owner, base, run-rate, in-year, evidence of removal, and status (identified, approved, executed, in run-rate).
- **Monthly one-page update to the board or audit committee:**
  - run-rate cost vs baseline
  - FTE, billable and support
  - utilisation
  - attrition and regretted attrition
  - contractor spend
  - one-offs
  - revenue per billable FTE

## Appendix D: Assumptions behind the illustrative figures

- **Headcount:** 600 people treated as 600 FTE (adjust for part-time).
- **Cost base:** 2026 opex is total operating cost including delivery staff: €70.0M, of which payroll €47.6M (68%) and non-payroll €22.4M. If billable staff sit in cost of sales and "opex" means overhead only, the revenue-risk argument shrinks, but the cut to support functions gets much steeper. Rework section 2 on that basis.
- **Loaded cost per FTE:**
  - 450 billable at €78k in 2026 (€81.4k in 2027)
  - 150 support at €83k in 2026 (€86.7k in 2027)
- **Revenue:** €72.9M, with EBIT of €2.9M (4%). About €65M comes from own billable staff (about €145k per billable FTE); the rest is delivered through contractors.
- **Non-payroll split (€M):**
  - contractors 6.0
  - IT and software 3.6
  - housing 3.4
  - lease cars 2.6
  - discretionary 4.4 (marketing and events 1.4, external advisors 1.2, travel 0.8, other 1.0)
  - recruitment 0.9
  - training 0.7
  - insurance and other fixed 0.8
- **2027 drift:** labour cost +4.4% and non-payroll +2.7% (CPB MEV 2027).
- **Phasing:** attrition is spread evenly, so non-backfill savings are about 50% in-year. Forced exits take effect around 1 July 2027, so about 40–50% in-year. Option A non-payroll lands at 80% in-year.
- **One-off costs:** €25–35k per forced exit, all-in.
- **Option A revenue at risk:** 36 billable roles × €145k = €5.2M capacity, of which 50–100% is lost depending on utilisation.
