I've built the model: **Restructuring_Savings_Model_2027.xlsx**. Before it goes into the board pack, the board needs to see one thing first.

**The €9m is a full-year run-rate (120 × €75k). It is not what 2027 will deliver.** It would only be a 2027 number if all 120 people were off payroll on 1 January 2027. With an announcement in December, the German and Dutch consultation, notification and notice rules rule that out.

## What the model shows (base case)

The base case assumes the proposal is announced in December, consultation is done properly and exits are compulsory.

| €m | Base case |
|---|---|
| Headline: 120 roles × €75k | 9.0 |
| Gross run-rate saving (2027 pay levels, after leaver mix) | 8.5 |
| Net run-rate saving (after 10% leakage: re-hires, contractors, overtime) | 7.7 |
| **2027 in-year net savings** | **2.4** |
| One-off restructuring costs | (5.9) |
| **2027 net P&L impact, if the charge falls in 2027** | **(3.5)** |
| 2027 net cash impact | (3.5) |
| 2028 net savings | 7.9 |
| Payback | about 9 months of run-rate |

**Bridge:** €9.0m + €0.3m 2027 pay rise − €0.7m leaver mix = €8.5m gross run-rate. Less €0.9m leakage = €7.7m net run-rate. Less €5.3m for exit timing = €2.4m banked in 2027. Less €5.9m one-off costs = (€3.5m).

**Why the timing slips:**
- **Netherlands:** works council advice, the collective-dismissal notification with its one-month waiting period, then UWV permits or settlement agreements, then notice. Staff leave payroll from July–August 2027.
- **Germany:** negotiating the Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan usually takes several months. Then come the mass-dismissal notification, a works-council hearing before each notice, and notice periods of 1–7 months to month-end depending on length of service. Staff leave payroll from September–December 2027.
- **If the German talks fail:** they go to the Einigungsstelle (conciliation board) and German exits move into 2028.

Even with every exit on day one (A), 2027 nets only €1.8m after one-off costs. Banking €9m net in 2027 on the base-case timeline would take about 445 roles, roughly a third of the workforce.

## What I'd put to the CEO before next month

1. **Restate the target as a run-rate.** Suggested wording: "€9m gross annualised savings (≈€7.7m net), at full run-rate from early 2028. 2027 in-year net savings of €2–4m, with one-off costs of €5–7m." If €9m in 2027 is already in the budget, guidance or covenant headroom, about €6.5m has to come from non-headcount levers. The board should see that now, not in Q3.
2. **Announce a proposal, not a decision. Say "up to 120 roles, subject to consultation."**
   - Netherlands: the works council must be asked for advice while it can still genuinely influence the decision. If it's presented as a done deal, the council can appeal to the Ondernemingskamer (enterprise chamber), which can order the decision withdrawn.
   - Germany: going ahead without trying for an Interessenausgleich lets affected employees sue for compensation under §113 BetrVG.
   - Skipping the process doesn't speed anything up. It adds cost and delay to the base case.
3. **Agree the accounting with the auditors before the announcement.** If the main features of a detailed plan are announced in December, the restructuring provision may fall into FY2026 (~€5.1m). 2027 would then show about +€1.7m. That's a 2026 results decision the board should take knowingly.
4. **Show the board scenario E.** It combines a hiring freeze with moving at-risk staff into open vacancies. Both countries require suitable vacancies to be offered before anyone is dismissed, so part of this is needed anyway. Against the base case it delivers ~39 of the 120 roles through attrition, cuts compulsory redundancies to ~81, lowers one-off costs by €1.4m and improves 2027 by €1.8m. The works councils and unions will ask what alternatives were considered, and this is the answer.

## Placeholders to replace before the pack is final (yellow cells)

- DE/NL split of the 120 (placeholder: 70/50).
- Actual cost, length of service and age of the at-risk roles. Placeholders: €75k in both countries; leavers costing 90% (DE) / 95% (NL) of the average, because selection rules tend to pick shorter-serving staff; 9 / 8 years' service.
- Existing Sozialplan, social plan and collective agreement terms. Placeholders: DE 0.75 months' pay per year of service (statutory benchmark 0.5); NL 1.5× the statutory transition payment (cap €102,000 in 2026; the 2027 cap is not yet published).
- Whether any of the 120 are billable or field roles. Currently set to 0%. If some are, revenue capacity leaves with them, and Inputs section 4 handles it.
- Voluntary turnover and open vacancies (placeholders: 8% turnover, 35% of leavers not replaced).
- German and Dutch employment counsel's view of the timeline, and the auditors' view on when the provision is recognised.

The biggest swing factors are German timing and severance levels. Across the sensitivity grids, 2027 in-year savings range from €0.4m to €3.7m and one-off costs from €4.8m to €9.0m.
