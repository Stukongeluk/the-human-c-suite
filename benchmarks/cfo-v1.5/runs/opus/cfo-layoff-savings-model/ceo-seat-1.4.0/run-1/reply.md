I wouldn't put "120 redundancies = €9m in 2027" in the board pack. On the model, that plan saves about **€4.5m gross in 2027** and is **about €1.9m cash-negative in 2027** once one-off costs are in. The €9m is a run-rate that arrives in 2028, about €7.9m a year after refilled roles. I'd put the honest model in the pack next to the alternatives, and ask the board to approve consultation on the target, not the announcement of a number.

I've built two files:
- **`savings-model.xlsx`**: live formulas, inputs in blue, placeholders in yellow, monthly phasing for 2027–28, and a checks sheet that reconciles.
- **`board-paper.md`**: a draft of the board pack section, written for the board.

**What the model shows (€m)**

| | Fast | Base | Slow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full saving from (DE / NL) | May / Apr | Aug / Jun | Nov / Sep |
| 2027 gross saving | 6.4 | 4.5 | 2.3 |
| After refilled roles and extra leavers | 5.4 | 3.8 | 1.9 |
| One-off costs | −5.7 | −5.7 | −5.7 |
| **2027 net cash** | **−0.3** | **−1.9** | **−3.8** |
| Payback | Jan 2028 | Apr 2028 | Jul 2028 |

**Why the gap.** 120 × €75k is exactly €9.0m, the full annual cost. So the €9m only holds if all 120 people leave on 1 January at no cost. If the plan is announced in December, these steps all come first:

- **Germany:**
  - negotiating a reconciliation of interests and a social plan with the works council (§§111–112 BetrVG)
  - the mass-dismissal notification, which can only be filed once consultation is complete. The Federal Labour Court [ruled on 1 April 2026](https://www.bundesarbeitsgericht.de/en/presse/massenentlassung-rechtsfolge-von-fehlern-im-anzeigeverfahren/) that filing it earlier makes the dismissals invalid
  - notice of up to seven months (§622 BGB)
- **Netherlands:**
  - works council advice (art. 25 WOR)
  - notifying UWV and the unions (WMCO)
  - UWV permits or settlement agreements
  - then notice

One-off costs are €5.7m (range €3.3–9.3m): German severance at 1.0 monthly pay per year of service, Dutch at 0.5 against the statutory ⅓, plus transition support and legal costs. Getting €9m gross in 2027 from redundancies alone would take about 240 exits, not 120.

If the plan is firm and announced before year-end, the restructuring provision may land in the 2026 accounts; check with the auditor. That moves the P&L charge between years, not the cash, and 2027 still shows only €3.8m.

**The trap.** The plan starts from the answer, 120 people, rather than from the problem the €9m is meant to solve. The number only adds up if the calendar and the law don't apply.

**What I'd put in the pack instead**
1. The model as built, with €9m labelled as a 2028 run-rate.
2. Reversible measures from January:
   - A hiring freeze with exemptions. As an illustration, at 8% turnover about 67 roles go unfilled: roughly €2.0m in 2027 and €4.3m run-rate, with no one-off costs. Replace the 8% with your HR data.
   - Third-party, subcontractor and agency spend, and contract pricing, sized from the ledger by end January.
3. A line saying the gap is management's to own, and a proposal to suspend 2027 executive bonuses before any frontline role goes. It's a signal, not a savings line.
4. Consultation with both works councils in December, at the problem stage, with the real numbers. Announcing a number first doesn't save time: the legal clock starts with consultation either way. It does make the contested, slow case more likely, and that costs about €1.9m more in 2027.
5. Compulsory reductions only for whatever the other measures can't close, done in one round, with a social plan and support from the day notice is given.

**Before the pack goes out**, talk to the CEO privately. A line you could use: *"I can model 120 roles, but I can't sign a board paper that says €9m in 2027. On our numbers it's €4.5m gross and a cash cost this year. Let's show the board the honest number and the options, and consult on the target rather than announce a headcount."*

**Two questions that would change the answer**
1. What is the €9m protecting: a covenant test, a budget gap, a margin or shareholder target? And is the group profitable and cash-positive today?
2. Where do the 120 sit: billable technicians or overhead? And is demand down temporarily or for good?
   - If the roles are billable, revenue leaves with them.
   - If the dip is temporary, Kurzarbeit bridges it in Germany (maximum 12 months from January 2027).

**Replace these first.** These are the yellow cells:
- a 60/60 country split
- 10 years' average service
- pay of €75k ÷ 1.25
- 10% of roles refilled
- +1 point turnover among the people who stay

Also check that the €75k is cost that actually goes when the role goes, not allocated overhead.

This isn't legal advice. Have employment counsel in both countries confirm the timeline before anything is announced.
