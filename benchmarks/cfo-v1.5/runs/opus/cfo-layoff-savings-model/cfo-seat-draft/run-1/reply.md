I'd put the model in the pack with an honest headline: 120 redundancies are worth about €9m a year from 2028, not €9m in 2027. In the base case they cost **€3.4m more cash than they save in 2027**, so I wouldn't ask the board to approve a December announcement of 120 roles as it stands.

I've built two things: `savings-model.xlsx`, a live model where every input is labelled From brief, Estimate or Placeholder and you can switch the timing case, and `board-paper.md`, a draft paper with the tables, alternatives, sensitivities and the legal timetable.

**Why 2027 doesn't work (base case, €m)**

| FY2027 | €m |
|---|---:|
| Headline: 120 × €75k | 9.0 |
| Payroll still paid while consultation and notice run (exits May–December 2027) | −5.9 |
| **Gross saving in 2027** | **3.1** |
| Severance, transition support, legal fees, claims | −5.1 |
| Work still to be done, lost contribution, extra attrition | −1.4 |
| **Net cash effect 2027** | **−3.4** |

2028 is +€6.1m, and cumulative cash pays back in July 2028. The lasting saving is about €6.25m, not €9m, unless the work these roles do stops with them. If everything goes right at once, 2027 comes out at +€1.4m. If everything goes wrong, it's −€6.9m.

The trap: 120 × €75k is a full-year run-rate presented as an in-year saving. In Germany and the Netherlands, consultation and notice periods decide when savings arrive, and the costs arrive first.

**Alternatives on the same basis (€m)**

| | 2027 net cash | 2028 net cash | Compulsory dismissals |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A. 120 compulsory redundancies (CEO proposal) | −3.4 | +6.1 | 120 |
| B. Hiring freeze with exceptions, internal moves | +1.8 | +6.0 | 0 |
| C. Voluntary programme first (60), then freeze (60) | +0.6 | +6.8 | 0 |
| D. Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 0 |

No option delivers €9m net in 2027. B and C only work if the reduction can be spread across the group rather than landing in one site or contract. I haven't been able to size the levers that cost no jobs, because they need the ledger: agency and contractor cover, procurement, pricing and indexation, working capital, leadership bonuses, and short-time work in Germany if the drop in work is temporary. Any of them that brings in positive cash in 2027 beats option A in 2027.

**What I'd recommend**

1. Start a hiring freeze with exceptions now, and stop agency cover where employees can do the work. Both can be reversed and both start saving in January.
2. By mid-January, size the other levers and agree what the €9m has to fix.
3. Put leaders first: suspend the leadership team's 2026–27 bonuses until the plan delivers.
4. From December, take the problem, not a decided number, to the works councils while they can still change the plan.
5. If a gap remains, launch a voluntary programme in Q1. Use compulsory redundancies only for what's left, in one round.

**Legal and accounting flags** (not legal advice; you'll need counsel in both countries and the auditors)

- **The announcement can only be a proposal.** In the Netherlands, the works council's advice has to be asked while it can still change the decision. The WMCO notification starts a one-month waiting period that also covers settlement agreements and can only be shortened with union consent ([CMS, Jan 2026](https://cms.law/en/nld/publication/timing-is-everything-how-the-wmco-waiting-period-shapes-your-reorganisation)). Agency workers and contractors in the same roles go first ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/ontslag/afspiegelingsbeginsel)).
- **Germany.** Where a site has a works council, you need a reconciliation of interests and a social plan, notification to the employment agency, and selection on social criteria. Notice runs up to seven months, so the German exits land in the second half of the year.
- **Group level.** Check whether there is a European Works Council. Assume any employee representatives on the German supervisory board will read this pack.
- **Accounting.** A December announcement could put the €5.1m charge into FY2026. FY2027 EBITDA would then show +€1.7m while 2027 cash is −€3.4m. Show both.

**Before it goes in the pack**, I'd walk the CEO through it privately. A line you could use: *"The €9m is real as a run-rate from 2028, but in 2027 this plan costs us about €3.4m of cash. If the board needs €9m next year, we need a different mix, and I'd rather we work that out together now than in front of the board."* The CEO may be right on one point: if the work has gone for good in specific sites, one well-run round now beats a slow drip, because each month of delay costs about €0.75m of gross saving. Even then, the 2027 cash cost has to be funded.

**Two questions that would change the numbers most**

1. What does the €9m have to fix, and by when: 2027 cash or covenant headroom, a 2027 EBITDA target, or a run-rate from 2028?
2. Is the work behind these 120 roles going away? Where, and for good?

**Key assumptions** (all estimates or placeholders):

- A 60/60 split between the Netherlands and Germany.
- Average service of 10 years for Dutch leavers and 8 for German leavers.
- Severance of 0.5 months' pay per year of service in the Netherlands and 1.0 in Germany.
- 15% of the work still has to be done after the exits.
- Voluntary attrition rises 25% for a year.
- €2m of revenue at risk.

Severance terms move 2027 the most (from −€1.8m to −€5.9m), then a Germany-heavy split (−€4.3m).
