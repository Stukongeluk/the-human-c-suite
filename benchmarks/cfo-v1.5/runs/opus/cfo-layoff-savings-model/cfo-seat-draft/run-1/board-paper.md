# Proposed 2027 restructuring: savings model, net and by quarter

*Draft for the board pack · Finance · 20 November 2026 · Every figure is an estimate unless marked otherwise; the live model, with every assumption, is in `savings-model.xlsx`. This paper is not legal, tax or accounting advice: employment counsel in both countries and the auditors need to confirm the points marked.*

## 1. Summary

**Recommendation: do not approve or announce 120 compulsory redundancies in December.** The proposal is worth about €9m a year from 2028, but in 2027 it costs more cash than it saves: **−€3.4m net cash in 2027** in the base case, **+€6.1m in 2028**, with cumulative cash payback in **July 2028**. If the board needs €9m in 2027, this plan cannot deliver it, and no option on the table can: the alternatives below get closer and do not make 2027 cash worse.

- **Why €9m is not a 2027 number.** 120 × €75k assumes everyone leaves on 1 January 2027 at no cost. Consultation in both countries and notice periods put the base-case exits between May and December 2027, so the gross saving in 2027 is €3.1m.
- **The costs come first.** Severance, transition support, legal fees and claims (€5.1m) and running costs (€1.4m) fall mostly in 2027.
- **Even the best case is far short.** Only if everything goes right at once (fast agreements, statutory-level severance, no backfill, no extra attrition, no lost revenue) does 2027 turn positive, at +€1.4m.
- **The lasting saving is nearer €6m than €9m** (€6.25m net run-rate by December 2028) unless the work these roles do stops with them.
- **The alternatives deliver more 2027 cash, with no compulsory dismissals.** A hiring freeze with internal moves (B) gives +€1.8m in 2027; a voluntary programme first, then a freeze (C), gives +€0.6m. Both reach a similar run-rate by the end of 2028. Neither works if the reduction has to land in one site or one contract (section 8).

## 2. The question to answer first

The model can't say which option is right until we know what the €9m has to fix, and by when:

- **2027 cash or covenant headroom?** Then option A makes 2027 cash worse by €3.4m, and the bank conversation has to start now.
- **A 2027 EBITDA or margin target?** Then the timing of the restructuring charge (section 5) matters, and so do the levers in section 7 that land inside 2027.
- **A run-rate from 2028?** Then all three options get there by the end of 2028; the choice is about cost, risk and people.

And: **is the work behind these 120 roles going away** (which sites, which contracts), and is that permanent or a dip? If the work stays, someone still has to do it (the 15% backfill line); if the dip is temporary, short-time work in Germany is the cheaper bridge.

## 5. Cash versus P&L

The restructuring charge (€5.1m in 2027, plus claims settled in 2028) is booked when the obligation arises. Under IFRS (IAS 37) that is when a detailed formal plan has been announced to those affected, so a December announcement could put the charge in **FY2026**, while the cash goes out in 2027. Under German or Dutch GAAP the timing can differ; the auditors need to confirm. If the charge sits in FY2026, **FY2027 EBITDA would show +€1.7m while 2027 cash is −€3.4m**. The board pack should show both, reconcile any adjusted figure to the reported one, and call the charge one-off only if it really is: a restructuring that recurs is a cost of running the business.

## 6. Why the exits arrive when they do

Base-case timetable. Check every step with employment counsel in both countries before anything is announced.

| | Netherlands | Germany |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 2026 | Proposal to the works council and advice requested while it can still change the decision (art. 25 WOR). Notification to UWV and the unions (WMCO, 20+ dismissals in one UWV region within three months) | Works councils and economic committee informed; negotiation of a reconciliation of interests and a social plan opens (§§111–112 BetrVG) |
| Jan–Feb 2027 | Advice; social plan with the unions. The one-month WMCO waiting period runs from UWV's confirmation that the notification is complete, applies to settlement agreements too, and can be shortened only with union consent | Negotiation. Implementing before a reconciliation of interests has been attempted exposes the company to compensation claims (§113 BetrVG) |
| Mar–May 2027 | Settlement agreements; UWV permit applications for the rest (typically 4–8 weeks). If the decision departs from the works council's advice, implementation is suspended for a month (art. 25(6) WOR), during which the council can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (art. 26) | Agreement by end April (base case); collective-dismissal consultation and notification to the employment agency (§17 KSchG; errors can make dismissals void); works council hearing before each notice (§102 BetrVG); notices end May |
| Exits | End May to end September 2027. Notice of 1–4 months, minus UWV time (at least one month) | End July to end December 2027. Notice up to seven months to the end of a month (§622 BGB); collective agreements may give longer notice or protect older long-serving staff |

Other rules that shape cost and timing:

- **Netherlands:** hired-in staff in the same roles (agency workers, self-employed contractors) go first, then on-call workers, people past state pension age and fixed-term contracts ending within 26 weeks; then selection follows the reflection principle across age groups. The statutory transition payment is one-third of a month's salary per year of service, capped at €102,000 in 2026.
- **Germany:** first check whether each site has a works council. Selection follows social criteria (service, age, maintenance obligations, severe disability; §1(3) KSchG), not performance rankings. There is no general statutory severance; social plans commonly use age × service × salary ÷ 50, which gives roughly 0.8–1.1 months per year of service at ages 40–55.
- **Group level:** with more than 1,000 employees in the EU, and if more than 150 work in each country, the group may have, or its employees can request, a European Works Council, which must be informed and consulted on a cross-border restructuring (Directive 2009/38/EC). If the German company has more than 500 employees, a third of its supervisory board are employee representatives; write this paper on the basis that they will read it.
- **The announcement:** in December it can set out the problem and a proposal for consultation, not a decision on 120 roles. Telling people just before the holidays also makes the human cost, and the attrition risk, worse.

- **B. Freeze with exceptions and internal moves.** About 112 people leave the group each year (8% assumed, retirements included; HR to confirm). Refill only critical, safety and customer-facing roles (40%), leave the rest open, and move people to where the work is. 120 roles are gone by October 2028, with no severance. The risks: attrition lands where it lands, not where the work has fallen, and at 5% attrition the 2027 figure drops to +€1.1m.
- **C. Voluntary programme first, then freeze.** Voluntary exits and early retirement where the rules allow, at a 25% premium on social-plan terms, agreed with the works councils; acceptance criteria protect critical skills. It still counts toward the collective-dismissal notifications in both countries, but avoids UWV permits, social selection and most claims. If a structural gap remains after this, one compulsory round, done once and properly.
- **D. Do nothing.** No cost and no saving; whatever the €9m is meant to fix remains unfixed. That consequence has to be sized before the options can be compared fairly.
- **The case for the proposal as it stands.** If the work behind these roles has gone for good, and sits where attrition can't reach it, one well-run compulsory round now is kinder than a slow drip, and every month of delay costs about €0.75m of gross saving. In that case option A, done once and properly, is the right call. Even then its 2027 cash effect is negative and has to be funded.

## 8. Recommendation and what would change it

**Recommended:**
1. **Now (December):** a hiring freeze with exceptions, and an end to agency and contractor cover where employees can do the work. Both are reversible and start saving in January.
2. **By mid-January:** size the levers above from the ledger and agree what the €9m has to fix and by when (section 2).
3. **From December:** open consultation with the works councils (and the European Works Council, if there is one) on the problem and the options, while their input can still change the plan.
4. **Leaders first:** the leadership team's 2026–27 bonuses and new incentive grants are suspended until the plan delivers, and leaders say plainly that the result is theirs.
5. **Q1 2027, if a gap remains:** a voluntary programme (option C). Compulsory redundancies only for the structural gap that is left, in one round.

**What would change it:**
- **The reduction is concentrated** (a site closure, a lost contract): attrition can't land it. Then a voluntary programme plus a targeted compulsory step at that site, done once, with its 2027 cash cost funded and agreed with the bank.
- **The €9m is about 2027 covenant headroom:** option A makes 2027 worse. Talk to the bank now, before any announcement.
- **Attrition is far below 8%:** option B is slower; C carries more of the load.
- **The drop in work is temporary:** fewer hours (short-time work in Germany), not fewer people.

## 10. People

- **Who:** 120 people, 8.6% of the workforce, plus the colleagues who take on the work that remains. In industrial services, fewer people doing the same work also means more overtime and a higher safety risk, so safety belongs on the dashboard.
- **What the evidence says:** a 1% downsizing has gone with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008); the model assumes far less. In US manufacturing firms, downsizing was followed by lower profitability, most strongly in industries with low capital intensity (Guthrie & Datta, 2008); a services group whose main asset is its people sits closer to that end. Replacing an employee costs one-half to two times their annual salary (Gallup); the model uses the low end.
- **If any exits go ahead:** transition support from the day of notice, not after the last day; selection by the legal criteria in each country, never by performance rankings; each person told by their own manager, in person, before any wider announcement; one round, not several; and the people who stay told the same day what it means for them.

## 11. What we will track, quarterly

Net cash against this model; exits against the timetable; overtime, agency and subcontractor spend; regretted attrition and absence; safety incidents; customer service levels, complaints and renewals at the affected sites.
