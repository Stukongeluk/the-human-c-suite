## Cover note: what I propose and why

**Proposal.** Adjusted EBITDA stays the main measure, at 70% of the bonus. What changes is the definition: restructuring and other change costs count in the EBITDA the plan pays on, instead of being excluded as one-off. The other 30% pays on customer retention (15%) and product quality, security and safety (15%).

**Why the definition changes.** We have restructured in each of the last three years. A cost that appears every year is a running cost of how we manage the company, and three restructurings in three years is a management result, mine included. The bonus plan should not pay us as if those costs belonged to someone else. Excluding them has three effects:

1. **It pays for restructuring.** The savings count in bonus EBITDA; the cost of getting them doesn't. Illustration (not our figures): a EUR 15.0m EBITDA target, management otherwise exactly on target, and a mid-year restructuring that costs EUR 1.5m and saves EUR 1.0m in 2027 (EUR 2.0m a year from 2028).

   | Case | EBITDA the plan measures | % of target | EBITDA element pays |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | No restructuring | EUR 15.0m | 100% | 100% |
   | Restructuring, cost excluded (current practice) | EUR 16.0m | 107% | 133% |
   | Restructuring, cost counted | EUR 14.5m | 97% | 83% |
   | Restructuring approved by the Board in advance, cost and in-year savings both removed (proposed, section 4.3) | EUR 15.0m | 100% | 100% |

   Real 2027 EBITDA falls by EUR 0.5m, and the usual definition pays a third more for it. Over two years this restructuring still adds EUR 1.5m, so a good restructuring stays worth doing under the proposal. It just stops being worth doing for the bonus.

2. **It makes letting people go cheaper than redeploying them.** Severance is excluded from the measure; retraining and redeployment count at full cost. The bonus therefore prefers redundancy to redeployment, even where keeping someone's product and customer knowledge is the better business decision, which in healthcare software it often is. Repeated restructuring has its own cost too: one study found that a 1% workforce reduction was followed by a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008).

3. **It pays on a number a buyer is unlikely to pay for.** Analysts who review adjusted EBITDA argue that restructuring charges which recur for several years should not be treated as add-backs (Taylor & Yozzo, 2023). US rules for listed companies, which don't apply to us but show the standard a sophisticated buyer applies, forbid calling a charge non-recurring when a similar charge occurred within the prior two years (SEC Regulation S-K, Item 10(e)). Lenders have also learned to discount add-backs: S&P Global Ratings found that 97% of speculative-grade companies that made acquisitions in 2019 missed their first-year earnings forecasts (reported by Bloomberg, February 2023). If we pay bonuses on adjusted EBITDA that will be adjusted back in due diligence, the annual bonus and the equity value pull in different directions.

**The case for the usual exclusion, and how this draft keeps it.** Restructuring costs are excluded so that managers aren't put off a change that creates value. That is a fair concern: research on large US companies found that compensation committees shielded executives' cash pay from restructuring charges for exactly this reason (Dechow, Huson & Sloan, 1994). The draft keeps the protection without paying for the cost. Planned change goes into the budget, so the target already reflects it. A mid-year restructuring the Board approves in advance has both its 2027 cost and its 2027 savings removed from the measure, so the bonus neither rewards nor punishes the decision and it is made on its merits.

**Why 30% on customers and quality.** EBITDA on its own can be raised by cutting what customers and a future buyer value most: support, security, maintenance and product quality. In healthcare software those cuts also carry patient-safety and data-protection risk, and they show up in churn a year or two later, after the bonus has been paid. Gross revenue retention and a short quality, security and safety scorecard catch that. If the Board prefers to keep 100% on EBITDA, the definition matters more than the weighting: keep sections 4, 6, 7 and 9 as drafted, and use retention and the scorecard as a modifier of up to plus or minus 15% on the EBITDA payout instead of as weighted measures.

**Options considered for restructuring costs**

| Treatment | Effect on management decisions | Effect at exit | View |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Exclude as one-off (current practice) | Savings count, costs don't: the plan pays for restructuring and makes severance cheaper than retraining | Bonus EBITDA sits above the EBITDA a buyer will accept | Not recommended |
| B. Count all costs, no exceptions | Clean and simple; could deter a necessary mid-year change | Aligned | Acceptable, but blunt |
| C. Count planned costs (in budget and target); remove both cost and in-year savings of Board-approved mid-year changes | Neutral: the decision is made on its merits | Aligned | **Recommended** |
| D. Exclude costs and in-year savings of all restructuring | Neutral in the year, but needs savings tracking for every action and invites relabelling of ordinary costs | Reported adjusted EBITDA still carries the add-back | Not recommended |

**Decisions for the Board**

1. The Plan EBITDA definition (section 4). This is the main decision.
2. Weighting: 70/15/15 as drafted, or 100% EBITDA with the modifier described above.
3. Target bonus levels and calibration of the payout curves (sections 2 and 5).
4. The leaders-first clause (section 7).

**Assumptions, and what would change my view**

- I have assumed restructuring costs in 2024–2026 were material and that some change cost is likely in 2027. If the actual figures were small, the definition change matters less, though the principle still holds. [CFO to insert: cost and realised savings of each restructuring, and add-backs as a share of 2026 adjusted EBITDA.]
- If a management equity plan already carries strong customer and quality conditions, the 30% could come down to [15–20%].
- If 2027 includes a genuinely exceptional event, such as an acquisition or a carve-out, section 4.4 handles it without reopening the definition.

## The plan

### 1. Purpose

To reward the management team for EBITDA that lasts: profit that customers keep paying for, from products that are safe, secure and reliable, earned without pushing costs into later years.

### 2. Plan year, participants and target bonus

- **Plan year:** 1 January to 31 December 2027.
- **Participants:** [the executive team and their direct reports, about N people], named in an individual participation letter.
- **Target bonus** (as % of base salary earned in the plan year): CEO [ ]%, executive team [ ]%, senior managers [ ]%.
- **Range:** on-target performance pays 100% of target bonus; the maximum is 150%.
- **Joiners:** pro rata from the start date for participants who join before [1 October 2027].

### 3. Measures and weights

| Measure | Weight | What it protects |
|---|---|---|
| Plan EBITDA (section 4) | 70% | Profit and cash generation |
| Gross revenue retention (section 5.2) | 15% | Customers and recurring revenue |
| Quality, security and safety scorecard (section 5.3) | 15% | Patients, customer data and reliability |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

All measures are company-level. There are no individual targets in this plan; individual contribution is handled in normal performance conversations, not in the formula.

### 4. Plan EBITDA

**4.1 Starting point.** EBITDA as reported in the audited 2027 consolidated accounts, under the accounting policies used to prepare the 2027 budget.

**4.2 Restructuring and change costs count.** Severance, notice pay, settlement payments, outplacement, retention payments linked to a reorganisation, retraining and redeployment costs, related adviser fees and lease exit costs are all included in Plan EBITDA. The 2027 budget includes planned change costs of EUR [ ] (for reference, the 2024–2026 average was EUR [ ]), and the Plan EBITDA target is set after them. Retraining and severance are treated the same way, so the plan has no preference between redeploying someone and letting them go.

**4.3 Board-approved changes during the year.** If the Board approves in advance, on a written business case, a restructuring that is not in the budget, the Committee removes both its 2027 costs and its 2027 savings from Plan EBITDA: both sides, or neither. The savings are then built into the 2028 budget and target.

**4.4 Other permitted adjustments.** Only the following, fixed now and not added to during the year:

- a. transaction costs of acquisitions, disposals and refinancing approved by the Board;
- b. for businesses acquired or sold during the year, the target is adjusted by the EBITDA in the Board-approved deal case, pro rata for the period owned;
- c. shareholder monitoring fees [if charged];
- d. the effect of changes in accounting standards or policies made after the budget was approved.

Every adjustment is symmetric: unbudgeted one-off gains (for example provision releases, insurance proceeds or grants) are removed on the same basis as one-off costs.

**4.5 Capitalised development.** Plan EBITDA is calculated at the development capitalisation rate in the 2027 budget ([ ]% of development spend). Any difference is neutralised, so moving cost from operating expense to capital has no effect on the bonus.

**4.6 Cap, approval and reconciliation.** Adjustments under 4.3 and 4.4 together may not exceed [5]% of target Plan EBITDA without approval of the full Board. Each adjustment is approved by the Committee in writing, before the cost is incurred wherever possible. The CFO prepares a reconciliation from reported EBITDA to Plan EBITDA, reviewed by [the audit committee / the external auditor], which the Board and all participants receive.

**4.7 Lender definitions are unaffected.** Covenant EBITDA under the facilities agreement is a separate definition and does not change.

### 5. Targets and payout curves

Payout is linear between the points below and zero below threshold. Each measure pays on its own result.

**5.1 Plan EBITDA**

| | Threshold | Target | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan EBITDA as % of target | 90% | 100% | 110% |
| Plan EBITDA in EUR | [ ] | [ ] | [ ] |
| Element pays (% of its target) | 50% | 100% | 150% |

**Affordability:** if Plan EBITDA is below threshold, the other two elements pay at most 100% of their target.

**5.2 Gross revenue retention (GRR)**

Definition: annual recurring revenue at 31 December 2027 from customers who were customers on 1 January 2027, excluding upsell and price increases above [contractual indexation], divided by their annual recurring revenue on 1 January 2027.

| | Threshold | Target | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| GRR | [ ]% | [ ]% | [ ]% |
| Element pays | 50% | 100% | 150% |

**5.3 Quality, security and safety scorecard**

Four items, each assessed as met or not met for the year:

1. Availability of production services at or above [99.9]%, measured against the service levels in customer contracts.
2. Critical and high security vulnerabilities fixed within the agreed time ([ ] days) in at least [95]% of cases.
3. Required certifications and regulatory audits ([ISO 27001 / NEN 7510 / SOC 2 / medical device software requirements, whichever apply]) maintained, with no major nonconformity open past its agreed deadline.
4. Service restored within [ ] hours for at least [90]% of severity-1 incidents.

| Items met | 0–1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Element pays | 0% | 50% | 100% | 150% |

No item counts the number of incidents reported. Rewarding fewer reported incidents rewards not reporting them.

### 6. Gates before any payout

Before any bonus is paid, the Committee confirms that:

- a. the 2027 accounts have been audited without a material restatement;
- b. the company has not lost, or had suspended, a certification or regulatory authorisation it needs to sell or run its products;
- c. any serious patient-safety or personal-data incident has been reviewed. The Committee may reduce payouts, up to 100%, where an incident resulted from a known risk that management chose not to address. Raising, reporting and fixing an incident promptly is never a reason to reduce a bonus; concealing or delaying one is a reason to forfeit it.

### 7. Leaders first

In any plan year in which the company makes compulsory redundancies to reduce cost, payouts for the executive team are capped at 100% of target, whatever the results. [Alternative for the Board: the executive team forgoes the EBITDA element above threshold in that year.] A management bonus above target in a year we ask colleagues to leave would cost more in trust than it pays in motivation.

### 8. Payment, deferral and leavers

- **Payment:** in the month after the 2027 audited accounts are signed [expected April 2028].
- **Deferral:** [one third] of any payout above 100% of target is deferred to [April 2029] and paid if 2028 GRR is at or above the 2027 threshold and no malus event (section 9) has occurred. The customer effects of 2027 decisions show up in 2028.
- **Good leavers** (redundancy, ill health, retirement, death, [other]): pro rata for time worked in the plan year, paid at the normal date on actual results.
- **Other leavers** (resignation, or dismissal for cause, before the payment date): [no payout / pro rata], subject to local law.

### 9. Malus and clawback

Before payment (malus), or within [two] years after payment where the law and the participant's contract allow (clawback), the Committee may reduce or recover a bonus if:

- the results it was based on are restated;
- Plan EBITDA was raised by costs moved into later years outside the budget, for example by deferring essential maintenance or security work, changing capitalisation or shifting supplier payment timing; or
- the participant concealed or delayed reporting a material incident.

Enforceability depends on local employment law and the terms of each contract; counsel to confirm before adoption.

### 10. Governance

- **Owner:** the Remuneration Committee of the Board. The CFO prepares the targets and the year-end reconciliation.
- **Discretion:** downward at any time, with reasons recorded. Upward only through the adjustments listed in section 4.
- **Transparency:** participants receive the definitions, targets and payout curves in writing by [31 January 2027], and the year-end reconciliation with their payout statement.
- **One definition for everyone:** any other bonus or profit-sharing scheme in the company uses the same Plan EBITDA definition.
- **Employee representation and law:** before adoption, counsel confirms whether a works council has consent or information rights over this plan, and that the plan complies with local employment and tax law. In the Netherlands, for example, a works council's consent is required for a pay system or profit-sharing scheme not fully covered by a collective agreement (article 27 WOR), and companies with 100 or more employees must inform it each year of the pay of groups of employees and of the board (article 31d WOR).
- **Annual review:** after the 2027 payout, the Committee reviews whether any measure was gamed or produced behaviour nobody intended, including by asking participants and their teams, and adjusts the 2028 plan.

### 11. How the plan could be gamed, and what stops it

| Route | Counter in the plan |
|---|---|
| Labelling recurring costs as one-off | Closed list of adjustments, symmetric, capped and approved in advance (4.4–4.6) |
| Restructuring to lift bonus EBITDA | Costs count, or cost and savings are both removed (4.2–4.3); leaders-first cap (7) |
| Moving development cost to capital | Capitalisation held at the budget rate (4.5) |
| Deferring maintenance, security or support | Quality scorecard and GRR (5.2–5.3); malus (9) |
| Under-reporting incidents | No incident counts in any measure; concealment forfeits the bonus (5.3, 6c) |
| Discounting renewals for early payment, pulling revenue forward | Revenue under the existing recognition policy; GRR measured on recurring revenue; cash conversion reported to the Committee alongside the payout |
| Cutting training and R&D to hit the number | R&D and training spend reported to the Committee as a three-year trend alongside the payout |
