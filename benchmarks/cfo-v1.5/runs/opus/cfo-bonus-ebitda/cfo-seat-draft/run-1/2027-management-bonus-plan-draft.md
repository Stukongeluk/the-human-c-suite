# 2027 Management Bonus Plan: draft for the Remuneration Committee

**Status:** draft for discussion · **Prepared by:** Finance · **Figures:** anything marked *illustrative* is a round number that shows how the plan works. Replace it with the 2027 budget and 2024–2026 actuals before approval. Amounts are in millions (m), in the currency of our accounts.

## Summary

**Recommendation:** pay the 2027 management bonus on a scorecard led by profit (Bonus EBITDA 50%, cash conversion 10%), with customer retention, service quality and regretted attrition making up the other 40%. Keep restructuring costs inside the EBITDA we pay on. Defer a third of the executive team's payout for two years, with malus and clawback.

- **EBITDA still decides.** Nothing pays unless Bonus EBITDA reaches 95% of budget. If EBITDA is below target, the other measures can't pay above target.
- **Restructuring stays in.** We have restructured in each of the last three years, so it is a recurring cost of running the business. The 2027 budget carries a reorganisation line and targets are set after it, so a year that goes to plan pays the same as it would if restructuring were excluded. Overruns and unplanned restructurings count, in the year they land.
- **Why it matters (illustrative):** if restructuring is excluded, a mid-year restructuring that *lowers* reported 2027 EBITDA by 0.5m *raises* the payout from 100% to 125% of target. Under this draft the same decision takes it to 82%. If the board approves the restructuring and wants management neutral on it, all its costs and all its savings are neutralised together, and the payout stays at 100%.
- **Leaders first, written in advance:** if costs have to come out during 2027, the management bonus accrual is released before any role outside management is cut.

## 1. Purpose and principles

The plan pays senior management for a business that is worth more at the end of 2027 and still worth more at exit: profit that is real, cash that arrives, customers who stay, a platform that care providers can rely on, and the teams who build and run it.

1. **One profit number, honestly defined,** reconciled line by line to the audited accounts.
2. **Results that last beyond the plan year:** part of the executive payout waits two years.
3. **No pay for cutting headcount as such,** and no credit for profit made by cutting protected investment.
4. **When results disappoint, management's bonus goes first.**
5. **Company-level measures from the systems of record.** No individual employee data in the scorecard.

## 4. Payout rules

- **Gate:** if Bonus EBITDA is below 95% of budget, no part of the plan pays.
- **Profit first:** if Bonus EBITDA is below target, the non-financial measures pay at most 100%.
- **Leaders first:**
  - If, during 2027, the board asks management to reduce costs to protect the budget, covenant headroom or cash, the first saving is the release of the management bonus accrual, before any reduction of roles outside management is considered.
  - In a year with compulsory redundancies, ExCo payouts are capped at target.
- **No reward for headcount reduction:** no measure, and no discretionary adjustment, pays for fewer people as such.
- **Board discretion:** the board may reduce any payout, including to zero, with written reasons (for example a serious incident, or a major customer loss traced to decisions in the plan year). It may increase a payout only in exceptional cases, with written reasons shared with participants.

## 5. Bonus EBITDA: definition

**Start from the audited accounts:** reported operating profit [IFRS / local GAAP] plus depreciation, amortisation and impairment equals **reported EBITDA**. Then apply only the adjustments on this closed list, fixed before the plan year starts.

**Allowed adjustments:**
1. Owners' transaction costs: the sale process, refinancing, and deal fees for board-approved acquisitions (deal fees only, not integration).
2. Owners' monitoring fees, if charged.
3. Non-cash charges for the management equity plan.
4. Acquisitions and disposals not in the budget: the target is adjusted for the business bought or sold, revenue and costs both.
5. Accounting changes required by the standards: neutralised in both directions.

**Not allowed (these stay in Bonus EBITDA):**
- restructuring, reorganisation, redundancy and severance costs, paid notice and settlements;
- advisers, consultants, outplacement and retention payments connected to a reorganisation;
- "transformation", "integration" (beyond deal fees) and other programme costs labelled one-off;
- recurring litigation, and any item excluded in 2024–2026 that recurs.

**Restructuring:**
- The 2027 budget includes a reorganisation line of [__m], set with reference to 2024–2026 actuals ([__m], [__m], [__m]) and anything already planned. The target is set **after** this line.
- Actual costs count: under budget helps the payout, over budget reduces it.
- **Symmetry.** If the board approves an exception, it records why a similar charge did not occur in the prior two years and is not reasonably likely in the next two. (This is the test the SEC applies to US-listed companies' "non-recurring" adjustments; we aren't bound by it, but it's the right test.) The savings or revenue that come from the excluded event are then excluded for the plan year too. **A cost is never excluded while its savings are counted.**
- Any 2027 reorganisation with compulsory redundancies goes to the board with a business case showing net figures by quarter: severance, notice and consultation time, work that still has to be done, rehiring, revenue at risk.

**Other guards:**
- **Capitalised development:** Bonus EBITDA uses the budgeted ratio of capitalised to total development cost ([x]%). Any capitalisation above that ratio is reversed.
- **Protected spend:** for product engineering, information security and compliance, training, and customer support and implementation, any underspend of more than [5]% against budget without prior board approval is not credited to Bonus EBITDA.
- **Reclassification:** moving costs between opex and capex, or between employees and contractors, does not change Bonus EBITDA against budget.

## 6. Deferral, malus and clawback

- **ExCo, including the CEO:** two-thirds paid after the audited 2027 accounts [April 2028]; one-third deferred to [April 2030] and paid if no malus event has occurred.
- **Senior managers:** any payout above 100% of target is deferred 12 months.
- **On a sale:** deferred amounts vest at completion, after a malus review that is finished before closing.
- **Malus** (reduce or cancel unpaid amounts) and **clawback** (recover amounts paid, up to three years after payment) apply where:
  - results are materially misstated or restated;
  - there was misconduct;
  - results were achieved by breaking the protected-spend, capitalisation or incident-reporting rules;
  - a serious quality, clinical-safety or data-protection failure occurs that the board finds was caused in material part by decisions taken in the plan year.
- **Leavers:** good and bad leaver terms aligned with the management equity plan [counsel to draft].

## 8. Why we are not recommending 100% adjusted EBITDA with restructuring excluded

**The case for the board's version.** One measure is simple and hard to argue with. It is the number the deal is valued and financed on, and it stops soft measures padding payouts. Excluding restructuring is meant to make sure management doesn't avoid changes the business needs, and it often matches the credit agreement's EBITDA definition.

**What this draft keeps:** EBITDA is the gate and the largest measure, and the budgeted reorganisation line means a planned, necessary change costs management nothing.

**Where the board's version goes wrong:**
1. **Three years running is not one-off.** Excluding a recurring cost overstates recurring profit. *Illustrative:* if the three charges averaged about 1.3m against 14m EBITDA, adjusted EBITDA overstates recurring profit by about 9.5%. A buyer's quality-of-earnings review is likely to ask whether these add-backs recur. If it treats them as recurring, at an illustrative 12× multiple that is about 16m of enterprise value we would be paying bonuses on and might not be paid for.
2. **It pays for the savings and never charges the cost.** See the illustration below.
3. **One year of EBITDA alone pays for cuts whose cost arrives later:** product engineering, security, training and support. In a healthcare product, that is where our customers' trust and our certifications sit.

**Illustration: an unbudgeted mid-year restructuring in 2027** (assumptions in the appendix)

**The options side by side:**

| | A. As requested | B. Fallback: EBITDA only, honestly defined | C. Recommended |

## 9. Legal, tax and accounting points to confirm

- **Employment law:** whether earlier bonus terms are contractual or customary, and what notice or consent a change needs; whether employee representatives (such as a works council, where one exists) have a say in changes to a remuneration system in our jurisdictions. *Counsel.*
- **Deferral, malus and clawback:** enforceability and drafting differ by country, as do tax and social-security treatment of deferred cash. *Counsel and tax advisers.*
- **Management equity plan:** avoid paying twice for the same result; align leaver and change-of-control terms. *The fund's legal team.*
- **Credit agreement:** covenant EBITDA definition and any limits on payments. The bonus definition stays separate.
- **Auditors:** agree the reconciliation and the scope of agreed-upon procedures before the year starts.
- **Regulation:** if our software is regulated as a medical device or processes patient data under health-data rules, our incident and vigilance reporting duties come first. The quality measure is built so that it never discourages reporting; our regulatory and quality lead should confirm.

This draft is for discussion. It is not legal, tax or investment advice.

## Appendix: illustration assumptions (all illustrative)

- 2027 Bonus EBITDA target 14.0m, set after the budgeted reorganisation line.
- Unbudgeted restructuring of 20 roles at a fully loaded 0.09m each: 1.8m run-rate saving. Exits take effect on 1 July after consultation and notice, so the 2027 saving is 0.9m.
- One-off costs 1.2m (severance, paid notice, advisers, outplacement). Work that still has to be done costs 0.2m (contractors, overtime) and stays in opex in every version.
- Reported 2027 EBITDA effect: +0.9 − 0.2 − 1.2 = −0.5m. Version A adds back the 1.2m: 14.0 + 0.9 − 0.2 = 14.7m. In the neutralised row, the 1.2m, the 0.2m and the 0.9m saving are all taken out, which leaves 14.0m.
- EBITDA payout curve: below 95% of target pays 0; 95% pays 50%; 100% pays 100%; 110% pays 150% (cap); linear in between. Version C assumes every other measure at target.
- Pool: 30 participants, aggregate base salary 4.0m, average target bonus 30%, so the target pool is 1.2m.
- 2028 follow-through, for context: four roles rehired mid-year, gross saving 1.62m less 0.28m rehiring and backfill, net +1.34m. Two-year net +0.84m, before extra voluntary attrition and revenue at risk, which aren't modelled. The restructuring in the illustration pays back over two years; the point is only that it should not raise the bonus in the year it lowers reported profit.
- Exit-value point: add-backs of 1.1m, 1.6m and 1.3m (average 1.33m, 9.5% of 14.0m), at 12×, about 16m of enterprise value.
