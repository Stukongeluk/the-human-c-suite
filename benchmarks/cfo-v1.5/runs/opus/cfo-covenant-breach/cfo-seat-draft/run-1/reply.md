**Don't announce the cuts before the bank meeting.** On my estimates, a 10% cut makes the 30 June test worse: the savings land after the test date and the costs land before it. The gap itself is small. For every €1m of EBITDA, 0.1x is about €100k of net debt or €29k of EBITDA, and you can close that without cutting jobs. Go to the bank early with that plan, the owner's own money on the table, and a request for headroom as a backstop.

The numbers below are my estimates for a company your size (revenue about €40m, EBITDA about €4m, net debt about €14.4m). The model and an outline for the bank pack are in the attached draft (covenant-plan-bank-review-draft.md). Replace them with your own.

**Why the cut doesn't fix Q2**
- **Timing.** Dismissing 26 people within three months counts as a collective dismissal under the WMCO. That means works council advice (art. 25 WOR), notifying UWV and the unions, then permits or settlement agreements, then one to four months' notice. Most exits would fall between April and August, so only about €90k of the €1.4m annual saving lands before 30 June.
- **Costs first.** Transition payments, settlement premiums, outplacement and advisers come to about €575k. Most of it is booked when you announce, and about €310k goes out in cash before June.
- **Result at 30 June: about 4.2x** if covenant EBITDA doesn't add back restructuring costs, and about 3.6x if it does. Doing nothing also gives 3.6x.
- **It could bring the breach forward.** Announced in December, the provision lands in your year-end figures. If you're also tested then, a year-end ratio of 3.3x becomes about 3.85x.
- **It's legally exposed.** Announcing before the works council has given its advice means you've decided without it, and the Enterprise Chamber can order the decision undone. You also don't choose who leaves: UWV's reflection principle selects by job category, age group and length of service.
- **It's out of proportion.** €1.4m a year is about twelve times the EBITDA gap. And you lose skilled people who are hard to rehire when orders come back.

**What I'd do in the next three weeks**
1. **Pin down the definitions** with counsel: how EBITDA and net debt are defined, add-backs, every test date, and whether there's an equity cure. Run base and downside cases to the end of 2027, plus a 13-week cash forecast.
2. **Build a cash plan to 30 June**, every line with an owner and a date (amounts are my estimates):
   - inventory down €0.5m
   - receivables collected four days faster: €0.44m
   - capex that isn't safety or maintenance moved past June: €0.3m (timing, not a saving)
   - 2% on new orders from February: €0.15m
   - indirect spend and energy: €0.1m
   - a hiring freeze, with exceptions for critical roles: €0.08m

   That takes about €1.7m off net debt: 2.9x if it all lands, 3.25x if half does. About 14% of it reaches 3.5x. Don't pay small suppliers later to get there.
3. **Owners and leaders go first.** No dividend from 2026. Suspend management bonuses for 2026 and 2027 (about €0.15m in cash; I've counted it as cash only, not EBITDA). Get a written standby commitment from the owner to put in equity or a subordinated loan if a test is at risk. To a bank, that's what "serious" looks like, and it costs no jobs.
4. **Call your relationship manager this week**, so the review holds no surprises. As a backstop, ask for headroom on the Q2 and Q3 2027 tests (say 4.0x) or a pre-agreed equity cure. My estimate of the cost is about €60k in fees and margin over six months. An amendment before a breach is cheaper than a waiver after one.
5. **Tell the works council now, in confidence.** They'll hear about the covenant anyway, and they know where the waste is.
6. **Agree a trigger instead of an announcement.** For example: if order intake stays below an agreed level for two months, or the downside case shows the drop is lasting, you start the works council process on capacity. Size it to the real volume and do it once. Showing the bank the trigger proves you'll act when the facts call for it.

There's no Dutch short-time work scheme for an ordinary downturn. The current one (werktijdverkorting) only covers calamities, and the broader crisis scheme ([bill 36940](https://www.eerstekamer.nl/nonav/wetsvoorstel/36940_wet_personeelsbehoud_bij)) is still in the Tweede Kamer. Bring in an employment lawyer, finance counsel and your auditor (on when the provision is booked). This isn't legal advice.

**The owner.** Talk to the owner privately first. The instinct is sound: the bank does want to see resolve, and a lasting drop in volume would make capacity a real question. But the bank will test the plan against cash. A line you could use: *"The bank will judge us on cash and a plan they can check, not on a headline. Let's give them both, with your own money on the table, and decide about jobs when the order book tells us to, not the meeting date."*

**Questions that would change my answer**
1. Is the 3.6x driven by falling EBITDA or rising debt, and what does your downside case show? If it's 4x or worse, you need the bank's headroom, not just working capital.
2. Does covenant EBITDA add back restructuring costs, and is there an equity cure?

**Assumptions:** a test at the end of calendar Q2 2027 on LTM EBITDA; about €55k fully loaded cost per person; about 11 years' average service. Working capital buys time, not earnings: if the decline is structural, you'll have to deal with it after June.
