# Covenant plan for the bank review: draft for discussion

**Recommendation:** close the 0.1x gap at the 30 June 2027 test with working capital, pricing, spend and the owner's own contribution, and ask the bank for headroom as a backstop. Do not announce a 10% reduction before the review: on these estimates it takes leverage at 30 June from 3.6x to about 4.2x, or about 3.6x if restructuring costs are added back.

**Read this first.** Every figure here is an estimate for a company of this size (260 employees, revenue about €40m, LTM EBITDA about €4.0m, net debt about €14.4m at 30 June 2027). Replace them with ledger, payroll and loan-agreement figures before anything goes to the bank. All tables were computed in code and add up; amounts are in € thousands unless stated.

---

## 1. The gap in euros

Leverage = net debt ÷ LTM EBITDA. Going from 3.6x to a lower ratio needs either less net debt or more EBITDA:

| Target at 30 June 2027 | Net debt reduction needed (EBITDA unchanged) | Or EBITDA increase needed (net debt unchanged) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3.5x, per €1m of LTM EBITDA | 100 | 29 |
| 3.3x (headroom), per €1m of LTM EBITDA | 300 | 91 |
| 3.5x, at an estimated €4.0m EBITDA | 400 | 114 |
| 3.3x, at an estimated €4.0m EBITDA | 1,200 | 364 |

Aim for about 3.3x, not 3.49x: a forecast that has already moved once can move again.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|

Option D's cost in the window (estimate): an amendment fee of about €22k (0.15% of a €15m facility) and a 0.5% margin step-up on €14m for six months, about €35k. Plan C needs only about 14% delivery to reach 3.5x and 43% to reach 3.3x.

**Recommended: D.** C does the work; the headroom covers the downside case and a forecast error.

## 3. The 10% reduction, net and by quarter (option B)

Assumptions (estimates): 26 people; €55k fully loaded cost each; average service 11 years; average monthly salary for the transition payment €3,600 including holiday allowance. Per person: statutory transition payment about €13.2k (one-third of a month per year of service), settlement premium and legal costs €4k, outplacement and training €3k. Plus €50k employer legal and advisory costs. Total one-off costs: about €575k. Works council advice requested in December, decision about March, UWV permits or settlement agreements from April. Exits: a quarter at the end of April, May, July and August each.

| € thousands | Q4 2026 | Q1 2027 | Q2 2027 | Q3 2027 | Q4 2027 | Total to end 2027 | 2028 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gross saving, from actual exit dates | 0 | 0 | 89 | 268 | 358 | 715 | 1,430 |
| One-off costs in P&L (provision when announced) | -575 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -575 | 0 |
| One-off costs in cash (paid at exit) | 0 | -50 | -263 | -262 | 0 | -575 | 0 |
| **Net P&L effect** | -575 | 0 | 89 | 268 | 358 | 140 | 1,430 |
| **Net cash effect** | 0 | -50 | -174 | 6 | 358 | 140 | 1,430 |

What this shows:
- **The quarter the decision is meant to fix gets worse.** Only €89k of saving falls in the twelve months to 30 June, while €313k of cash goes out and the full €575k is booked.
- **The breach could come earlier.** A provision recognised in December lands in the 2026 accounts. If leverage is also tested at 31 December and the definition doesn't add back restructuring costs, an illustrative 3.3x year-end becomes about 3.85x.
- **Through the end of 2027 it barely breaks even.** Losing about €350k of revenue in 2027 (at an estimated 40% contribution margin) wipes out the €140k net saving. The real saving starts in 2028.
- **Not in the table (estimates):** extra resignations among the people who stay (a 1% downsizing was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year; Trevor and Nyberg, 2008); if five more skilled people leave, replacing them at one-half to two times a €45k salary (Gallup) costs about €110k–€450k. Also overtime, agency staff or late deliveries if orders recover, and lost know-how in trades that take years to learn.
- **You don't choose who goes.** With a UWV permit, selection follows the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel): per group of interchangeable jobs, across age groups, by length of service. Settlement agreements are faster but cost more, and they still count towards the collective-dismissal threshold.
- **Proportion.** €1.43m a year is about twelve times the €114k EBITDA gap. It is an answer to a structural fall in volume. If that is the real problem, it should be decided on that evidence, through the works council, and sized to the volume, not timed to a bank meeting.

## 4. Plan without job cuts (option C)

| Lever | LTM EBITDA by 30 June | Net debt reduction by 30 June | Owner | Confidence | Human impact |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| Inventory: slow movers, safety stock, purchasing to the order book (about 7% of an estimated €7m) | 0 | 500 | Operations and purchasing | Medium | None |
| Receivables: chase overdue dealers, resolve disputes, invoice on dispatch (4 days faster) | 0 | 438 | Finance and sales | Medium-high | None |
| Capex: move non-safety, non-maintenance projects past 30 June (timing, not a saving) | 0 | 300 | MD and operations | High | None, if maintenance and safety stay funded |
| Pricing: +2% on new orders from 1 February, 25% erosion assumed | 150 | 150 | Sales | Medium | None internally; customer risk |
| Indirect spend and energy: renegotiate before cancelling | 100 | 100 | Purchasing | Medium | None |
| Hiring freeze with exceptions: 6 non-critical vacancies held about 3 months | 83 | 83 | MD and HR | High | More load on teams; review monthly |
| Leadership bonuses for 2026 and 2027 suspended (cash only; no EBITDA credit taken) | 0 | 150 | Owner and board | High | Leaders |
| **Total** | **333** | **1,721** | | | |

Notes:
- EBITDA gains are assumed to turn into cash by 30 June; price increases lag by the collection period, so treat the pricing line as optimistic on cash.
- **Not included on purpose:** paying suppliers later. Stretching small suppliers moves our problem onto businesses with less room than we have, and the bank sees through payables that jump back after the test date.
- **What we protect:** maintenance, safety, quality, training and apprentices. Cutting them would show up as breakdowns, rework and skills gaps after June.
- **Agency and flex hours:** if they're scaled to the order book, treat that as a people decision too. Give notice, follow the on-call rules, and give these people first call on permanent vacancies.
- **Working capital and capex timing buy time, not earnings.** If EBITDA keeps falling, they won't hold the covenant through 2027. See the triggers in section 7.

## 5. Owner and leadership contribution

- No dividend or distribution from the 2026 result. If the current forecast includes one, every euro not paid comes straight off net debt (not counted above).
- Management bonuses for 2026 and 2027 suspended; leadership pay frozen.
- A written standby commitment from the owner: an equity injection or subordinated shareholder loan, sized to cure the Q2 test if the plan falls short (about €400k at these estimates). Check whether the facility has an equity-cure clause and how subordinated loans are treated in net debt.
- In the owner's own terms: family firms that plan for the next generation run frugally, borrow less and avoid layoffs; they lag in booms and hold up better in downturns (Kachaner, Stalk and Bloch, 2012). The firm's name in the region is worth protecting.

## 6. What we ask of the bank

- Headroom on the Q2 and Q3 2027 tests (for example 4.0x, stepping back to 3.5x by Q4 2027 or Q1 2028), or a pre-agreed equity cure.
- In return: monthly covenant forecasts, a 13-week cash forecast updated weekly, the action tracker in section 4, and the owner's commitment in section 5.
- Why now: an amendment before a breach is cheaper and calmer than a waiver after one. A breach can move the file to the bank's special assets team (bijzonder beheer), which costs more and narrows the choices.

## 7. Triggers: what would change the plan

Agree these with the owner now and show them to the bank:
- Order intake below an agreed level (to be set from the order book) for two consecutive months, or a downside case that shows the drop in volume is lasting: start the works council advice process on capacity, sized to the real volume, done once.
- Plan delivery below 40% by 31 March: draw on the owner's standby commitment.
- Downside leverage above 4.0x at any 2027 test: reopen the bank conversation straight away, not at the next review.

## 8. Bank review agenda (pack outline)

1. Where we are: LTM EBITDA bridge, order book and net debt bridge, and what moved against our earlier forecast (say it first).
2. Covenant forecast per quarter to the end of 2027: base and downside, with and without actions.
3. 13-week cash forecast, base and downside.
4. Actions: owner, date, value and confidence for each, and how the bank will see progress every month.
5. Owner and leadership contribution.
6. The request: headroom or an equity cure, and what we offer for it.
7. Triggers: what we will do if the downside happens.

## 9. Legal and process checkpoints (check with counsel; not legal advice)

- **Works council (WOR):** a works council is mandatory at 260 employees. Advice on a reorganisation or workforce reduction must be requested while it can still substantially influence the decision (art. 25). A decision taken without advice, or against it, can be appealed to the Enterprise Chamber, which can order it undone (art. 26). After a decision that departs from the advice, implementation is suspended for one month. The works council is entitled to the company's financial information (art. 31a), and confidentiality can be imposed where needed (art. 20).
- **Collective dismissal (WMCO):** 20 or more dismissals in one UWV work area within three months means notifying UWV and the unions and consulting them, before the decision is final. Employer-initiated settlement agreements count. UWV generally waits a month after notification unless the unions confirm they have been consulted.
- **Dismissal route and costs:** a UWV permit with the reflection principle, or settlement agreements; notice of one to four months by length of service, minus the time the UWV procedure took; the statutory transition payment (subject to its annual cap); and anything the collective agreement (CAO) adds, such as a social plan.
- **No short-time work for this:** the current scheme (werktijdverkorting) covers only calamities outside normal business risk. The broader crisis scheme, the Wet personeelsbehoud bij crisis (bill 36940), is still before the Tweede Kamer with no start date. Don't plan on it.
- **Accounting:** when a restructuring provision must be recognised (RJ 252 or IAS 37). Agree it with the auditor before anything is announced.
- **Facility agreement:** EBITDA and net-debt definitions, add-backs, lease treatment, test dates, equity cure, and any duty to tell the bank about material events. Check with finance counsel.
