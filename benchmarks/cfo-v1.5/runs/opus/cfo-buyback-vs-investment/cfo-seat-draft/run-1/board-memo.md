## 10. How we explain it to shareholders

> "We are investing €120m, in stages and against published milestones, in the two things that decide whether customers buy electronics from us in five years: our online platform and the service in our stores. We are returning €[130]m of cash we don't need now, and we will review further returns at full-year results as cash generation and the programme's milestones allow. We will miss EPS guidance by €0.[0x] and tell you exactly why."

**The activist's case, taken seriously:**
- Idle cash earns little.
- Retailers have a record of platform projects that never pay back.
- Returning cash keeps management disciplined.

**Our answer:** a published order for using cash, gated tranches with the right to stop, and real money back now. We differ on sizing only: we size by the cash forecast, not the EPS gap.

**Evidence the board can cite:**
- **Earnings smoothing.** Of 401 US finance executives surveyed, 78% would give up economic value for smoother earnings, and many would delay a valuable project to hit a target. Source: Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal, *Journal of Accounting and Economics* (2005).
- **EPS-driven buybacks.** Firms just short of EPS forecasts were more likely to buy back shares, and those buybacks went with lower employment and investment. Source: Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, *Journal of Financial Economics* (2016).
- **Long-term companies.** Companies classified as long-term grew revenue 47% and earnings 36% more than others between 2001 and 2014. Source: McKinsey Global Institute (2017). Limits: US listed companies, with long-term behaviour measured indirectly.
- **Our sector.** Best Buy's turnaround after 2012 included better training, pay and benefits for store staff, price-matching and using stores for online pickup. Source: CTA (2019).

## 11. What would change this recommendation

- **A smaller liquidity buffer** in the cash forecast: a larger buyback now.
- **A business case that doesn't clear the hurdle:** shrink or stop the investment, and return more.
- **Shares well below our estimate of intrinsic value:** weight returns towards the buyback. **Shares above it:** weight them towards a special dividend.
- **A weaker operating outlook or tighter supplier credit:** hold more cash.
