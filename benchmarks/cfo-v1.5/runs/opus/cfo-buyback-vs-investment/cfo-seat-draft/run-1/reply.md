**My recommendation: fund the €120m in stages, return only the cash that is genuinely spare (about €130m on my illustrative buffer, not €300m) through a buyback sized to the cash forecast rather than to the EPS gap, and tell the market plainly that EPS will land a few cents short, and why.** €300m plus €120m comes to €420m: more than the €400m we have, before keeping anything back for the seasonal low.

The trap, in one line: a buyback that exists to close an EPS gap buys a number, not value. The cents come from a smaller share count, not a better business. The miss is probably telling us what the COO already is: our online and service model needs work.

### The options, side by side

I've assumed €150m to cover our intra-year cash low plus minimum liquidity. That figure is illustrative: replace it with the real one from the cash forecast.

| Option | Cash out | Net cash left | Against €150m buffer | EPS against guidance | Long term |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Activist: €300m buyback, investment deferred | €300m | €100m | −€50m | Hits, if bought early enough | Platform and service model wait another year |
| B. Both in full | €420m | −€20m | −€170m | Probably hits; investment opex eats into it | Borrowing to fund a payout |
| **C. Invest €120m in stages + ~€130m buyback** | €250m | €150m | At buffer | Misses by a few cents plus investment opex, explained | Fixes the business, returns real excess |

| E. Invest only | €120m | €280m | +€130m | Misses by more | Keeps cash we don't need |

With a €100m buffer the buyback could be €180m. With €200m it would be €80m. If the €120m is spread over two or three years and partly paid for from operating cash flow, more can go back sooner.

### Why not €300m now

- **Cash.** In our sector the year-end figure flatters us: payables from peak season unwind afterwards. If suppliers' credit insurers tighten cover, payment terms shorten and our working capital goes with them. "Net cash" also leaves out lease liabilities, which lenders and rating agencies count.
- **It may not even deliver the cents.** Only shares bought early count fully in this year's weighted average. Bought evenly over 12 months, you get about half the full-year effect. Over the last six months it's a quarter, over the last quarter an eighth, and you lose the interest on the cash. Check the claim before anyone relies on it.
- **The evidence.** Of 401 US finance executives surveyed, 78% said they would give up economic value for smoother earnings, and many would delay a valuable project to hit a target ([Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal, 2005](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10550)). Deferring the €120m to pay for a buyback is exactly that trade. Firms that would just have missed EPS forecasts were sharply more likely to buy back shares, and those buybacks went with cuts in jobs and investment ([Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v119y2016i1p168-185.html)).
- **Our sector.** Best Buy's turnaround after 2012 rested on better training, pay and benefits for store staff, price-matching online rivals and stores used as pickup points for online orders ([CTA, 2019](https://www.cta.tech/articles/how-hubert-joly-broke-the-mold-and-saved-best-buy-1/)). That is the COO's bet.

### Conditions on the €120m

- Split it into the platform rebuild and the training, each with its own return case against our hurdle rate.
- Release the money in tranches against milestones: online conversion and share of sales, service revenue and attach rate, store customer scores, store staff retention.
- Ask the CTO to review the platform's execution risk, and favour a step-by-step migration over a big-bang rebuild.
- Show how much is expensed this year (all of the training, and possibly part of the platform), and so how much bigger the miss gets.
- If the service model changes store jobs, bring employee representatives in while the design can still change. If the case assumes fewer store hours, say so openly.

### Guidance, legal and incentives

- **Guide honestly.** A miss of a few cents with a clear reason and an investment plan is usually easier to recover from than a beat everyone can see came from the share count. Either way, show the buyback's EPS effect separately.
- **Get counsel early.** A likely EPS miss may be inside information under the EU Market Abuse Regulation that must be disclosed promptly, and we shouldn't buy back shares while sitting on it. Also check the AGM authorisation (often capped at 10% of issued capital), distributable reserves and any loan terms that restrict payouts. This isn't legal or investment advice.
- **Leaders first.** If our bonuses or long-term incentive plan pay out on EPS, the remuneration committee should strip out the buyback effect and let the miss count, mine included. That removes any suspicion that management picked the option that pays it.

### The activist's best case

Idle cash earns little, retailers have a record of platform projects that never pay back, and handing cash back keeps management disciplined. All fair, and option C answers each point: a published order for using cash (liquidity floor, investments that clear the hurdle, dividend, then excess returned), gated tranches, real money back now and a further tranche reviewed at full-year results. We differ only on sizing: by the cash forecast, not the EPS gap. I'd test this with our larger long-term holders before the board meets, without sharing inside information.
