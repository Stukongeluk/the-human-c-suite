**Recommend funding the €120m programme in stages, returning about €150m through a buyback spread over 12 months, and telling the market about the EPS miss instead of buying your way to guidance.**

€300m plus €120m is €420m, more than the €400m you have, before any buffer for the seasonal cash low. And the €300m is sized to a few cents of EPS, not to what the business can spare. The trap is letting a few cents decide where €300m goes.

**Why not the full €300m this year**

- Moving this year's average share count means buying early and fast, whatever the price. A buyback creates value for remaining shareholders when you buy below what the shares are worth; buying to a date works against that.
- Ask why earnings are short. If the core business is under pressure, the programme goes at the cause and the buyback only at the number.
- US firms that would just have missed EPS forecasts were much more likely to buy back, then cut employment, capex and R&D over the next four quarters (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, 2016). Firms that just beat on low-quality earnings got a short-term lift, then trailed firms that missed on high-quality earnings (Bhojraj et al., 2009).
- Your sector has both versions. Circuit City used about $0.9bn of buyback authority in 2003–2007, laid off 3,400 of its most experienced salespeople in March 2007 and went bankrupt in November 2008, with the credit crunch as a factor too. Best Buy, from 2012, didn't start with layoffs and store closures; it retrained frontline staff and shipped from stores, and its shares went from $11 to $110 by 2020. One company each, not a law.

**The activist is right about three things.** Idle cash should go back to the owners. Big platform rebuilds overrun: of 1,471 IT projects, one in six went over budget by 200% on average (Flyvbjerg & Budzier, 2011). And management has to earn the right to reinvest. So offer a clear capital rule, a programme with gates, and a firm promise: money a failed gate leaves unspent goes back to shareholders.

**Options**

| | Net cash after | This year's EPS | 3 years out | People | Reversible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. €300m buyback, no programme | €100m | Meets | Cause of the miss untouched | Store staff carry the next cut if the business weakens | No |
| B. €300m buyback + €120m | −€20m if spent at once | Meets, unless programme costs pull it under | No buffer at the seasonal low | Plan exposed to one bad quarter | Barely |
| **C. €120m in stages + €150m buyback over 12 months** | €130m once fully spent, before free cash flow | Misses by a few cents, explained | Cause addressed, surplus returned | Paid training, designed with staff | Gates stop spending |
| D. €120m, no return | €280m | Misses | Cash idle | As C | Yes, but invites a proxy fight |

**How I'd set out C for the board**

1. **Liquidity floor first.** Size the buyback from the cash low point, not the balance-sheet date. Add leases, and test a downside where suppliers or their credit insurers shorten terms. The €150m is my estimate, assuming a floor near €130m; treasury should confirm or change it.
2. **Release the €120m in stages.** Split platform from service model; release about a third now and the rest on measured results: online conversion, services attach rate, store customer scores, staff turnover. Ask technology whether the platform can be replaced piece by piece rather than in one cut-over, and product which customer problem each piece fixes. Training costs will deepen this year's miss slightly; say so in the guidance update.
3. **Buy back on value.** Run it over 12 months under the AGM authority, with price limits. A second tranche next year comes from free cash flow above the floor.
4. **Own the miss.** Say plainly, in the paper and the market update, that the miss is management's responsibility. Calculate any EPS-linked bonus without the buyback effect, as UK investors already expect (Investment Association, 2024). If the board wants a signal, leadership's bonus takes the hit before any store budget does. If your own pay is tied to EPS, declare it in the paper.
5. **Design the service model with the people who will run it.** Paid training in working hours, store staff in the design, and the works council in while it can still change the plan; in the Netherlands and Germany, changes to store work and systems that can track staff carry formal advice, consent or co-determination rights. Measure stores and teams, never rank individual salespeople on platform data.

**Ask counsel before the board meets** (not legal advice): is the expected miss inside information that must be disclosed now under Article 17 of MAR, can a buyback run while it isn't disclosed, and does the current AGM authority cover the amount?

**Review quarterly:** net cash against the floor, programme return against plan, staff turnover, training applied on the shop floor, customer scores per store.

**Assumptions:** you're eurozone-listed, with works councils in some countries. I haven't seen your treasury forecast, the business case or the remuneration policy, and two questions would change the answer most: is there room for €150m above the cash low once leases and supplier terms count, and does the €120m clear your hurdle rate? If the shares are deeply undervalued, a bigger buyback can earn its place, sized on value rather than the EPS gap.
