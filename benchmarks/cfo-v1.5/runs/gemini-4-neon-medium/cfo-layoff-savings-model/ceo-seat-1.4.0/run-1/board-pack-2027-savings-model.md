# Board Pack Paper: 2027 Cost & Margin Savings Model

**Prepared by:** Finance Director  
**Date:** 20 November 2026  
**Status:** Draft for CEO review prior to Board circulation  
**Decision requested:** Agree the 2027 financial target, approve immediate operational and attrition levers for Q1 2027, and defer any collective redundancy decision until the structural gap is verified and legal consultation is scoped.

## Executive Summary

**A 120-person redundancy programme initiated in December 2026 will not deliver €9.0M of savings in 2027.** The €9.0M figure (120 × €75,000) is a gross, full-year **2028** run-rate. In Germany and the Netherlands, mandatory consultation, collective dismissal notifications, statutory notice periods, and severance/social plan costs push most exits into Q3–Q4 2027 while front-loading cash outflows.

- **Base-case 2027 net impact of 120 redundancies:** **-€2.70M** (a net cash and P&L outflow, €11.70M below the €9.0M target), reaching cash payback in **April 2028** (~17 months from start).
- **Best-case 2027 net impact:** **+€0.30M** (still €8.70M below target).
- **Downside (contested) 2027 net impact:** **-€6.40M**, if announced as a final decision before works council and union consultation.
- **Alternative 2027 package** (non-payroll spend, pricing/contract indexation, hiring freeze & natural attrition): estimated **+€5.4M to +€9.6M** net in 2027 with minimal one-off cost and high reversibility, before any involuntary exits are considered.

## 3. Legal & Consultation Constraints (Germany & Netherlands)

*This section is a commercial summary for planning, not legal advice. Employment counsel in both jurisdictions and HR must verify entity thresholds, applicable collective agreements (CAO / Tarifvertrag), and existing works councils before any communication.*

- **Do not announce 120 redundancies as a final decision in December 2026.** Doing so before statutory consultation risks injunctions, void notices, and significantly higher settlement costs.
- **Netherlands** (checked November 2026):
  - **Works Council (OR):** Mandatory from 50 employees; has a statutory advisory right on reorganisations (Art. 25 WOR), with a one-month waiting period and right of appeal to the Enterprise Chamber if the employer proceeds against negative advice.
  - **Collective Dismissal (WMCO):** Dismissing 20 or more employees within three months in one UWV work area (including employer-initiated settlement agreements) requires advance notification and consultation with UWV and unions before the decision is final.
  - **Selection & notice:** Business-economic dismissals require UWV permission (or mutual settlement agreements) and follow the reflection principle (*afspiegelingsbeginsel*). Notice periods run 1–4 months by tenure (minus UWV procedure time, minimum 1 month), plus statutory *transitievergoeding*.
- **Germany** (checked November 2026):
  - **Works Council (*Betriebsrat*):** Confirm which German sites have an elected works council (and note supervisory board one-third employee representation from 500 employees). Where a works council exists, a reduction of this scale is a major operational change (*Betriebsänderung*, §§111–112 BetrVG) requiring negotiation of a reconciliation of interests (*Interessenausgleich*) and a social plan (*Sozialplan*).
  - **Collective Dismissal Notification (§17 KSchG):** Mandatory advance notification to the Federal Employment Agency (*Agentur für Arbeit*) above statutory thresholds (e.g. >10% or >25 employees in establishments of 60–499 within 30 days; employer-initiated termination agreements count). Procedural errors can render dismissals void.
  - **Social selection & notice:** Operational dismissals require statutory social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, §1(3) KSchG: tenure, age, maintenance obligations, severe disability — not performance). Statutory notice periods run up to 7 months to month-end after 20 years of service (§622 BGB).
  - **Short-time work (*Kurzarbeitergeld*):** If the issue is a temporary demand dip, German short-time work bridges payroll with state support (requires works council agreement under §87(1) no. 3 BetrVG; maximum duration reverts from 24 months to 12 months from 1 January 2027 unless extended). In the Netherlands, *werktijdverkorting* covers only non-business calamities; counsel should verify the status of the *Wet personeelsbehoud bij crisis* bill.

**Combined non-redundancy package (Options 1 + 2 + 3):** Estimated **+€5.4M to +€9.6M** net in 2027 and **+€8.25M to +€13.25M** in 2028, with near-zero severance cash drain.

### View from Each Seat
- **Finance:** Redundancies worsen 2027 cash flow and EBITDA (via restructuring provisions) while Options 1–3 improve both in Q1 2027.
- **Operations:** Cutting 8.6% of industrial services headcount without exiting matching customer contracts creates immediate overtime, safety, and SLA penalty risk.
- **Customers:** Service continuity and technician familiarity drive renewals; a contested restructuring signals distress to industrial clients.
- **People:** If results have disappointed, leadership taking the first visible cut (bonuses/pay/perks) and engaging works councils at the problem stage builds the trust needed for any structural change that follows.

## 5. Recommendation

1. **Do not announce 120 redundancies in December 2026.** It will not deliver the 2027 target and creates acute legal and cash-flow risk.
2. **Launch Options 1–3 in Q1 2027** (non-payroll spend/subcontractor discipline, contract pricing/indexation, and a selective hiring freeze with internal redeployment) to deliver **€5.4M–€9.6M** in 2027 cash and margin. If leadership targets were missed, executive bonuses and discretionary perks should be cut first.
3. **Run a 30-day diagnostic with Operations, HR, and Works Councils** on the 2027 order book and site-level capacity. If a temporary dip is found at German sites, use *Kurzarbeit* to bridge it. Only if a verified, permanent structural gap remains after Options 1–3 should the Board scope a voluntary programme — and, as a last resort, a single well-prepared redundancy round budgeted realistically for **2028** run-rate savings, with transition support funded from day one of notice.

## 6. Key Assumptions & What Would Change This Recommendation

- **Assumption:** Average tenure is ~8–10 years across DE/NL industrial sites, driving 3–5 months of notice/consultation and €35k average severance. *Would change the model:* If affected roles are predominantly short-tenure (<2 years) or agency/fixed-term, exit speed and severance would improve — though 2027 net savings would still fall well short of €9M.
- **Assumption:** The company is not facing an immediate insolvency event within 90 days. *Would change the plan:* An acute liquidity crisis requires immediate lender engagement and emergency cash preservation, where front-loaded severance cash outflows would be even more dangerous.
- **Assumption:** Work volume has not permanently dropped by 8–10% across all sites. *Would change the plan:* If a major multi-year contract has been permanently lost, ring-fenced site restructuring (starting with redeployment and voluntary exits) should be scoped immediately with the relevant works council.
