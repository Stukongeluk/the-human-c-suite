# 2027 Management Bonus Plan: Board Proposal & Scorecard

**Prepared for:** Board of Directors / Compensation Committee  
**Company profile:** Private-equity-owned healthcare software company (~350 employees)  
**Status:** Draft for Board review (illustrative targets marked `[Estimate]` pending final 2027 budget)

---

## 1. Recommendation

Do **not** adopt a 2027 bonus plan paid 100% on Adjusted EBITDA with restructuring costs excluded. After three consecutive years of restructuring (2024–2026), restructuring is a recurring operating cost, not a one-off item. Paying management solely on an adjusted number that treats layoffs as free encourages short-term cuts to engineering, quality, security, and support that depress the company's exit multiple.

**Adopt a balanced, multi-year 2027 Management Bonus Plan** instead:
- **55% Financial (honestly defined):** 35% Bonus EBITDA (**including** all recurring restructuring and severance costs) + 20% Free Cash Flow Conversion.
- **45% Value-Protection & Durability:** 20% Customer Retention (GRR/NRR) + 15% People Health (Regretted Attrition & Engagement) + 10% Product Quality, Security & Clinical Compliance.
- **Multi-year alignment:** 65% paid in cash in Q1 2028 after audited results; 35% deferred over two years (Q1 2029 and Q1 2030), subject to malus and clawback.
- **Leaders-first discipline:** Management bonuses are cut or zeroed before frontline jobs are reduced, and no bonus is ever paid for headcount reduction.

---

## 2. Why the Proposed 100% Adjusted EBITDA Structure Hurts Exit Value

The board's instinct—keeping management focused on EBITDA and debt-service capacity—is understandable. In a private-equity-owned healthcare software business, however, a single-metric Adjusted EBITDA plan with a restructuring carve-out works against the owners' own exit thesis:

1. **Buy-side due diligence will reverse the adjustment anyway.** Restructuring in three consecutive years is no longer unusual or non-recurring. For public companies, SEC Regulation S-K Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B) bars excluding items labelled non-recurring when a similar charge occurred in the prior two years or is reasonably likely within two years. Private-equity buyers and Quality of Earnings (QoE) advisers apply the same standard: recurring severance and reorganization costs get normalized back into run-rate opex. Carving them out of the bonus plan pays management for paper EBITDA that a buyer will not pay a multiple on.
2. **Covenant EBITDA is not Incentive EBITDA.** If the credit agreement permits certain restructuring add-backs for bank covenant testing, keep that calculation strictly for lender compliance. Every board pack should reconcile **Reported EBITDA -> Bonus EBITDA -> Lender Covenant EBITDA** side by side.
3. **A single profit metric rewards value-destroying cuts.** Healthcare software buyers pay high multiples for sticky recurring revenue, regulatory trustworthiness, and capable technical teams. Cutting QA, security, customer support, or training lifts 2027 EBITDA while creating churn, compliance exposure, and technical debt in 2028–2029. Research shows downsizing is associated with lower subsequent profitability—especially in R&D-intensive, low-capital-intensity industries (Guthrie & Datta, 2008)—and even a 1% workforce reduction is associated with 31% higher voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg), with replacement costs running 0.5x to 2.0x annual salary (Gallup).

## 3. Options Compared

| Dimension | Option A: Board Draft (100% Adj. EBITDA, restructuring excluded) | Option B: Financial-Only Honest EBITDA (70% Reported EBITDA / 30% FCF) | Option C: Recommended Balanced Multi-Year Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Headline alignment** | Maximizes near-term Adjusted EBITDA | Fixes restructuring loophole; protects cash | Aligns cash, honest profit, and exit-multiple drivers |

| **Gaming risk** | **High:** incentivizes 4th restructuring, deferred maintenance, and support cuts | **Moderate:** still rewards cutting training, QA, and CS to hit 2027 profit | **Low:** customer, people, and quality weights + 2-year deferral |
| **Exit-value protection** | Weak (QoE add-back risk + churn/attrition lag) | Moderate (clean QoE, but churn risk unguarded) | **Strong** (protects GRR/NRR, compliance, and team capability) |
| **Human impact** | High risk of repeated layoffs and burnout | Moderate risk of under-investment in staff | Low; leadership absorbs downside before frontline cuts |

## 5. Honest Earnings Reconciliation (Required in Every Board Pack)

To eliminate ambiguity between bank reporting and management incentives, every quarterly board pack and the year-end bonus calculation must display this reconciliation:

| Line Item | Illustrative 2027 ($M) `[Estimate]` | Included in Bonus EBITDA? | Included in Lender Covenant EBITDA? | Notes |
|---|---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Reported EBITDA (GAAP / IFRS)** | **$12.0** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Audited operating baseline; includes $1.5M of severance/restructuring |
| (+) Recurring restructuring & severance | $1.5 | **No** (remains an expense) | Per credit agreement | 4th consecutive year; treated as normal operating cost |
| (+) Pre-approved M&A transaction fees | $0.5 | **Yes** (capped at Board approval) | Per credit agreement | Only for closed/board-approved acquisitions, never internal reorganizations |
| **Resulting EBITDA Measure** | — | **$12.5** | **$14.0** | **$1.5M difference** is explicitly visible every quarter |

*Arithmetic check:* `$12.0M + $0.5M = $12.5M` (Bonus EBITDA); `$12.0M + $1.5M + $0.5M = $14.0M` (Lender Covenant EBITDA); `$14.0M - $12.5M = $1.5M`.

## 6. Worked Payout Examples (Illustrative $1,000,000 Target Pool)

*Assumes an illustrative `[Estimate]` target management bonus pool of $1,000,000.*

### Scenario A: Durable Performance (Clean Growth, No Restructuring)
- **Bonus EBITDA:** 100% of budget -> 100% payout × 35% = **35.00%**
- **FCF Conversion:** 90% (midpoint of Target 85% and Stretch 95%) -> 125% payout × 20% = **25.00%**
- **Customer Retention:** At Target -> 100% payout × 20% = **20.00%**
- **People Health:** At Target -> 100% payout × 15% = **15.00%**
- **Quality, Security & Compliance:** At Target -> 100% payout × 10% = **10.00%**
- **Total Weighted Payout:** `35.00% + 25.00% + 20.00% + 15.00% + 10.00%` = **105.00%** = **$1,050,000**
  - **Paid in Cash (Q1 2028, 65%):** **$682,500**
  - **Deferred (35%):** **$367,500** — vesting 50% in Q1 2029 (**$183,750**) and 50% in Q1 2030 (**$183,750**)

*Arithmetic check:* `$682,500 + $367,500 = $1,050,000`; `$183,750 × 2 = $367,500`.

### Scenario B: Short-Term EBITDA Hit via a 4th Restructuring
Management cuts support and engineering roles in Q2 2027 to lift Adjusted EBITDA to 110% of budget, incurring $1.5M in severance; GRR slips to 89% and regretted attrition rises to 17%.
- **Under Board Draft (100% Adj. EBITDA, restructuring excluded):** Management receives **150% payout = $1,500,000** in immediate cash—despite weaker retention, higher attrition, and a $1.5M cash outflow.
- **Under Recommended Plan:**
  - Bonus EBITDA (with $1.5M restructuring included) lands at 95% of budget -> 75% payout × 35% = **26.25%**
  - FCF Conversion (depressed by severance cash) below Threshold -> **0.00%**
  - Customer Retention (89% GRR, below 91% Threshold) -> **0.00%**
  - People Health (17% regretted attrition, worse than 14% Threshold) -> **0.00%**
  - Quality, Security & Compliance (at Target) -> 100% × 10% = **10.00%**
  - **Scorecard Subtotal:** `26.25% + 0.00% + 0.00% + 0.00% + 10.00%` = **36.25%** ($362,500)
  - **After Leaders-First Gate (involuntary restructuring triggered):** Pool is capped at **$0** (see Section 7).

## 7. Governance, Deferral, Gates & Legal Review

1. **Multi-Year Deferral (65% / 35%):** 65% of any earned bonus pays in cash following completion of the 2027 audit. The remaining 35% is deferred over two years (50% Q1 2029, 50% Q1 2030). On a change of control / clean exit, unvested deferrals accelerate only if exit due diligence confirms no material undisclosed churn, compliance, or reporting issues.
2. **Hard Gates (Zero Payout Triggers):**
   - **Liquidity & Covenant Gate:** No bonus pays if the company is in covenant breach or below board-mandated minimum cash headroom.
   - **Patient-Safety & Compliance Gate:** No bonus pays if the company suffers a reportable healthcare data breach, regulatory enforcement action, or patient-safety-impacting software failure due to control lapses.
   - **Leaders-First Gate:** If results disappoint or the company executes an involuntary workforce reduction in 2027, the management bonus pool is automatically reduced to zero before frontline roles are cut. No bonus is ever paid for headcount reduction.
3. **Malus & Clawback:** Unvested deferrals are subject to reduction (malus) and paid cash bonuses are subject to recovery (clawback) for three years in the event of financial restatement, material error in non-financial metrics (e.g., misreported churn or incident severity), or misconduct.
4. **Required Professional Sign-Off:** Before adoption, this plan must be reviewed by:
   - **Employment counsel** (enforceability of malus/clawback and deferral forfeiture rules across all jurisdictions where executives sit, plus any works council or employee-representative consultation duties if applicable in European subsidiaries);
   - **Tax advisers** (timing of income and payroll tax on deferred cash or phantom-equity awards, e.g., Section 409A in the US or local equivalents);
   - **External auditors** (verification of the EBITDA reconciliation and software capitalization policy).

*Note: This document is a financial and governance proposal, not legal, tax, or investment advice.*
