### Why the board's draft hurts its own exit value

The board's instinct is understandable: one number tied to debt service and EV/EBITDA keeps management focused. The trap is that **after three consecutive years of restructuring (2024–2026), restructuring is an operating cost, not a one-off**.

- **Buyers will reverse the add-back in due diligence.** For US-listed firms, SEC Regulation S-K Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B) forbids excluding items labelled non-recurring when a similar charge occurred in the prior two years or is likely within two years. Buy-side Quality of Earnings (QoE) teams apply the same test in private equity exits: three or four years of severance gets normalized back into run-rate opex. Excluding it from the bonus pays management for paper EBITDA that a buyer will not pay a multiple on.
- **Covenant EBITDA is not Incentive EBITDA.** If your credit agreement permits restructuring add-backs for lender covenants, keep that strictly for bank reporting and reconcile **Reported EBITDA -> Bonus EBITDA -> Lender Covenant EBITDA** in every board pack.
- **100% EBITDA rewards value-destroying cuts.** In healthcare software, buyers pay for sticky recurring revenue, regulatory trustworthiness, and stable engineering/clinical teams. Paying solely on annual EBITDA encourages cutting QA, security, training, and support—lifting 2027 profit while pushing churn and technical debt into 2028–2029. Downsizing is associated with lower subsequent profitability, especially in R&D-intensive industries (Guthrie & Datta, 2008), and even a 1% workforce reduction is associated with 31% higher voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg), with replacement costs running 0.5x to 2.0x annual salary (Gallup).

### Key plan mechanics & human impact

- **Multi-year alignment:** 65% paid in cash in Q1 2028 after audited results; 35% deferred over two years (50% Q1 2029, 50% Q1 2030), subject to **3-year malus and clawback** for restatements, misreported metrics, or compliance failures.
- **Leaders-first & hard gates:** Zero payout if the company breaches a bank covenant, suffers a reportable data breach or patient-safety-impacting software failure, or executes an **involuntary workforce reduction** in 2027. Management bonuses absorb downside before frontline jobs are cut, and no bonus is ever paid for headcount reduction.
- **Human impact:** Removing the restructuring carve-out stops the annual cycle of fourth-quarter headcount cuts that erode morale, institutional clinical knowledge, and customer support quality across your 350-person team.
