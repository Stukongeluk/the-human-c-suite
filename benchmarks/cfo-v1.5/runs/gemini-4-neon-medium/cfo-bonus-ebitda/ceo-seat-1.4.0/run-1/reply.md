Do not tie 100% of the 2027 management bonus to Adjusted EBITDA with restructuring excluded; instead, take back a plan weighted **60% to real earnings and cash (Unadjusted EBITDA with restructuring included + Free Cash Flow), 25% to customer retention and product reliability, and 15% to people stability**, backed by hard covenant and safety gates. I have drafted the board-ready plan on that basis in [2027-management-bonus-plan.md](file:///G:/agy-bench/ws/cc0480e11d2c/2027-management-bonus-plan.md).

**The trap:** Restructuring in each of the last three years is an operating pattern, not a one-off—and excluding it from a 100% EBITDA bonus pays management to cut headcount in Q4 whenever the commercial plan slips.

### Why this is a PE value problem, not just a people problem

The board's instinct is understandable: with debt to service and an exit ahead, they want a single, hard-edged target tied to the headline multiple. In healthcare software, though, their proposed design works against the exit price they want:

- **Quality of Earnings (QoE) will strip it out anyway.** A fourth consecutive year of "non-recurring" restructuring add-backs is a textbook red flag in sell-side due diligence. Buyers will reclassify it as a normal operating cost, lowering normalised EBITDA and compressing the multiple.
- **Healthcare buyers pay for durability and low key-person risk.** At 350 people, clinical workflow knowledge, hospital integrations, and regulatory/security compliance live in a relatively small number of heads. Repeated annual cuts drive out experienced staff, push up churn, and raise the odds of a clinical-impacting incident—all of which show up in diligence.
- **Survivors stop building.** After three restructurings in three years, your teams are already bracing for round four. Formalising a restructuring loophole in executive pay signals that headcount is the balancing item for leadership bonuses.

### What to pull before the board meeting

Bring four numbers from your systems of record to make this a commercial conversation rather than a philosophical one:
1. **Total cash spent on restructuring in 2024–2026** alongside reported Adjusted EBITDA—so the board sees the real cash gap.
2. **Lender covenant definitions** (especially whether lenders cap or disallow restructuring add-backs).
3. **2026 Net and Gross Revenue Retention** and any implementation/support backlog trends after the last three restructurings.
4. **2026 regretted attrition and key-person concentration** across engineering, clinical, and compliance teams.

Before finalising any executive plan changes, have employment counsel check existing executive service agreements and any local works council or employee-representative consultation requirements in your operating jurisdictions.

### Key assumptions and what would change my mind

- **Assumption:** The 2024–2026 restructurings were recurring cost-out exercises rather than separate, board-approved post-acquisition integrations. **Would change my mind:** If all three were genuinely tied to distinct M&A deals with ring-fenced synergy budgets—though even then, a pre-approved M&A carve-out is far cleaner than a blanket restructuring exclusion.
- **Assumption:** The company is not facing an immediate liquidity or covenant crisis requiring emergency cash preservation. **Would change my mind:** If covenant headroom is tight for FY2027, in which case FCF and liquidity gates should carry even more weight, and leadership bonuses should be deferred or reduced first before any headcount action.
