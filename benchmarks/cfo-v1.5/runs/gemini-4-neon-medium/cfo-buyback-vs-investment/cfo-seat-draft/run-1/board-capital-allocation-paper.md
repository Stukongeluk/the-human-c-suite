**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Chief Financial Officer

## 1. Recommendation

**Reject the €300m buyback this year, take the small EPS guidance miss honestly, and adopt a disciplined capital-allocation framework for our €400m net cash:**

1. **Protect liquidity first:** Ring-fence **€150m `[estimate]`** for seasonal working-capital swings, peak-to-trough cash needs and covenant headroom.
2. **Invest in the business on strict gates:** Ring-fence up to **€120m** for the e-commerce rebuild and store service-model training—**not** as a blank cheque, but in three gated tranches starting with a **€25m `[estimate]`** Phase 1 (architecture + store pilot).
3. **Return genuine excess cash:** Commit **€130m `[estimate]`** to shareholder returns (buyback or special dividend, subject to valuation and tax advice) over 12–18 months, **explicitly decoupled from this year's EPS guidance**.

Do **not** buy back shares to close an EPS gap of a few cents. Research across listed companies shows that buybacks timed to beat EPS forecasts are followed by cuts to investment and employment (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, *Journal of Financial Economics*, 2016), and 78% of surveyed CFOs admitted they would sacrifice long-term economic value just to smooth earnings (Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal, *Journal of Accounting and Economics*, 2005). That is the trap in front of us.

## 3. Why the Activist's Argument Deserves a Serious Answer—Not a Blank "No"

The activist shareholder is right about one thing: **sitting on €400m of net cash without a clear plan destroys confidence.** Cash above operational needs earns less than our cost of equity, and long-term institutional shareholders will also ask why we are holding it.

Where the activist is wrong is the **size (€300m in one year)** and the **purpose (manufacturing EPS)**:
- **Retail cash is seasonal.** In consumer electronics, working capital swings sharply between the Q4 inventory build, supplier payables, warranty/returns exposure and thin gross margins. Dropping net cash to €100m (Option A) or €20m net debt (Option B) leaves no room for a soft peak season or supplier-credit tightening.
- **Mechanical EPS is not value.** Institutional investors see through an EPS beat bought with cash; it solves one press release and weakens the business they own for the next five years. Companies managed for the long term outperformed peers on revenue (+47%) and earnings (+36%) over 2001–2014 (McKinsey Global Institute, 2017).
- **Our counter-offer in shareholders' terms:** Publish a clear capital-allocation hierarchy (1. Liquidity & balance sheet; 2. Organic investments clearing our hurdle rate; 3. Disciplined return of 100% of genuine excess cash). That gives long-term investors a reason to back the Board and takes the oxygen out of a proxy fight.

## 4. Putting Discipline Around the COO's €120m Proposal

Backing the COO's strategic direction does **not** mean approving €120m on a single slide.

- **Respect the base rate on big IT rebuilds.** Across more than 16,000 major projects, only 8.5% finished on budget and on time, and only 0.5% also delivered the promised benefits (Flyvbjerg & Gardner, *How Big Things Get Done*, 2023).
- **Why the store-staff training matters economically.** In consumer electronics, pure-play online retailers compete on price. Our physical stores win only when customers get reliable advice, setup, repair and omnichannel fulfilment. Retailers that invest in frontline training, cross-skilling and stable schedules achieve higher sales and productivity—in a randomised trial across 28 Gap stores, predictable schedules raised median sales by 7% and labour productivity by 5% (Williams et al., 2018; Zeynep Ton, *The Good Jobs Strategy*, 2014).
- **How we structure the €120m in three gates `[estimate]`:**

| Gate | Timing | Envelope (€m) | Scope | Exit criteria to unlock next gate |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **Gate 1: Prove** | Months 1–6 | 25 | E-commerce architecture & modular plan; store service-model pilot & training in 10–15% of stores co-designed with store staff | Verified conversion/NPS lift in pilot stores; fixed-scope engineering plan; ROIC > WACC + execution margin |
| **Gate 2: Build** | Months 7–18 | 60 | Phased platform build (cutovers strictly outside Q4 peak); regional store rollout & training | Uptime & checkout conversion on target; unit economics holding; store staff retention & absence stable |
| **Gate 3: Scale** | Months 19–24 | 35 | Final migration, legacy system decommissioning, full-estate training & reinforcement | Legacy run-cost retired; target payback and customer/people metrics met |
| **Total** | **24 months** | **120** | | **Unspent funds revert to excess-cash pool if any gate fails** |

*CTO and operational checks required before Gate 1 closes:* offline/fallback modes for tills and store devices, peak-season freeze windows, and store-floor usability testing with frontline staff before lock-in.

**Key sensitivities (euro-for-euro rule):**

- **If Gate 1 fails its hurdle rate:** we stop after €25m, releasing **€95m** back to the excess-cash pool rather than throwing good money after bad.

## 6. Honest Guidance, Incentives & Leadership Accountability

- **Own the EPS miss plainly.** Guide honestly on the few-cent shortfall, explain the operational driver, and present the capital-allocation framework on the same day.
- **Leaders go first on pay.** If the EPS miss reduces this year's executive bonus pool, senior management takes that hit—no discretionary upward adjustment.
- **Fix the incentive trap (Remuneration Committee):** For future cycles, calculate bonus EPS on a **constant share count** (neutralising buybacks), pair profit with cash conversion, customer satisfaction/NPS and store-staff regretted attrition, and defer a meaningful share over three years with malus and clawback.

## 7. Governance, Legal, Tax & People Signposts

*This paper is financial analysis for Board discussion, not legal, tax or investment advice. Before any Board resolution or market announcement, obtain sign-off from:*

- **External legal counsel & Company Secretary:** Market Abuse Regulation (MAR) inside-information and disclosure timing for the guidance update; AGM shareholder authority and EU/national safe-harbour limits on daily buyback volume and pricing.
- **Tax advisers & Auditors:** Tax efficiency of share buybacks vs special dividends for our shareholder register; accounting treatment (capex vs opex) of the e-commerce rebuild and store training, and disclosure of any non-IFRS metrics (no recurring transformation costs labelled "one-off").
- **Treasury & Lending Banks:** Verification of revolving credit facility (RCF) covenants, IFRS 16 lease ratios, and supplier credit-insurance thresholds under base and downside cases.
- **HR & Employee Representatives (Works Council / Unions):** Early consultation on the store service model, training hours, scheduling guardrails and any new store-floor digital tools—while the design can still be shaped by the people who will run it.

## 10. Pre-Mortem: How This Plan Could Fail in 12 Months

1. **E-commerce rebuild overruns cost or slips into Q4 peak** → *Guard:* hard Q4 code/migration freeze; Gate 2 funds locked until modular architecture and vendor caps are signed.
2. **Store training becomes unpaid squeeze or clashes with under-staffed shifts** → *Guard:* budget backfill hours explicitly inside the €120m; co-design rosters with store managers and employee representatives.
3. **Activist launches a public campaign anyway** → *Guard:* brief top 10 long-term shareholders immediately with the full waterfall, hurdle rates and €130m return commitment.
4. **Consumer demand drops sharply** → *Guard:* €150m liquidity buffer untouched; Tranche 2 of the return (€50m) and Gate 2/3 spend are both pauseable before any store jobs are threatened.
