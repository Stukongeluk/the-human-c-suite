### Why this is the right recommendation

1. **Never buy back shares to hit EPS guidance.** That is the single clearest trap here. Almeida, Fos & Kronlund (*Journal of Financial Economics*, 2016) found that companies using buybacks to clear EPS forecasts subsequently cut employment and investment. Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal (*Journal of Accounting and Economics*, 2005) found 78% of CFOs would give up real economic value to smooth earnings. Take the few-cent miss honestly, explain why it happened, and **have senior leadership take any resulting hit on this year's bonus pool**. Ask the Remuneration Committee to move bonus EPS to a constant share count going forward, balanced with cash conversion, customer NPS and store-staff retention over three years.

2. **Steelman the activist—don't just say "no".** Sitting on €400m of net cash with no stated capital-allocation framework *does* invite a proxy fight, and long-term shareholders will privately agree with the activist that idle cash earns less than your cost of equity. Committing **€130m `[estimate]`** of genuine excess cash to a measured buyback (only if shares trade below intrinsic value; otherwise a special dividend) over 12–18 months shows capital discipline and gives long-term institutions a clear reason to back the Board.

3. **Back the COO's direction, not a blank €120m cheque.** In consumer electronics, pure-play online rivals compete on price; knowledgeable store staff and reliable omnichannel fulfilment are your moat. Evidence from retail backs investing in frontline capability and stable scheduling (Zeynep Ton, *The Good Jobs Strategy*, 2014; Williams et al.'s 28-store Gap trial, 2018, where predictable schedules raised median sales by 7% and labour productivity by 5%). **However**, big IT rebuilds have a brutal base rate—only 8.5% finish on budget and on time, and 0.5% also deliver promised benefits (Flyvbjerg & Gardner, 2023).
   - Structure the €120m in **three strict gates**: e.g., **€25m `[estimate]`** for Gate 1 (Months 1–6: modular architecture + a store-service pilot and paid training in 10–15% of stores, co-designed with store staff and CTO sign-off on peak-season uptime/offline tills), **€60m** for Gate 2 (Months 7–18, cutovers outside Q4 peak), and **€35m** for Gate 3 (Months 19–24, legacy decommissioning).
   - If Gate 1 fails your hurdle rate (WACC + execution risk), you stop after €25m and release **€95m** back to shareholders.

4. **Protect the people running the stores.** Budget explicit backfill hours inside the €120m so training happens on paid time without squeezing under-staffed shifts, and bring works councils / employee representatives in early while the service model and scheduling guardrails can still be shaped.
