# Q2 Covenant & Bank Review Plan

**Prepared for:** Owner / Managing Director and CFO  
**Context:** Forecast Q2 leverage of 3.6x against a 3.5x covenant limit; bank review in 3 weeks (260 employees, Netherlands)

---

## 1. Executive Recommendation

**Do not announce a 10% headcount reduction before the bank review.** Closing a 0.1x leverage gap requires reducing Net Debt by ~2.8% or lifting LTM EBITDA by ~2.9% (or a 1.5% / 1.4% combination). Announcing 26 redundancies now will not improve Q2 leverage: cash leaves the business first through statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*) and notice periods, while payroll savings arrive after Q2. It also exposes the company to legal challenge in the Netherlands before the bank meeting even takes place.

Instead, go into the bank review in three weeks with:
1. A **13-week rolling cash forecast** and updated Q2–Q4 covenant model (base and downside cases).
2. A **working-capital and non-payroll cash plan** targeting a 3–5% reduction in Net Debt before Q2 close.
3. A **visible owner and leadership commitment** (paused dividends/owner draws, frozen executive bonuses, and—if permitted under the facility agreement—readiness for a modest subordinated loan or equity cure).
4. A **proactive waiver or temporary headroom request** (e.g., 3.75x–4.0x for Q2–Q3), paired with pre-agreed downside triggers if the order book deteriorates further.

---

## 2. Why a Pre-Meeting Layoff Announcement Backfires

| Dimension | Impact of Announcing 10% Cuts (26 Roles) Before the Bank Review |
|---|---|
| **Q2 Covenant Math** | Notice periods (1–4 months by length of service) and statutory transition payments push cash out before payroll falls. Even if restructuring charges are excluded from covenant EBITDA, cash outflow raises Net Debt. |
| **Dutch Legal Floor** *(not legal advice — verify with Dutch employment counsel)* | With 260 employees, a works council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) is mandatory (50+ threshold) and has a formal advisory right on reorganisations (**Article 25 WOR**), with appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*). Cutting 26 roles also crosses the **20-employee WMCO threshold** (within 3 months in one UWV work area), requiring advance notification and consultation with UWV and unions before any decision is final. Employer-initiated settlement agreements count toward the 20. |
| **Bank Perception** | Relationship banks want control, forecast accuracy, and cash discipline—not a unilateral announcement that risks an Enterprise Chamber injunction, union dispute, or production disruption. |
| **Long-Term Business Value** | Skilled furniture makers, machine operators, and planners take years to recruit and train. Losing them for a 0.1x gap risks quality failures, missed deliveries, and key-person loss when demand recovers. |

## 3. Options to Close the 0.1x Gap (Ordered by Speed & Reversibility)

| Option | Leverage Effect & Timing | Reversibility | Human & Operational Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Working-capital sprint** (reduce timber/fabric/WIP stock, tighten receivables, align supplier terms, defer non-critical capex) | **Directly lowers Net Debt within weeks**; a ~2.8% Net Debt cut closes 3.6x to 3.5x on its own | High | None on jobs; requires disciplined floor/purchasing coordination |
| **2. Owner & leadership first** (pause dividends/draws, freeze executive bonuses, temporary leadership pay cut, or subordinated shareholder loan / equity cure) | **Immediate cash & equity signal** to the bank; lowers Net Debt if injected | High | Protects staff trust; proves skin in the game |
| **3. Margin & non-payroll spend** (scrap/rework reduction, energy/vendor renegotiation, repricing low-margin SKUs, external hiring freeze & natural attrition) | **Builds EBITDA and cash over Q2–Q3** | High | Minimal; protects core workshop capability |
| **4. Temporary covenant waiver / reset** (e.g., 3.75x–4.0x for Q2–Q3 backed by 13-week cash flow and downside triggers) | **Removes breach risk immediately**; buys time for operational fixes to land | Medium (may carry a modest waiver fee or margin ratchet) | Protects continuity; avoids panic cuts |
| **5. Temporary hours / redeployment** *(only if demand dip is temporary)* | Reduces labour cost without losing skills; note Dutch *werktijdverkorting* currently covers only calamities outside normal business risk (check with counsel whether the *Wet personeelsbehoud bij crisis* bill has taken effect and applies) | Medium | Shared burden; requires OR and CAO check |
| **6. Structural headcount reduction** *(last resort, only if order book shows permanent decline)* | **Too slow for Q2**; net cash positive only in H2 or later after consultation, notice, and transition payments | Low (irreversible loss of skills and regional reputation) | High; must follow Art. 25 WOR, WMCO, applicable CAO, and a fair social plan |

## 4. Three-Week Action Plan Before the Bank Review

### Week 1: Verify Facts & Align Internally
- **Facility agreement review (CFO):** Confirm exact definitions of Net Debt and LTM EBITDA, permitted restructuring add-backs, equity-cure rights, and notice deadlines for a potential breach.
- **Systems-of-record check (CFO + Operations):** Pull aged inventory (raw materials, WIP, finished goods), aged receivables, open order book vs. 12-month trend, scrap/rework rates, and overtime/agency spend from ERP.
- **Legal check:** Confirm works council status, applicable collective labour agreement (e.g., CAO Meubelindustrie), and WMCO/WOR boundaries with Dutch employment counsel.
- **Owner alignment:** Agree on dividend/draw pause and whether a temporary subordinated loan is available as a backstop.

### Week 2: Build the Bank Pack & Test Downside
- **13-week rolling cash forecast** updated weekly.
- **Base and downside scenarios (Q2–Q4):** Show clearly whether the 0.1x gap is a temporary working-capital/demand timing issue or a structural margin decline.
- **Action tracker with named owners:** Quantify each euro of inventory release, receivables acceleration, capex deferral, and non-payroll savings, with realistic dates.
- **Downside triggers:** Define objectively what order-book or EBITDA level would trigger deeper measures (including early consultation with the OR).

### Week 3: Bank Review & Follow-Through
- **Present proactively:** Share the pack ahead of the meeting. Lead with ownership of the forecast, the working-capital and owner-commitment plan, and—if headroom remains tight—a specific, time-bound waiver or reset request.
- **Engage the works council early if changes affect work:** If the numbers show a deeper or structural problem that will affect shifts, overtime, or roles, bring the OR into the real numbers early enough for them to help shape the plan.

---

## 5. Metrics to Track Weekly

- **Cash & debt:** 13-week cash balance, Net Debt vs. target, DSO (receivables days), inventory/WIP days.
- **Operations & margin:** Order intake vs. shipments, gross margin by product line, scrap/rework rate, on-time delivery.
- **People & stability:** Voluntary attrition in key workshop trades, sickness absence, agency/overtime hours.
