**Do not announce 10% job cuts before the bank review.** A forecast of 3.6x against a 3.5x covenant is a **0.1x gap**—about **2.8% of Net Debt** or **2.9% of LTM EBITDA** (or a 1.5% / 1.4% mix of both). Announcing 26 redundancies now will not fix Q2 leverage, will make the company look *less* in control to the bank, and carries serious Dutch legal risk.

Announcing a headcount cut to signal seriousness before diagnosing the gap is an irreversible move for a small, potentially reversible problem—and it risks losing the skilled craftspeople a family furniture maker takes years to build.

### Why the owner's move backfires

- **Q2 math works against you:** Under Dutch rules, employer notice periods (1–4 months by length of service) and statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*) push cash *out* before payroll drops. Even if your facility agreement excludes restructuring costs from covenant EBITDA, cash outflow increases Net Debt.
- **Dutch legal floor** *(not legal advice—confirm with Dutch employment counsel)*: At 260 employees, a works council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) is mandatory (50+ threshold) and holds an advisory right on reorganisations (**Article 25 WOR**), with appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*). Cutting 26 people also crosses the **20-employee WMCO threshold** (within three months in one UWV work area), requiring advance notification and consultation with UWV and unions before any decision is final—and employer-initiated settlement agreements count toward the 20. Announcing a cut as a done deal invites a legal freeze right before your bank meeting.
- **What the bank actually wants:** The owner is right that the bank wants to see decisiveness. For a 0.1x forecast breach at a family firm, a relationship bank reads decisiveness as **cash visibility and owner commitment**—not a unilateral layoff headline that risks union disputes or missed customer deliveries.
