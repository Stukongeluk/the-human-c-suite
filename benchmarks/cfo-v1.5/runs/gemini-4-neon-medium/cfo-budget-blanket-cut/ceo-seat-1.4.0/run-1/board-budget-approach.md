# FY2027 Budget Approach: Achieving 8% Opex Efficiency Without Eroding Delivery Capacity

**Prepared for:** Board of Directors · **Prepared by:** CFO (with Executive Team) · **Date:** November 2026 · **Decision needed:** Board meeting (3 weeks)

---

## 1. The Problem and What Is at Stake

Our margin is positive, which means we are not in a cash crisis, but it is thin enough that a modest drop in client demand or utilisation would push us into loss. The board has asked for an 8% reduction in next year's operating expenses to build margin resilience.

Leadership owns the fact that our cost base has grown too close to our revenue. The choice before the board is *how* we close the gap: a flat 8% cut across every cost centre, or a differentiated, phased budget that targets non-payroll spend, internal overhead, and commercial leakage first while protecting billable delivery capacity.

## 3. Why a Flat 8% Cut Across Cost Centres Fails the Math

A uniform 8% mandate is fast to communicate and avoids internal politics, which is its main appeal. In a 600-person B2B services business where payroll is 68% of opex, however, it creates three structural problems:

1. **It cuts revenue-generating capacity alongside overhead.** Client delivery teams typically carry a higher payroll share (75%–85%+ of team opex) than central functions. Cutting them by 8% forces reductions in billable staff or client support, lowering revenue and shrinking the margin we are trying to protect.
2. **It leaves savings on the table in central spend.** IT, facilities, procurement, external advisors, and software licences often hold 10%–15%+ in reducible spend. Stopping those cost centres at 8% under-delivers where cuts are safest.
3. **Forced redundancies do not deliver Year 1 net savings in the Netherlands.** Under Dutch employment law (see Section 6), Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) consultation, union/UWV notification if 20 or more roles are affected within three months, notice periods of one to four months, and statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*) push cash costs into H1 while payroll savings arrive in H2.

## 4. Options Considered

All financial effects are expressed as a percentage of total current opex (100.0% = 68.0% payroll + 32.0% non-payroll).

| Option | Year 1 Opex / EBIT Effect (% of current opex) | Time to Cash Effect | People Affected & How | Hidden Costs | Reversible? | Main Risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Do nothing** | 0.0% | Immediate | None | None | Yes | Thin margin remains exposed to a client loss or demand dip |
| **B. Flat 8% cut per cost centre** | Est. 3.5%–5.5% net in Year 1 (8.0% run-rate only in Year 2, before revenue loss) | 6–9 months for payroll portion | Est. 25–45 FTE across delivery and support; high workload on remaining staff | Severance, UWV/legal fees, lost billable revenue, regrettable attrition of top performers | No (rebuilding client teams takes 6–12 months) | Revenue drops alongside cost; OR dispute or Enterprise Chamber appeal if rushed |
| **C. Differentiated, phased budget (Recommended)** | **5.5%–8.9% opex reduction** (plus **1.5%–3.0% EBIT equivalent** from pricing/scope discipline) | Q1–Q2 for non-payroll; Q2–Q4 for attrition & commercial levers | No involuntary exits in H1; hiring freeze on non-billable roles, internal redeployment, executive bonus freeze first | Management time for vendor renegotiation and internal reskilling; slower headline speed than a blanket decree | Mostly yes (freeze and vendor cuts can be adjusted quarterly) | Requires real discipline from cost centre heads and CEO backing to hold the line on non-billable backfills |

### View From Each Leadership Seat (Option C)
- **Customer / Commercial:** Delivery teams stay intact; service quality and account continuity are protected while unbilled scope creep and underpriced renewals are fixed.
- **Finance:** Cash savings begin in Q1 from non-payroll spend without upfront severance outflows; Year 1 net EBIT impact is higher and more predictable than Option B.
- **Operations:** Pausing low-value internal projects removes administrative load so teams absorb attrition without burnout.
- **Technology:** Consolidating overlapping SaaS licences, cloud spend, and external contractors cuts waste without stalling client-facing systems.
- **People:** Leadership takes the first cut on variable pay; staff see the real numbers early and keep clear career paths through internal redeployment.

## 5. Recommended Approach: Five Sequenced Levers

We recommend **Option C**, combining opex discipline with commercial margin protection, with a formal **Q2 checkpoint** before any structural headcount reduction is considered.

| Lever | Target (% of total opex) | Share of Base | Timing | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Non-payroll spend reset** (top-50 vendors, idle SaaS/cloud, external advisors/contractors with fair notice, facilities, travel) | **3.0%–4.5%** | 9.4%–14.1% of non-payroll | Q1–Q2 | CFO & CIO |
| **2. Stop low-value internal work** (pause non-essential internal initiatives, cut unused reporting/approvals) | **0.5%–1.0%** | Direct spend + freed capacity | Q1 | COO & Dept. Heads |
| **3. Selective hiring freeze, attrition & redeployment** (pause non-billable backfills; redeploy from low-utilisation teams into open billable roles) | **2.0%–3.4%** | 2.9%–5.0% of payroll | Q1–Q4 | CHRO & Delivery Leads |
| **Subtotal: Direct Opex Reduction** | **5.5%–8.9%** | — | **Full year** | — |
| **4. Commercial margin levers** (rate-card indexation, unbilled scope control, exit/reprice loss-making accounts) | **1.5%–3.0%** (EBIT equivalent) | Revenue / margin | Q1–Q3 | CCO / Sales Leads |
| **5. Leadership goes first** (freeze/reduce executive bonuses and perks until margin target is met) | **0.1%–0.3%** (trust signal) | Executive comp | Q1 | CEO & Executive Team |
| **Total Year 1 EBIT Impact (Opex + Commercial)** | **7.0%–11.9%** | — | **Full year** | — |

*Note: Individual lever subtotals for Direct Opex Reduction sum exactly: 3.0 + 0.5 + 2.0 = 5.5% (low) and 4.5 + 1.0 + 3.4 = 8.9% (high). Total EBIT impact excludes the 0.1%–0.3% leadership signal to stay conservative: 5.5 + 1.5 = 7.0% (low) and 8.9 + 3.0 = 11.9% (high).*

**Contingency (Q2 Gate):** If audited Q2 actuals show we are tracking below 6.0% run-rate EBIT improvement—or if client demand falls materially—we will evaluate voluntary exit options or targeted structural changes in H2, following full Dutch statutory consultation.

## 6. Human Impact and Dutch Legal Safeguards

*This section outlines key Dutch employment law obligations as of November 2026 for planning purposes. It is not legal advice; Dutch employment counsel and HR must review the final plan before any workforce decision is taken.*

| Area | Assessment & Safeguard |
|---|---|
| **Who is affected (Option C)** | Central/support teams face a hiring freeze and role consolidation through attrition; external contractors whose contracts end receive honest notice rather than abrupt termination; delivery teams are protected and gain internal mobility paths. |
| **Workload & retention risk** | Freezing backfills without cutting work causes burnout and drives top performers out. Every unfrozen role must be paired with explicit work stopped (Lever 2) and paid onboarding/reskilling time for redeployed staff. |
| **Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR)** | Mandatory for Dutch companies with 50+ employees. Under **Article 25 of the Works Councils Act (WOR)**, the OR has an advisory right on major organisational or financial changes, with a right of appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*). Involving the OR at the problem stage—with real numbers, before the budget is locked—reduces legal delay and surfaces operational waste leadership cannot see. |
| **Collective dismissal (WMCO)** | If any later H2 contingency affects **20 or more employees** in one UWV work area within three months (including employer-initiated settlement agreements), the **Collective Redundancy Notification Act (WMCO)** requires notifying UWV and relevant trade unions and consulting them *before* a decision is final. |
| **Notice & transition costs** | Statutory employer notice periods in the Netherlands run **1 to 4 months** by length of service (minus UWV procedure time), plus the statutory transition payment (*transitievergoeding*). Any future reorganisation budget must model these upfront cash costs explicitly. |
| **Monitoring & analytics tools** | If any new productivity or utilisation tracking software is introduced as part of cost control, **Article 27(1)(l) WOR** gives the OR a consent right over systems suited to monitoring employee attendance, behaviour, or performance. Reports must remain at team/project level, never individual activity rankings. |

## 7. Three-Week Preparation Plan and FY2027 Rollout

### Next 3 Weeks (Before the Board Meeting)
- **Week 1 (Data & Alignment):** Align privately with the CEO on the differentiated approach. Pull trailing 12-month GL non-payroll spend (top 50 vendors, SaaS, cloud, contractors, facilities), HRIS attrition by department, and PSA billable utilisation by practice.
- **Week 2 (Zero-Based Review & Early OR Briefing):** Run working sessions with cost centre heads to separate *delivery-protecting* spend from *overhead/waste*. Hold an informal, early briefing with the OR chair on the financial context and the goal of avoiding involuntary redundancies.
- **Week 3 (Board Paper & Pre-Mortem):** Finalise the verified numbers, run a 60-minute leadership pre-mortem ("It is December 2027 and this budget failed—why?"), and submit the board pack with clear Q1/Q2 milestones.

### FY2027 Execution & Review Rhythm
- **Q1 (Reset):** Lock non-payroll cuts, pause low-value internal projects, implement selective hiring freeze and executive bonus freeze, launch rate-card/scope reviews.
- **Q2 (Review Gate):** Audit realised savings, billable utilisation, voluntary attrition, and employee pulse/workload indicators. Decide whether H2 contingency measures are needed.
- **Q3–Q4 (Settle):** Maintain redeployment and reskilling support; review monthly against both financial KPIs (opex run-rate, gross margin, cash) and people KPIs (regrettable attrition, sickness absence, workload pulse).

## 8. Key Assumptions and What Would Change This Recommendation

- **Assumption 1:** Non-payroll spend (32% of opex) contains 9.4%–14.1% of reducible waste/vendor spend without harming client delivery.
  - *Would change our mind:* If the Week 1 vendor audit shows <5% is contractually reducible in FY2027, we must rely more heavily on commercial margin levers and faster attrition/redeployment.
- **Assumption 2:** Voluntary attrition is at least 10% annually and is spread broadly enough to allow internal redeployment into billable roles.
  - *Would change our mind:* If attrition has stalled (<5%) or is concentrated solely in scarce billable skills, a hiring freeze will not move payroll fast enough.
- **Assumption 3:** The board's underlying goal is margin resilience and cash safety, meaning commercial EBIT gains count alongside opex cuts.
  - *Would change our mind:* If a lender covenant or investor mandate strictly requires an 8% nominal opex reduction regardless of revenue, we need to state that constraint explicitly and model the H2 contingency earlier.
