# Board Paper Draft: Next-Year Budget Approach (8% Opex Equivalent Target)

> **Status:** Working draft for CEO alignment and Board pack preparation  
> **Note on numbers:** All euro figures below are **illustrative estimates** scaled to a 600-FTE Dutch B2B services cost base (€75.0M opex; 68% payroll at €85k fully loaded per FTE = €51.0M; 32% non-payroll = €24.0M; 8% target = €6.0M). Replace with actuals from the general ledger, aggregated payroll, vendor ledger, and CRM in Week 1.  
> **Legal & regulatory note:** References to Dutch employment and co-determination rules reflect the position as of November 2026 and are not legal advice. Confirm all thresholds, timelines, and obligations with Dutch employment counsel before acting.

## 1. Executive Recommendation

**Deliver the €6.0M (8% opex-equivalent) margin improvement through a sequenced, activity-based plan—zero-based non-payroll spend, pricing and billing-leakage discipline, selective hiring freeze with natural attrition and internal redeployment, and leadership pay restraint—rather than a blanket 8% cut across cost centres.**

On an illustrative €75.0M opex base:
- **Blanket 8% cut across cost centres** promises €6.0M annualised, but under Dutch consultation, notice, and transition-payment rules it is **cash-negative in Q1–Q3 (-€0.38M cumulative)**, nets only **+€0.27M in Year 1**, and nets **+€3.70M in Year 2** after contractor backfill, lost delivery margin, and elevated voluntary turnover.
- **Sequenced budget plan** delivers **+€4.75M net opex reduction** plus **+€1.50M pricing/leakage recovery** = **+€6.25M net EBITDA and cash in Year 1** (cash-positive from Q1), rising to **+€7.72M in Year 2** (including **€5.72M / 7.6% net opex reduction**), with **zero involuntary redundancies** and ~€1.1M of additional one-time cash headroom from receivables discipline.

## 2. Framing the Real Problem

"Cut opex by 8%" is a target, not a diagnosis. With a thin positive margin, the Board's underlying need is **sustainable EBITDA and cash headroom** so a 2–3% revenue dip does not push the business into loss or covenant pressure.

Why a blanket 8% per cost centre fails in a 600-person B2B services firm:
1. **The math forces headcount cuts in delivery.** Payroll is 68% of total opex—and typically 80–90% inside client-facing cost centres. Cutting 8% per cost centre means either cutting non-payroll by 25% (impossible in delivery units) or cutting ~48 FTEs across the board, hitting efficient revenue-generating teams at the same rate as bloated overhead.
2. **Dutch timing breaks Year 1 cash.** With 600 employees, a Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) is mandatory and holds an advisory right on reorganisations (art. 25 WOR), with appeal to the Enterprise Chamber if rushed. Dismissing 20+ employees within three months in one UWV region—including employer-initiated settlement agreements—triggers collective dismissal notification and union consultation (WMCO). Combined with statutory notice periods (1–4 months by tenure) and statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*) plus social plan and outplacement, cash goes out in Q1–Q3 while payroll savings arrive late in the year.
3. **The base rate on blanket cuts is poor.** Research shows downsizing is associated with lower subsequent profitability, particularly in low-capital-intensity, knowledge-intensive industries (Guthrie & Datta, *Organization Science*, 2008). Only ~10–12% of cost-reduction programmes sustain their gains after three years (McKinsey; Bain). A 1% downsizing is associated with 31% higher voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg), and replacing an employee costs 0.5× to 2× annual salary (Gallup).

## 3. Side-by-Side Options Model (Illustrative €M, Net Cash & P&L by Quarter)

*Assumptions (estimates): €75.0M opex (€51.0M payroll / 600 FTE @ €85k; €24.0M non-payroll). Target = €6.0M (8% of opex).*

### Option A: Do Nothing
- **Year 1 & Year 2 net effect:** €0.00M.
- **Risk:** Leaves thin margin exposed to any dip in utilisation, pricing pressure, or wage inflation. Fails the Board's resilience objective.

## 4. What We Protect, What Stops, and Leaders First

- **Protected investments (explicit criteria):** Client-facing billable capacity, mandatory training/certifications, security, and service quality. Any proposed cut to these lines must show its multi-year cost (churn, incident risk, rehiring) alongside the saving.
- **What stops or waits:** Low-value internal projects, duplicate SaaS/licences, discretionary external advisory/consultants, unapproved non-billable hiring, and office/travel overhead that does not support client delivery.
- **Headcount through flows:** Hiring freeze on non-billable roles with CEO/CFO exception sign-off; natural attrition (~48–72 exits/year at 8–12% normal turnover) absorbs ~22 overhead roles while billable and critical roles continue to be backfilled; internal mobility and reskilling fill priority gaps first.
- **Leaders first:** Executive cash bonuses are paused or converted into a multi-year deferred scorecard (EBITDA honestly defined with no "one-off" recurring charges, cash conversion, client retention, regretted attrition, and quality) before any employee-facing cost is touched. Executive perks and discretionary spend are cut in Q1.

## 6. Key Sensitivities & Triggers ("What Would Change This Budget")

- **Sensitivity 1 — Non-payroll capture:** If zero-based vendor savings hit 10% (€2.4M) instead of 12.5–15% (€3.0M–€3.6M), Year 1 net opex drops by €0.6M (still €5.65M total EBITDA with pricing).
- **Sensitivity 2 — Voluntary attrition rate:** If normal attrition slows from ~10% to ~6%, natural headcount reduction takes 2–3 quarters longer, shifting ~€0.5M of savings from Year 1 into Year 2.
- **Contingency triggers (agreed upfront with the Board):**
  - If Q1–Q2 revenue misses budget by >3% or 13-week cash headroom falls below minimum threshold, trigger Phase 2 levers in order: deeper discretionary freeze → temporary voluntary reduced hours / sabbatical options → voluntary exit scheme → only if structural demand has permanently fallen, a targeted reorganisation designed with the OR and counsel.

## 7. Governance & Monthly Scorecard

Review both financial and people indicators together every month so savings do not hide future costs:
- **Money:** 13-week cash forecast vs minimum headroom, EBITDA (reported and reconciled—no recurring restructuring labelled "one-off"), opex run-rate by lever, DSO, and realised rate/leakage recovery.
- **Customers & People:** Net revenue retention, client satisfaction/escalations, billable utilisation, regretted voluntary attrition, time-to-fill on critical roles, and sickness absence.
