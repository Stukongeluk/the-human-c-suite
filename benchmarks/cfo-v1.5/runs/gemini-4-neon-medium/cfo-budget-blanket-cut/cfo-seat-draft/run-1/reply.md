### Why the blanket 8% trap breaks your numbers

A blanket 8% across every cost centre in a 68%-payroll B2B services firm is a headcount cut in disguise—it cuts efficient, billable teams at the same rate as bloated overhead, and in the Netherlands the cash leaves before the savings arrive.

Your CEO's instinct makes sense on the surface: an across-the-board percentage is fast, looks fair on paper, avoids horse-trading between department heads, and gives the board a clean headline in three weeks. **Have a private conversation with the CEO this week before circulating anything**, using the quarterly cash math to show why the shortcut puts *their* margin and credibility at risk:

- **Dutch timing kills Year 1 savings.** At 600 employees, your Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) has an advisory right on reorganisations (art. 25 WOR), and 20+ dismissals within three months in one UWV region—including employer-initiated settlement agreements—triggers collective dismissal rules (WMCO). Add 1–4 months' statutory notice and statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*) plus social plan/outplacement, and payroll savings land in Q3–Q4 while cash leaves in Q1–Q3. *(Not legal advice—confirm thresholds and timelines with Dutch employment counsel.)*
- **The base rate is poor.** Downsizing is associated with lower subsequent profitability in knowledge-intensive, low-capital-intensity industries (Guthrie & Datta, 2008), only ~10–12% of cost programmes stick after three years (McKinsey; Bain), and a 1% downsizing is associated with 31% higher voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg), at 0.5×–2× salary to replace (Gallup).
