# Board Business Case: AI Automation in Finance & Back-Office

**Prepared for:** Board of Directors · **Prepared by:** CFO · **Date:** November 2026  
**Scope:** Finance & Back-Office (90 FTEs: Accounts Payable, Reconciliations, Reporting)  
**Status:** Draft for Board Discussion — *All financial figures below are illustrative estimates pending internal ERP/HRIS actuals and employment-counsel review.*

---

## Executive Recommendation

**Do not approve a 30% (27-FTE) forced headcount reduction for next year.** On realistic Swedish timelines and implementation costs, a Year 1 redundancy programme delivers an estimated **-4.4 MSEK net cash impact in Year 1** (not a saving) and exposes regulated Solvency II / IFRS 17 reporting to avoidable control failure and rehiring regret.

**Approve Option 2 instead:** a 24–36 month programme combining immediate non-headcount cost cleanup, phased AI automation co-designed with the team and unions, a hiring freeze in routine roles, natural attrition (~16 FTEs / ~18% over 3 years), and targeted reskilling into higher-value finance work. It is **cash-neutral in Year 1 (0.0 MSEK)**, delivers **+22.7 MSEK cumulative net cash over 3 years** (+15.9 MSEK/yr steady-state run-rate by Year 4), and preserves the institutional knowledge required for regulated insurance operations.

---

## 1. The Problem We Are Solving

The Board has asked whether AI can remove 30% of our 90-person finance and back-office team next year to improve our expense ratio. Before committing to a headcount target, we need to separate two distinct questions:

1. **What is the financial objective?** Improving the expense ratio and removing manual toil in AP, reconciliations, and reporting—without compromising close accuracy, audit trails, or Finansinspektionen / Solvency II compliance.
2. **What can AI realistically deliver, and when?** Automating tasks is not the same as removing whole jobs in Year 1, and in Sweden, forced redundancies carry legal timelines and selection rules that push cash savings out of Year 1 while pulling costs forward.

## 2. Why "30% Next Year" Breaks on Reality

### A. Task-Level Feasibility (Tasks ≠ Whole Jobs)

AI delivers real gains on repetitive tasks, but regulated insurance finance retains significant human accountability:

- **Accounts Payable:** High automation potential for standard invoice capture and PO matching. Exceptions, supplier disputes, fraud checks, and approval bottlenecks remain human.
- **Reconciliations:** AI can accelerate matching and flag breaks across policy administration, claims, reinsurance, and the general ledger. Investigating root causes and signing off balances requires institutional knowledge.
- **Reporting (Solvency II, IFRS 17, Finansinspektionen, Board):** AI can automate data pulls and draft commentary, but legal accountability stays with the company and its officers (cf. *Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149, confirming firms remain liable for AI errors). Under Solvency II and the EU Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), automated reporting pipelines require documented validation, audit trails, and human sign-off.
- **New work created by AI:** Exception handling, rule/model maintenance, audit evidence, and quality assurance absorb part of the gross hours saved. Across enterprise deployments, realistic net capacity freed in production is **12–20% over 18–24 months**, not 30% in Year 1.

### B. External Base Rates

- **Implementation risk:** Gartner (June 2025) forecasts that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 due to escalating costs, unclear value, or inadequate risk controls.
- **Regret and rehiring:** In a Careerminds survey of 600 HR leaders (February 2026), two in three companies that cut jobs for AI were already rehiring (52% within six months), and 30.9% reported rehiring costs exceeded savings. In an Orgvue survey of 1,000+ senior leaders (2025), 55% of those who made AI-driven redundancies said they made the wrong decision. *(Note: both are vendor surveys; treat as directional signals, not precise probabilities.)*
- **Quality reversal:** Klarna announced in February 2024 that its AI handled the work of 700 agents and shrank headcount ~22% largely via attrition, then resumed hiring human agents in May 2025 after CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski noted cost had dominated over quality (Bloomberg, May 2025).
- **Redeployment upside:** Ingka Group (IKEA) used AI to handle 47% of call-centre queries and retrained ~8,500 staff into remote advisory roles rather than cutting them, generating ~€1.3 billion in 2022 (Reuters, 2023; Forrester, 2026).

### C. Swedish Employment Law & Timeline Reality

*(Summary checked November 2026; not legal advice—must be verified with Swedish employment counsel and HR before any action.)*

- **Co-determination (MBL):** Mandatory union negotiation *before* deciding on major reorganisations or introducing AI tools that materially change work. Allow 1–2 months.
- **Advance notice to Arbetsförmedlingen (*varsel*):** Dismissing **26 to 100 employees** requires at least **4 months'** advance notice (compared with 2 months for 5–25 employees). Cutting 27 roles crosses the 4-month threshold.
- **Selection order (LAS *turordning*):** Statutory redundancy order follows length of service per operational unit and collective agreement area—not job category or individual performance. Since 2022, employers may exempt only three employees unless a negotiated list (*avtalsturlista*) is agreed with the union. Without a negotiated agreement (which typically requires paid concessions), forced cuts remove the newest, often most digitally fluent staff while keeping senior staff who must be retrained anyway.
- **Notice periods & re-employment priority:** Statutory and collective-agreement notice runs **1 to 6 months** by tenure (often longer in insurance collective agreements for older employees). Dismissed staff with >12 months' service in the last 3 years hold **9 months' priority for re-employment** (*företrädesrätt*).
- **EU AI Act:** High-risk workplace AI obligations (Article 26(7), duty to inform worker representatives) apply from **2 December 2027** following the 2026 Digital Omnibus—not yet in force, though Swedish MBL already requires consultation today.
- **Net timing effect:** MBL + 4-month *varsel* + individual notice means 27 forced exits leave payroll roughly **6 to 9 months into Year 1** (modeled below as 4 months off payroll in Year 1), while AI build, legal, and severance costs hit upfront.

### Arithmetic Breakdown (MSEK)

- **Option 1:** Year 1: +1.8 vendor/licence savings (H2) - 0.5 config = **+1.3**. Years 2–3: **+3.5/yr**. Total: 1.3 + 3.5 + 3.5 = **+8.3**.
- **Option 2 (Recommended):**
  - *Year 1:* +1.8 vendor/licence savings + 2.7 attrition (6 routine exits not backfilled, avg 6 mos) - 3.5 staged AI pilot/AP/recon rollout - 1.0 reskilling = **0.0**.
  - *Year 2:* +3.5 vendor/licence + 8.1 attrition (6 prior FTEs full yr = 5.4; 6 new FTEs half yr = 2.7) - 2.5 AI expansion & licences - 0.5 reskilling = **+8.6**.
  - *Year 3:* +3.5 vendor/licence + 12.6 attrition (12 prior FTEs full yr = 10.8; 4 new FTEs half yr = 1.8) - 2.0 ongoing AI licences/audit = **+14.1**.
  - *3-Year Total:* 0.0 + 8.6 + 14.1 = **+22.7** (Year 4 steady-state run-rate: 3.5 + 14.4 - 2.0 = **+15.9 MSEK/yr**, excluding upside from claims/reinsurance recovery and avoided growth hiring).
- **Option 3a (Forced 30% — Base Case):**
  - *Year 1:* +8.1 gross payroll saved (27 FTEs × 0.90 × 4/12 mos) - 6.0 big-bang AI build/licences/DORA validation - 4.5 severance/*avtalsturlista*/legal - 2.0 productivity dip/contractor cover = **-4.4**.
  - *Years 2–3:* +24.3 payroll (27 × 0.90) - 2.5 AI run-rate - 3.6 AI oversight/exceptions (4 FTEs) = **+18.2/yr**.
  - *3-Year Total:* -4.4 + 18.2 + 18.2 = **+32.0**.
- **Option 3b (Forced 30% — Downside / Regret Case, if AI frees 18% net and 9 FTEs must be rehired/contracted at 1.0 MSEK/yr):**
  - *Year 1:* **-4.4**.
  - *Year 2:* +24.3 - 2.5 AI - 9.0 (9 FTEs/contractors) - 1.5 recruiting/onboarding = **+11.3**.
  - *Year 3:* +24.3 - 2.5 AI - 9.0 (9 FTEs ongoing) = **+12.8**.
  - *3-Year Total:* -4.4 + 11.3 + 12.8 = **+19.7**.

### View From Each Seat

- **Shareholders / Finance:** Option 2 protects Year 1 cash (+4.4 MSEK better than Option 3), avoids sunk severance and rehiring risk, and still captures ~16 MSEK/yr recurring savings by Year 4.
- **Operations & Risk / Compliance:** Option 2 keeps Solvency II, IFRS 17, and DORA controls intact during transition; Option 3 risks audit findings and late/restated filings.
- **Technology:** Option 2 funds staged integration on real data rather than a high-failure big-bang rollout.
- **People & Unions:** Option 2 aligns with Swedish MBL co-determination and avoids the survivor turnover spike seen after layoffs (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008: 1% downsizing associated with 31% higher voluntary turnover the next year).

## 4. Recommended Path (Option 2)

1. **Immediate non-headcount savings (Months 1–3):** Audit finance software licences, external consultants, and recurring reports nobody reads; target 3.5 MSEK/yr run-rate savings.
2. **Early union & team co-design (Months 1–2):** Bring union representatives (MBL) and frontline AP/reconciliation/reporting staff into the problem framing before selecting tools. The OECD (2023) finds AI adoption produces better outcomes when worker representatives are consulted early. Give a clear Year 1 commitment: *no forced redundancies from this automation; roles will change*.
3. **Pilot before scaling (Months 3–6):** Deploy AI first on high-volume AP invoice processing and automated reconciliation drafting. Measure net hours freed (accounting for exceptions and review time) and error rates before expanding to reporting.
4. **Hiring freeze + natural attrition + redeployment (Months 1–36):** Stop backfilling routine roles as natural attrition (~7 FTEs/yr) occurs. Reskill staff into exception management, AI control/audit, reinsurance recovery, and claims-leakage analysis—using paid learning time and, where employees wish to retrain more deeply, Sweden's *omställningsstudiestöd* (public transition study support covering ~80% of salary up to a ceiling; nearly 21,000 users in 2025 per CSN).
5. **Stage-gate at Month 18:** Only if production data shows verified excess capacity beyond attrition and valuable redeployment should the Board consider voluntary exits (backed by TRR job security council support from day one; OECD 2015 found >85% of displaced Swedish workers find new work within a year).

## 7. Pre-Mortem: "It Is November 2027 and This Failed. Why?"

1. **AI handled only the easy 60% of invoices/breaks; exceptions swamped the remaining team.** *Mitigation:* Measure net hours after exception handling in Months 3–6 before expanding.
2. **A Solvency II or IFRS 17 reporting error slipped through an AI-drafted workflow, triggering a Finansinspektionen query.** *Mitigation:* Keep mandatory human sign-off and parallel runs for all regulatory filings.
3. **Senior staff withheld edge-case knowledge because they feared automating themselves out of a job.** *Mitigation:* Pair early union involvement with an explicit Year 1 commitment against automation-driven forced layoffs.
4. **Top performers left during uncertainty, leaving us short of both domain and technical skills.** *Mitigation:* Transparent communication, visible internal career paths, and zero surprise restructurings.
5. **AI vendor licences and integration overran budget while savings lagged.** *Mitigation:* Staged spend capped at 3.5 MSEK in Year 1; no multi-year enterprise lock-in until pilot ROI is proven.

## 8. Governance & Dual Scorecard (Reviewed Quarterly)

We will track financial and people/quality indicators together—and **never use AI tools to rank or monitor individual employees** (team-level workflow metrics only):

- **Financial:** Net cash impact vs. plan (MSEK), finance expense ratio, total AI spend (licences + integration + maintenance), non-payroll savings realised.
- **Quality & Risk:** Days to monthly/quarterly close, reconciliation break aging, AP late-payment rate, audit findings / regulatory exceptions (target: zero).
- **People & Capacity:** Voluntary attrition (overall and among critical roles), sickness absence, paid training hours completed, quarterly pulse on workload and psychological safety.

## 9. Key Assumptions & What Would Change This Recommendation

- **Assumption 1:** The company is solvent and cash-positive, and this is an expense-ratio / competitiveness initiative rather than an immediate liquidity crisis. *Would change my mind:* An acute solvency or cash shortfall requiring emergency cost reductions across the entire company (starting with executive pay and discretionary spend before frontline roles).
- **Assumption 2:** Natural attrition in the 90-person team is ~6–10%/yr. *Would change my mind:* If attrition is <3%/yr, organic reduction takes longer and voluntary exit packages (Option 2 + Month 18 voluntary window) become more relevant.
- **Assumption 3:** Actual FTE fully loaded cost is ~0.90 MSEK/yr and AP/reconciliations represent the majority of the 90 FTEs. *Would change my mind:* Actual HRIS/ERP data will refine the exact MSEK figures before final approval.
