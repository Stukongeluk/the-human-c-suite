**Subject:** Capital Allocation, Activist Buyback Proposal (€300M), and Omnichannel & Store Service Investment (€120M)

## 1. Executive Recommendation

**Recommend against the €300M share buyback, approve a stage-gated €120M investment in the e-commerce platform and store service model subject to hurdle-rate and milestone gates, ring-fence a prudent seasonal liquidity buffer, and return only genuine excess cash.**

Doing both the €300M buyback and the €120M investment costs **€420M**, putting the business into **-€20M net debt** with zero operating buffer. Doing the €300M buyback alone leaves **€100M** in net cash—thin for a seasonal consumer electronics retailer—while starving the core business and using balance-sheet cash to manufacture a few cents of EPS.

## 3. Why Not the €300M Buyback (and Why Not Buy EPS)?

1. **Buybacks do not fix operating performance.** Spending €300M to retire shares to cover a few cents of EPS shortfall is financial engineering, not value creation. Research on US listed firms found that companies that would just have missed analysts' EPS forecasts were sharply more likely to repurchase shares—and those EPS-driven buybacks were associated with subsequent cuts in employment and investment (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, 2016).
2. **Retail needs a seasonal working-capital buffer.** Consumer electronics carries thin gross margins, rapid product obsolescence, warranty/return liabilities, and sharp Q4/holiday inventory peaks. Reducing net cash to €100M (Option A) or -€20M (Option B) leaves inadequate protection against a supplier-terms tightening or a soft trading quarter.
3. **Market credibility.** Listed shareholders reward durable cash generation and honest guidance. Pre-announcing or explaining a minor EPS miss transparently—while showing a disciplined capital-allocation framework—protects long-term valuation better than a one-year share-count boost. *(Note: any guidance update must be reviewed with securities counsel and corporate brokers under applicable market-abuse and disclosure rules.)*

---

## 4. Why Stage-Gate the COO's €120M (Not a Blank Cheque)

The activist is right about one thing: retail IT and transformation programmes often overrun and under-deliver. The €120M should be **approved in principle, funded in stages**, and held to clear hurdle rates (cost of capital plus execution risk):

- **Phase 1 (€30M–€40M, *estimate*):** Core e-commerce architecture fixes, checkout/fulfilment pain points, and a controlled pilot of the new store service model in a representative set of stores.
  - **Design with store staff, not just consultants:** Store colleagues use the POS, stock, and service tools every shift; involve them on the floor before locking in specifications, and plan offline resilience and roll-outs outside peak trading.
  - **Pair training with stable operations:** Evidence from retail field experiments shows that operational stability—such as predictable scheduling in a 28-store randomised trial at Gap—raised median sales by 7% and labour productivity by 5% (Williams, Lambert, Kesavan et al., 2018), consistent with research on high-performing retailers that invest in cross-trained store teams (Ton, 2014).
- **Phase 2 & 3 (€80M–€90M, *estimate*):** Unlocked only when Phase 1 hits pre-agreed gates: conversion lift, basket/attachment margin, return-rate reduction, store-staff time saved, and customer satisfaction.

---

## 5. Returning Genuine Excess Cash & Aligning Incentives

- **Return only what is truly excess:** Once treasury confirms the minimum operating cash and peak-to-trough working-capital buffer (e.g., €180M *illustrative*) and Phase 1–3 capital is ring-fenced (€120M), any remaining cash (e.g., €100M *illustrative*) can be returned via a sustainable dividend or a smaller, non-EPS-contingent buyback—provided the board has valid shareholder authority.
- **Remove the bonus conflict:** If executive bonuses are tied to this year's EPS target, management has a personal financial stake in the buyback. Recommend that the Remuneration Committee explicitly exclude share-count effects from this year's bonus calculation and ensure leadership pay takes the first hit on any guidance miss.
- **Consultation & legal checks:** Involve employee representatives/works councils early where store roles, schedules, or training models change; consult tax, legal, and corporate advisers before any buyback or guidance announcement. *(This memo is financial analysis for board discussion, not legal, tax, or investment advice.)*

3. No hidden debt maturities, lease-covenant triggers, or pension commitments that further reduce excess cash.
