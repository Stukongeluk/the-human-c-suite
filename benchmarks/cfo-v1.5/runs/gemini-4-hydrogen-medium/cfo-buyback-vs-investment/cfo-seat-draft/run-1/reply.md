**Recommend against the €300 million buyback; instead, approve a stage-gated €120 million investment in e-commerce and store service, ring-fence a prudent seasonal liquidity buffer, and return only genuine excess cash.**

Doing the €300M buyback alone leaves **€100M** in net cash—thin for a thin-margin, seasonal consumer electronics retailer—while starving the business and spending €300M to manufacture a few cents of EPS.

### Why This Is the Right Call

1. **Never buy EPS with balance-sheet cash.** Retiring shares to close a few cents of EPS guidance creates zero operating value. Empirical research (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, 2016) shows that firms repurchasing shares just to avoid missing EPS forecasts subsequently cut employment and investment. Take the guidance miss honestly—checking disclosure timing and Market Abuse Regulation obligations with securities counsel and your corporate brokers—and make sure executive bonuses exclude any share-count effect so leadership takes the miss first.
2. **Steelman the activist—then answer them.** The activist has a fair point: €400M sitting in cash is lazy capital, and retail IT projects have a poor track record of blowing through €120M. Answer that by **stage-gating** the €120M: release Phase 1 (e.g., €30M–€40M *estimate*) for core e-commerce fixes and a store-service pilot, and unlock Phases 2–3 only when conversion, attachment margin, and return rates clear your hurdle rate. Return the remaining genuine excess cash (e.g., ~€100M *illustrative*) via a dividend or modest, non-EPS-driven buyback once treasury confirms the seasonal trough.
3. **Human and operational impact.** Don't let the €120M become a consultant-led IT rewrite that store staff hate using. Bring store colleagues and managers into designing the service tools on the floor, plan roll-outs outside peak trading, and involve employee representatives/works councils early if schedules or roles change. Retail field evidence (Williams, Lambert, Kesavan et al., 2018 at Gap; Zeynep Ton, *The Good Jobs Strategy*) shows that investing in well-trained, predictably scheduled store teams lifts sales and productivity rather than just adding cost.
