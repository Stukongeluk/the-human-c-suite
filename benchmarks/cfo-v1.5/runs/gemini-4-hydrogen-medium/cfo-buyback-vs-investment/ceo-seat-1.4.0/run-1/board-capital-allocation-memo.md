# Board Capital Allocation Memo: Net Cash Deployment (€400M)

**Prepared by:** Chief Financial Officer  
**For:** Board of Directors  
**Decision:** Capital allocation across liquidity reserve, omnichannel & store-service investment, and shareholder returns  

---

## 1. The Decision in One Sentence

We have **€400 million** in net cash and competing demands totalling **€420 million** (€300M buyback + €120M operational investment); the board must decide whether to spend cash on a one-year EPS target or fund a disciplined turnaround of our core retail engine while protecting balance-sheet resilience.

## 2. Recommendation

**Decline the €300M buyback this year, own the small EPS guidance miss openly, and approve a stage-gated €120M investment envelope starting with a €25M Phase 1 pilot—while ringing off €200M as an operating liquidity floor and reserving up to €80M for a conditional capital return.**

Specifically:
1. **Do not buy back €300M of shares to hit EPS guidance.** Retiring shares to close a gap of a few cents creates no operating value and consumes 75% of our net cash. In Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal's (2005) survey of 401 financial executives, 78% said they would sacrifice economic value to smooth earnings; this board should not make that trade.
2. **Do not write a single €120M cheque upfront.** Big-bang IT rebuilds and simultaneous store rollouts carry high execution risk (Flyvbjerg's database of 16,000+ major projects shows only 8.5% hit budget and schedule, and 0.5% deliver promised benefits). Approve **€25M for Phase 1** (6–9 months) to fix high-friction e-commerce bottlenecks and pilot the new service model and paid staff training in a representative group of stores. Release the remaining **€95M** only against agreed conversion, service, and staff-readiness gates.
3. **Set an explicit €200M liquidity floor and address idle cash.** Holding €400M with no stated floor invites activist pressure. Publishing a clear capital-allocation framework—**€200M** working-capital and downside buffer, up to **€120M** in gated high-return reinvestment, and **€80M** available for disciplined shareholder returns once peak seasonal working capital is confirmed—answers the activist's legitimate point about idle cash without jeopardising the business.

## 5. Operational, Technology, and People Safeguards

Before releasing Phase 1 funds, management should confirm three checks across Operations, Technology, and People:

- **Technology before a full rebuild:** Have the CTO and Product team verify whether the €120M assumes a ground-up platform replacement or whether targeted fixes (search, checkout latency, real-time store inventory visibility, returns flow) capture most of the conversion gain at lower cost and risk.
- **Co-design on the store floor:** Store staff and store managers use these systems every shift. Pilot the new service model and tooling with a small group of stores first, fund paid training time and shift backfill (rather than squeezing existing rosters), and involve employee representatives / works councils early where applicable.
- **No hidden cuts to fund the buyback:** Explicitly rule out funding share repurchases by trimming store hours, training budgets, or maintenance. In retail, under-staffing and unpredictable schedules show up directly as lost sales and higher turnover.

## 6. Investor & Market Communication

1. **Own the EPS miss plainly:** State that management is accountable for the few cents of EPS shortfall and explain the operational drivers without blaming external noise.
2. **Publish the capital-allocation framework:** Show investors the exact math (€200M liquidity floor, €120M stage-gated investment with clear ROIC/conversion hurdles, and €80M earmarked for shareholder returns as milestones are met). Long-term shareholders back transparent discipline over financial engineering.

## 7. Pre-Mortem: What Would Make This Wrong?

- **Assumption:** The EPS miss is small and temporary, and investors will accept a clear capital-allocation plan over a buyback. *Would change my mind:* Debt covenants or working-capital lines are far tighter than assumed, or the core business is structurally unprofitable even after e-commerce fixes.
- **Assumption:** €200M is the right liquidity floor for our seasonal inventory cycle. *Would change my mind:* Treasury's peak-to-trough working-capital swing is under €100M (which would allow a larger conditional return) or over €250M (which would require pausing any buyback).
- **Assumption:** Phase 1 pilot results will translate across the store estate. *Would change my mind:* Pilot stores fail to show a measurable lift in conversion, attachment rate, or customer retention within 6 months—in which case we halt the remaining €95M rather than sinking it.
