Recommend that the board **reject the €300M buyback this year, own the small EPS miss openly, and approve a stage-gated €120M investment envelope starting with a €25M Phase 1 pilot—while ringing off €200M as an operating liquidity floor and reserving up to €80M for a conditional capital return.**

The trap here is simple: spending €300M of net cash to close an EPS gap of a few cents buys an optical win for one quarter at the cost of your balance-sheet resilience—and €300M + €120M (€420M) exceeds your €400M net cash anyway.

### Why this beats both extremes

- **Don't fund the €300M buyback:** Retiring shares doesn't fix e-commerce conversion or store service, and in consumer electronics—where inventory cycles and thin margins can swing working capital fast—dropping to €100M cash (or taking on debt to fund the COO) leaves no margin for error. Own the few-cent EPS miss plainly as leadership's responsibility rather than paying €300M to mask it.
- **Don't write a single €120M cheque upfront either:** Big-bang platform rebuilds and all-at-once store rollouts rarely land on budget or deliver their promised lift. Approve **€25M for Phase 1** (6–9 months) to fix the highest-friction e-commerce bottlenecks and pilot the new service model in a subset of stores, with the remaining **€95M** gated on real conversion, attachment, and staff-readiness metrics.
- **Steelman the activist's real point:** Sitting on €400M of unexplained idle cash *does* invite activist pressure. Setting an explicit **€200M liquidity floor** and earmarking up to **€80M** for a disciplined buyback or dividend once peak seasonal working capital is verified shows the market capital discipline without starving the business.

### Two checks before the board meets

1. **Technology:** Have your CTO and Product lead verify whether €120M actually requires a ground-up e-commerce rebuild, or whether targeted fixes (search, checkout latency, real-time store stock visibility) capture most of the gain faster and cheaper.
2. **Stores & people:** Make sure the COO's budget includes **paid training time and shift backfill** rather than squeezing existing store rosters, co-design the tools with store staff in the pilot, and involve employee representatives or works councils early where applicable.
