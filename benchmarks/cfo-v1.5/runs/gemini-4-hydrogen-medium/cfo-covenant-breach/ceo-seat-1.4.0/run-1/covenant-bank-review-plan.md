# Q2 Covenant & Bank Review Plan (Draft for Owner & Finance)

**Context:** Family-owned furniture manufacturer (Netherlands, 260 employees). Forecast Q2 leverage is **3.6x** against a **3.5x** covenant limit (~2.8% gap). Bank review is in **3 weeks**.

---

## 1. Why a 10% Job Cut Announcement Backfires Before Q2

Announcing 26 redundancies before the bank meeting will **worsen** Q2 cash and leverage rather than fix them:

- **Cash goes out before savings arrive:** Under Dutch law, with 260 employees a Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, mandatory from 50 employees) has an advisory right on reorganisations (Article 25 WOR). Cutting 20+ roles within three months also triggers collective redundancy notification and consultation with the UWV and unions (WMCO). Add 1–4 months of statutory employer notice and statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*), and Q2 takes upfront severance and advisory costs with zero payroll relief before quarter-end. *(Note: this is not legal advice; confirm timelines and obligations with Dutch employment counsel.)*
- **Banks distrust theatre:** Credit committees look for cash visibility, covenant headroom, and owner commitment—not an unconsulted headcount target that creates Works Council and UWV execution risk.
- **Irreversible skill loss:** In furniture manufacturing, skilled CNC operators, finishers, and upholsterers take years to replace. Cutting 10% across the board risks hollowing out delivery capacity just as orders recover.

| **D. Targeted restructuring (only if demand decline is structural)** | **Negative Q2, positive Q4+** | 3–6 months | **One-way door.** Must follow WOR/WMCO consultation | High; only justified if B+C cannot restore sustainable margin |

## 3. Recommended 3-Week Plan for the Bank Review

### Week 1: Align Privately with the Owner
1. **Show the Q2 math:** Demonstrate that 26 redundancies will *increase* Q2 cash burn (transition payments + notice) and miss the Q2 covenant anyway, whereas reducing Net Debt by ~3% via working capital and capex deferral hits Q2 directly.
2. **Leaders go first:** Agree to pause/cut the owner's dividend or draw and freeze executive bonuses/pay increases before touching shop-floor jobs. If needed, explore a temporary subordinated shareholder loan or equity cure as a backstop.
3. **Engage Dutch employment counsel** quietly on any contingent scenarios so you never step outside WOR or WMCO rules.

### Week 2: Build the Bank Pack (What Credit Committees Actually Want)
1. **13-week rolling cash flow forecast** + Q2–Q4 covenant bridge (base case and downside case).
2. **Immediate Net Debt & EBITDA levers (quantified):**
   - Inventory and WIP reduction (raw timber/board, fabrics, slow-moving finished stock).
   - Tighter receivables collection and supplier term alignment.
   - Deferral of non-safety, non-maintenance capex.
   - Hiring freeze, natural attrition, and flexing agency/overtime spend.
   - Pricing/mix review on low-margin SKUs and scrap/rework reduction on the floor.
3. **Clear trigger points:** Show the bank what further steps (e.g. reduced hours or formal restructuring with the Works Council) are pre-agreed *only if* the downside case materialises.

### Week 3: Bank Meeting & Works Council Transparency
- **At the bank:** Present the forecast, the owner's financial commitment, and the immediate cash levers. Ask for a **temporary Q2 covenant waiver or reset (e.g., to 3.75x–4.0x for two quarters)** as a bridge, not a rescue.
- **With the Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*):** Share the financial reality honestly at the problem stage—before any irreversible decisions are made—so they can help spot operational waste and shape any needed adjustments.

---

## 4. Indicators to Track Monthly

- **Financial:** Weekly cash balance vs. 13-week forecast, Net Debt / EBITDA trajectory, inventory days, scrap/rework cost.
- **People & Operations:** Voluntary attrition, sickness absence, on-time delivery, safety incidents/near-misses (cutbacks must never compromise plant safety).
