### Why Option 1 Fails the Board's Own Test
- **Swedish labour law sets the clock:** MBL negotiations must happen before the decision, dismissing 26–100 staff requires **4 months' notice** to *Arbetsförmedlingen*, and individual LAS notice runs **1–6 months**. Under LAS *turordning* (last-in, first-out, with 3 exemptions unless a list is agreed with the union), you also cannot simply pick which 27 roles leave.
- **Insurance controls don't automate overnight:** AP invoice matching and bank/sub-ledger reconciliations are strong candidates for AI *if* ERP feeds are clean, but Solvency II / IFRS 17 reporting, DORA controls, and payment approvals still require segregation of duties and human sign-off.
- **Rehiring and survivor turnover wipe out the saving:** Vendor surveys from Careerminds (2026) and Orgvue (2025) show two-thirds of firms that cut jobs for AI were already rehiring (52% within six months), and 55% of leaders regretted the cuts. Trevor & Nyberg (2008) found a 1% downsizing was linked to 31% higher voluntary turnover the next year—just 6 extra voluntary exits in your reporting/actuarial-finance team would keep Option 1 net-negative past Year 2.

### What Option 2 Does Instead (Human & Operational Impact)
1. **Non-payroll spend first:** Audit finance SaaS licences, duplicate tools, advisory spillover, and manual bank/print fees (+0.90 MSEK in Y1, +1.20 MSEK/year ongoing).
2. **Stage-gated AI pilots in AP & Reconciliations:** Measure hours freed against a baseline before reducing capacity, keeping 100% human sign-off on payments and regulatory close.
3. **Natural attrition & redeployment:** Pair a selective hiring freeze with internal reskilling (and TRR / *omställningsstudiestöd* where helpful) so ~10 FTEs of normal turnover in Year 1 and ~8 in Year 2 are absorbed safely without compulsory redundancies or panic exits. Bring the union in at the design stage under MBL.
