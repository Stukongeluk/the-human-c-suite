# Budget Approach: 8% Opex Reduction Without Cutting Revenue Capacity

**Prepared for:** Board of Directors & Executive Team · **Owner:** CFO · **Board Meeting:** In 3 weeks · **Jurisdiction:** Netherlands (~600 FTE)

---

## 1. The Problem in One Sentence

The board requires an 8% opex reduction for next year to strengthen our thin operating margin, while a flat 8% cut across every cost centre risks cutting billable delivery capacity, reducing revenue, and triggering front-loaded severance costs and statutory consultation delays.

---

## 3. Options Considered

| Option | Opex Effect (Yr 1) | Time to Effect | People & Customer Impact | Hidden Costs & Risks | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Do nothing** | 0% | — | No disruption, but margin remains thin and vulnerable to any revenue dip. | Fails board mandate; leaves no buffer for shocks. | Yes |
| **2. Flat 8% cut per cost centre** | -8.0% gross; **~4.5%–6.0% net in Y1** | 6–9 months (after consultation & notice) | Cuts billable and non-billable teams equally (~48 FTEs if taken in headcount). Service delays, client churn, survivor attrition. | Statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*), 1–4 month notice periods, Works Council (OR) appeal risk, and lost revenue from cut delivery capacity. | No (one-way door) |
| **3. Sequenced & differentiated plan (Recommended)** | **-7.1% to -8.9%** (8.0% midpoint) | 1–3 months for non-payroll; 3–12 months for payroll | Protects billable delivery and client quality. Payroll reduction achieved via selective hiring freeze, natural attrition, and reskilling/redeployment. Leaders take the first cut on bonuses/perks. | Requires tighter central governance on hiring and vendor spend; risk of under-delivering if attrition is below estimate (mitigated by Q2 trigger). | Mostly yes |

### View Across Functions (Option 3)
- **Finance:** Protects cash flow in Year 1 by avoiding upfront severance cash-out while still hitting the 8% run-rate target.
- **Operations / Delivery:** Keeps client-facing capacity intact; removes low-value internal reporting and non-billable toil instead of cutting delivery heads.
- **People / HR:** Avoids forced redundancies as a first move; maintains trust and retention among high performers.
- **Commercial:** Pairs opex discipline with pricing and scope-creep reviews on thin-margin accounts so margin improves from both sides.

## 4. How the Recommended Plan Works

1. **Leadership restraint first (signal of shared ownership):** Freeze executive bonuses and discretionary leadership perks before asking any team to tighten its belt.
2. **Aggressive non-payroll reset (-12.5% to -15.0% on 32% of opex = -4.0% to -4.8% total opex):**
   - Audit and cancel idle SaaS licences, duplicate tooling, and unused cloud/infra capacity.
   - Renegotiate top 20 vendor contracts and consolidate external agency/consultancy spend (giving fair notice where contracts end).
   - Trim discretionary travel, events, and underused office/facilities costs.
3. **Selective hiring freeze, natural attrition & redeployment (-4.5% to -6.0% on 68% of opex = -3.1% to -4.1% total opex):**
   - Freeze all non-billable and overhead backfills; require CFO + functional head sign-off for any replacement hire.
   - Let natural attrition reduce headcount in lower-demand or administrative areas, and fund paid reskilling to redeploy bench/overhead staff into billable demand.
   - Cut internal low-value projects and redundant reporting so existing teams have capacity without overtime.
4. **Margin protection (commercial lever):**
   - Review bottom-quartile margin client contracts for underpricing, unbilled scope creep, and renewal uplifts.
5. **Pre-agreed Q2 contingency trigger:**
   - If non-payroll savings and natural attrition track below the 8% run-rate by end of Q2, leadership will evaluate targeted role reductions in specific declining or non-billable areas—never across-the-board—with full Works Council consultation.

## 6. Dutch Legal, Works Council & People Safeguards

*(Note: Flagged for employment counsel and HR review; not legal advice.)*

- **Works Council (Ondernemingsraad, OR):** Mandatory in the Netherlands from 50 employees. Under **Article 25 of the Works Councils Act (WOR)**, any major reorganisation, significant reduction/change in activities, or major change in group structure requires formal prior advisory rights (*adviesrecht*), with a one-month standstill period and right of appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*) if bypassed. Involving the OR early on the problem—before locking into headcount cuts—reduces delay and improves plan quality.
- **Collective Redundancy (WMCO):** If targeted reductions ever become necessary at the Q2 trigger and reach **20 or more employees within 3 months** in one UWV work area (including employer-initiated settlement agreements / *vaststellingsovereenkomsten*), advance notification and consultation with UWV and unions are legally required.
- **Cash & Timing Reality:** Statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*) and 1–4 month employer notice periods mean involuntary dismissals cost cash up front and deliver limited Year 1 net savings. Avoiding them preserves Year 1 liquidity.
- **Communication:** Once the board approves the budget, brief managers first with clear numbers and FAQs, then communicate to all staff the same day with honest context—never via mass surprise emails or late-Friday announcements.

## 8. Key Assumptions & What Would Change This Plan

- **Assumption:** Natural attrition is sufficient (alongside a selective freeze) to deliver 3.1%–4.1% of opex savings over 12 months. *Would change the plan:* If trailing 12-month attrition is below 6%, we will need either deeper non-payroll cuts or earlier voluntary/targeted exit planning.
- **Assumption:** Non-payroll spend (32% of opex) contains 12.5%–15.0% of addressable waste, vendor overlap, or deferrable spend. *Would change the plan:* If >80% of non-payroll is locked into multi-year non-cancellable contracts, payroll or pricing must carry more weight.
- **Assumption:** The board's goal is sustainable margin improvement, not an immediate cash crisis. *Would change the plan:* If liquidity covenants are at risk within 90 days, faster cash-preservation measures are required.
