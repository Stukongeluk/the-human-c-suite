# Next Year Budget Approach: Protecting Margin Without Cutting Billable Capacity

**Prepared for:** Board of Directors & Executive Committee  
**Prepared by:** Chief Financial Officer  
**Status:** Draft framework for the Board meeting in three weeks (all figures below are illustrative estimates per 100 units of current opex, pending ledger and payroll extraction in Week 1)

---

## 1. Executive Recommendation

**Do not cut every cost centre by 8%.** Instead, target an **8.0% run-rate opex reduction** through a **differentiated plan**: zero-basing non-payroll spend (~15.6% of non-payroll opex), capturing natural attrition and internal redeployment under a selective hiring freeze (~3.7% of payroll over 12 months), and resetting leadership variable pay and perks first (~0.5% of total opex). Pair this with a commercial margin recovery track (rate-card discipline and unbilled-work capture) to protect our thin positive margin.

On an illustrative 100-unit opex base, this differentiated plan delivers **6.13% net opex reduction in Year 1** (with zero severance) versus **2.90% net opex reduction** under a blanket 8% cut—while both reach **8.00%** in Year 2 run-rate.

---

## 2. Why a Blanket 8% Cut Fails Here

Payroll is **68% of our opex** and our margin is thin but positive. In a 600-person B2B services business, an across-the-board 8% cut creates three predictable problems:

1. **It hits billable capacity and revenue.** Delivery cost centres are predominantly payroll. Shaving 8% off delivery teams reduces billable hours faster than it reduces fixed overhead, putting revenue and client retention at immediate risk.
2. **Year 1 net savings are much smaller than the headline.** An 8% headcount reduction equals roughly **48 roles**. In the Netherlands, a reorganisation of this scale triggers mandatory Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, WOR Article 25) advisory procedures, and 20 or more dismissals within three months in one UWV work area trigger collective redundancy notification and union consultation (WMCO). Combined with statutory employer notice periods (1–4 months, minus UWV procedure time) and statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*), exits slip into Q2/Q3 while exit costs land up front.
3. **Hidden attrition and rehiring costs erode Year 2.** Research by Trevor & Nyberg found that a 1% downsizing was associated with 31% higher voluntary turnover the following year, and Gallup estimates employee replacement costs at 0.5x to 2.0x annual salary. Across industry studies, only ~10% of cost-reduction programmes sustain results after three years (McKinsey) and ~12% of transformations deliver lasting performance improvement (Bain).

## 4. What the Differentiated Plan Changes

| Lever | Run-Rate Target (of Opex) | Year 1 Impact (of Opex) | Lands In | Owner | How It Works |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Zero-base non-payroll spend** | -5.00% | -4.25% | Q1–Q2 | CFO & Procurement / IT | Challenge every non-payroll line from zero: consolidate SaaS licences, renegotiate vendor/cloud contracts, cut external contractors/consultants, rationalise office/travel spend. |
| **2. Selective hiring freeze & attrition** | -2.50% | -1.38% | Q1–Q4 | HR & Practice Leads | Freeze non-billable backfills; use natural attrition (~22 FTEs equivalent) and internal redeployment from low-utilisation areas into billable delivery. |
| **3. Leaders first** | -0.50% | -0.50% | Q1 | CEO & RemCo | Executive and senior leadership variable pay and perks are reduced or deferred first, before any broader workforce measures. |
| **Total Opex Levers** | **-8.00%** | **-6.13%** | **Q1–Q4** | | **Zero forced redundancies; full 8.0% run-rate by Year 2.** |
| **4. Commercial & margin discipline** *(EBITDA track)* | *+1.50–2.00%* | *+1.50–2.00%* | Q1–Q3 | COO & Sales | Eliminate unbilled scope creep, enforce approval on discounting, and reprice or exit structurally loss-making accounts. |

### What We Protect (and Why)
- **Billable delivery capacity** in profitable client accounts (cutting here shrinks revenue faster than cost).
- **Core client retention & account management** (replacing a lost B2B client costs far more than the overhead saved).
- **Mandatory training, cybersecurity, and quality controls** (only cut if the future liability or remediation cost is explicitly priced next to the saving).

### What Stops or Waits
- Non-billable hiring and discretionary contractor spend.
- Low-value internal projects, unmeasured software tools, and non-essential travel/events.
- Margin-dilutive client discounts without executive sign-off.

## 6. Governance, Incentives, and Legal Check

- **Honest reporting:** No recurring cost or annual restructuring will be labelled "one-off." Board packs will show people indicators (regretted attrition, utilisation, time-to-fill) and client retention alongside P&L and cash.
- **Incentives:** Leadership bonuses will not reward headcount reduction or EBITDA achieved by deferring maintenance, training, or client quality.
- **Dutch legal & works council note:** This paper is a financial framework, not legal advice. Any structural reorganisation, change to monitoring/analytics tools, or collective exit requires formal review with Dutch employment counsel and HR under the Works Councils Act (WOR Art. 25 / Art. 27) and WMCO.

---

## 7. Key Assumptions & Trigger Points

1. **Assumptions:** Non-payroll spend (32% of opex) contains 15%–18% addressable waste/renegotiable spend; natural attrition runs at ~8%–10% annually, allowing ~3.7% net payroll reduction via selective freeze without forced exits; bank covenants are not breached in Q1–Q2.
2. **Triggers:** If Q1–Q2 revenue falls >5% below plan or cash headroom tightens, the Board pre-approves a secondary contingency (further contractor/capex deferral and, only if structural, a targeted, OR-consulted restructuring).
