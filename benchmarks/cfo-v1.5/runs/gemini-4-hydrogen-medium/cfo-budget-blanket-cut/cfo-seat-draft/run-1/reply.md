### 1. Why the CEO's Blanket 8% Fails the Math
With payroll at **68% of opex**, an 8% cut across every cost centre requires cutting **5.44%** of total opex from payroll (roughly **48 roles** in a 600-person firm) and **2.56%** from non-payroll. In the Netherlands, that runs straight into three realities:
- **Timing and exit costs:** 48 exits trigger mandatory Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, WOR Art. 25) advice and collective redundancy rules (WMCO: >=20 exits in three months requires UWV and union notification). With 1–4 months statutory notice and *transitievergoeding*, Year 1 payroll savings land late (~50% timing = -2.72 units) while exit costs (~2.00 units) hit immediately—leaving only **-2.90% net** in Year 1.
- **Revenue risk:** Cutting 8% of billable delivery centres cuts revenue faster than fixed overhead. If unreplaced capacity costs even 3%–4% of contribution margin, the Year 1 net saving disappears.
- **Follow-on attrition:** Research (Trevor & Nyberg) shows a 1% downsizing is associated with 31% higher voluntary turnover the following year, and Gallup estimates replacement costs at 0.5x–2.0x annual salary.

### 2. The Recommended Budget Approach
Reframe the board conversation from *"cut 8% everywhere"* to *"deliver an 8.0% run-rate opex reduction and protect our positive margin"* using four levers in order:

1. **Zero-base non-payroll spend (-5.00% run-rate / -4.25% Year 1):** Challenge the 32% non-payroll base from zero—SaaS/licence consolidation, vendor/cloud renegotiation, external contractors, and office/travel. Fast, reversible, no severance.
2. **Headcount flows, not layoffs (-2.50% run-rate / -1.38% Year 1):** Selective hiring freeze on non-billable backfills, natural attrition (~22 FTEs over 12 months), and redeploying under-utilised staff into billable work.
3. **Leaders first (-0.50% Year 1 & Year 2):** Reset or defer executive/senior variable pay and perks before asking anything of the broader workforce.
4. **Commercial margin discipline (+1.50% to +2.00% EBITDA buffer):** Stop unbilled scope creep, tighten discount approvals, and reprice or exit loss-making accounts.
