### The Trap
Excluding restructuring costs for a fourth straight year turns a "one-off" accounting adjustment into a recurring incentive to cut people to hit a margin target, while hiding the customer churn and lost institutional knowledge that a buyer will discount at exit.

### The Board & CFO View (Steelman)
Private-equity boards push for 100% Adjusted EBITDA because deals are underwritten on EBITDA and exit multiples, debt covenants use Adjusted EBITDA, and single-metric plans keep management focused on margin. That logic holds for **lender reporting**, not for **management incentives**: in healthcare software, buyers in due diligence price recurring revenue durability (NRR), system uptime, and key-person retention—and healthcare switching cycles mean the churn from a 2027 cut often shows up right in a 2028–2029 exit process.

### Human Impact & Governance
- **Stability after three restructurings:** A fourth cycle of cuts in a 350-person healthcare software company hits the engineers and clinical implementation staff who hold customer trust and regulatory know-how.
- **Leaders own the result:** Under the draft plan, if involuntary layoffs are triggered (outside board-approved M&A overlap), executive bonus payout is capped at 0% before broader staff pay or roles are touched.
- **Compliance:** Any plan terms affecting broader staff or workforce changes must still follow local employment law and works council consultation rules where applicable.

- **Assumptions:** No transformative M&A integration is already committed for 2027; the 2027 EBITDA target can be reached through revenue retention, pricing, and non-headcount waste reduction rather than further layoffs.
