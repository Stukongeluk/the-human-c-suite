**My recommendation: don't put "120 redundancies = €9m of savings in 2027" in the board pack. The arithmetic only holds as a 2028 run-rate. On realistic German and Dutch timelines, 120 exits deliver about €4.1m gross in 2027 and a net cash effect of roughly -€1.1m, because the costs arrive before the savings.**

The model is built and attached: `savings_model_120_redundancies.xlsx` (live formulas, three timing cases, every estimate in a yellow cell). It's a draft on my assumptions, not on your payroll data, so please read the figures below as estimates.

**Why €9m isn't a 2027 number**

120 x €75,000 = €9.0m is a full-year, full-staffing run-rate. A 2027 saving only starts when each person actually leaves, and announcing next month doesn't make anyone leave next month.

| FY2027, EUR m (estimates) | Fast: all out by 31 Mar | Base: exits Apr-Sep | Slow: exits Jul-Oct |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross saving | 6.75 | 4.13 | 2.63 |
| Severance, social plan, outplacement | -3.48 | -3.48 | -3.48 |
| Legal and advisory | -0.40 | -0.40 | -0.40 |
| Work still to be done (10% of saving) | -0.68 | -0.41 | -0.26 |
| Rehiring (5% of roles, from Q4) | -0.20 | -0.20 | -0.20 |

| **Net cash effect** | **+1.24** | **-1.12** | **-2.47** |
| Gross as % of the €9m target | 75% | 46% | 29% |

- **Base case by quarter (net cash):** Q1 -€0.4m, Q2 -€1.4m, Q3 -€0.7m, Q4 +€1.4m. Cumulative cash turns positive in early 2028, and the full-year 2028 net effect is about €7.7m (about €9.0m gross, less work still to be done and rehires).
- **To reach €9m gross in 2027 at base timing you'd need about 260 roles, 19% of the group.** Net of costs, far more.
- **Even the fast case needs every exit agreed by 31 March** in two countries. I'd treat it as the ceiling, not the plan.
- **The P&L can mislead.** If the restructuring charge (about €3.9m) is provided in FY2026 once the plan is announced, FY2027 reported profit shows about +€2.8m while the cash goes the other way. Check the provision timing with your auditors, and show the charge next to any "adjusted" figure. A charge that recurs isn't one-off.

**What sets the timeline (check all of this with employment counsel)**

- **Netherlands:** the works council has an advisory right on a reorganisation (art. 25 WOR) and must be asked while the decision can still change. Dismissing 20 or more people in one UWV work area within three months triggers a notification and consultation with UWV and the unions (WMCO), and employer-initiated settlement agreements count toward the 20. Notice periods run one to four months, and the transition payment is statutory.
- **Germany:** if there's a works council, expect an Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan negotiation (§§111-112 BetrVG). Collective-dismissal thresholds (§17 KSchG) require advance notice to the employment agency, and errors can void dismissals. Social selection (Sozialauswahl) applies, so you can't simply pick roles or people. Notice runs up to seven months.
- **"Announce next month"** is the risky part. Announcing a decision before consulting can expose the group legally and make the works councils adversarial, which pushes you toward the slow case. Announcing a proposal and opening consultation is the safer sequence.

**What I'd bring to the board instead**

1. **The problem behind the €9m.** Is it a covenant, a margin target, cash, or a 2027 budget gap? If it's a covenant, talk to the bank now about headroom and timing. A waiver may be cheaper than a restructuring that is cash-negative until 2028.
2. **Options side by side, including doing nothing.** Redundancy should come last. One option I could size without your data is a partial hiring freeze: not backfilling half of expected leavers (112 a year at an assumed 8% attrition) saves about €2.1m in 2027 and €4.2m a year after, with no severance and no consultation delay. It only works outside billable and safety-critical roles. The other levers need your ledger to size: working capital, vendor and spend reviews, pricing and revenue leakage, stopping low-value work, voluntary exits, reduced hours if demand is only temporarily weak (Germany's Kurzarbeit falls back to 12 months from January 2027 unless extended). The board should also see leadership bonuses and pay taken first.
3. **If cuts are still unavoidable:** one round, done once, with the work reduced as well as the people, and a decent package. Cutting 120 people and leaving the same work behind is how you get rehires and resignations.
4. **Risk to flag:** I used 20 extra voluntary leavers (€0.75m) as an estimate. Research links downsizing to higher turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008) but doesn't give a safe number, so replace it with your own attrition data. A rough break-even: in the fast case, about €5m of lost revenue (at an assumed 25% contribution margin) wipes out the 2027 net.

**Sensitivities (base case net cash, EUR m):** one-off costs +25% gives -2.0; only 80% of the €75k actually avoidable gives -1.9; every exit one month later gives -1.8; 40 extra leavers gives -1.9.
