The €9 million is the full-year run-rate from 2028 (about €8.1 million after leakage), not a 2027 saving.

The workbook is `redundancy_savings_model_2027.xlsx`, saved next to this reply. It has inputs, three timing scenarios, a sensitivity grid and a timeline.

**Why 120 x €75,000 does not equal €9 million in 2027**

The CEO's figure assumes all 120 are gone on 1 January at no cost. In Germany and the Netherlands that cannot happen:

- **Consultation comes first.** In the Netherlands the works council must be asked for advice before the decision is made, and 20 or more dismissals in one UWV area trigger a notification to UWV and the unions. In Germany, where there is a works council, you negotiate an Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan, and the German collective-dismissal notification has to be right or the dismissals can be void.
- **Notice periods keep payroll running.** Dutch notice runs one to four months and German notice up to seven by service, so people are paid after the notices go out.
- **Costs come first.** Transition payments, social plan severance and transition support are paid at exit, and the saving only builds afterwards.

**What I would tell the CEO**

1. **Present it as a proposal, not a decision.** Announcing "120, €9 million" next month undermines the consultation you are legally required to run, and a plan the works councils cannot change invites delay, which cuts 2027 savings further. This is not legal advice, so please have counsel check the Dutch and German steps before the date is set.
2. **Start from the gap, not the headcount.** "Where does €9 million in 2027 come from?" has other answers that act faster than redundancies and need no severance. These are non-core spend, vendors, energy, scrap and rework, pricing and contract terms, a hiring freeze with natural attrition, redeployment, and voluntary exits. I have no data on those, so I have not sized them. Your warehouse and ERP can, and I'd put them in the pack next to this model.
3. **Check the business case.** Is the group profitable and cash-positive? If the real driver is a covenant, a margin target or an owner expectation, the board should see that stated plainly, because it decides how much risk is acceptable.
4. **Leaders go first.** Board papers on cuts land better when executive pay, bonuses and perks are cut before frontline jobs. If results disappoint, leadership owns that, in the paper and in the announcement.
5. **Cut work, not only people.** Decide which work stops. Otherwise 1,280 people carry the load of 1,400, and the leakage assumption in my model is probably too low.
6. **Do it once.** A single well-prepared round costs less than several rounds. Fund transition support from the first day of notice.

**Two questions that would change my advice**

- What is the €9 million for: a covenant, a margin target, or a one-time ask from the owners? If it is a 2027 profit number, other levers should carry most of it. If it is a structural gap, redundancies may be part of the answer, but you would show them as a 2028 run-rate with a 2027 phasing.
- Which sites and roles are affected, and what is the cause of the cost gap there (demand, utilisation, overtime, agency cover)? If it is demand that will return, short-time work (Kurzarbeit in Germany) may bridge it. That needs works council agreement in Germany, and the Dutch scheme is narrower, so check what is open to you.
