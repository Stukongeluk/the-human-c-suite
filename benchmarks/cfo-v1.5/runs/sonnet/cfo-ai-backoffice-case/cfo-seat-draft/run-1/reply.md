I'd build the board a case for a 30% reduction by the end of year 3, reached mostly through freeze, attrition and redeployment, and not for 27 redundancies next year. On my placeholder numbers the 27-exit route is worse over 24 months (SEK -6.5m vs -4.1m for the staged route, both including AI costs), because the automation won't cover the work by the time the people are gone. **What the numbers say (SEK m, estimates)**

- 90 people cost about 72m a year fully loaded (est. 0.8m each). Thirty percent is 27 people, 21.6m a year gross, roughly 16.5m after AI running costs of about 5m. That prize is real.
- The AI programme costs about 8.7m in year 1 (integration, licences, and two FTE for controls and output review) and 5.1m in year 2. It does not pay back in year 1 under any option. Any slide showing a year-1 net saving of 21.6m would be wrong.

| Net, incl. AI cost | Year 1 | Year 2 | 2-yr total |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| B. 27 compulsory exits, effective start of Q4 | -14.4 | +7.9 | -6.5 |
| C. Staged: freeze, attrition, redeploy, voluntary exits | -6.8 | +2.7 | -4.1 |

- B's saving can't arrive before Q4. Under Swedish law we must negotiate with the unions first (MBL) and give the Public Employment Service four months' notice for 26-100 people, on top of individual notice periods.
- B loses its saving to backfill and attrition. Automation frees about 7 of the 27 FTE by Q4, so contractors or rehires fill the gap (6.0m in Q4 alone), and I assumed 6 more regretted leavers.
- B does beat C in year 2 on paper (7.9 vs 2.7) if the automation catches up. If it delivers half my assumed capacity, B's year 2 drops to 3.7 and C's stays at 2.7.
- At roughly 10% attrition, about 9 people leave a year, so the 30% is reachable in about three years with few or no compulsory dismissals, provided capacity is freed in step.

**Where AI helps most.** The estimated touchless shares, which the pilot should replace with our own: AP about 50-70%, reconciliations about 40-60%, reporting about 20-30%. For an insurer, IFRS 17 and Solvency II inputs and supervisory filings are the last place to cut capacity.

**Things the board should hear before they hear a number**

- **LAS** sets redundancy order by length of service within each unit and agreement area (three exemptions per employer). We can't choose who stays, and the most recently hired, often the most digitally fluent, leave first.
- **Insurer controls:** Finansinspektionen's expectations, DORA for AI vendors, GDPR, segregation of duties, audit trails and a named human approving anything that moves money. Brief the external auditor before design. Counsel and compliance should confirm all of this; none of it is legal advice.
- **Base rates are poor.** One much-cited study found about 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots with no measurable P&L impact (method criticised, so a direction only), and Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by end 2027.
- **Leaders first.** Tie leadership bonus to a multi-year balanced scorecard (close quality, audit findings, retention), never to headcount reduction as such, and make leaders' pay the first to give if it under-delivers.

**What I'd ask the board to approve:** the 30% as a year-3 target, a 90-day pilot of AP and bank reconciliation with a measured baseline, early dialogue with the unions, and quarterly gates (touchless rate, error rate, close days, audit findings, regretted attrition). A missed gate pauses further release; it doesn't bring cuts forward.

**Two questions that would change the numbers:**
1. Is the real driver cost, speed or control, and is there a date or covenant behind "next year"? If it is a hard cash need in year 1, I'd look at working capital, vendor and licence spend and pricing before payroll.
2. What do payroll and HR show for loaded cost per role band, attrition and age and tenure profile? That decides how much attrition alone can deliver.
