**Why the announcement backfires**

- **It doesn't help the covenant in time.** Severance, transition payments and notice periods are costs that land first. Savings come later. If your covenant definition doesn't add back restructuring costs, the cut could push the ratio *higher* at the end of Q2. Check that definition today.
- **The law sets the order.** At 260 employees you have a works council. It must be asked for advice before the decision is taken, and announcing a number first undermines that (art. 25 WOR). Twenty-six people is above the 20-in-three-months collective dismissal threshold, which means notifying UWV and the unions (WMCO). If you announce first, you risk a challenge at the Enterprise Chamber and a delay. This is not legal advice, so get employment counsel in before anyone speaks.
- **It signals panic, not control.** A bank reads a headcount number as a sign that you have no plan. In a family firm it also damages the regional reputation, and your best people start looking elsewhere.

**Options, from least harm to most**

| Option | Effect on the ratio by end of Q2 | Who carries the cost |
|---|---|---|
| Working capital: inventory and WIP, debtor collection, supplier terms, customer deposits | Cuts net debt directly, and is often the fastest lever in manufacturing | Nobody's job |
| Pause capex, and pause the owner's dividend or draw | Cuts net debt now | The family |
| Scrap, rework, energy, materials and pricing on the margin mix | Adds EBITDA, but more slowly | Nobody's job |
| Subordinated shareholder loan, equity injection or a cure right if your facility has one | Fixes the ratio on paper | The family |
| Hiring freeze, end of agency and overtime, attrition | Adds EBITDA gradually | Nobody's job |
| Executive and owner pay cut, leaders first | Small, but it is the signal the bank and staff actually read | Leaders |
| Reduced hours (only if demand is temporarily down) | Needs a public scheme. In the Netherlands, werktijdverkorting currently covers only calamities. Check whether the crisis scheme bill has passed | Shared |
| Voluntary exits, then involuntary reductions | Slow, costly and hard to reverse | Staff |

**Recommendation for the bank meeting**

1. Bring a 13-week cash forecast and a base and downside case through Q2 and Q3, with the actions each would trigger.
2. Show the working-capital and spend actions with owners and dates. Ask for a covenant reset or a waiver with a reporting rhythm (monthly numbers), not a surprise at the test date.
3. The owner announces first what the family is doing: dividend paused, a pay cut, and a loan or injection if that is possible. A seriousness signal that costs the owner something carries more weight than one that costs 26 colleagues their jobs.
4. Tell the works council now, with the real numbers, before the bank meeting. They will find better savings than you will, and early involvement is both the law and good practice.
5. If the downside case shows a structural problem (overcapacity, lost customers, margins that won't recover), then cut once, deep enough not to repeat, and decently. That means consultation first, then phased exits, paid outplacement from day one and the age-proportional selection the Dutch process requires. Decide that on evidence in January, not under a three-week deadline.

**The steelman for the owner.** Banks do respond to decisiveness, and if demand is structurally lower, delay costs money. That is why the downside case decides this. If the covenant is only tight because of a bad half-year and orders are recovering, cutting skilled furniture makers and then rehiring costs far more than 0.1x.
