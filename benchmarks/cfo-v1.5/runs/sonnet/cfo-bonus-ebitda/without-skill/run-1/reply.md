# 2027 Management Bonus Plan: draft for Compensation Committee review

I've drafted this to the board's brief: payout driven by adjusted EBITDA, with restructuring adjusted out. One part of that brief needs changing before it goes to the board, so I hardened it and flagged it below.

## The issue to raise with the board first

You have restructured in each of the last three years. A cost that recurs every year is an operating cost, not a one-off. If restructuring is excluded in full, the plan pays managers on a profit figure that has never existed in cash terms. Two further effects follow:

1. **Double benefit.** The savings from a restructuring show up in EBITDA, but its cost does not. Management gets paid for the savings and never carries the cost.
2. **Incentive to restructure.** The people who decide on and run restructurings are the plan participants. Full exclusion makes a restructuring a free way to lift their own bonus. In a 350-person company that is a retention and trust risk, and it will be read that way by the people affected.

Over a three-year hold, a PE buyer and a lender will also normalise this. An exit EBITDA that has been adjusted every year invites a diligence haircut, which is the opposite of what the bonus is meant to protect.

The draft keeps "adjusted EBITDA" but makes the restructuring adjustment rule-based and capped. The board can still say it excludes restructuring, in a controlled way.

## Definition of Adjusted EBITDA

Start from reported EBITDA under the group's accounting policies, consistently applied. Permitted adjustments are listed exhaustively below. Anything not listed is not adjusted.

**Permitted adjustments**
- Transaction costs for acquisitions, disposals and refinancings, approved by the board.
- Sponsor monitoring fees.
- Non-cash share-based payment charges.
- Foreign exchange on intercompany balances.
- Restructuring costs, only under the rules below.

**Restructuring rules (the key change)**

1. **Named programme only.** A cost is excluded only if it belongs to a restructuring programme approved by the Compensation Committee or board in writing before the cost is incurred, with a stated scope, budget and expected savings. Ad hoc costs are not excluded.
2. **Savings count in full, so the cost does not vanish.** Savings from any programme (2027's and earlier years') are in the EBITDA baseline and in the target. They are never netted out.
3. **Recurring allowance.** Because restructuring has happened every year, a fixed allowance equal to the three-year average annual restructuring cost of [EUR __]m is treated as an ordinary operating cost and is not excluded. Only restructuring cost above the allowance can be excluded. The board can reset the allowance annually.
4. **Cap.** Total excluded restructuring cost cannot exceed [__]% of budgeted revenue or [EUR __]m, whichever is lower. Overruns against an approved programme budget count against EBITDA.
5. **Payback test.** A programme's excluded cost must have an expected payback of [24] months or less. Longer-payback programmes count against EBITDA.
6. **Participant conflict.** Participants may not approve the exclusion of their own plan's costs. The Committee decides, with CFO analysis, and records the reasoning.

*If the board insists on full exclusion*, the minimum safeguard is rules 1, 2 and 6 plus disclosure to the Committee each quarter of cumulative excluded cost and what it would have done to the payout.

## Guardrails against gaming EBITDA

A 100% EBITDA plan can be hit by cutting things that damage the business later. The guardrails below do not change the payout formula.

- **Capitalisation and cost deferral.** Capitalised development cost, deferred hiring or maintenance, and shifted revenue recognition are reviewed by the Committee. Capitalised R&D above [__]% of total R&D, or a change in capitalisation policy, requires Committee approval to be counted.
- **Healthcare risk gate.** If a material event occurs in 2027, the Committee may reduce the payout by up to [__]% (or to zero). Events: a reportable data breach, a patient-safety incident attributable to the software, a major regulatory finding, or a breach of customer SLAs. For healthcare software this is a business-critical downside, and it should not be possible to earn a bonus while it is happening.
- **Customer health check.** The Committee reviews gross revenue retention, net revenue retention and ARR against budget before approving payout. If the EBITDA result came from underinvesting in support, engineering or customer success while ARR or retention fell materially, the Committee may reduce the payout.
- **Discretion.** The Committee may adjust the final payout by +/-[20]% for factors outside management's control or for quality of earnings, and must record its reasons.

## Other terms

- **Leavers.** Good leavers (redundancy, retirement, death, disability) receive a pro-rata bonus on actual results. Bad leavers and anyone who resigns or is under notice at payment lose the bonus.
- **Joiners.** Pro-rata from start date if employed by [1 Oct] 2027.
- **Change of control or exit.** Plan runs to completion on actual results unless the board decides otherwise. Any exit-related bonus is a separate decision and is not part of this plan.
- **Clawback.** Bonuses are recoverable for [3] years if the accounts are restated or if there was fraud or serious misconduct.
- **Governance.** The CFO prepares the calculation. It is reviewed by the Compensation Committee and, where appropriate, reconciled to the audited accounts. Disputes go to the Committee, whose decision is final.
- **Legal.** Employment-law review is needed for the jurisdictions where participants sit (works council or employee consultation, where applicable, and treatment of the bonus in holiday pay, pension and notice). I have not reviewed jurisdiction-specific rules, so counsel should sign off on the final text.

## My recommendation

If the board will not accept a plan that is not 100% EBITDA, then deliver the plan as drafted, including the restructuring rules. If there is room, I would move 20 to 25% of the weight to recurring revenue or net revenue retention. The aim is to stop the plan rewarding profit that comes from cutting people and investment, which is the usual route to short-term EBITDA and the usual cause of the next restructuring.
