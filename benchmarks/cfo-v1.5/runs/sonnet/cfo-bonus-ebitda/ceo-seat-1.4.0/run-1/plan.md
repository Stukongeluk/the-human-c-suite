# 2027 Management Bonus Plan: Draft for Remuneration Committee

Status: draft for discussion. Figures in [brackets] are placeholders to be set from the 2027 budget. Not legal or tax advice; employment counsel to review before issue.

## 1. Purpose

Reward management for building a company that is worth more to its customers and to its next owner: profitable, retaining customers, safe to run in healthcare, and led by a team that people stay with. Adjusted EBITDA is the largest single measure. It is not the only one.

## 3. Scorecard

| Measure | Weight | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| Adjusted EBITDA | 60% | The owners' value measure and the covenant measure |
| Gross revenue retention (ARR basis) | 15% | A buyer pays for customers who stay; cost-out that harms service shows up here |
| Service quality and safety: availability of customer-facing systems, severity-1 incidents, open critical security and regulatory findings | 15% | Healthcare customers depend on the software; this protects patients and the company's licence to operate |
| Regretted attrition (people leaving whom the company wanted to keep) and employee engagement trend, at company level | 10% | Three restructurings in three years is a risk to knowledge and delivery; this makes leaders accountable for it |
| Total | 100% | |

Weights add to 100 (60 + 15 + 15 + 10).

## 4. Payout curves

Adjusted EBITDA (linear between points):

| Result vs. budget | Payout on this component |
|---|---|
| Below 90% | 0% |
| 90% | 50% |
| 100% | 100% |
| 110% or more | 150% (cap) |

Other three measures: threshold, target and stretch levels set per measure at budget approval, same 50% / 100% / 150% shape, with a counter-condition: no payout above 100% on any measure if another measure is below threshold (so EBITDA cannot be bought with retention or quality).

## 5. Definition of adjusted EBITDA (the part that decides whether the plan is fair)

Set by the Remuneration Committee before the year starts, reviewed by the finance function and reported by the CFO to the committee, with the working shown.

1. **Restructuring add-back is capped.** Restructuring costs approved in advance by the board, with a written business case, may be added back up to [2% of budgeted EBITDA]. Anything above the cap counts in full against the bonus.
2. **Repeated restructuring is not one-off.** Restructuring costs in a function or business unit that already had restructuring costs added back in 2025 or 2026 count in full. The 2025, 2026 and 2027 add-backs are shown side by side in every bonus report.
3. **Savings must be delivered.** Each restructuring case states the annual savings promised. If realised savings at year end are below plan, the shortfall reduces the EBITDA result for bonus purposes.
4. **No borrowing from the future.** Bonus EBITDA is adjusted for: any increase in the share of development cost capitalised, deferral of planned maintenance, security, compliance or customer support spend, and pulling revenue forward from later periods.
5. **Other add-backs** (transaction fees, one-off legal settlements, FX) only if listed in advance, approved by the committee, and not recurring.
6. **Discretion goes one way.** The committee may reduce a payout where results were reached by harming customers, quality, safety or people. It may not raise one beyond the curve.

## 6. Deferral and clawback

- 25% of any payout is held for 12 months. It is released if gross revenue retention and service-quality results for the following year stay at or above threshold.
- Payouts are recalculated and recovered where the underlying figures are restated, or where a serious breach of regulation or company policy is established.
- This is deliberately aligned with the horizon of a sale: a bonus earned by cost-out in the year before an exit is held back until customers and teams have shown they are still there.

## 7. Leadership accountability

- Three restructurings in three years is a leadership result, not an unavoidable cost of business. The plan says so and acts on it.
- In any year with involuntary headcount reductions, executive team payout is capped at 100% of target on every measure, and the executive team's EBITDA component is reduced first, before any frontline measure or pay is touched. Any further restructuring in 2027 requires a written case showing which earlier options were used first.
- Executive and senior bonus outcomes are reported to all employees at the same level of detail as the board sees them (aggregate, by measure).

## 8. Governance and process

- Remuneration Committee approves measures, weights, curves and the EBITDA definition before 31 [January] 2027; no changes after results are known.
- Targets are set from the approved 2027 budget; measures are defined in writing with data source and owner.
- Participants sign off the rules at the start of the year. Employee representatives (works council, where there is one) are consulted: in the Netherlands a pay scheme for staff typically needs the works council's consent; confirm with counsel which participants and which rules apply here.
- Payment after audited or independently reviewed year-end figures.

## 9. Review

Measures reviewed each year: does each one still change a decision, and has anyone found a way to hit it while making the company worse? Retire any that fail.
