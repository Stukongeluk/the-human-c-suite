Don't pay 100% on adjusted EBITDA with restructuring excluded: after three restructurings in three years, that exclusion isn't a one-off, and it pays managers for repeating what is probably costing you customers and knowledge. I've drafted the plan (full text in plan.md) at 60% adjusted EBITDA, with the rest on retention, service quality and regretted attrition, and with a capped, defined restructuring add-back.

**What the draft does**

- **Scorecard (60 + 15 + 15 + 10 = 100):** adjusted EBITDA 60%; gross revenue retention 15%; service quality and safety (availability, severity-1 incidents, open critical security and regulatory findings) 15%; regretted attrition and engagement trend, company level, 10%.
- **EBITDA curve:** nothing below 90% of budget, 50% at 90%, 100% at target, capped at 150% at 110%. No payout above 100% on any measure if another is below threshold.
- **Restructuring:** added back only up to a cap [proposed 2% of budgeted EBITDA, set from the budget]; restructuring in a function already added back in 2025 or 2026 counts in full; if promised savings aren't delivered, the shortfall counts. The three years of add-backs are shown side by side in every bonus report.
- **No borrowing from the future:** bonus EBITDA is adjusted for more capitalised development, deferred maintenance or security spend, and revenue pulled forward.
- **Holdback and clawback:** 25% held for 12 months, released only if retention and quality hold. This matters most if an exit is near, because a buyer's due diligence tests churn and key-person risk, and cost-out shows up there.
- **Leaders first:** in any year with involuntary reductions, executive payout is capped at target and their EBITDA component is cut before any frontline measure. The plan says plainly that three rounds is a leadership result.

**Options for the board**

| | A: 100% EBITDA, restructuring excluded | B: EBITDA 100% with guardrails | C: Scorecard (my draft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rewards | Margin by any route | Margin, with a quality/retention modifier | Margin, retention, quality, people |
| Risk | Repeated cost-out, churn, deferred spend | Modifier can feel bolted on | Board sees EBITDA diluted to 60% |
| Fit with an exit | Weak: buyer checks churn | Medium | Strongest |

If the board insists on EBITDA as the whole plan, B is the fallback: keep sections 5 to 7 of the draft (capped add-back, repeat rule, savings check, holdback, leader cap) and apply retention and quality as a +/-20% modifier on the payout. I would not accept A without at least the cap and the repeat rule.

**The board's and CFO's view, fairly:** one measure is simple, hard to argue about, matches the lenders' covenant and the PE return model, and discretion invites politics. That's right, which is why the EBITDA definition is fixed in writing before the year and the committee's discretion only goes downward.

**What I need to know, and what I haven't checked**

1. What drove each of the three restructurings, and did the promised savings arrive? If they did and customers stayed, the cap can be looser. If not, this plan is the fix.
2. How close is an exit, and how tight are the covenants? That decides the holdback length and the add-back cap.
3. I don't have your figures: all numbers in brackets (cap, bonus pool, thresholds) are placeholders, and retention, incident and attrition baselines need pulling from billing, your incident tracker and HR, in aggregate.
4. Where participants work and whether there's a works council. In the Netherlands, pay schemes typically need its consent, so bring it in before the board paper is final. This is not legal advice; have counsel review.
