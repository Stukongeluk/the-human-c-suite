# Budget 2027: close the 8% through spend, attrition and leaders' pay first, with a people decision only at a fixed checkpoint

DRAFT for the board meeting in three weeks. Every figure is an index (this year's opex = 100) and an ESTIMATE until the ledger, payroll aggregates and contract data are loaded. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice; counsel and the auditor should review the sections marked (L).

## 1. The target and why

- Board ask: next year's opex 8% lower, i.e. 8 index points. Payroll is 68, everything else is 32.
- Open definition (to settle before the meeting): 8% below this year's actual, below this year's forecast, or below the budget we would otherwise have had? Does opex include subcontractors and freelancers, and is it opex excluding cost of delivery? A different base moves the target by several points.
- What the 8% is really for (to confirm with the board): thin positive margin means the underlying need is probably margin or covenant headroom, not opex as such. If so, revenue levers (rates, utilisation, leakage) count towards the goal and the paper should say so.

## 2. Why not 8% for every cost centre

| | Blanket 8% | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| Payroll share of the cut | 5.4 of 8 points (68% x 8%) | About 48 of 600 FTE if taken as headcount; the same 8% in non-payroll would be a 25% cut of the 32 |
| Where it lands | Equally on lean and bloated teams | Billable and customer-facing teams are cut as hard as low-value overhead |
| Timing (L) | Savings arrive late | WOR advice from the works council, UWV/WMCO notification if 20 or more in one UWV region within 3 months, notice periods of 1 to 4 months: most exits fall in Q3 |

Estimated net effect of the blanket cut in year 1 (estimates; assumptions in section 7):

| Index points | Year 1 | Run-rate year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Gross payroll saving | 1.9 | 5.4 |
| One-off exit costs (transition payments, notice, social plan, outplacement, legal) | -1.7 | 0 |
| Backfill, contractors and extra attrition after the cut | -0.2 | -1.9 |
| Revenue at risk | -0.3 | -0.5 |
| Non-payroll share (8% of 32) | 2.6 | 2.6 |
| **Net, year 1 / year 2** | **about 2.3** | **about 5.6** |

Read this as: the blanket cut delivers under a third of the target in the year the board wants it, and under 6 of 8 points after that, because the people-side savings arrive late and partly come back. (Arithmetic of the table is from the model in section 7; year-1 net combines the lines above and rounds.)

## 3. Recommended approach: close the gap in this order

| Lever | Run-rate (pts) | Year 1 (pts) | Owner | Confidence | Human impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-base non-payroll lines (vendors, licences, cloud, travel, office, consultants, events), 10-14% of 32 | 3.2 to 4.5 | about 3.0 | CFO with each cost-centre owner | Medium, until contract renewal dates are known | None |

Also on the table, outside the opex line: price increases at renewal where value supports them, billing leakage (unbilled work, unapproved discounts), receivable days. These raise margin, which is probably what the board wants, and are not counted above.

Honest reading: without jobs being cut, the plan reaches about 5 of 8 points in 2027 and 6 to 10 at run-rate. The remaining 3 points in year 1 are the real decision for the board, and are covered in section 4.

## 4. The gap and the checkpoint

Instead of deciding on layoffs today, fix a checkpoint (suggest end of Q1) with triggers:

- Non-payroll savings are signed (contracts renegotiated or cancelled) for less than 2.5 of 3.2 to 4.5 points: management reports why and what replaces them.
- Attrition runs below 8% (fewer natural exits): consider voluntary exits or reduced hours before involuntary steps.
- Margin or covenant headroom falls below the minimum the bank requires: bring the bank in then, with the 13-week cash forecast, rather than later.

If, after these levers, a structural reduction is still needed, it is done once, decently, and only after the works council has been consulted (advice right under article 25 WOR, before the decision), with severance, notice and support costed in the same table. If the gap is a temporary dip, reduced hours with leaders first is the first option to test; the Dutch short-time scheme currently covers only calamities outside normal business risk, so check the status of the proposed crisis scheme before relying on it. (L)

## 5. What we protect, and what stops or waits

- Protected: training and certification budgets for billable staff (loss shows as lower utilisation and attrition), security and quality assurance, customer-facing capacity on key accounts. Cut only with the future cost written next to it.
- Stops or waits: lower-value internal projects; discretionary events and travel; new non-critical tooling; non-essential consultants.
- Leadership: bonus and perks paused first; the bonus scheme for next year should be on profit plus customer retention, regretted attrition and quality, not profit alone. No bonus for headcount reduction.

## 6. Risks, sensitivities and what would change this budget

- Biggest sensitivity: non-payroll savings (3.2 to 4.5 points). Each 10% shortfall is about 0.3 to 0.45 points.
- Attrition: each 1 point of annual attrition not backfilled is about 6 FTE, about 0.7 points run-rate (estimate).
- Revenue: break-even for a payroll cut is about 0.5 points of lost revenue; lose more and the cut costs more than it saves. Check the pipeline, renewals and key accounts before any people step.
- Change triggers: loss of a top-10 account, a covenant forecast breach, or attrition above 15%.
- Reporting: any adjusted EBITDA shown to the board must reconcile to reported figures; restructuring cost is shown as it falls, and not labelled one-off if it recurs.

## 7. Assumptions and model (estimates, to replace with ledger data)

- Opex index 100: payroll 68, non-payroll 32. 600 FTE, so about 0.113 points per FTE fully loaded (68/600).
- Blanket case: 8% of payroll = 48 FTE; 35% of the annual saving lands in year 1 because most exits fall in Q3; one-off exit cost 31% of loaded annual cost (transition payment and social plan 18%, outplacement and legal 5%, paid notice and handover 8%); backfill and extra attrition 35% of the saving at run-rate; revenue at risk 0.5 points at run-rate and half that in year 1.
- Lever ranges in section 3 are first-pass hypotheses, not findings.
