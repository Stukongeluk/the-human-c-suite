I would not take "every cost centre cuts 8%" to the board. I recommend a sequenced plan that works spend, hiring flow and leaders' pay first, with an explicit checkpoint for any people decision. My first-pass estimate is that it reaches about 5 of the 8 points in 2027 and 6 to 10 at run-rate. A blanket cut reaches about 2.3 points in 2027 and about 5.6 afterwards. The full board paper is in `budget-board-paper.md`.

All figures are index points of this year's opex (= 100) and are my estimates, because I have no ledger or payroll data yet.

**Why the blanket cut falls short**
- Payroll is 68% of opex, so 8% everywhere puts 5.4 of the 8 points on payroll. That is about 48 of your 600 people. Asking non-payroll to give the same 8% of its 32 points would mean cutting it by 25%, so the arithmetic pushes towards headcount anyway.
- In the Netherlands those savings arrive late. The works council has an advisory right on a reorganisation (article 25 WOR) before the decision is final. If 20 or more people go in one UWV region within three months, UWV and the unions must be notified and consulted. Then come notice periods of one to four months. Most exits would land in Q3.
- Exit costs fall in the same year: transition payments, a social plan, paid notice, outplacement and legal fees. I assumed about 31% of loaded annual cost per exit.
- After the cut, some work comes back as contractors, backfills and extra attrition. I assumed 35% of the run-rate saving.
- That gives about 1.9 gross payroll saving in year 1, minus about 1.7 in one-off costs, minus about 0.5 in backfill and revenue risk. Adding the 2.6 from non-payroll gives about 2.3 net. After that it is about 5.6 net: the payroll saving of 5.4, minus 1.9 backfill and 0.5 revenue risk, plus the same 2.6 non-payroll.
- It also cuts billable and customer-facing capacity as hard as low-value overhead, which is the opposite of what a thin margin needs.

**What I propose instead (run-rate / year 1, in points)**
1. Zero-base the 32 points of non-payroll spend (vendors, licences, cloud, travel, office, consultants): 3.2 to 4.5 / about 3.0.
2. Hiring freeze with named exceptions, and not backfilling about half of natural leavers: 1.5 to 2.5 / about 1.0. I assumed 12% attrition, about 72 leavers a year.
3. Subcontractor and bench management, covering utilisation and rate cards: 0.8 to 1.5 / about 0.5.
4. Leaders first: pause executive and senior bonuses and perks, with a voluntary pay reduction for the top team: 0.3 to 0.6 / about 0.3.
5. Stop explicitly low-value internal projects: 0.5 to 1.0 / about 0.4.

Total: 6.3 to 10.1 at run-rate, about 5.2 in year 1. I would also count pricing at renewal, billing leakage and receivable days, which are not in those numbers. They raise margin without cutting anyone, and with thin margins that may be what the board actually wants.

**The honest gap and how to handle it**
About 3 points remain in year 1. Don't decide on layoffs in three weeks. Set a Q1 checkpoint with triggers: non-payroll savings actually signed, attrition below 8%, or covenant headroom shrinking. If a structural reduction is then unavoidable, do it once, after consulting the works council, with severance and support costed in the same table. For a temporary dip, test reduced hours first, leaders first. The Dutch short-time scheme currently covers only calamities, so check the status of the proposed crisis scheme. Counsel should review the Dutch employment law points.

**The case for the CEO's view**
Simple, fair-looking rules are easy to communicate and hard to game, and the board gets a clear number. The plan above keeps the simplicity by giving every cost-centre owner a single target: show your savings by activity, and what stops, by a date. It trades the percentage for ownership.

**Three questions that change the numbers**
1. 8% below what: last year's actual, this year's forecast, or the budget we would have set? Does opex include subcontractors?
2. What is the 8% for: margin, a covenant, or a valuation? If it is margin, revenue levers count.
3. Is there a works council, and when can it be briefed?
