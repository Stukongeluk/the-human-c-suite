I'd take a targeted plan to the board, not an equal 8% per cost centre: reach 8% on a run-rate basis by year-end, build it bottom-up with spend and attrition first, protect billable delivery, and keep involuntary cuts out unless a gate in Q1 or Q2 shows we are off track. My rough estimate is that this delivers 5.2 to 7.9 points of opex in year one. That is an estimate until your data replaces it, and the board should hear that range from you, not discover it later.

A draft board paper is in `board_paper.md` in this folder: problem statement, options table, recommendation, human impact, legal floor, three-week plan and questions for the board.

**Why not the blanket cut**

The CEO's case is fair: it is simple, looks even-handed and is quick. But:

- **Cost centres are not equal.** Delivery teams produce revenue. With a thin margin, cutting 8% of delivery capacity can lose more margin than it saves. Cutting 8% of overhead does not.
- **The numbers push it onto people.** Equal shares mean 5.44 of the 8 points come from payroll and 2.56 from non-payroll (opex = 100). That is roughly 48 of 600 FTE if done through exits (estimate). Protecting payroll entirely would need a 25% cut in everything else, which is not credible either.
- **It is hard to reverse.** Lost skills and client relationships cost more to rebuild than the saving was worth, and Dutch notice periods, consultation and transition payments mean the first-year saving is well below the annual figure.
- **It rewards padded budgets** and punishes lean teams.

**Options (shares of this year's opex, estimates)**

| Option | In-year effect | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | 0 | Yes |
| A. Equal 8% per cost centre | up to 8.0 on paper, less in year one | No |
| B. Targeted: line-by-line non-payroll review (2.5 to 3.5), stop low-value work (1.0 to 1.5), hiring freeze with attrition (1.5 to 2.5), leadership pay and bonus cut (0.2 to 0.4) | 5.2 to 7.9 | Mostly |
| C. B plus involuntary reductions | B plus up to about 2 | No |

Leaders go first: freeze executive pay and cut bonuses and perks before any employee measure, and say plainly that the thin margin is leadership's result to own. If you are behind at a quarterly gate, the next steps in order are temporary reduced hours, then voluntary exits, then (only with the board's decision and full numbers) involuntary reductions.

**Netherlands floor** (not legal advice; confirm with counsel)

- A works council is mandatory from 50 employees and has an advisory right on major organisational changes (article 25 WOR). Brief it in week 1 with the real numbers. Ask the board to approve the approach and the envelope, with workforce measures following the council's advice.
- If involuntary reductions ever come up, 20 or more dismissals in one UWV region within three months triggers UWV and union notification and consultation (WMCO), plus transition payments.

**Three weeks**

- **Week 1:** pull attrition by team, utilisation, bench, vendor and freelancer spend and pipeline; brief the works council; settle the two questions below.
- **Week 2:** cost centre owners submit their own savings proposals; run a pre-mortem ("it is a year from now and this failed, why?"); replace my estimates with real numbers.
- **Week 3:** final paper, works council advice requested, leadership pay decision taken.

**Two questions that change the answer most**

1. What is the 8% protecting: a covenant, a return to shareholders, a pricing squeeze? "8% lower" is a solution; if the real goal is margin, pricing and contract indexation could close part of it with less harm.
2. Is 8% measured against this year's actual or next year's budget? If it is this year's actual, wage indexation (estimated 3 to 4%; check the CAO) means the real reduction in activity is closer to 11 to 12%.

**Assumptions:** attrition around 10 to 15% (estimate, unchecked), and 8 to 11% of non-payroll spend is removable. If attrition is well below that, B delivers less and voluntary exits come into play sooner.

What am I missing, and what would make this wrong? In particular, if you have a covenant or a cash position weaker than the margin suggests, the answer shifts towards a deeper, faster cut.
