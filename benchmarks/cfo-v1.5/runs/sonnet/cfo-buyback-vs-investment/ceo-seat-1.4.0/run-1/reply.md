**Recommend to the board: fund the €120m rebuild in full, return a smaller buyback of about €100m, and reset the EPS guidance openly instead of buying the few cents.** That leaves roughly €180m of net cash (400 - 120 - 100), and the cash position is the reason you have a choice at all. The €300m buyback and the €120m investment can't both happen without taking net cash to about -€20m, so the board is choosing between them. Treat the €100m as a starting point to test, not a settled figure (see the open items below).

**What is really being decided**

The EPS gap is a few cents. The activist's real question is whether €400m of idle cash is better used by you or by shareholders. The COO's real question is whether the business that earns that cash will still be competitive in three years. Both are fair. Buying back €300m to close a gap of a few cents is the weakest reason on the table, because it ties a permanent decision to one quarter's number. A survey of 401 US finance chiefs found 78% would give up economic value to smooth earnings (Graham, Harvey and Rajgopal, 2005). Companies run for the long term grew revenue 47% and earnings 36% more than their peers (McKinsey Global Institute, 2017, US large caps, indirect measure).

**Options**

| | A. Activist plan: €300m buyback, no rebuild | B. COO plan only: €120m, no buyback | C. Recommended: €120m phased + about €100m buyback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net cash after | ~€100m | ~€280m | ~€180m |
| EPS this year | Guidance met (buyback effect, my estimate; share count needed) | Miss widens: training and platform costs that can't be capitalised hit the P&L | Miss narrows but probably remains; reset guidance |
| Customer and store impact | Ageing platform and service model stay as they are | Addressed | Addressed |
| Cash buffer for a weak Q4 or a bad year | Thin | Strong | Adequate, to be confirmed |
| Reversibility | Low: shares can't be un-bought | High: tranches can be paused | Mixed: the buyback is flexible in size, the investment is gated |

**Why C**

- **The investment is the part you can't buy back later.** Retail frontline work shows what pays: in a randomised trial in 28 Gap stores, more predictable schedules raised median sales by 7% and labour productivity by 5%. Trained, stable store staff and a platform customers can use both bear on sales.
- **A buyback is flexible.** You can do €100m now and add more next year if the plan is on track. The reverse isn't true: a rebuild started and then cancelled costs money and credibility with both customers and staff.
- **Returning some cash is fair.** Net cash of €400m is more than a retailer needs, and you can show the activist a clear capital allocation policy (a minimum cash buffer, investment, then returns), which is better than being seen to resist.
- **Don't hit the number by cutting.** If anyone proposes trimming training, store hours or headcount to close the EPS gap, say no. That is shortening the business to flatter a quarter, and it would sabotage the very plan you're funding.

**How to make the investment safe for the board**

1. **Release the €120m in tranches against agreed milestones**, for example platform conversion, online sales, returns and delivery complaints, store service scores. The COO should bring measures, not only a budget. Ask for the business case behind the €120m: what it replaces, what it earns, and what happens if it slips.
2. **Treat the service model as a change for people, not just a cost line.** Paid training time, store managers involved in the design, and no job cuts hidden inside it. Take it to the works council or employee representatives early enough that they can change the plan, which is also a legal requirement in many EU countries (take advice from counsel).
3. **Leaders go first.** If guidance is reset, executive bonuses tied to EPS should not be adjusted to cover the miss. The decision is the leadership team's, and so is the result.

**Telling the market**

Reset or widen guidance now, say why, and show the spend, the milestones and the buyback in one message. Investors forgive a clear, explained miss better than a quiet one that turns out to have been bought. Make the case to long-term holders ahead of the activist so it doesn't arrive as a surprise.

**What I'd check before the board meeting (I don't have your numbers)**

- Share price and share count, to see how much EPS €100m of buyback really adds. My claim about the buyback closing the gap is an estimate.
- How much of the €120m is expensed versus capitalised, and when it lands.
- The true usable cash: lease obligations, seasonal working capital for Q4 inventory, and any covenants. "Net cash" can overstate what you can spend.
- Whether the €400m is already earning more than a buyback would return.
- Whether the €100m is the right amount or €150m fits better.

**Risks to this recommendation**

The activist may refuse and push a vote. The COO's plan may be bigger than €120m once it is scoped. A weak holiday season would argue for holding more cash. Those are the reasons to gate the money, not to skip the investment.
