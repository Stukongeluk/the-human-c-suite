**Recommend to the board: approve the €120m investment, in gated tranches, and offer shareholders a smaller buyback of up to about €100m. Take it from excess cash only, decide it on its own merits, and do not use it to close the EPS gap. Tell the market the EPS shortfall plainly.** That leaves about €180m of net cash. The two asks as written cannot both be met: €300m + €120m = €420m, which is €20m more than the €400m we have, before any cash buffer.

**Why not the €300m buyback**

- It would use 75% of our net cash and leave €100m. For a consumer electronics retailer that is thin. Working capital swings hard around Q4, and we need a buffer for lease payments and for a bad Christmas. I've assumed a minimum of about €150m. Please replace that with our treasury's real figure.
- The EPS link is the weak point. Companies that would just have missed their EPS forecast were sharply more likely to buy back shares, and those buybacks went with cuts in employment and investment (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, 2016). A €300m buyback paired with deferring the COO's plan is that pattern.
- It probably isn't needed to fix the miss. A few cents is roughly 1-3% of EPS. Retiring even 2-3% of the shares would cover that, so €300m is far more than the gap requires. The activist is asking for a lot more than the guidance needs.
- The numbers below are illustrative. I don't have our market cap, share count or deposit rate. I assumed a €2bn market cap, 3% pre-tax interest and 25% tax:

| Option | Net cash left | Shares retired (illustrative) | After-tax interest forgone (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Buyback €300m only | €100m | about 15% | about €6.8m a year |
| B. Both as asked | -€20m | n/a | n/a |
| C. Invest €120m only | €280m | 0% | about €0 extra |
| D. Recommended: €120m + up to €100m | €180m | about 5% | about €2.3m a year |

**Why the €120m comes first**

- Investment that clears our cost of capital comes before payouts. Buybacks are for what is left over. A retailer that cannot sell well online and cannot serve customers in store is not a company whose shares are cheap for good reasons.
- It is a business case, not a fixed sum. I haven't seen the COO's numbers, so I'd ask for four things before releasing the cash:
  - the expected return and payback against our hurdle rate;
  - the share of the platform and training spend that is capitalised and the share expensed (the expensed part will lower EPS in the years we spend it);
  - milestones with go/no-go gates, for example three tranches of about €40m;
  - customer and staff measures alongside the financial ones: online conversion, NPS, store-staff retention.
- The service model depends on store staff. Training and retention of people we already have is the cheapest part of this plan. It should not be trimmed to protect a quarter.

**The EPS miss**

- Don't hit it by cutting training, hiring, maintenance or marketing. These cuts bring the number back for a quarter and cost us later.
- Do two things. First, tell the market now that we are investing and that guidance moves down by a few cents. A small, early, explained miss costs less than a surprise. Second, check that the new spend is reported honestly: nothing recurring labelled one-off, and any adjusted EPS reconciled to reported EPS.
- Bonuses for the executive team should not turn on this year's EPS alone. If the result disappoints, leaders' bonuses come down first, and the board should say so up front.

**What I'd say to the activist**

We take their case seriously. Our plan returns up to €100m this year from excess cash, keeps a safe balance sheet, and invests to grow earnings that a buyback cannot create. After the first gate (about 12 months) we revisit the return programme with evidence of the investment's results. A buyback that stops us serving customers lowers the share value they hold.

**What would change my mind**

- Treasury shows a lower minimum cash need (then a larger buyback fits).
- The COO's case fails the hurdle rate, or can't be gated (then reduce the investment and return more).
- The shares trade well below intrinsic value on our own analysis (then a larger buyback has a stronger case, but still after the investment).
- Loan covenants or local rules limit buyback size. Legal, audit and the board's advisers should confirm authorisations, timing and disclosure. This is not legal, tax or investment advice.

**Two questions that would sharpen the numbers:** What is our true minimum cash balance across the seasonal peak, and what return does the COO's plan promise in year 1-3, split between capitalised and expensed spend?
