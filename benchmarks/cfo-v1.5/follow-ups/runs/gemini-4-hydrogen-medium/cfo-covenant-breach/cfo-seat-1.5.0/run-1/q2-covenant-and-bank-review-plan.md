## 1. Executive Recommendation

**Do not announce a 10% workforce reduction before the bank review.** A 3.6x vs. 3.5x forecast is a **0.1x (~2.8%) gap**, which is a cash and headroom problem, not a structural headcount crisis. Under Dutch law, cutting 26 jobs delivers **€0 in Q2 payroll savings** while triggering mandatory Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) consultation, collective dismissal notification (WMCO), and cash exit costs that worsen Net Debt.

Instead, close the Q2 gap through a **Working Capital & Capex Sprint**, **suspend owner drawings and leadership bonuses first**, and **bring a 13-week cash forecast and a proactive covenant-buffer request** to the bank in three weeks.

## 4. The Cost-Effective Levers (Least to Most Human Harm)

1. **Working Capital Sprint (Target: €300k–€500k by Q2 close)**
   - **Inventory & WIP:** Discount and clear aged finished goods and slow-moving fabric/wood SKUs; tighten batch sizes.
   - **Receivables:** Enforce terms on overdue B2B/retail accounts and resolve billing disputes immediately. *Do not stretch payments to small local suppliers.*
   - **Capex:** Defer non-safety, non-maintenance capex (showroom refreshes, non-urgent upgrades) for two quarters.
2. **Non-Payroll Spend & Order Acceleration**
   - Audit freight consolidation, energy, external consultants, and IT licences; renegotiate before cancelling.
   - Prioritise shipping existing order backlog to convert WIP to Q2 revenue and cash.
3. **Owner & Leadership First (Target: €100k+ cash + backstop)**
   - Suspend owner dividends/drawings and freeze management bonuses before touching frontline pay or jobs.
   - Prepare a **€300k subordinated shareholder loan** commitment as a contingency backstop if working capital falls short.
4. **Proactive Bank Engagement (3-Week Review)**
   - Request a temporary covenant buffer (e.g., **3.75x for Q2–Q3**, stepping back to **3.50x in Q4**) even if the working capital sprint clears 3.50x, so a demand dip doesn't trigger a panic.
5. **People-Preserving Capacity Management (If Demand Remains Soft)**
   - Implement a selective hiring freeze (safety/bottleneck exceptions only) and rely on natural attrition.
   - Respect Dutch **WAB** rules on on-call workers (4-day notice). Check with employment counsel whether the *Wet personeelsbehoud bij crisis* applies if demand drops further, or use voluntary leave/overtime drawdowns and cross-training.

## 5. Three-Week Execution Plan to the Bank Meeting

- **Week 1 (Internal Alignment & Numbers):**
  - Build a **weekly 13-week cash forecast** (base and downside cases) from ERP, bank, and order book data.
  - Have a **private conversation with the owner** (see talking points below) to pause the layoff announcement and agree on suspended drawings/bonuses + a working-capital sprint.
  - Assign named owners and weekly targets for inventory clearance, AR collection, and capex deferral.
- **Week 2 (Bank Pack & Legal Check):**
  - Confirm exact covenant definitions (e.g., treatment of subordinated shareholder loans, restructuring charges, IFRS 16 leases) with your auditor/loan agreement.
  - Draft the **Bank Review Pack**: 13-week cash flow, Q2–Q4 base & downside leverage scenarios, trigger-based action matrix, and owner commitment letter.
  - Brief the Works Council (OR) informally on the market situation and working-capital measures—transparency builds trust without triggering a premature Art. 25 process.
- **Week 3 (Bank Review):**
  - Present the package early and calmly. Banks back management teams that spot a 0.1x pinch early, bring hard cash actions, and put owner money ahead of job cuts.

Worse, the bank knows Dutch labour law and will see an unconsulted layoff as a sign we're panicking over a €300k (0.1x) gap. Let's pause the announcement, freeze drawings and management bonuses, pull €400k–€600k out of inventory, receivables, and capex, and go to the bank with a 13-week cash plan and a temporary buffer request. That protects our cash, our skilled craftspeople, and the family's name in the region."*
