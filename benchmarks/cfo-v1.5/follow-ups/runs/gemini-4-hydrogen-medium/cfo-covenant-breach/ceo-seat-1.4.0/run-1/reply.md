### Why the owner's shortcut backfires
- **Cash goes out before savings arrive:** At 260 employees in the Netherlands, a Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*) has an advisory right on reorganisations (Article 25 WOR), and cutting 20+ roles within three months triggers collective dismissal notification and consultation with the UWV and unions (WMCO). Combined with 1–4 months of employer notice and statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*), Q2 absorbs severance and legal costs with zero payroll relief before quarter-end. *(This is not legal advice; confirm timelines with Dutch employment counsel.)*
- **The bank wants control, not theatre:** An unconsulted layoff announcement signals panic and legal risk, and risks losing scarce woodworking, CNC, and upholstery skills you will need when orders recover.

### What to do in the next three weeks
1. **Have a private conversation with the owner first—before any wider forum.** Walk them through the Q2 math. A line you can use:
   > *"Announcing 26 cuts now puts cash out the door in Q2 through transition payments and notice periods, without improving our Q2 leverage. If we want the bank's trust in three weeks, we need a 13-week cash plan, immediate working-capital cuts that actually land in Q2, and our own draws and bonuses paused first."*
2. **Take the reversible levers first:**
   - **Leaders first:** Pause or cut the owner's dividend/draw and freeze executive bonuses and pay before touching shop-floor jobs.
   - **Direct Net Debt relief for Q2:** Pull down timber/fabric/finished-goods inventory and WIP, tighten receivables, defer non-safety capex, freeze hiring, and trim agency/overtime spend (respecting WAB call-off notice rules).
   - **Operational margin:** Cut scrap and rework and review pricing on low-margin lines.
3. **Bring a credit-ready pack to the bank:** A 13-week cash forecast, base and downside covenant scenarios, the owner's commitment, and pre-agreed downside triggers—paired with a request for a temporary Q2 covenant waiver or reset (e.g., to 3.75x–4.0x).
4. **Involve the Works Council early if cuts remain needed:** Share the real numbers at the problem stage before any decision is final.
