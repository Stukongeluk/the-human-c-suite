# Board Business Case: AI Automation & Finance Operating Model (Year 1–2)

**Prepared by:** CFO  
**Status:** Draft for Board Review — *all financial figures are illustrative estimates pending GL, payroll, and vendor verification*  
**Scope:** 90-FTE Finance & Back-Office function (Accounts Payable, Reconciliations, Regulatory & Financial Reporting)

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Recommendation

**Recommendation:** Reject a target of 30% compulsory redundancies (27 FTEs) in Year 1. Instead, approve a **stage-gated, task-first automation programme** paired with **non-payroll vendor savings, a selective hiring freeze, and natural attrition/redeployment**.

- **Board's 30% Year-1 redundancy scenario (Option 1)** is projected to **cost -8.66 MSEK net in Year 1** and yield only **+0.26 MSEK cumulative net over 24 months**, while creating high regulatory, audit, and "survivor" attrition risk.
- **Recommended task-first path (Option 2)** is projected to deliver **+1.30 MSEK net in Year 1** and **+14.20 MSEK cumulative net over 24 months**, turning cash-positive in Q3 with zero compulsory redundancies.

Why the difference? Under Swedish labour law (MBL negotiations, 4-month advance notice to *Arbetsförmedlingen* for 26–100 exits, and 1–6 month LAS notice periods), meaningful payroll savings from redundancies cannot land until late Q3/Q4—while AI build costs, legal/transition costs, and contractor backfill hit immediately.

---

## 2. Baseline & Problem Framing

- **Current baseline:** 90 FTEs at an estimated **0.96 MSEK/year** fully loaded cost per FTE (0.24 MSEK/quarter; ~56 kSEK/month average base salary plus 31.42% statutory *arbetsgivaravgifter*, occupational pension, and IT/workspace), totalling **86.40 MSEK/year** (21.60 MSEK/quarter).
- **What is actually at stake:** Efficiency and scalable capacity across AP, reconciliations, and reporting—without compromising Solvency II / IFRS 17 reporting accuracy, DORA operational resilience, or segregation of duties under Finansinspektionen oversight.
- **Task suitability differs sharply by function:**
  - **Accounts Payable & Reconciliations:** High potential for AI-assisted invoice capture, coding, and transaction matching once ERP/bank feeds are clean. Human exception handling and payment sign-off remain mandatory.
  - **Reporting (IFRS 17, Solvency II, statutory close):** Low near-term replacement potential. AI can assist with variance drafts and data checks, but controllers and actuaries remain legally and audit-accountable for judgement and sign-off.

## 4. Legal, Regulatory & Evidence Check

1. **Swedish Labour Law & Collective Agreements (not legal advice—verify with employment counsel):**
   - **MBL (Co-determination Act):** Requires union negotiation *before* deciding major organisational or tech-driven changes.
   - **LAS (Employment Protection Act):** Redundancies follow *turordning* (length-of-service order per operational unit and collective agreement area, with only 3 statutory exemptions unless a list is agreed with the union). Management cannot simply select 27 specific roles to exit.
   - **Arbetsförmedlingen:** Dismissing 26–100 employees requires **4 months' advance notice**; individual notice runs **1–6 months**. Displaced staff hold **9-month re-employment priority** if you need to rehire.
   - **TRR & *Omställningsstudiestöd*:** Sweden's job security council (TRR) and transition study support (~80% of salary up to ceiling) work best when paired with planned redeployment, not rushed layoffs.
2. **External Evidence & Base Rates:**
   - **AI delivery risk:** MIT NANDA (2025) found ~95% of enterprise GenAI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact; Gartner (June 2025) projects >40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027 due to cost, unclear value, or weak risk controls.
   - **Rehiring after AI cuts:** Careerminds (Feb 2026) found two-thirds of companies that cut jobs for AI were already rehiring (52% within 6 months), and 30.9% spent more rehiring than they saved; Orgvue (2025) found 55% of leaders who made AI redundancies regretted the decision.
   - **Survivor turnover:** Trevor & Nyberg (2008) found a 1% downsizing was associated with a 31% increase in voluntary turnover the following year; Gallup estimates replacement costs at 0.5× to 2× annual salary.
3. **Insurance Controls (Solvency II, DORA, Audit):** Any AI touching AP payments, reconciliations, or regulatory reporting requires full audit trails, model-error monitoring, and human sign-off. Under the EU AI Act (Article 26(7), taking effect December 2027 per the 2026 Digital Omnibus), high-risk workplace AI also carries worker-information duties—and MBL consultation applies today.

## 5. Sensitivities & Governance

- **Break-even (Option 1):** Just **6 additional voluntary exits** (~6.0 MSEK replacement/ramp cost) or a **15% AI overrun** (~1.1 MSEK) keeps Option 1 net-negative beyond 24 months.
- **Sensitivities (Option 2):** If natural attrition is 6% instead of 11%, Year 1 payroll savings drop from +4.80 MSEK to ~+2.60 MSEK, keeping Year 1 roughly break-even and Year 2 strongly positive (+9–10 MSEK).
- **Quarterly Stage-Gates & Indicators:**
  - *Financial:* Net savings vs. budget, contractor spend, vendor spend.
  - *Operational & Risk:* Month-end close days, reconciliation backlog, audit findings, AI exception/error rate.
  - *People:* Regretted attrition, engagement, internal redeployment rate.
