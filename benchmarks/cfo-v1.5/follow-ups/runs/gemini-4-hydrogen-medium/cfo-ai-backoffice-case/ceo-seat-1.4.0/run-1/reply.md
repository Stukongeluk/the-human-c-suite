### Why the 30% Year-1 Cut Doesn't Pencil Out
A target without a diagnosis usually ends with cutting what's easiest to count, and in a regulated Swedish insurer that creates three immediate traps:
1. **Timing & Swedish law:** Under MBL (union co-determination), a mandatory **4-month advance notice to Arbetsförmedlingen** for 26–100 redundancies, and 1–6 month LAS notice periods, forced exits wouldn't take effect until late Year 1. You capture roughly 3 months of payroll (~SEK 5.7M gross on an estimated SEK 850k/FTE loaded cost) against ~SEK 9.0M in upfront implementation, licensing, and transition costs.
2. **LAS seniority rules (*turordning*):** Aside from three statutory exemptions, dismissals follow seniority order rather than management's skill preference—risking the loss of scarce IFRS 17 and Solvency II expertise.
3. **Liability stays human:** AI handles routine Accounts Payable matching and standard reconciliations well, but as *Moffatt v. Air Canada* (2024) underscored, the company remains legally liable for AI errors. Finansinspektionen and audit sign-offs still require human accountability.

### Human Impact & Execution
- **Start with a clear commitment:** Tell the team and unions upfront that nobody loses their job *because* of AI in Year 1. Like Ingka Group (IKEA)—which automated 47% of call-centre queries and retrained 8,500 staff into higher-value advisory work—this turns the people who know the edge cases into co-designers of the automation.
- **Bring unions in early:** Open MBL dialogue at the problem stage before selecting tools. Use **TRR** and Sweden's *omställningsstudiestöd* (~80% salary support for upskilling) if roles shift significantly.
- **Gate the rollout:** Pilot AP and reconciliations first (Months 3–5), measure *net* hours freed after human review, and only decide on Year-2 voluntary exits (Option 3) once real capacity is proven.
