### Why the Board's 30% Target Breaks in Practice
A percentage target without task diagnosis usually ends up cutting what is easiest to count, then paying twice to fix it:
1. **Swedish timelines delay Year 1 savings to Q3:** Under the Co-determination Act (MBL), union negotiation comes before a final decision. Dismissing 27 people (the 26–100 bracket) requires **4 months' advance notice** to *Arbetsförmedlingen*, plus 1–6 months' LAS notice. No forced-exit payroll saving lands in H1, while AI integration, legal, and severance costs hit immediately.
2. **LAS *turordning* & re-employment priority:** Selection follows length of service per unit and collective-agreement area (with only 3 exemptions), not AI-readiness—and dismissed staff hold 9 months' priority for re-employment (*företrädesrätt*) if AI underperforms.
3. **Base rates are unforgiving:** Careerminds (2026) found two in three firms that cut jobs for AI were already rehiring (52% within six months), and Trevor & Nyberg (2008) showed a 1% downsizing raises voluntary turnover by 31% the following year.

### Human & Control Plan
- **Engage unions early under MBL:** Co-design task automation and reskilling before locking in vendors.
- **Protect Solvency II & audit controls:** Keep segregation of duties, immutable audit trails, and named human sign-off on all postings, reconciliations, and filings.
- **If any surplus remains in Year 2+:** Activate **TRR** from day one of notice (over 85% of displaced Swedish workers find new work within a year, per OECD) and pair with *omställningsstudiestöd*.
