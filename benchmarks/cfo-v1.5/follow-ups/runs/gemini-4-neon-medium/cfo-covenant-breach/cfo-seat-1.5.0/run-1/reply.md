### Why the owner's proposal backfires (financially, legally, and with the bank)

- **The Q2 timing trap:** 10% of 260 employees is **26 roles**. Under Dutch law, a works council (*Ondernemingsraad*, mandatory from 50 employees) has a prior advisory right on reorganisations (Art. 25 WOR, with appeal to the Enterprise Chamber), and dismissing **20 or more people** within three months triggers advance notification and consultation with UWV and the unions (WMCO—employer-initiated settlement agreements count toward the 20). Add a one-month waiting period plus 1–4 months' notice, and exits land in **Q3–Q4**, not Q2.
- **It worsens Q2 leverage:** On an illustrative baseline scaled to a 260-employee Dutch furniture manufacturer (€40.0M revenue, €4.00M LTM EBITDA, €14.40M forecast Q2 Net Debt = **3.60x**), cutting 26 roles costs an estimated **€665k** in legal fees, *transitievergoeding* / social plan and outplacement, plus plant disruption. In Q2 that pushes leverage to **3.62x** even if the bank adds back restructuring charges—or **4.34x** if it doesn't.
- **It signals panic to a Dutch bank:** Relationship managers know Dutch employment law. Announcing 26 cuts without OR advice or WMCO consultation looks like legal exposure and loss of control, not discipline. And in a family furniture plant, losing skilled woodworkers, upholsterers or CNC operators—plus the knock-on turnover among people who stay—takes years and 0.5x–2x salary per role to rebuild.
