## 1. Why Announcing 10% Job Cuts Before the Bank Meeting Backfires

Announcing ~26 redundancies (10% of 260 employees) in the next three weeks does not solve a Q2 covenant breach and creates immediate financial, legal, and operational damage:

1. **Wrong quarter for the saving, right quarter for the cost.** Under Dutch law, a 26-person reduction triggers:
   - **Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) advisory right** (mandatory for companies with 50+ employees; **Article 25 WOR**). Announcing a cut as final before consulting the OR exposes the company to an appeal at the Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*) and a forced freeze or reversal.
   - **Collective Dismissal Notification Act (*Wet melding collectief ontslag*, WMCO):** Dismissing 20 or more employees within three months in one UWV work area (including employer-initiated settlement agreements) requires prior notification to UWV and unions, consultation, and a one-month waiting period before termination can proceed.
   - **Notice periods and severance:** Statutory employer notice runs 1–4 months depending on length of service (minus UWV procedure time, minimum 1 month), plus the statutory transition payment (*transitievergoeding*), likely a negotiated social plan, and legal/advisory fees.
   - **Net Q2 effect:** Advisory fees, severance provisions, and cash outflows hit *before* payroll savings land (typically late Q3/Q4 or later). Net Debt rises or stays flat in Q2, and unless your loan agreement explicitly adds back restructuring charges without a cap, Q2 EBITDA is hit too.
2. **Operational and retention fallout.** In furniture manufacturing, skilled makers, finishers, and machine operators are hard to replace. Research on downsizing shows a 1% workforce reduction is associated with a 31% increase in voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008), and replacing an employee typically costs 0.5x to 2x annual salary (Gallup).
3. **Wrong signal to the bank.** A relationship bank facing a 0.1x projected breach wants predictable cash generation, early transparency, and owner commitment—not a rushed, legally vulnerable announcement that risks shop-floor disruption and customer delivery delays.

## 3. Immediate Checks from Your Systems of Record (Days 1–5)

Pull these before speaking with the bank:

- **Loan agreement:** Exact definitions of Net Debt and Consolidated EBITDA (are operating leases included, are there permitted add-backs, is there an equity cure right, what is the exact Q2 test date and cure period?).
- **13-week cash forecast (ERP / treasury):** Weekly inflows and outflows, base case and downside case (e.g. -10% orders or 15-day slower collections).
- **Working capital ageing (ERP / AR & Inventory):** Overdue receivables >30 days, disputed invoices, raw-material stock days (timber, fabric, hardware), and slow-moving finished furniture lines.
- **Order book & margin by product/customer (ERP / CRM):** Is the 3.6x forecast caused by a temporary volume dip, rising input/energy costs, unapproved discounting, or working-capital build?
- **Non-payroll spend (GL / AP):** Top 20 vendors, external consultants, freight, energy, and discretionary spend that can be paused or renegotiated before Q2 close.
- **Payroll & workforce flows (HR / payroll in aggregate only):** Expected natural attrition over the next 6 months, open requisitions, flex/agency hours, and overtime—no individual names needed for the model.

## 4. Three-Week Plan to the Bank Review

### Week 1: Align Internally & Verify the Numbers
- **Day 1–2 (Private meeting with the owner):** Walk through the Q2 timing math and Dutch legal reality (Art. 25 WOR, WMCO). Agree not to announce layoffs. Put owner distributions and executive bonuses on pause first.
- **Day 3–5 (Finance & Operations):** Build the 13-week cash forecast, check the loan agreement with banking counsel if needed, and quantify working-capital and non-payroll levers with the Operations and Sales leads (with named owners and dates).

### Week 2: Build the Bank Pack & Test Downside Triggers
- **Day 6–8:** Assemble the bank pack (see Section 5). Include base and downside scenarios and the exact pre-agreed actions if the downside materialises (e.g., if orders fall another X%, activate further capex deferral, hiring freeze, or owner equity cure).
- **Day 9–10:** Dry-run the bank meeting with the owner. Send the pack to the relationship manager **5–7 days before the meeting**—banks reward early, honest warning and penalise surprises.

### Week 3: Bank Review & Governance
- **Day 11–14:** Hold the bank review. Present the operational plan to get back to <=3.4x and, if downside sensitivity still touches 3.5x, request a proactive temporary covenant reset (e.g. 3.85x for Q2 and Q3) rather than risking a technical breach.
- **Day 15:** Brief the Works Council (OR) proactively on the business outlook and the steps being taken to protect the company and jobs—before rumours start.

## 5. What the Bank Pack Should Contain (6–8 Pages Max)

1. **Executive summary:** The 3.6x Q2 forecast vs. 3.5x limit, why it happened (honest root cause), and the concrete ask (confirmation of headroom via operational plan, plus a precautionary temporary reset if needed).
2. **13-week cash flow forecast:** Weekly liquidity headroom under base and downside cases.
3. **Covenant bridge (Q2–Q4):** Line-by-line bridge from 3.6x to <=3.4x showing working capital, non-payroll spend, pricing/margin, and owner/leadership actions—each with a named owner, euro amount, and cash date.
4. **Owner & leadership commitment:** Paused dividends/drawings, deferred leadership bonuses, and (if applicable) willingness to provide an equity cure or subordinated support under downside triggers.
5. **Downside scenario & triggers:** What happens if orders drop another 10–15%, and which pre-agreed actions trigger automatically.
6. **Operational & customer health:** Order book, on-time delivery, scrap/rework, and key-customer retention—showing the core business is sound and worth backing.

## 6. Indicators to Review Monthly (Money, Customers, and People)

Track these together in the monthly management pack so short-term cash fixes do not quietly erode the business:

- **Financial:** Rolling 13-week cash headroom, Net Debt / LTM EBITDA, debtor days (DSO), inventory days (DIO), gross margin by product line.
- **Operational & Customer:** On-time delivery rate, quality/rework rate, preventive maintenance completion, key-account retention.
- **People:** Voluntary (especially regretted) attrition, sickness absence, overtime hours, and apprentice/key-skill retention.
