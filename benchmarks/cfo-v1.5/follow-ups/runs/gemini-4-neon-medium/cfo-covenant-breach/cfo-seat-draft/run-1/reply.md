**Do not announce 10% job cuts before the bank meeting.** A 0.1x covenant gap (3.6x against 3.5x) is roughly **2.8% of Net Debt** or **2.9% of LTM EBITDA** (about **5.6%** of Net Debt, or a **3% + 3%** mix of debt reduction and EBITDA lift, to reach 3.4x with headroom). ### Why 10% cuts backfire on Q2—financially and legally

- **The cash and P&L timing is backwards for Q2.** At 260 employees in the Netherlands, you have a mandatory Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) with an advisory right on reorganisations (**Article 25 WOR**); announcing a decision as final before consulting them risks an Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*) appeal. Cutting 26 roles also crosses the **20-person collective dismissal threshold (WMCO)** within three months, requiring prior notification and consultation with UWV and the unions (even employer-initiated settlement agreements count). Add 1–4 months of statutory employer notice, the statutory transition payment (*transitievergoeding*), a likely social plan, and legal fees, and your cash outflows land in Q2–Q3 while net payroll savings do not arrive until late Q3/Q4 at the earliest. *(Not legal advice—confirm exact timelines and any applicable CAO rules with Dutch employment counsel.)*
- **You risk losing the wrong people.** In furniture manufacturing, skilled makers, finishers, and machine operators take years to replace. Downsizing is also associated with sharply higher voluntary turnover among the people who stay (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008), and replacing skilled staff typically costs 0.5x–2x annual salary (Gallup).

### What the owner wants vs. what the bank actually wants

The owner's instinct is understandable: they want to look decisive, keep control of the family business, and not walk into the bank review empty-handed. That instinct is right; the lever is wrong.

A relationship bank looking at a **0.1x** projected breach does not want a rushed, legally vulnerable layoff announcement that risks shop-floor disruption and customer delays. It wants:
1. **No surprises**—the numbers and plan sent 5–7 days before the meeting.
2. **Liquidity certainty**—a rolling **13-week cash forecast** with base and downside cases.
3. **Fast, Q2-effective levers**—actions that actually reduce Q2 Net Debt or lift Q2 EBITDA before the test date.
4. **Owner commitment**—paused family dividends/drawings and deferred leadership bonuses (and, if needed, willingness to support a small subordinated loan or equity cure).
5. **A clean ask**—either confirmation that the operational plan clears 3.5x, or a proactive temporary reset (e.g., 3.85x for Q2–Q3) paired with agreed downside triggers.

### What to do in the next three weeks

1. **Have a private conversation with the owner in the next 48 hours**—before any wider management meeting. Frame it in family-business terms: protecting the firm's independence, the family's name in the region, and the skilled workforce you will need when demand recovers (family firms that avoid reactive layoffs tend to outperform over the cycle; Kachaner, Stalk & Bloch, *HBR* 2012).
   - *A line you can use:* "I completely agree we need to walk into the bank showing we're in control. I ran the Q2 timing on a 10% cut: with the OR, WMCO, notice periods, and severance, the costs hit Q2 while the savings don't land until Q4—so it actually pushes our Q2 leverage *above* 3.6x. If we pause distributions and bonuses, tighten working capital and non-payroll spend, and send the bank a 13-week cash plan next week, we show real seriousness without hurting Q2 or losing skilled people."
2. **Check the loan agreement first.** Confirm the exact definitions of Net Debt and EBITDA, whether operating leases or specific add-backs apply, and whether you have an equity-cure right or cure period.
3. **Close the 0.1x gap with Q2-speed levers first** (pulling exact figures from your ERP and GL):
   - **Working capital & capex:** Collect overdue receivables, resolve billing disputes, trim excess raw-material and slow-moving finished-goods stock, and defer non-essential capex. *Do not* stretch small local suppliers or cut preventive maintenance.
   - **Non-payroll spend & pricing:** Challenge energy, freight, packaging, trade-show/marketing, and consultant spend; stop unapproved dealer discounts and bill customisations consistently.
   - **Owners & leaders first:** Pause family dividends/drawings and defer executive bonuses immediately.
   - **Headcount only through flows (if demand is structurally lower):** Hiring freeze (protecting scarce trades and apprentices), natural attrition, and internal redeployment. *(Note: Dutch short-time work, werktijdverkorting, currently covers only calamities outside normal business risk; check with counsel whether the pending crisis-retention bill has taken effect.)*
4. **Brief the Works Council early** once the operational plan is set, so rumours don't start on the shop floor.
