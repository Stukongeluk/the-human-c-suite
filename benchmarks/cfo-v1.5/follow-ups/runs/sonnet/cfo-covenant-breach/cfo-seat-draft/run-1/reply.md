**Don't announce the cuts. Go to the bank first, within days, with a covenant plan that closes a 0.1x gap without job losses, and bring the owner's own contribution to the table.** A 10% cut (about 26 people) would not fix the Q2 test and may make it worse.

**Why the cuts don't solve the covenant problem**

- *Timing.* With 26 jobs in one region, you are in collective-dismissal territory (20 or more within three months, WMCO): notify UWV and the unions, and consult them. You also have a works council (mandatory from 50 employees) with an advisory right on a reorganisation (art. 25 WOR). It must be consulted before the decision is taken. Announcing first is a legal risk and a trust problem, and counsel must confirm the route. After consultation, a UWV procedure and notice periods of one to four months follow, so most exits land in Q3 or later.
- *Cost.* Transition payments and advisers fall in Q1 and Q2, ahead of any saving. Illustrative estimates, to be replaced with your payroll data: 26 people at about €65k loaded is about €1.7m a year gross, against about €0.65m of one-off costs. Unless your covenant definition adds back restructuring costs (check the facility agreement), Q2 EBITDA gets worse, not better.
- *Signal.* A bank hears "10% cuts, three weeks before review" as "the order book is weaker than you said". Showing them a plan is more persuasive than showing them a headcount number.
- *Hidden costs.* Downsizing is associated with higher voluntary turnover afterwards, and skilled furniture makers are hard to replace.

**What the gap actually is**

3.6x against 3.5x is about 2.9% too much leverage. Put your numbers in: at, say, €10m EBITDA and €36m net debt (placeholder), you need about €0.3m more EBITDA or about €1.0m less net debt by the Q2 test date. That is small, and it can be closed by working through the levers below in order.

**Plan, in order, before any job goes**

1. **Cash and working capital (reduces net debt).** Collect receivables and chase disputed invoices. Right-size raw material and finished goods stock, and time capex. Do not squeeze small suppliers. Inventory and debtors alone can cover most of the €1.0m in a manufacturer your size, if the data supports it (to verify).
2. **Waste and spend.** Energy contracts, freight, packaging, agency labour, consultants, licences. Renegotiate before cancelling.
3. **Price and mix.** Review discounts nobody approved, price increases where value supports them, and low-margin lines.
4. **Owner and leaders first.** Dividends and owner drawings stop. Leadership bonuses are suspended or cut, starting with yours and the owner's. This is also what the bank will want to see, and it is the honest version of "showing we're serious".
5. **Hiring freeze and natural attrition.** With 260 people, normal turnover frees up positions without anyone being dismissed. Show expected flows and which roles must still be filled.
6. **Financing.** Ask for a covenant waiver or a temporary reset (headroom of 0.2-0.3x for two quarters), or an equity cure from the family. A fee and a margin step-up are likely, and cheaper than a restructuring.
7. **Only if demand is weak and the levers above fall short.** Reduced hours for a dip that will pass (note that Dutch short-time work currently covers only calamities outside normal business risk; check whether the new crisis scheme has passed). Then voluntary exits. Involuntary cuts last, once, with works council consultation and support from the day of notice.

**For the bank meeting (three weeks)**

- A 13-week cash forecast, updated weekly.
- Covenant headroom by quarter: base case and downside (for example, 5% lower revenue).
- What each lever contributes, by quarter, net of its costs, with owner and confidence.
- What the owners and leaders are contributing.
- A request: either a waiver or reset, or a stated cure if Q2 comes in at 3.6x.
- If cuts are ever needed, a contingency trigger ("if X happens, we start a consultation"), not an announcement.

**Handling the owner**

His instinct, show the bank you are serious, is right. Steelman it: banks do want evidence of decisive action. But the best evidence is the owner putting his own money and bonus on the table first, and a plan with numbers. A visible 10% cut that triggers consultation, a possible works council dispute, regional press and a weaker Q2 does the opposite. For a family firm that values its name in the region, there is also a reputation cost that does not show in the model. Offer him a one-page comparison: "announce 10%" vs "covenant plan" vs "do nothing", by quarter.

**What I would check first (these change the numbers)**

- The exact covenant definition: net or gross debt, EBITDA adjustments, test dates, cure rights.
- How much cash and debtor/stock release is realistic before Q2.
- Whether demand is falling, and whether the problem is structural or temporary.
