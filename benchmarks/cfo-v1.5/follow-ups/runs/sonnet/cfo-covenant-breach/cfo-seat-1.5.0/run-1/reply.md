Go to the bank first, with a plan that closes the gap in cash and holds job cuts back as a trigger, not a headline.** The gap is small and the cuts would arrive too late to fix it. At 3.6x against 3.5x you need EBITDA about 2.9% higher or net debt about 2.8% lower. Ten percent of 260 people is 26 jobs, roughly €1.7m a year, more than three times what you need.

**Why the announcement doesn't work**

- **The savings arrive after the test.** In the Netherlands your works council (mandatory from 50 employees) has an advisory right on a reorganisation (art. 25 WOR). It must be asked for advice before the decision, in time to change it. 26 dismissals within three months in one UWV area also triggers a WMCO notification to UWV and the unions. A realistic path, by my estimate: council advice 4 to 8 weeks, then the UWV procedure, then notice of 1 to 4 months. That puts first exits around May to July. The 30 June test would see about €0.1m of saving.
- **The one-off costs hit first.** Statutory transition payments, paid notice, outplacement and advisers come to roughly €20k to €30k per person, so about €0.5m to €0.8m (estimate; it depends on tenure). Announcing to the people affected can also force a restructuring provision straight away (check with your auditor). Unless your facility agreement adds restructuring costs back, your covenant EBITDA could get worse before it gets better.
- **It breaks the process.** Announcing before the works council has advised makes the consultation look like a formality. That invites an Enterprise Chamber challenge and an angry workforce, in a family firm where the name counts in the region.
- **The bank may read it as panic.** Banks want to see headroom, a 13-week cash forecast, scenarios, and what the owner is contributing. Cuts alone don't show that, and they hurt the output and quality your EBITDA depends on.

The owner's instinct to show seriousness is right. The best proof is the owner's own contribution, not 26 colleagues.

**What the recommended plan contains** (each lever to be sized with your numbers; I count none of them as closing the gap until it is)

1. **Cash:** inventory (a 5% to 8% release is about €0.3m to €0.5m), receivables (each day of DSO is about €0.12m at €45m revenue, my assumed revenue), customer deposits, and deferral of non-essential capex (about €0.2m to €0.4m). Safety and maintenance stay protected. Don't squeeze small suppliers.
2. **Owner and leaders first:** pause dividends and owner's drawings, and cut leadership pay and bonuses for the year. Net debt falls directly.
3. **Price and spend:** a 1% price rise is about €0.45m if volumes hold. Renegotiate energy and materials before cancelling anything.
4. **Hiring freeze and attrition:** at 6% to 10% attrition, about 9 to 15 people leave by June, which is a few tens of thousands in the test but about €0.7m to €1m run-rate if you don't refill. Redeploy and reskill within that.
5. **Financing:** ask for an amendment, a covenant holiday or a step-up of the limit now, rather than after a breach. Ask whether the facility has an equity cure, and have the owner say they are willing to inject capital.
6. **Contingency:** put a dated trigger in the plan. If the monthly covenant bridge slips by a set amount by March, you start works-council consultation, with voluntary exits and reduced hours (agreed with the council) considered before any dismissals. That shows resolve without announcing something you can't yet justify.
