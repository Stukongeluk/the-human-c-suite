# Board paper: AI in finance and back-office operations

**Prepared by:** CFO · **Decision needed:** approval of a phased programme and its guardrails · **Status:** draft for CFO review. Every figure marked (E) is an estimate built on placeholder assumptions in section 7; replace them with ledger, HR and vendor data before this goes to the board.

## 1. Recommendation

Approve a three-year programme to automate accounts payable, reconciliations and reporting, with a target of **about 25% lower finance and back-office capacity cost by year 3 (about 22 of 90 FTE, range 12 to 33) (E)**, delivered through attrition, a hiring freeze in affected teams and redeployment, with no redundancies in year 1.

A 30% reduction (27 FTE) in the next 12 months is not supported by the evidence we have. Our own estimate puts the work AI can safely remove in year 1 at about 6 FTE (7%) (E). The remaining 21 FTE of work would still exist and would have to be done by fewer, more stretched people or by consultants, in a function that closes our books and produces our regulatory reporting.

## 2. What the board is trying to achieve

The request is framed as a headcount number. The underlying goal needs confirming: a lower expense ratio, a cost-income target, a margin commitment, or capacity to absorb growth without hiring? The answer changes the plan.

- If the goal is **cost**, the programme below delivers about SEK 20m a year at maturity (E), plus savings outside finance (vendor, licence and process waste) that we have not yet sized.
- If the goal is **speed and quality** (faster close, fewer breaks, better data for underwriting and claims), the programme delivers that earlier than it delivers savings.
- Finance is also the function that has to keep working through IFRS 17 and Solvency II reporting, DORA operational-resilience duties and audit. Capacity cuts there carry regulatory risk as well as cost risk.

Freed hours are net of the work AI creates: reviewing outputs, handling exceptions, maintaining rules and data, and model oversight. We assume that eats 20% of gross savings (30% in the low case, 15% in the high case) (E). Pilot results usually look better than production, so these shares must be re-measured on our own invoices and ledgers in the first 90 days.

Savings turn into cash only when a post stays vacant. At about 9% annual turnover in finance (about 7 leavers a year, E) a hiring freeze in these teams absorbs roughly 21 posts over three years, which matches the mid case. If turnover is lower, the saving arrives later. If it is higher, we must stay careful not to lose the people we most need.

## 6. People, law and transition

- **Commitment.** Nobody leaves because of this programme in year 1. Roles will change. After year 1 the board reviews the evidence before any further commitment is made. We should only promise what we can keep, so the promise is stated as year 1 plus a review.
- **Reskilling and redeployment.** Staff with ledger and process knowledge are our best candidates for exception handling, controls, data quality, analysis for underwriting and claims, and supervising the automation. Paid learning time is budgeted in the build cost.
- **Co-design.** Finance staff and their union representatives help choose and tune the tools from the first workshop, before any vendor is selected. They know the edge cases.
- **Law (not legal advice; confirm with employment counsel).** In Sweden the Co-determination Act (MBL) requires negotiation with the unions before major changes in operations. Any later redundancies would follow the Employment Protection Act (LAS): last in, first out within the collective agreement area and operational unit, so we cannot choose to keep our strongest people, and the youngest and often most digitally skilled would go first. Five or more redundancies require advance notice to the Public Employment Service. Job security councils such as TRR support affected staff from the day of notice. Most finance back-office use of AI is not high-risk under the EU AI Act, but tools that evaluate or allocate work to individuals can be, so we do not use them that way. Staff must be told when they interact with an AI system, and national consultation rules apply to any monitoring.
- **Not a measure of individuals.** Progress is reported per team and process, never per person.

## 7. Assumptions to replace with real data

- Team split (24 / 22 / 28 / 16), loaded cost per FTE (SEK 0.9m: salary plus employer contributions and pension), turnover (9%), severance (about nine months of cost per leaver), build cost (SEK 9m) and run cost (SEK 3m a year). All are placeholders.
- Share of hours AI can take, by process: to be measured on our own invoices, bank feeds and close calendar in a 90-day pilot.
- Whether the function carries outsourced or interim staff whose contracts could end earlier and more easily; those people deserve the same notice and honesty.

## 8. Risks and a pre-mortem

Imagine it is December next year and the programme failed. The likely reasons:

1. Pilot accuracy did not hold in production, and exception volumes grew. (Most enterprise AI pilots have not shown measurable profit impact so far, and analysts expect a large share of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027.)
2. Close quality dropped: late closes, reconciliation breaks, audit or regulator findings.
3. The people who knew the exceptions left first.
4. Data quality and system integration cost more than planned.
5. An AI error reached a supplier payment or a regulatory report with no human check. A named human owner approves every automated control and every external report.

Evidence for caution: Klarna said in 2024 that its AI did the work of 700 agents, then in 2025 its CEO said cost had been too dominant a factor and quality had suffered, and it began hiring people again. In a 2025 Orgvue survey of senior leaders who had made people redundant because of AI, 55% said they had made wrong decisions (a vendor survey; treat it as a signal, not a rate).

## 9. Metrics (reviewed quarterly)

- **Money:** cost per invoice processed, cost per reconciliation, close duration, run cost against plan, vacancies left open versus savings booked.
- **Quality:** payment errors, reconciliation breaks and ageing, audit and regulator findings, restatements, exception rate.
- **People:** voluntary turnover in finance, internal moves, hours of paid learning taken, an anonymous pulse on workload and trust. Reported by team only.

## 10. Leadership accountability

The finance leadership team owns the result. Its variable pay is tied to delivery of quality and control metrics and to the commitments in section 6, not to headcount reduction. If results fall short, leadership bonuses are reduced before any frontline role is affected.

## 11. Decision requested

1. Approve the 90-day pilot (budget SEK 2m, E) to measure real automation rates on AP and reconciliations.
2. Approve the hiring freeze in affected teams and the redeployment and reskilling budget.
3. Agree to review at day 90 and at 12 months, with the 25% three-year target as the planning figure and 30% as a stretch, not a commitment.
