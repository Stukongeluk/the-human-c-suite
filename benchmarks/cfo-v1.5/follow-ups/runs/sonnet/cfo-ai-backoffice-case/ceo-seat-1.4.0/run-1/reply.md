I can't build an honest case for replacing 30% of the team next year, but I can build one for about 25% lower capacity cost by year 3 (roughly 22 of 90 FTE, range 12 to 33), reached through attrition and redeployment. In year 1 the realistic figure is about 6 FTE (7%). All of these numbers are my estimates on placeholder assumptions, and the draft board paper is saved next to this reply as `board-paper.md`.

**Why 30% next year doesn't hold up**

- At task level, AI takes the most from accounts payable (about 36% of hours, net of the review and exception work it creates), then reconciliations (32%), reporting (20%) and the rest (8%). That adds up to about 25% at maturity, and it takes about three years to get there.
- Getting 27 people out in 12 months would also be slow in practice. In Sweden that means union negotiation under MBL first, then notice to Arbetsförmedlingen four months ahead for 26 to 100 people, then individual notice periods. Under LAS, last in, first out decides who goes, so you lose the people you most want to keep.
- In year 1 the cut would still leave about 21 FTE of work to do. My rough numbers put the year-1 net at about SEK -17m once severance and build costs are counted, against about SEK -2m for the phased route.
- The function closes your books, produces IFRS 17 and Solvency II reporting and supports audit. Control failures there cost more than the salaries saved.
- Klarna said in 2024 that its AI did the work of 700 agents, and in 2025 its CEO said cost had been weighted too heavily and quality had suffered. In one 2025 survey (a vendor survey, so a signal rather than a rate), 55% of leaders who made AI-driven redundancies said they had made wrong decisions.

**Options**

| Option | Cash effect (SEK m, estimate) | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| A. Do nothing | 0 | Falls behind on cost and speed |
| B. Cut 30% next year | about +12 in year 1 before about 27 of severance and build cost; +24 a year later | Close delays, control failures, knowledge loss |
| **C. Phased, attrition-led (recommended)** | +5.5, +13.2, +20.3 over years 1 to 3; net -2.0, +7.2, +17.3 after SEK 9m build and SEK 3m a year run | Slower than the board wants; relies on vacancies arising |
| D. C plus voluntary exits | C plus about 0.9 per leaver | Losing people you wanted to keep |

Option C assumes about 9% annual turnover, which frees about 7 posts a year, so a hiring freeze in these teams absorbs most of the reduction. It needs a day-90 measurement on your own invoices and ledgers before the board commits to any number.

**What I'd put to the board**

1. A 90-day pilot (about SEK 2m) to measure real automation rates on AP and reconciliations.
2. A hiring freeze in affected teams, plus paid reskilling for exception handling, controls and analysis.
3. A year-1 promise that nobody leaves because of the programme, with a review at day 90 and month 12. I would not promise more than that, because you can only promise what you can keep.
4. Finance leadership bonuses tied to quality and control outcomes, not headcount. If results fall short, leadership pay is reduced before any frontline role is affected.
5. Staff and union representatives in the tool design from the first workshop, which MBL requires you to consult on anyway.

**The trap in the request**

A target with no diagnosis usually ends with cutting whatever is easiest to count. I've read "30%" as the board's solution. The problem underneath is probably an expense ratio, a margin commitment or capacity for growth, and each of those leads to a different plan. Cost is also not only a headcount question: vendor, licence and process waste sit above headcount and I haven't sized them.

**Two questions that would change my answer**

1. What is the board's actual goal (expense ratio or cost-income target, a figure by a date), and is the business currently profitable and cash-positive? If a covenant or a hard deadline is driving this, the options change.
2. What do you actually have: the real split of the 90 across AP, reconciliations and reporting, current turnover, and any interim or outsourced staff?

If the board insists on 30%, the defensible version is "30% as a stretch, 25% as the plan, reviewed at day 90". Please check the legal points with employment counsel, since this is not legal advice.
