I can build you a case the board can rely on, but not one that says AI replaces 30% of the 90 people next year. On my estimates the work supports about 12-28% of capacity (central case about 20%, roughly 18 FTE-equivalents), reaching payroll by end of 2028 through attrition, a hiring freeze and redeployment. A 27-person cut in 2027 saves about SEK 4.8m that year and costs about SEK 16m one-off, so it ends 2027 around SEK 26m negative. The full draft board paper is in business-case.md in this folder.

All numbers below are my estimates scaled to a 90-FTE team (loaded cost SEK 0.85m per FTE, an assumed split of the team, 10% attrition). Please replace them with your payroll aggregates, ERP volumes and vendor quotes before the board sees them.

**Why 30% in 2027 doesn't hold**
- **Task sizing:** AP (about 22 FTE) and reconciliations (about 20) are the real opportunity, with 35-55% and 30-50% of tasks automatable. Reporting, closing and the judgement work behind IFRS 17 and Solvency II move far less. After exceptions and review, I get 11-26 FTE-equivalents, so 30% sits above the top of the range.
- **Swedish timing:** union negotiation (MBL), a four-month notification to Arbetsförmedlingen for 26-100 dismissals, and notice of one to six months mean a January decision gives first exits in Q3, mostly Q4. LAS selection by length of service also means you cannot pick the roles the AI took over, and you may keep the most expensive people.
- **Evidence:** surveys of leaders who cut for AI found 55% of them regretted decisions (Orgvue) and two in three were already rehiring (Careerminds). Both are vendor surveys, so they are a signal, not a rate. About 95% of gen-AI pilots showed no P&L impact (MIT NANDA, a contested method).

**Options (SEK m, estimates)**

| | Do nothing | 27 exits in 2027 | Gated plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net 2027 | 0 | about -26 | about -9 to -11 |
| Net 2028 run-rate | 0 | +13 to +19 | +5 to +12 |
| Cumulative to end 2028 | 0 | about -13 to -7 | about -5 to +3 |
| Reversible? | n/a | No | Yes, at each gate |

If 73% of the 27 roles come back as rehires or contractors, the 2028 saving is gone. Neither option pays back inside 2027, so if the board needs a 2027 cash number, we should say what the number is for and look at spend, working capital and the hiring freeze first.

**What I recommend**
1. Approve a gated AI programme. Gate 1 (end Q2): measured pilot results on AP and reconciliations, and no payroll saving booked before it. Gate 2 (end Q4): capacity freed for two consecutive closes with no worse audit findings. Only then does a range become a headcount commitment.
2. Release capacity through attrition, a freeze with named exceptions, redeployment and reskilling. Protect junior roles.
3. Open talks with the unions and employee representatives now, with these numbers.
4. Keep controls in: segregation of duties, audit trail, human sign-off on payments, early review with compliance, internal audit and your auditors. Check Finansinspektionen and DORA requirements on ICT and outsourcing.
5. Leaders go first: if 2027 misses because the programme takes longer, leadership bonuses move before frontline jobs, with no bonus for headcount reduction as such.

If a cut is still needed after Gate 2, do it once, voluntary exits first, TRR support from the day of notice, with the timeline built in. This is not legal advice; Swedish employment counsel needs to confirm the process and the collective agreement terms.

**Two questions that would change the numbers**
- What is the 30% for: expense ratio, margin, a cost-per-policy target, or a date? If the real need is a 2027 cash number, other levers come first.
- What do payroll and the collective agreement say about actual loaded cost, attrition and severance terms?
