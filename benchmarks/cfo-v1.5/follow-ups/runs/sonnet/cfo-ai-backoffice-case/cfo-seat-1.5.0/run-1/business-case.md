# Board paper (draft): AI in finance and back-office, 2027 plan

Status: draft for CFO review. Every figure is an ESTIMATE scaled to a 90-FTE team and must be replaced with payroll aggregates, ERP volumes and quotes before the board sees it. SEK m unless stated. Not legal, tax or investment advice; employment-law steps need Swedish counsel and the union side.

## 1. Recommendation

Approve a gated AI programme with a range of 12-28% of team capacity (central estimate about 20%, about 18 FTE-equivalents) reaching payroll through attrition, a hiring freeze with named exceptions and redeployment by end of 2028. Do not approve a 30% (27-person) reduction in 2027: the task sizing does not support it, the law and notice periods mean it would save almost nothing in 2027, and it would cost more in 2027 than the staged plan. Turn the plan into a headcount commitment only at the gate below.

## 2. What the board is actually trying to fix

"30%" is a solution. The underlying question is probably one of: a cost-income or expense-ratio target, a margin target, or competitiveness in claims and back-office cost per policy. The case should say which, and by when. If the target is cost per policy or expense ratio, spend outside payroll and process redesign count too (section 7).

## 5. Timing in Sweden: why B saves little in 2027

- Negotiation with the unions before the decision (MBL).
- Dismissing 26-100 people must be notified to Arbetsförmedlingen at least four months ahead.
- Notice runs one to six months by service.
- LAS fixes the selection order by length of service per operational unit and collective-agreement area; every employer may exempt three people. Management cannot choose which 27 roles go, so the people who leave may not be the people whose tasks the AI took over, and the average cost of those leaving will not equal the average cost of the team.
- Dismissed staff have re-employment priority for nine months (people with more than 12 months' service in the last three years).
- Job security council support (TRR for private-sector white-collar staff) starts at notice and is funded through the collective agreement; the insurer's own top-up is on top. Check which agreement applies.

So with a January decision, the first exits land in Q3 at best, most in Q4. That is the 4.8 in the table.

## 6. Evidence on cutting jobs for AI before the proof

- Orgvue (survey of 1,000+ senior leaders, 2025): 39% had made people redundant because of AI, and 55% of those said they had made wrong decisions.
- Careerminds (600 HR leaders, February 2026): two in three companies that cut for AI were already rehiring, 52% of those within six months, and for 30.9% rehiring cost more than the saving. Both are vendor surveys, so a signal, not a rate.
- MIT NANDA (2025): about 95% of enterprise gen-AI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact. The method is contested; direction only.
- Gartner (June 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027 on cost, unclear value or weak risk controls.
- Trevor & Nyberg: a 1% downsizing went with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year.

## 8. Gates and controls

- Gate 1, end Q2 2027: measured pilot results on AP and reconciliations against a baseline: touchless rate, error rate, exception rate, reviewer hours. No payroll saving is booked before this.
- Gate 2, end Q4 2027: capacity actually freed for two consecutive closes, with no rise in audit findings or close delays. Only here does a range become a headcount commitment.
- Controls: segregation of duties, audit trail, human sign-off on payments and on anything affecting policyholders or staff; model-risk and ICT third-party review with compliance and the actuarial and internal audit functions. As an insurer you are under Finansinspektionen supervision (check requirements on outsourcing and ICT third-party risk, including DORA). Auditors should see the design early.
- AI Act: finance-process tools are mostly not high-risk, but any tool that monitors, allocates tasks to or evaluates staff is; the duty to inform workers before using high-risk systems applies from 2 December 2027 (date moved by the 2026 Digital Omnibus). Swedish co-determination applies already.

## 9. People plan

- Say plainly to the finance team what the plan is: no cut is planned from this case; capacity is released through attrition, freeze and redeployment; the gates decide the rest.
- Bring unions and employee representatives in now, with these numbers, while the plan can change.
- Redeploy to where the work is growing (data, controls, analysis, the close, claims-side finance). Fund reskilling (about 2.5 in 2027, estimate). Protect junior roles: they are where the next controllers come from and where AI layoffs were reported to hit hardest.
- Leaders first: if the 2027 expense target is missed because the programme takes longer, leadership bonuses move before frontline jobs. Do not pay any bonus for headcount reduction as such.
- If a cut is still needed after Gate 2: once, with voluntary exits first, TRR support from day one, and notice and consultation timelines built into the plan.

## 10. Key assumptions and sensitivities

- Loaded cost per FTE SEK 0.85m (salary about 0.62 plus charges, pension, overhead): replace with payroll aggregate. Every SEK 0.1m changes the B run-rate by 2.7 and the C central case by about 1.8.
- Natural attrition 10% a year (about 9 FTE): replace with the HR figure. It sets C's ceiling.
- Realisation 60-80% of sized capacity; sizing shares in section 3 are expert estimates, not measurements.
- Severance and agreement payments at 9 months' pay: Sweden has no statutory severance; actual terms depend on the collective and individual agreements.
- The AI programme costs are placeholders pending vendor quotes.

What would change this recommendation: a pilot showing more than 50% touchless on AP and reconciliations with falling error rates; attrition well above 10%; or a regulatory or financing deadline that makes a 2027 cash saving unavoidable (then we revisit the ladder: financing, spend, hiring freeze, shared sacrifice and voluntary exits before involuntary exits).
