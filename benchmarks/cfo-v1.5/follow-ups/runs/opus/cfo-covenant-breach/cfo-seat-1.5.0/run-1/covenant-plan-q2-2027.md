# Leverage covenant, Q2 2027: options and bank meeting plan

*Draft for the CFO and the owner. Prepared 20 November 2026, before the bank review in three weeks.*

**Recommendation:** do not announce job cuts before the bank meeting. Go to the bank early with a sized cash and margin plan, the family's own contribution and a request for covenant headroom against the downside case, plus an agreed trigger for a capacity review if the downside comes. On illustrative figures this gets the Q2 2027 test to about 3.0–3.1x without dismissals. A 10% cut lands at 3.5–4.0x in the central case, because Dutch law sets the timetable: the costs come before 30 June and most of the saving comes after.

**Read every euro figure below as an illustrative estimate.** They are scaled to a 260-person Dutch furniture manufacturer, using revenue of about €45m, a forecast LTM EBITDA at 30 June 2027 of €4.0m and forecast net debt of €14.4m (3.6x). Replace them with the ledger, the forecast and the loan agreement before anything goes to the bank. The ratios scale. The euro amounts do not.

How the Option A cases are built. The gross saving is €125.7k a month: 26 FTE at an estimated €58k fully loaded each. Best case: exits from 1 April (three months saved), restructuring costs added back to covenant EBITDA, low costs, all paid by June. ## 3. Why the 10% cut lands after the test

Cutting 26 people at one site in three months is a collective dismissal (WMCO), and a works council is mandatory at 260 employees.

| Step | Fastest plausible | Typical |
|---|---|---|
| Advice request to the works council on the intended decision, with the alternatives (art. 25 WOR). WMCO notification to UWV and the unions before the decision is final | early Dec 2026 | early Dec 2026 |
| Works council advice. Christmas falls in this window, and they may hire an expert at the company's cost | mid-Jan 2027 | Feb 2027 |
| If the decision departs from the advice: one-month suspension, and the works council may appeal to the Enterprise Chamber | — | Mar 2027 |
| UWV dismissal permits: about 4 weeks once the file is complete, longer with extra rounds. UWV asks what else you did to avoid dismissals, and treats applications as premature until the advice process is finished | mid-Feb 2027 | Mar–Apr 2027 |
| Notice: 1–4 months depending on service, minus the procedure time, with at least 1 month left. Selection by the reflection principle (afspiegeling), not by choice | exits from Apr 2027 | exits Jun–Aug 2027 |

Settlement agreements count towards the 20, carry a one-month waiting period after the WMCO notification (unless the unions agree), and usually cost more than the statutory payment. Short-time work (WTV) only covers calamities. The new crisis scheme (Wet personeelsbehoud bij crisis) is still in the Tweede Kamer, so it can't be planned on.

Not in the table, all estimates:
- **Work that stays.** The gross saving is real only if volumes have fallen by about 10% for good. Otherwise the work comes back as overtime, temporary staff or lost orders.
- **Rehiring when demand returns.** Replacement costs half to twice annual salary, about €21–86k per person. If 13 people have to be rehired, that is €0.28–1.11m.
- **People who leave next.** A 1% downsizing has been associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year. In a tight Dutch labour market, skilled furniture makers are the people with options.
- **Revenue and supply risk.** A public announcement can reach dealers and trade credit insurers. If insurers cut your suppliers' limits, the suppliers shorten your payment terms.

Leverage for B = (14,400 − net debt levers − EBITDA levers + own costs) / (4,000 + EBITDA levers). EBITDA gains count as cash, before tax. Low case: 13,416 / 4,287 = 3.13x. High case: 12,562 / 4,595 = 2.73x. **Plan on the low case and show the bank that.**

These are not counted until confirmed:
- stopping any dividend already in the forecast;
- €0.5–1.0m from the family as an equity cure or a subordinated shareholder loan, if the agreement lets it count. Adding €0.75m to the low case gives 2.95x in the base case and 3.36x in the downside.

## 5. The bank meeting

**Before:** call the relationship manager this week or next, so the problem isn't a surprise in the room.

**The pack:**
1. One page: what changed and why (volume, price, mix, raw materials, cao wages, energy, capex or stock), and what you forecast for each test date to mid-2028, in a base and a downside case.
2. A 13-week cash forecast, updated weekly, in base and downside, showing what each lever buys and when.
3. The lever table above with your real numbers, owners and monthly milestones. Present the low case as the plan.
4. What the family and the leadership put in (section 6).
5. The ask: a reset, for example 4.0x through Q4 2027, 3.75x at Q1 2028 and 3.5x from Q2 2028, set so the downside case with the plan still passes. Or, at minimum, a waiver for the Q2 2027 test agreed now. In return, offer monthly reporting, no distributions while leverage is above about 3.25x, and a fee.
6. The trigger plan (section 8).

**The bank's view, fairly stated:** it wants proof that management sees the problem, has actions it controls and has owners who stand behind the company. Its questions on any cost cut will be: when does the saving land, what does it cost first, where is the works council, and what happens to revenue? Option A has weak answers to all four before 30 June.

## 6. Owner and leaders first

- No distribution for FY2026, and none while leverage is above about 3.25x.
- 2026–27 management bonuses and the owner-director fee on hold until the plan is delivered.
- A family contribution if the agreement allows one to count. For a family firm this is the clearest sign of seriousness a bank gets, and it protects what the family actually owns: the firm and its name in the region.

## 7. Works council and people

- Inform the works council now with the real numbers. You can ask for confidentiality (art. 20 WOR). Ask for their ideas on cost, stock and capacity before anything is decided.
- No announcement about jobs. If people ask: "We are working on a plan with the bank. No decisions about jobs have been made, and if any change to jobs were ever needed, the works council would be involved first."
- Protect training, apprentices, safety and maintenance. Cutting them now creates next year's cost.

## 8. Trigger plan: if the downside comes

Review monthly, with a formal check at the end of February on January–February actuals:
- **Money:** forecast leverage at each test date, 13-week cash headroom, order intake against plan, gross margin, inventory days, DSO.
- **People:** regretted leavers, absence, temporary and agency hours, overtime.
- **Trigger:** order intake 8–10% below plan for three months, together with a forecast that shows structural excess capacity into 2028. If that happens, start a capacity review with the works council by asking advice on an intended decision with the alternatives open: annualised or reduced hours where the cao allows, redeployment, attrition, voluntary departures, and only then dismissals, done once and decently. Set the number from the order book, not from a percentage.

## 10. Assumptions, sensitivities and what would change this

- **Key assumptions:** LTM EBITDA €4.0m and net debt €14.4m at 30 June 2027; 26 FTE at €58k fully loaded; transition payment of a third of a month's pay per year of service (on average 4–8 years for those selected); levers sized as shown.
- **Most sensitive:** (1) whether the agreement lets you add back restructuring costs, which swings Option A by about 0.5x; (2) how much of the stock and receivables release is real; (3) whether the downside case becomes the base case.
- **What would change the recommendation:**
  - If volumes have fallen for good, for example after losing a major account, capacity has to adjust. Start the works council process properly now, with attrition and voluntary options first. That is credible to a bank; a pre-announced percentage is not.
  - If the bank says it will not reset without a cost programme, show it the trigger plan and the process timetable.
  - If stock and receivables are already lean, the gap moves to price and the family's contribution.

This is not legal advice. Check the dismissal route with employment counsel, and the covenant definitions and the equity cure with your lawyer, before the meeting.
