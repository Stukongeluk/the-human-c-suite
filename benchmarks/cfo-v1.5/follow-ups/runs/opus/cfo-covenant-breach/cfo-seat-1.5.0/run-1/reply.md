**Don't announce the cuts before the bank meeting.** Dutch law sets the timetable for a 10% reduction (26 people), and on that timetable it costs about €0.25–0.7m up front and saves at most €0.4m by 30 June. In the central case that leaves you at 3.5–4.0x instead of 3.6x. The gap you actually have is small: about 3% of EBITDA, or 3% of net debt. A cash and margin plan plus the family's own contribution closes it without dismissals. Take that to the bank, and ask for headroom against the downside.

All euro figures here are illustrative estimates on LTM EBITDA of €4.0m and net debt of €14.4m. Replace them with yours. The full model is in the attached *covenant-plan-q2-2027.md*.

**Why the cut doesn't fix Q2**
- **Timing.** 26 exits in three months is a collective dismissal. You have to notify UWV and the unions, and ask the works council for advice on the intended decision while it can still change it (art. 25 WOR). Then come the UWV permits, about four weeks once the file is complete, and notice of one to four months minus the procedure time, with at least one month left ([UWV](https://www.uwv.nl/nl/ontslag/collectief-ontslag), [KHN](https://khn.nl/kennis/ontslag-om-bedrijfseconomische-redenen-een-stappenplan)). Exits would start in April at the very earliest, more likely June–August.
- **Cost before saving.** Transition payments, a likely social plan, advisers and outplacement come to roughly €245–724k. It is provisioned when you announce, so it falls inside the period the bank tests. The saving is about €126k a month, from each exit date.
- **Result at 30 June.** About 3.3x only if everything runs at legal top speed *and* your loan agreement lets you add back restructuring costs. In the central case: 3.5x with that add-back, 4.0x without it. At worst, 4.4x.
- **It can backfire.** Announcing a decision before the works council has advised invites an appeal to the Enterprise Chamber. UWV treats permit applications as premature until the advice process is finished, and asks what else you did to avoid dismissals ([Dirkzwager](https://www.dirkzwager.nl/kennis/artikelen/geen-toestemming-voor-ontslag-zonder-positief-advies-van-de-or)). A public announcement can also reach dealers and trade credit insurers. And your best craftspeople are the ones with other options.
- **It may not be a saving at all.** If volumes haven't fallen 10% for good, the work stays and comes back as overtime, temporary staff or lost orders. Rehiring half of the 26 later would cost an estimated €0.3–1.1m.

**What to do in the next three weeks**
1. **See the owner privately first.** A line you can use: *"If we announce 26 job losses now, we pay for them before June and save after it. The bank will do that sum in the meeting. Let's show them our own money and a plan they can test."*
2. **Call your relationship manager** before the review, so nothing surprises them in the room.
3. **Build the bank pack.** It needs a 13-week cash forecast, a covenant forecast for each test date to mid-2028 (base and downside), and the levers with an owner, a date and a confidence level for each. Present the low case as your plan.
4. **Size the levers from the ledger** (illustrative ranges):
   - inventory down 5–10%: €0.30–0.60m off net debt
   - receivables 2–4 days faster: €0.25–0.49m
   - non-safety capex moved to H2: €0.20–0.40m (timing only)
   - price or raw-material surcharge, plus procurement, energy and freight: €0.15–0.30m EBITDA
   - management bonuses and the owner-director fee on hold: €0.05–0.15m
   - a hiring freeze with exceptions, and fewer temporary renewals where volume is down: €0.09–0.15m

   The low case gets you to about 3.1x. Even if only half of it lands, you're at about 3.4x. Don't squeeze small suppliers to get there.
5. **The family goes first.** No FY2026 dividend while leverage is above about 3.25x. If the agreement has an equity cure, or lets a subordinated shareholder loan count, put in €0.5–1.0m. A bank reads that as seriousness: the family standing behind its company.
6. **The ask.** A covenant reset, for example 4.0x through 2027 and back to 3.5x during 2028, set so your downside case still passes. At minimum, a waiver for the Q2 test agreed now. In return, offer monthly reporting and no distributions. Budget €50–250k for fees, a margin step-up and any review the bank wants. Doing nothing means a breach at 3.6x, or 4.1x if EBITDA comes in 10% lower, and negotiating from weakness.
7. **Inform the works council now,** with the real numbers and confidentially if needed, before anything is decided. Ask for their ideas.
8. **Agree a trigger.** If Q1 order intake and margin confirm the downside, start a capacity review with the works council: hours, redeployment, attrition and voluntary departures first, with the number set from the order book. Don't count on short-time work. The WTV covers only calamities, and the new crisis scheme is still in the Tweede Kamer ([Eerste Kamer](https://www.eerstekamer.nl/nonav/wetsvoorstel/36940_wet_personeelsbehoud_bij)).

**The owner's view, fairly.** Banks do want decisive action on costs, and if demand has dropped for good, waiting makes the adjustment bigger later. If the order book shows that, starting the works council process properly now is credible to a bank. A pre-announced percentage isn't.
