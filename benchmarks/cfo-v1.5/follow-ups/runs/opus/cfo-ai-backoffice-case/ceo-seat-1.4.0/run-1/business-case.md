# Business case: AI and automation in Finance & Back Office, 2027–2029

**Draft for board discussion** · Prepared by: CFO · Prepared: 20 November 2026 · Decision needed by: [board date]

Figures marked *(est.)* are planning estimates built from public benchmarks and Swedish cost data. The diagnostic in Q1 2027 (section 10) replaces them with our own measured data before the board commits to the 2028 and 2029 targets.

---

## 1. Summary

**Recommendation.** Approve an automation programme that frees up to 30% of Finance & Back Office capacity by the end of 2029. Headcount falls from 90 to about 68 through natural attrition, a backfill freeze for automatable roles and moves into open roles elsewhere in the company. Nobody is made redundant because of the programme before the end of 2028. We do not recommend making 27 roles redundant in 2027.

| SEK m, planning estimates | Recommended: automate, freeze, redeploy | Alternative: 27 redundancies in 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Net effect 2027 | −10.6 | −20.9 |
| Net effect 2027–2029, cumulative | +4.7 | −14.9 |
| Net effect per year from 2030 | +16.2 | +18.9 |
| Roles fewer by end 2029 | ~22, plus ~4 FTE moved to higher-value work | 27 |
| Can we stop or slow down if AI underdelivers? | Yes, at each gate | No |

**Why not 27 redundancies in 2027:**

1. **The work won't be gone in 2027.** Our central estimate is that automation removes about 6% of the team's work by the end of 2027, 20% by the end of 2028 and 29% by the end of 2029, after counting the new oversight work it creates. Even the best accounts payable functions still handle about half their invoices by hand.
2. **The Swedish process sets the clock.** MBL negotiation, notifying Arbetsförmedlingen at least four months ahead for 26–100 people, and notice of up to six months under the sector agreement mean most exits take effect around Q4 2027 at the earliest. At most a quarter of the annual saving lands in 2027, while the costs land in full.
3. **The remaining work still has to be done.** Between the exits and the tools working at scale, about 15–22 FTE of work would have to be covered by consultants or by an overstretched team. Covering it with consultants costs more than the payroll saving in 2028.
4. **We would not choose who leaves.** Selection follows length of service across the operational unit and agreement area, not the tasks being automated.
5. **2027 is a year of reporting change.** The Solvency II amendments apply from 30 January 2027, including an audit requirement for the Solvency II balance sheet in the SFCR unless we qualify for an exemption.

**The board's direction is right.** Finance back-office work is among the most automatable work in an insurer. Benchmarks show top accounts payable functions run with less than a third of the staff of the weakest, and doing nothing leaves us behind peers. Our proposal keeps the ambition (30% of capacity) and changes two things: the date (2029, not 2027) and the mechanism (attrition and redeployment, not dismissals).

## 2. The question and what is at stake

The board asked for a case showing that AI can replace 30% of the 90-person Finance & Back Office team (accounts payable, reconciliations, reporting) in 2027. This paper tests that and sets out how to capture the opportunity.

- **Size of the prize.** The team costs about SEK 81m a year (90 FTE × SEK ~0.9m fully loaded, est.). 30% is 27 FTE, or about SEK 24m a year.
- **What we ask the board to confirm.** Is the 30% a cost target (expense ratio against peers, a profit gap), a capacity target (handling growth without hiring), or a view on what AI makes possible? The answer changes the plan. A target of about SEK 16–20m a year from 2030 is reachable on the recommended path. SEK 24m of cash in 2027 is not reachable on any AI-based path, and would need a different discussion.
- **What we don't know yet.** Hours by process, invoice and reconciliation volumes, the share of e-invoices, consultant and temporary spend in finance, three-year attrition, and which reports are actually used. The diagnostic measures all of these.

**What we take from it.** The opportunity is real and large, especially in accounts payable and high-volume reconciliations. Much of the reliable gain comes from proven automation (e-invoicing, matching rules, machine-learning capture), with generative AI handling exceptions, explanations and drafting. The risk is in timing and quality. Gains arrive over two to three years, pilots look better than production, and companies that cut first have often rehired.

## 4. Where AI and automation can take over work, and where they can't

The split of the 90 FTE by area is illustrative until the diagnostic confirms it.

| Area (FTE, illustrative) | What automation does well | What stays with people | Work removed, FTE (est.): end 2027 / 2028 / 2029 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounts payable (22) | E-invoice intake, capture of PDF invoices, PO and contract matching, coding suggestions, duplicate detection, supplier queries with a human route | Exceptions and disputes, new suppliers and bank-detail changes (fraud risk), payment approval | 4.4 / 8.8 / 12.1 (20% / 40% / 55%) |
| Reconciliations and accounting operations (30) | Auto-matching of bank, premium and claims-payment sub-ledgers, custody against the ledger, intercompany; drafted explanations of breaks | Judgement on unexplained differences, reinsurance and complex items, write-offs, journal approval, control sign-off | 3.0 / 9.0 / 12.0 (10% / 30% / 40%) |
| Reporting: financial, Solvency II, IFRS 17, management (26) | Data collection and validation, drafted variance commentary, management pack production, questions over ledger data | Judgement, sign-off, dialogue with the auditor and FI, the new Solvency II requirements in 2027 | 0.0 / 3.1 / 5.2 (0% / 12% / 20%) |
| Management, finance systems and control (12) | Little; the programme depends on this group | Leading the change, controls, vendors | 0 / 0 / 0 |
| New work the programme creates | | Running the automation, exception queues, testing automated controls, model and vendor oversight | −2.0 / −3.0 / −3.5 |
| **Net work removed** | | | **5.4 / 17.9 / 25.8 (6% / 20% / 29%)** |

**Levers that don't need AI, to start in parallel.** Require e-invoices from suppliers and consolidate the supplier base (fewer invoices to handle). Stop recurring reports nobody uses. Move low-risk accounts to quarterly rather than monthly reconciliation. Bring consultant work back in-house as capacity frees up. The diagnostic will size each of these.

## 6. Recommendation

We recommend option C. It captures most of the saving the board is looking for, about SEK 16m a year from 2030 and more if the tools beat our estimates. It pays back a year earlier than B and avoids a SEK 15m three-year loss. It keeps the knowledge we need to run a regulated close in a year of reporting change. Because headcount follows measured results rather than a forecast, we can speed up, slow down or stop at each gate. If attrition runs below plan in 2028 and the work has demonstrably gone, the next step is a voluntary programme, decided at Gate 3. Any proposal for redundancies would come back to the board with measured data, after MBL negotiation.

**What would change this recommendation:** pilot results on our own full production volume showing roughly twice the work removed that we estimate (B only beats C over three years at that level); a cash or capital need that can't wait until 2029; or attrition well below 5%.

## 8. Human impact (recommended path)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who is affected? | All 90 people in accounts payable, reconciliations and reporting, and their managers. Most of the change falls in accounts payable and reconciliations. |
| What changes for them? | Tasks shift from keying and matching to exceptions, analysis and controls. Some people move to open roles elsewhere in the company. The team gets smaller as leavers aren't replaced. Workload rises during parallel running. |
| Who is affected disproportionately? | People whose work is most routine, often long-serving. We will check in aggregate (age, gender, part-time, people on leave) that access to training and to new roles is fair. People on parental or sick leave get the same information and choices. |
| What would be hard to replace? | Knowledge of our suppliers, ledgers, reinsurance and the exceptions. That knowledge designs the automation and catches its errors. |
| What do we offer? | Nobody is made redundant because of the programme before the end of 2028. Paid learning time (about 40 hours per person in 2027, est.). Career conversations with everyone in Q1 2027. First call on open roles across the company. Support for anyone who wants to use Sweden's transition study support (omställningsstudiestöd) to retrain. |
| What do the people who stay experience? | More varied work and, during parallel running, more of it. We will watch overtime and sick leave monthly and slow the next wave if they rise. |
| Customers and service quality? | Supplier payments, closes and regulatory filings must not slip. Each gate tests this before the next wave. |
| Legal and consultation steps? | Section 9. |
| How will we know it is going badly, and what will we do? | Overtime, sick leave or regretted leavers rising; post-close adjustments, late filings or audit findings increasing. If so, we pause the next wave and fix the cause before going on. |

Where automation removes work currently done by finance consultants or temporary staff, we reduce those engagements first. We give the notice their contracts provide and tell them early and honestly, because it still ends someone's income.

## 10. Transition plan and gates

| Phase | When | What happens | Support for people | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Jan – Feb 2027 | Six-week diagnostic: hours by process, volumes, e-invoice share, consultant spend, reports in use. Vendor quotes. Union representatives involved; MBL negotiation. | Honest information from the start; the job-security commitment; career conversations begin | Measured baseline; plan negotiated |
| Pilot | Mar – Jun 2027 | Accounts payable (e-invoicing and matching) and two high-volume reconciliations, run alongside today's process on full production volume, not a selected sample | Paid learning time; the people doing the work design the rules and the exception handling | **Gate 1 (end June 2027):** touchless rate, error rate and hours saved, measured on our own data; controls signed off by internal control and discussed with the auditor |
| Expand | Jul 2027 – Jun 2028 | Waves: the rest of accounts payable, the remaining reconciliations, then reporting data pipelines after the first year-end under the new Solvency II rules | Buddying; old and new processes run side by side; first call on open roles | **Gate 2 (Jan 2028):** close no slower, no material reporting errors, people indicators stable. **Gate 3 (Jun 2028):** set the 2029 target and decide whether a voluntary programme is needed |
| Settle | Jul 2028 – 2029 | Old processes switched off; team sized to the remaining work | Career conversations; workload review | Indicators back at baseline; savings on track |

Each wave starts only when the previous one is stable.

## 12. Communication plan

1. **Board meeting:** the board sets the direction: a capacity target, no redundancies because of the programme before the end of 2028, and the gates.
2. **Within one week, in this order, so nobody hears it as rumour.** If the board meets in mid-December, all of this happens in the first full week of January rather than just before the holidays.
   - **Union representatives:** the CFO briefs them with the real numbers from this paper and invites them into the diagnostic and the pilot design. MBL negotiation follows before the programme decision is final.
   - **Managers,** with a script, answers to likely questions and support for themselves.
   - **The whole team,** from the CFO in person: why, what is decided, what is still open, what it means for each person, and the next dates. No mass email, and not on a Friday afternoon.
   - **The rest of the company,** the same day as the team.
3. **Follow-up:** 1:1 career conversations in Q1, a monthly update and Q&A, and a pulse survey after six weeks.

## 13. Risks: a pre-mortem

*It is June 2028 and the programme has failed. Why?*

| Likely cause | Early warning | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot results flattered by easy invoices | Touchless rate falls as volume widens | Pilot on full production volume; count rework and repeat exceptions |
| Hidden exception work grows | Exception backlog and its age rise | Track exception hours as a cost; size new work honestly (already in the estimate) |
| A reporting error or late filing | Post-close adjustments, auditor comments | Controls designed with internal control and discussed with the auditor; no reporting reductions in 2027 |
| The people with the most knowledge leave | Regretted leavers, pulse scores | Job-security commitment, career conversations, early roles in the programme |
| Attrition below plan, so savings lag | Leavers below 6 a year | Gate 3 decides on a voluntary programme; no involuntary step without a new board decision after MBL |
| Vendor lock-in or DORA gaps | Contract terms, exit plan missing | Exit plans and the register of information before go-live |

## 14. Accountability

Finance leadership owns the delivery of this programme. If it underdelivers, that is ours to explain to the board, and the response will be to re-plan the gates, not to cut faster. We propose that the finance leadership team's variable pay for 2027 and 2028 is tied to net savings delivered, close and reporting quality, and the team indicators in section 11, not to the number of roles removed.
