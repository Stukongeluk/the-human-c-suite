Since it’s late November, hitting a **March 31** exit date in Berlin is tight—especially for roles with 3-month notice periods—and requires careful sequencing under German labor law.

### 1. The Process
- **Document the business case:** Under the *Kündigungsschutzgesetz* (KSchG, applies at >10 staff), you need a written management/board decision (*unternehmerische Entscheidung*) detailing why these 15 roles are permanently eliminated.
- **Run *Sozialauswahl*:** Within comparable role groups, rank affected staff by tenure, age, dependents, and severe disability, and screen for special dismissal protection (pregnancy, *Elternzeit*, or severe disability requiring prior *Integrationsamt* approval).
- **File a mass layoff notice (*Massenentlassungsanzeige*, § 17 KSchG):** At 140 staff, dismissing **14 or more people** (10%) within 30 days triggers mandatory notification to the *Agentur für Arbeit* Berlin **before** serving any termination notices—otherwise the terminations are void. Having no works council spares you *Interessenausgleich/Sozialplan* negotiations, though keep plans confidential until filing so staff don't rush to form one.
- **Serve notices by Dec 31:** Tech contracts often carry a 3-month notice to month-end. To have staff off payroll by March 31, notices (or mutual *Aufhebungsverträge*) must be delivered by **December 31**, with the § 17 filing submitted beforehand. Budget for *Kündigungsschutzklagen* (3-week filing window); most settle with severance (typically 0.5–1.0× monthly gross salary per year of tenure).
