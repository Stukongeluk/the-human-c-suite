Hold off on committing to 15 cuts by March: at 140 people in Germany, 15 exits trigger collective-dismissal law and will likely save only **~€320k–€420k net this year** (estimate), not €1.44M. A headcount target before diagnosing the cash gap usually cuts what is easiest to count while leaving the same work behind.

**The process** *(not legal advice—verify with German employment counsel)*:
- **Collective dismissal (§17 KSchG):** 15 roles is 10.7%, crossing the 10% threshold (14 people in a 60–499 establishment within 30 days). You must notify the *Agentur für Arbeit* in advance; employer-initiated settlements count, and filing errors can void the dismissals.
- **Social selection (§1(3) KSchG):** Operational dismissals require eliminating the underlying work, offering open internal roles first, and selecting by tenure, age, dependants and severe disability (*Sozialauswahl*)—not performance rankings.
- **No works council:** You avoid a §§111–112 BetrVG social plan, though staff can initiate an election if they feel blindsided.

**What it actually saves this year (estimate):**
At an estimated €96k/year fully loaded (€8k/month), 15 roles cost €1.44M annualised. Serving notices by March with ~3 months' notice (§622 BGB runs 1–7 months) keeps payroll running until roughly June, leaving ~€720k gross this year. Subtract settlements/severance, legal fees and day-one transition coaching (~€300k–€400k), and net cash savings are **~€320k–€420k**—before lost knowledge and regretted exits.

**Faster, lower-harm moves to bring to the board first:**
- Cut cloud, licence and vendor waste, and freeze hiring immediately.
- Leadership owns the miss and takes the first pay/bonus cut.
- If the dip is temporary, use *Kurzarbeit* (requires individual consent without a works council) to lower payroll faster without losing engineers; if structural, model voluntary exits and redeployment before involuntary cuts.
