**Don’t start with 15 layoffs—in Berlin, end-of-March dismissals will likely save only ~€350k–€600k net this year (estimate), after cash goes out first.** You can save faster and reversibly by cutting non-headcount spend, freezing hiring, and—if the dip is temporary—using *Kurzarbeit*, with leadership owning the revenue miss and taking the first pay/bonus cut.

A headcount target by March looks decisive to a board, but in Germany it delays cash relief while leaving the revenue problem untouched.

**The German process (not legal advice—engage German employment counsel before acting):**
- **§17 KSchG mass-layoff filing:** At 140 staff, 15 roles exceeds the 10% (14-person) 30-day threshold. You must notify the *Agentur für Arbeit* in advance; employer-initiated settlement agreements (*Aufhebungsverträge*) count too, and filing errors can void dismissals.
- **No works council:** You skip *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan*, though staff can still trigger a council election once rumours start.
- **Selection & notice:** Operational dismissals (*betriebsbedingte Kündigung*) require a documented business decision and statutory social selection (*Sozialauswahl*—tenure, age, dependants, disability; never performance rankings) under §1(3) KSchG, plus 1–7 months' notice (§622 BGB; often 3 months contractually). Settlements reduce litigation risk but cost severance.

**What it actually saves this year (estimates):**
- **Annual run-rate:** 15 roles at ~€100k–€120k fully loaded = **€1.5M–€1.8M/year**.
- **This year’s net cash:** Notices by 31 March plus ~3-month notice means payroll runs through June, leaving ~€750k–€900k gross H2 savings. Subtract severance, unused leave, legal fees, and transition support (~€300k–€400k): **~€350k–€600k net**—before lost knowledge and slower delivery.

**Better sequence before involuntary cuts:**
1. Cut cloud, licences, and vendor spend plus freeze hiring (immediate cash).
2. Leadership cuts its own pay and bonuses first.
3. If demand will recover, use *Kurzarbeit* (needs individual agreements without a works council; statutory 12-month cap from Jan 2027).
