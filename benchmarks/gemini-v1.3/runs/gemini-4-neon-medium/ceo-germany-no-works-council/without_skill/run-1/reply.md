Without a works council, you skip *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan* negotiations—saving months—but German law still sets strict traps here.

### The Process
1. **Mass layoff filing (§ 17 KSchG):** At 140 employees, cutting **14+ roles (10%) within 30 days** triggers a mandatory mass layoff notice (*Massenentlassungsanzeige*) to the Berlin *Agentur für Arbeit* **before** issuing any notices or mutual termination agreements (*Aufhebungsverträge*). Missing or bungling this can invalidate the dismissals. (Alternatively, stagger cuts across two 30-day windows—e.g., 8 then 7.)
2. **Operational justification & social selection:** Under the *Kündigungsschutzgesetz*, document the board's restructuring decision, confirm no comparable open roles exist, and run a *Sozialauswahl* (ranking comparable staff by tenure, age, dependents, and disability). Check for special dismissal protection (parental leave, pregnancy, severe disability).
3. **Confidentiality:** Keep this airtight until delivery. If three employees catch wind and initiate a works council election, they gain dismissal protection and can stall the restructuring.
4. **Execution:** Deliver wet-ink signed termination letters (§ 623 BGB) with proof of receipt—or negotiate *Aufhebungsverträge* with severance to avoid Berlin Labor Court claims (employees have 3 weeks to sue).
