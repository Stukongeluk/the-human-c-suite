### The Process

Having no works council saves you months of *Sozialplan* negotiations, but at 140 employees, cutting 15 roles still triggers strict German dismissal rules:

1. **Mass Dismissal Filing (§ 17 KSchG):** Cutting ≥10% (14+ staff) within 30 days—including employer-initiated mutual terminations (*Aufhebungsverträge*)—requires filing a *Massenentlassungsanzeige* with the Berlin *Agentur für Arbeit* **before** serving notices. Skipping or botching this risks invalidating every dismissal and triggers a 1-month waiting period before exits take effect. *(Cutting ≤13 in 30 days avoids this.)*
2. **Operational Case & Social Selection (§ 1 KSchG):** Document the board’s restructuring decision, verify no suitable vacant roles exist, and run a *Sozialauswahl* across comparable roles (ranking by tenure, age, dependents, and severe disability). Screen for special protection (pregnancy, *Elternzeit*, severe disability, data protection officer).
3. **Execution & Risk:** Notices and separation agreements require **wet-ink signatures** (§ 623 BGB). Keep planning tight—if word leaks, employees can quickly initiate a works council election, granting organizers dismissal protection.
4. **Timeline Reality:** Standard Berlin tech contracts carry 3-month notice periods. To get roles off payroll by March 31, notices must land by **December 31**; otherwise, exits slip into Q2.

### What It Actually Saves This Year

Assuming €95k average fully loaded cost per role (salary + ~21% employer social contributions):

* **Gross payroll avoided (Apr–Dec, 15 roles):** ~€1.07M
* **Less severance/settlements:** Expect most affected staff to negotiate *Aufhebungsverträge* or file suit within the 3-week deadline (§ 4 KSchG). Budget 0.5–1.0 month’s gross salary per tenure year (~€150k–€280k total for typical tech tenures).
* **Less legal fees & accrued vacation payouts:** ~€50k–€90k.
* **Less notice/litigation slippage:** ~€100k–€150k if exits push past March.

**Realistic net savings this year: ~€550k–€750k**—roughly **50–70%** of the headline Apr–Dec run-rate. Because severance and payouts hit upfront, you won’t feel real cash relief until Q3. Engage a German employment lawyer (*Fachanwalt für Arbeitsrecht*) before drafting anything.
