# Reorganisation Plan and Staff Announcements (22 Roles, Amsterdam)

> **Not legal advice.** Dutch collective dismissal and works council law is strict, and sequence mistakes can void dismissals or lead to an Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*) injunction. Review every step, timeline, and draft below with Dutch employment counsel and HR before taking action or communicating to staff.

---

## Part 1: Reorganisation Plan

### Scope and Baseline
- **Current headcount:** 180 employees (Amsterdam)
- **Proposed reduction:** 22 roles (12.2% of headcount), concentrated in Operations and Support
- **Target headcount after transition:** 158 employees (87.8%)
- **Goal:** Complete the reduction in **one** well-prepared round, reduce the workload alongside the headcount, and treat every departing and remaining colleague with honesty and dignity.

---

### Phase 1: Legal and Governance Sequence (Before Any Announcement)

At 180 employees in the Netherlands and 22 roles affected, two statutory regimes apply immediately:

1. **Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, OR) — Article 25 WOR**
   - A works council is mandatory from 50 employees under the Dutch Works Councils Act (*Wet op de ondernemingsraden*, WOR).
   - Under **Article 25 WOR**, the OR has an advisory right (*adviesrecht*) on reorganisations and significant workforce reductions.
   - **Critical rule:** The request for advice must be submitted while the OR can still influence the decision. Frame the board's outcome strictly as a **proposed decision** (*voorgenomen besluit*), never as a fait accompli. Share the real financial numbers, the alternatives already considered, the proposed organisational design, and the draft social plan.
   - *(If no OR is currently in place despite having 180 employees, consult Dutch employment counsel immediately on compliance and employee consultation before proceeding.)*

2. **Collective Dismissal Notification (WMCO) — UWV and Unions**
   - Because **22 roles** meets the threshold of **20 or more employees within three months** in one UWV work area (Amsterdam), the Collective Dismissal Notification Act (*Wet melding collectief ontslag*, WMCO) applies ([UWV](https://www.uwv.nl/nl/ontslag/collectief-ontslag)).
   - **Settlement agreements (*vaststellingsovereenkomsten*) initiated by the employer count toward the 20.**
   - Notify UWV and the relevant trade unions and consult them **before** the decision is final. Build the statutory waiting period and union consultation into the cash-flow model — payroll savings will arrive later than the announcement date, while severance and transition costs arrive up front.

3. **Leadership Accountability First**
   - Before going to the OR or staff, agree and document what executive leadership is giving up first (executive pay reduction, cancelled executive bonuses, or cut perks). This is not a substitute for the budget gap; it is the minimum standard of trust when asking frontline teams to carry the cost of leadership's earlier bets.

4. **Selection Criteria and Legal Route**
   - Confirm with counsel whether exits will run through a **Social Plan** (*sociaal plan*) with voluntary settlement agreements (*vaststellingsovereenkomst*), UWV dismissal permits for business-economic reasons (*bedrijfseconomische redenen*), or both.
   - Where roles are interchangeable, selection must follow the Dutch **reflection principle** (*afspiegelingsbeginsel*) by age bracket and length of service — never ad-hoc performance rankings, ticket counts, protected characteristics, or who is on leave.
   - Map **internal vacancies and redeployment** (*herplaatsingsplicht*) first, and check whether a **voluntary exit window** (capped and structured with counsel) can reduce involuntary exits.

---

### Phase 2: Cut the Work Along With the Roles

Cutting 22 roles in Operations and Support without cutting work leaves the remaining 158 people with the same queue, higher burnout, and regulatory or customer risk. Before implementation day, the Operations, Support, Product, and Tech leads must publish a concrete **Stop-Doing List**:

- [ ] **Service levels & channels:** Which support channels, hours, or SLAs are being adjusted or retired?
- [ ] **Manual processes:** Which manual operational checks or handoffs are being removed or simplified (not merely dumped on remaining staff)?
- [ ] **Regulatory & compliance floor:** Confirm with Risk/Compliance that KYC/AML, incident response, and fintech regulatory obligations remain fully staffed and audited with the smaller team.
- [ ] **Owner:** Name a single executive owner for the redesigned Ops/Support operating model and run old and new workflows side by side during handover, planning explicitly for a temporary dip in throughput.

---

### Phase 3: Departure Package and Day-One Transition Support

Use Dutch statutory minimums as the floor, not the ceiling, to the extent the company's cash position allows:

| Element | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Severance** | Statutory transition payment (*transitievergoeding*) plus an agreed enhancement in the Social Plan / settlement agreement (confirm multiplier with Finance and counsel). |
| **Notice period** | Full statutory/contractual employer notice (1–4 months in the Netherlands by length of service), with paid release from duties (*vrijstelling van werk*) where practical so people can job-hunt full time without losing income. |
| **Day-one transition budget** | Coaching, outplacement, CV/LinkedIn support, and **paid training** starting on the **first day of notice**, not after the last day — modelled on Sweden's job security councils ([OECD, *Back to Work: Sweden*](https://www.oecd.org/employment/back-to-work-sweden_9789264246812-en)) and Finland's change-security benchmark of training worth one month's salary ([Job Market Finland](https://tyomarkkinatori.fi/en/employers-and-entrepreneurs/changes-to-the-employment-contract/change-security-for-the-employer)). |
| **Legal review budget** | Fixed contribution (e.g. €750–€1,500 excl. VAT) for each departing employee to have their settlement agreement reviewed by an independent Dutch employment lawyer. |
| **Equipment** | Allow departing staff to keep their laptop and/or work phone after company data is wiped, where security policy permits. |
| **References & rehiring** | Written reference letter, a named leader committed to positive reference calls, priority consideration for future openings, and an opt-in alumni network. |
| **Wellbeing support** | Confidential psychological and financial counselling extended through the notice period and [3] months beyond. |
| **High-impact individual care** | Dedicated HR and immigration-lawyer support for anyone on a **Highly Skilled Migrant (*kennismigrant*) visa** (to protect their search period and residency status), anyone close to retirement, or anyone on long-term leave. |

### Phase 4: Notification Day Protocol (After OR Advice and WMCO Consultation)

1. **Own manager first, live:** Every affected person hears the news from their **own manager** (or direct senior leader) with HR present, in person or on a live video call. Never by mass email, never in a group call, and never by discovering a locked account.
2. **Security with dignity:** Schedule any system-access changes *after* the individual conversation has concluded, never before or during it. For non-sensitive roles, allow a dignified handover or goodbye window.
3. **Same-day written pack:** Send each affected person a clear written summary the same day covering their timeline, package, legal-review budget, transition coaching contact, and who to reach with questions.
4. **Time to say goodbye:** Let departing colleagues choose how and whether they want to say goodbye to their teams.
5. **Support the managers:** Brief and rehearse with every manager delivering the news beforehand, give them written FAQs, and schedule debriefs and support for them afterwards.

---

### Phase 5: Supporting the 158 People Who Stay (Days 1–180)

- **Same-day all-hands:** Once all individual notifications are complete, hold a live all-hands to explain what happened, why, how departing colleagues are being supported, what work is stopping, and whether this round is over (**say it is over only if that is genuinely true**).
- **Re-plan workloads together:** Within two weeks, every affected team sits down with its manager to drop items from the Stop-Doing List and agree realistic targets.
- **1:1s within 14 days:** Every remaining employee has a dedicated 1:1 focused on their workload, questions, and career path.
- **Measure people as seriously as money:** Track the following monthly for 6–12 months and review them at the leadership table alongside cash and margin:
  - Actual net savings vs. forecast (including severance, legal fees, notice pay, and temporary productivity dip)
  - Regretted attrition (voluntary exits among remaining staff)
  - Sickness absence and overtime hours in Operations and Support
  - Customer-facing quality and compliance metrics (ticket backlog, resolution quality, incident rate)
  - Quarterly anonymous engagement pulse
- **Post-mortem (Day 90):** Write an honest internal review of what the reorganisation actually saved, what it cost (including hidden costs), and what changes in company planning are needed so the business does not end up here again.

**Subject:** Proposed changes to our team structure — what we are proposing and what happens next

I am writing to share difficult news directly with you, before rumours start and while the process is still open for consultation.

Over the past [X months], our [revenue growth / unit economics / cash runway — insert real numbers, e.g. "operating costs have grown X% ahead of revenue, leaving us with a €[X]M annual gap to reach sustainability by [date]"]. Last week, leadership reviewed alternatives to reducing headcount with the board. Even after cutting [insert non-people cuts already made, e.g. vendor spend, hiring freeze, discretionary budgets], we have not closed the gap fast enough.

I want to be plain about responsibility: **this is on leadership, not on you.** We [insert honest cause — e.g. "hired ahead of revenue and built processes that cost more to run than our business can currently support"]. Because this is our responsibility, [insert leadership-first action — e.g. "the executive team is taking a [X]% pay reduction and cancelling executive bonuses for [year]"].

As a result, leadership has submitted a **proposed reorganisation** (*voorgenomen besluit*) to our Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*) and notified UWV and the relevant trade unions under Dutch collective dismissal rules.

**What is proposed**
- A reduction of **22 roles** out of our 180-person team, primarily in **Operations and Support**.
- Alongside fewer roles, we are proposing to stop or simplify [briefly name the work/services being reduced] so the teams that remain are not asked to carry the same workload with fewer people.

**What is not decided yet**
- This is a **proposal**, not a final decision. Our Works Council has the right to advise on it, and we are consulting with the unions on a fair Social Plan.
- No individual has been selected or notified yet. Any final selection will follow objective Dutch legal criteria (*afspiegelingsbeginsel*), and we will look first at internal redeployment and voluntary options where possible.

**How we will handle this**
- **No surprises:** When a final decision is reached, anyone whose role is affected will hear it first in a private, live conversation with their own manager and HR — never by email.
- **Real support:** We are proposing a Social Plan that goes beyond the statutory minimum, including transition coaching and a paid training budget from day one of notice, independent legal review support, and dedicated help for colleagues on work visas.
- **Open timeline:**
  - **[Date]:** Formal request for advice submitted to the Works Council and WMCO notification filed
  - **[Date range]:** Consultation with the Works Council and unions
  - **[Target date]:** Expected update on the outcome of consultation and next steps

I know this creates real uncertainty, especially for colleagues in Operations and Support. We are holding an **all-hands today at [time]** to walk through the numbers openly and take your questions — including the hard ones. If you prefer to ask anonymously, you can submit questions here: [link]. Managers in Operations and Support will also hold team sessions right after.

Thank you for the work you put into this company and our customers. We owe you straight answers, and we will keep giving them to you at every step.

Team,

Every colleague affected has now spoken live with their manager and HR. Out of respect for them, we will not share a list of names in this email; departing colleagues will choose if and how they want to say goodbye to their teams over the coming days.

I want to repeat what I said when we started this process: **the people leaving today did not fail. Leadership made bets and built a cost structure that the business could not sustain, and we own that result.**

**How we are supporting departing colleagues**
