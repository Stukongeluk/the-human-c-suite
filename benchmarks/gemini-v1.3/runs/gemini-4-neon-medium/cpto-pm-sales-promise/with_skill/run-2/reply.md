Don’t tell the CEO no—bring him a clear trade-off instead. Your job is to protect both the customer relationship and your team by making the capacity math visible and letting him choose what moves, rather than absorbing the work quietly and missing everything.

Start from his perspective: he likely made that promise to protect or grow your biggest account, and his credibility is now on the line. Approach the conversation as an ally helping him keep his word, not as someone policing the process.

**What to do next:**

1. **Understand the real customer need first.** Sit down with your engineering lead (and whoever was in the customer meeting) to get the three requests in writing. Find out the actual problem behind each feature, which parts are truly needed by end of Q1, and whether a smaller version, configuration, or workaround solves it. Get a rough engineering range based on past throughput—not estimates made under pressure.
2. **Put the trade-off on one page.** Show your team's real Q1 capacity (already accounting for unplanned work, support, and reliability—never closing the gap with overtime or skipped testing) alongside the existing roadmap and the three new items. Bring three honest options:
   - **Swap:** Build the three features (or their smallest viable versions) in Q1 and explicitly move specific roadmap items to Q2, naming the customers and revenue affected.
   - **Phase:** Ship the most urgent piece or a workaround in Q1, and commit to a firm discovery and delivery timeline for the rest in Q2.
   - **Re-scope with the customer:** Go back to the customer together with a realistic date once engineering has estimated it.
3. **Align with engineering and your manager before the meeting.** Walk in with shared numbers so this reads as a real capacity constraint, not personal resistance.
4. **Fix the habit after the fire is out.** Once this is resolved, propose a simple norm: leadership and sales can promise discovery and a date for an answer, while delivery dates come after the team estimates the work.
