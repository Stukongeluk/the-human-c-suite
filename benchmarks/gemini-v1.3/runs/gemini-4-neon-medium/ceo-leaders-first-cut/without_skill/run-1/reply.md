Boards ask for layoffs to "show discipline" when they fear leadership is in denial. Your job Thursday isn't to argue against discipline—it's to prove that a 15% cut is *bad* discipline, and put a sharper alternative on the table.

**1. Reframe the math and Dutch legal reality.**
Cutting ~38 people in Utrecht triggers the WMCO (collective redundancy), mandatory Works Council (OR) advice, union consultations, and statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*) plus likely social plan costs. After severance, legal fees, and months of distraction, Year 1 net savings will be modest (~€1.5–2M)—barely moving a €40M runway—while risking delays to your 2028 launches, where the real value sits.

**2. Match the savings without gutting the studio.**
Present a concrete cost-containment plan delivering comparable 2027 savings immediately:
- **Hiring freeze + natural attrition:** Normal attrition in games (8–10%) trims ~20–25 roles organically over a year with zero severance or morale shock.
- **Reallocate from the flopped title:** Move experienced devs onto the 2028 projects to replace external co-dev and outsourcing spend.
- **Cut discretionary opex:** Trim contractors, events, and non-essential vendors.
- **Visible executive skin in the game:** Freeze leadership bonuses and raises until 2028 milestones are met.

**3. Give the board hard stage-gates, not blind trust.**
Boards back off headcount cuts when they see binding guardrails. Propose:
- **Quarterly cash-floor reporting** with a clear runway buffer through 2028.
- **Objective 2027 go/no-go milestones** (vertical slice, external playtest metrics) for both projects.
- **Pre-agreed triggers:** If a title misses its gate or cash breaches an agreed floor, you commit to descoping, cancelling a project, or making targeted cuts then.

**4. Own the post-mortem.**
Open the meeting with a blunt breakdown of why this year's launch failed and the exact production or greenlight changes already applied to the 2028 slate.

Walk in showing you're harder on the numbers than they are—just smarter about where you cut.
