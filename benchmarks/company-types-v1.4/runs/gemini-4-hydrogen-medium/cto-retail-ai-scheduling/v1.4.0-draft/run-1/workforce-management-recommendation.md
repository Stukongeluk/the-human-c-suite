# Recommendation: AI Workforce Management Rollout

**To:** Chief Operating Officer & Executive Board  
**From:** Chief Technology Officer  
**Date:** 20 November 2026  
**Subject:** Proposed March 2027 Rollout of AI Workforce Management (140 Stores / 9,000 Staff)

---

## Executive Recommendation

**Do not commit to a 140-store rollout by March 2027, and reject the 3-day schedule notice and on-the-day shift adjustment model.** Instead, run a **12-week controlled pilot in 6–8 representative stores** in Q1–Q2 2027—after Works Council (OR) consultation and CAO/legal verification—testing **AI demand forecasting paired with stable, 2-week schedules**. Measure **net paid labour cost, sales, shelf availability, and staff turnover**, not just scheduled hours.

---

## Why the Current Vendor Proposal Will Backfire

The vendor promises **12% fewer labour hours** by scheduling in 15-minute slots, publishing rosters 3 days ahead, and adjusting shifts on the day, with all 140 stores live by March 2027. In a Dutch supermarket operation of our scale, that model fails on three fronts:

### 1. Dutch Labour Law & Payroll Reality (WAB)
* **Notice period & cancellation pay:** Under the *Wet arbeidsmarkt in balans* (WAB, in force since 2020), on-call workers (*oproepkrachten*) must be called at least **4 days** in advance, or they have the right to refuse the shift (a collective agreement / CAO can shorten this to no less than **24 hours**). Crucially, if we cancel or shorten a shift within that window, **we still have to pay for the original shift**.
* **Phantom savings:** Adjusting shifts on the day does not eliminate labour cost—it converts working hours into paid non-working hours while creating payroll disputes. *(Note: the *Wet meer zekerheid flexwerkers*, passed by the Senate in July 2026 and intended to take effect in 2028, keeps an exception for students' side jobs, but WAB notice and cancellation-pay rules apply today. HR and employment counsel must verify the exact windows in our applicable CAO.)*

### 2. Works Council (OR) & Regulatory Compliance
* **Mandatory co-determination:** With ~9,000 employees, our Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*) has a **consent right** (*instemmingsrecht*, Article 27(1)(l) WOR) over any system suited to monitoring attendance, behaviour, or performance, and an **advisory right** (Article 25 WOR) on major organisational changes. Attempting to push 140 stores live in under four months—across the December peak—bypasses meaningful OR involvement and creates serious legal and Enterprise Chamber risk.
* **EU AI Act:** AI systems used for task allocation, monitoring, or evaluating workers are classified as **high-risk** (Annex III). While the EU AI Act obligation to formally inform workers' representatives and affected staff (Article 26(7)) applies from **2 December 2027** (following the 2026 Digital Omnibus), Dutch WOR consent and advisory rights apply immediately.

### 3. Store Operations, Student Retention, and Lost Sales
* **Predictability beats micro-optimisation:** Most of our 9,000 colleagues are students balancing lectures, exams, and other commitments. Publishing rosters 3 days ahead and fragmenting shifts into 15-minute slots drives no-shows, declines in shift acceptance, and higher turnover—shifting cost from store payroll to recruitment and onboarding.
* **Revenue risk:** Cutting store hours by 12% without changing store workload (shelf replenishment, checkouts, fresh counters) shows up as empty shelves, longer queues, and lost sales. In a randomised controlled trial across 28 Gap stores (Williams, Lambert & Kesavan, 2018), **more predictable schedules (2 weeks' notice, no on-call shifts) increased median sales by 7% and labour productivity by 5%**.

## Recommended Path Forward

| Phase | Timeline | Scope & Actions | Exit Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Legal, CAO & OR Alignment** | Nov – Dec 2026 | Verify CAO notice rules with HR/counsel. Bring the OR and 6–8 store managers into the problem definition. Agree data-privacy boundaries (store/team-level reporting only; no individual worker rankings). | OR agreement on pilot scope & data rules. |
| **2. Integration & Store Design** | Jan 2027 | Integrate POS/footfall feeds and payroll in read-only/sandbox mode. Configure schedules for **14-day notice** and workable shift blocks, using voluntary swap pools for same-week gaps. | Clean data sync + store manager sign-off on usability. |
| **3. Controlled Pilot** | Feb – Apr 2027 (12 weeks) | Run in **6–8 representative stores** (urban, suburban, high/low student mix) against matched control stores. Keep offline/manual fallback and shift-hour IT support. | Proven net savings without sales or turnover loss. |
| **4. Go / No-Go & Wave Rollout** | May 2027+ | Review pilot data jointly with COO, CFO, HR, and OR. If successful, roll out in waves of 20–25 stores. | Formal OR consent for chain-wide rollout. |

---

## Pilot Success Criteria (Measured vs. Matched Control Stores)

1. **Net paid labour cost** (including any cancelled-shift pay, overtime, and agency spend)—not just "scheduled hours."
2. **Store revenue, gross margin, and on-shelf availability** (to ensure hours aren't saved at the expense of lost sales).
3. **Staff turnover, absence, and shift-fill rate** at the store level.
4. **Store manager admin time** spent fixing or overriding AI-generated rosters.

---

## Key Assumptions

* Our store workforce is governed by a Dutch retail CAO subject to WAB notice and cancellation-pay rules.
* The vendor's 12% savings claim is modelled on *scheduled* hours rather than *net paid* hours and excludes turnover/onboarding costs.
* POS, footfall, and payroll integrations are not yet built, tested, or validated for 140 stores.

*Note: The legal points above are operational flags for planning, not formal legal advice; HR and employment counsel should confirm our exact CAO and WOR obligations before we sign or configure the tool.*
