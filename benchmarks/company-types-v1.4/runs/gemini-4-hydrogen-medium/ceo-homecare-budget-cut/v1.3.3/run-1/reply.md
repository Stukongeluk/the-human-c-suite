### Why the Supervisory Board's three measures will backfire

1. **Replacing 15% of district nurses (*wijkverpleegkundigen*) with lower-qualified carers**
   - **Operational reality:** District nurses do the statutory care indications (*indicatiestelling*) and clinical triage that keep total hours per client down. Cutting them in a tight Dutch nursing market hollows out your clinical backbone and pushes complex cases into crisis or hospital readmissions.
   - **Financial & legal reality:** Forced exits of 20+ staff within three months trigger collective redundancy rules (**WMCO**: advance UWV notification and union consultation), statutory transition payments (*transitievergoeding*), and 1–4 months of notice—meaning Year-1 net savings are far smaller than a spreadsheet wage-rate cut suggests. It also triggers the Works Council's (**OR**) advisory right under **Article 25 WOR**.

2. **Minute-by-minute visit registration app**
   - **Operational reality:** When you measure minutes, people optimise for the clock (Goodhart's law)—rushing vulnerable clients, splitting visits, and burning out under surveillance. In Dutch home care, nurses leave organisations that treat them like clock-punchers.
   - **Legal reality:** Under **Article 27(1)(l) WOR**, any system suited to monitoring employee attendance, behaviour, or performance requires the **consent (*instemmingsrecht*) of your Works Council**, not just advice. Expect an immediate block and a collapse in trust.

3. **Moving all planning from the teams to one central office**
   - **Operational reality:** Central planners don't know neighbourhood routes or which carer works best with a specific client. Centralisation typically *increases* travel time, fragments continuity of care, and adds central office payroll—the exact overhead you need to cut. It also triggers **Article 25 WOR**.
