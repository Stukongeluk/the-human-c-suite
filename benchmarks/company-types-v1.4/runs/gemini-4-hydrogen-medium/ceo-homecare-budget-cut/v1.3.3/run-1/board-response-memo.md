# Board Memo: Closing the 7% 2027 Funding Gap Without Hollowing Out Care

**To:** Supervisory Board (Raad van Toezicht)  
**From:** Director (Bestuurder)  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Response to the 7% cost-tariff gap and assessment of the three proposed measures  

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Next year's contracts with health insurers (Zvw) and municipalities (Wmo) leave an estimated **7.0% gap** against our current cost base across our 650 nurses and carers. Closing this gap is non-negotiable for our financial continuity.

However, the three measures currently on the table—replacing 15% of district nurses with lower-qualified carers, introducing minute-by-minute visit registration, and centralising all planning—will not deliver 7% in net savings next year. They carry high transition and severance costs, trigger statutory Works Council (OR) consent and advisory hurdles under the Dutch Works Councils Act (WOR), and risk accelerating turnover and sickness absence in a tight labour market.

**Recommendation:** Reject the three measures as proposed. Instead, close the 7% gap through a phased, four-part plan: (1) cutting central overhead, agency spend, and leadership/management costs first; (2) reducing sickness absence and administrative burden; (3) renegotiating or exiting structurally underwater municipal contracts; and (4) using natural attrition and team-led skill mix where needed.

---

## 2. Assessment of the Supervisory Board's Three Proposals

| Proposed Measure | Intended Effect | Operational, Financial & Legal Reality | Net Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Replace 15% of district nurses with lower-qualified carers** | Lower direct wage bill per hour | District nurses (wijkverpleegkundigen) perform statutory care indications and keep total hours per client down through clinical triage and self-reliance coaching. Cutting them risks higher care intensity, loss of hard-to-replace staff, and UWV/transition-payment costs if forced (WMCO applies at 20+ dismissals in 3 months). Subject to **OR advisory right (Art. 25 WOR)**. | **Reject forced quota.** Use natural attrition and team-level skill-mix review only where caseloads allow. |
| **2. Minute-by-minute visit registration app** | Tighter billing and productivity control | Measuring minutes incentivises rushing clients and gaming visit splits (Goodhart's law), while increasing administrative burden and turnover. Under **Art. 27(1)(l) WOR**, systems suited to monitoring employee attendance, behaviour, or performance require **OR consent (instemmingsrecht)**—not merely advice. | **Reject.** Track team-level outcomes, contracted vs. delivered hours, and client satisfaction instead. |
| **3. Centralise all team planning into one office** | Economies of scale and higher utilisation | Central planners lack local knowledge of clients and neighbourhood routes, which typically increases travel time, fragments continuity of care, and adds central office payroll. Also triggers **OR advisory right (Art. 25 WOR)**. | **Reject.** Keep planning in neighbourhood teams with clear capacity guardrails and better routing tools. |

## 3. Alternative Plan to Close the 7.0% Gap

Before reducing frontline care capacity, we should address the structural drivers of the gap in order of least to most disruption to client care:

| Lever | Target Impact | Concrete Actions | Timing & Governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Overhead, agency & vendor spend (leaders first)** | **2.0%** | Freeze executive/management pay and bonuses first; cut non-essential central office roles, external consultants, and IT/vendor licences; replace expensive agency/flex cover with internal flex pools. | Immediate (Q1). No OR consent needed for vendor/pay-freeze steps; Art. 25 WOR if central roles are restructured. |
| **2. Sickness absence & administrative toil** | **2.0%** | Every 1 percentage point reduction in sickness absence across 650 staff recovers ~6.5 FTE of productive capacity. Remove unused reporting steps and invest in early, practical return-to-work support. | Q1–Q3. Joint design with team leads and OR. |
| **3. Contract & tariff discipline** | **1.5%** | Separate Zvw vs. Wmo margins. Renegotiate structurally loss-making municipal Wmo contracts using audited cost data; decline or phase out contracts that cannot cover direct care costs. | Q1–Q2 (for next contracting cycle and mid-year indexation talks). |
| **4. Natural attrition & team-led skill mix** | **1.5%** | Freeze external hiring in over-staffed functions; let neighbourhood teams adjust the nurse-to-carer ratio through natural turnover and paid internal upskilling, rather than forced exits. | Ongoing across 2027. Discussed with OR at problem stage. |
| **Total Planned Impact** | **7.0%** | | |

## 4. Legal & Stakeholder Process (Netherlands)

1. **Works Council (OR) early involvement:** Under the WOR (mandatory for organisations with 50+ employees), major organisational changes require timely OR advice (Art. 25), and any monitoring/time-tracking system requires OR consent (Art. 27(1)(l)). Engaging the OR now—at the problem stage with the full 7% numbers—avoids months of delay and Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer) risk.
2. **Employment Counsel & CAO:** All contract-change, redeployment, and sickness-absence measures will be checked against the applicable *CAO VVT* and employment counsel before implementation.
3. **Balanced Scoreboard:** Every financial target will be paired with a care-quality and people counter-metric (e.g., delivered vs. contracted hours paired with client satisfaction, sickness absence, and regretted attrition), reviewed monthly.
