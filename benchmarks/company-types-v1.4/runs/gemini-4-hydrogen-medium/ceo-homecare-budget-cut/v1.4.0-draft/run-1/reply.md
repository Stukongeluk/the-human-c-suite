### Why the board's three measures backfire
1. **Replacing 15% of district nurses with lower-qualified carers** is a one-way door. In Dutch home care, district nurses handle clinical triage (*indicatiestelling*) and complex care that keeps clients independent and out of hospital—KPMG's 2015 study of Buurtzorg for the Ministry of Health found that skilled, autonomous nursing teams achieved lower total costs precisely because they used fewer hours per client. Forced downgrades or redundancies across 650 staff will also trigger transition payments, 1–4 month notice periods, and likely collective dismissal consultation (WMCO), eating up Year 1 cash savings while pushing remaining nurses into scarce-market resignations and expensive agency/ZZP cover.
2. **Minute-by-minute visit tracking** requires prior Works Council (OR) consent under **Article 27(1)(l) WOR** (any system suited to monitoring employee attendance, behaviour, or performance). Even if approved, clock-watching increases admin load and shifts focus from client self-reliance to filling minutes.
3. **Centralising all planning** strips teams of local route and client knowledge, increasing travel time and sickness absence—and as a major reorganisation, it triggers the OR's advisory right under **Article 25 WOR**.

### What to do instead (estimated 6%–9% total impact over 12–18 months)

1. **Leadership and overhead first (est. 1.5%–2.5%):** Freeze management pay rises and perks, and audit office space, idle software licences, external consultants, and central admin layers before asking frontline nurses to carry the gap.
2. **Cut sickness absence and agency/ZZP spend via stable team planning (est. 2.0%–3.0%):** Keep scheduling in the teams with better routing support, pair it with early-dialogue absence support, and convert expensive external ZZP/agency hours into predictable internal team hours.
3. **Open-book contract and scope discipline (est. 1.5%–2.0%):** Go back to municipalities (Wmo) and insurers (Zvw) with full-cost transparency. Where a municipal contract is structurally below cost, renegotiate the scope or decline the loss-making tender rather than subsidising it by diluting clinical quality.
