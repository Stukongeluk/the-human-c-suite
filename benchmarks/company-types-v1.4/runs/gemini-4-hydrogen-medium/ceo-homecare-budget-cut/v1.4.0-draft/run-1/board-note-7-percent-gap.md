# Closing Next Year's 7% Funding Gap Without Hollowing Out Care

**To:** Supervisory Board (Raad van Toezicht)  
**From:** Director  
**Subject:** Response to the 7% contract deficit and assessment of proposed cost measures  

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Next year's contracts with health insurers and municipalities leave an estimated **7% gap** against our current cost base across our 650 nurses and carers. We take full responsibility for closing that gap so the organisation remains solvent and able to deliver safe care.

However, the three proposed measures—**replacing 15% of district nurses with lower-qualified carers**, **introducing minute-by-minute visit tracking**, and **centralising all planning**—are likely to **worsen both our cash position in Year 1 and our clinical and retention risks**:

1. **First-year savings are overstated:** Forced role changes or dismissals trigger statutory transition payments, notice periods (1–4 months), and Works Council (OR) / union consultation timelines, while severance and turnover costs hit immediately.
2. **Turnover and agency spend will rise:** District nurses are scarce. Diluting skill mix, removing team scheduling autonomy, and introducing individual time surveillance historically drive higher sickness absence and resignations, pushing us back onto expensive agency/ZZP cover.
3. **Legal and governance friction:** Under the Dutch Works Councils Act (WOR), centralising planning and restructuring nursing roles fall under the OR's advisory right (Article 25 WOR), and a minute-tracking app requires OR consent (Article 27(1)(l) WOR).

**Recommendation:** Pause the three measures for a **30-day diagnostic and co-design sprint** with finance, team leads, and the OR. Close the 7% gap through a phased, reversible sequence first: overhead and vendor savings (starting with leadership restraint), absence and agency reduction through stable team scheduling, open-book tariff/scope negotiations with municipalities and insurers, and natural attrition—keeping clinical quality and team autonomy intact.

## 3. Why the Three Proposed Measures Carry High Risk

### A. Replacing 15% of District Nurses with Lower-Qualified Carers
- **Clinical and total-cost risk:** District nurses handle complex clinical assessments (*indicatiestelling*), triage, and supervision that prevent avoidable hospitalisations. Research by Aiken et al. (*JAMA*, 2002) shows that higher nurse workloads and diluted nursing skill mix are associated with higher patient mortality and nurse burnout. In Dutch home care, KPMG's 2015 study for the Ministry of Health on Buurtzorg found that self-managing teams of qualified nurses achieved low total costs per client precisely because they used fewer hours per client—not by substituting cheaper, less-qualified staff.
- **Employment law and timing:** A forced 15% reduction or demotion across 650 staff will likely exceed the 20-employee threshold for collective dismissal (WMCO) if implemented via redundancies or employer-initiated settlement agreements, requiring advance notification and consultation with UWV and unions, alongside Article 25 WOR advice.

### B. Minute-by-Minute Visit Registration App
- **Co-determination:** Any system capable of monitoring employee attendance, behaviour, or performance requires the **prior consent of the Works Council** under **Article 27(1)(l) WOR**. Pushing this through creates immediate conflict with the OR.
- **Perverse incentives:** Timing visits to the minute shifts focus from client self-reliance to clock-watching, increases administrative burden, and signals distrust—driving away experienced nurses in a tight labour market.

### C. Centralising All Planning
- **False efficiency:** Central planners lack local knowledge of client routines, neighbourhood travel, and team dynamics. Losing local scheduling control increases travel friction, fragments continuity of care, and removes one of the main reasons nurses stay in home care.

## 4. Recommended Action Plan (4-Step Sequence)

Before asking frontline staff to absorb a cut, leadership must own the deficit and exhaust non-harmful levers first:

| Step | Action | Target Contribution (Est.) | Owner & Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Overhead & Leadership Restraint** | Freeze executive/management pay increases and perks; audit office space, idle software licences, external consultants, and non-clinical admin layers. | **1.5% – 2.5%** | Director & Finance (Weeks 1–4) |
| **2. Absence & Agency Reduction** | Keep scheduling in the teams with better routing/planning support; launch early-dialogue absence support; convert expensive ZZP/agency hours into predictable internal team hours. | **2.0% – 3.0%** | Operations & Team Leads (Months 1–6) |
| **3. Payer & Scope Discipline** | Open-book negotiations with municipalities (Wmo) and insurers (Zvw) on true full-cost delivery—avoiding the "non-profit starvation cycle" (Gregory & Howard, 2009). Decline or re-scope structurally loss-making municipal tenders. | **1.5% – 2.0%** | Director & Contracting (Months 1–4) |
| **4. Natural Attrition & Team-Level Outcomes** | Adjust skill mix gradually through natural turnover and paid upskilling/redeployment where appropriate. Replace individual time-tracking with **team-level** capacity and client-outcome targets co-designed with the OR. | **1.0% – 1.5%** | HR, OR & Teams (Months 3–18) |
| **Total Illustrative Range** | | **6.0% – 9.0%** | |

## 5. Immediate Next Steps (Next 30 Days)

1. **Diagnose the real drivers of the 7% gap:** Break down the deficit by payer (Zvw vs. Wmo), sickness absence rate, agency/ZZP spend, non-billable travel/admin time, and central overhead.
2. **Convene the Works Council (OR) early:** Share the full 7% financial reality and invite the OR to co-design the diagnostic and alternative measures before any formal Article 25 or 27 request is filed.
3. **Engage Dutch employment counsel and HR:** Verify CAO VVT rules, WMCO thresholds, and WOR procedures before committing to any staffing changes.
4. **Set joint success metrics:** Track both financial health (margin, ZZP spend, overhead ratio) and workforce/care health (sickness absence, nurse retention, client satisfaction) monthly.
