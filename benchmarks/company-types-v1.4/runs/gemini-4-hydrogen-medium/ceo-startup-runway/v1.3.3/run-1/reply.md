### Why a 30% cut "this month" fails the math in Sweden
*(Not legal advice—verify your specific agreements with Swedish employment counsel before acting.)*

1. **Cash savings arrive late:** Under Sweden’s Employment Protection Act (LAS), notice periods run **1 to 6 months** depending on length of service. Dismissing 5 to 25 people also requires notifying *Arbetsförmedlingen* at least **2 months in advance**, and if you have a collective agreement, the Co-determination Act (MBL) requires union negotiation *before* decisions are final. You keep paying full salaries through consultation and notice.
2. **You lose selection flexibility and rehiring speed:** LAS sets a seniority order per unit (with a 3-person exemption since 2022) unless you agree on a different list with the union, and dismissed staff generally hold **9-month re-employment priority** (for those with >12 months’ service in the last 3 years). That makes a hasty cut hard to reverse if Series B closes on time.
