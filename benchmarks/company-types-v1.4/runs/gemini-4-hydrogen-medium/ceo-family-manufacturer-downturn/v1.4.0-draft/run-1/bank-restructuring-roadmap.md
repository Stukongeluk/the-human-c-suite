# Restructuring & Bank Renewal Roadmap

**Prepared for:** Managing Director (3rd-generation family-owned machine builder, Stuttgart region)  
**Workforce:** 210 employees (196 regular staff, 14 apprentices)  
**Trigger:** 35% order cut from top two automotive-supplier customers (one cyclical/uncertain, one structural due to EV drivetrain transition); bank credit-line renewal in February 2027  
**Date:** 20 November 2026  

> **Note:** This is an operational and financial planning framework, not legal or tax advice. Confirm all employment-law steps, works-council duties, and *Kurzarbeit* filings with German employment counsel (*Fachanwalt für Arbeitsrecht*), your employers' association (*Arbeitgeberverband*), and your tax adviser.

---

## 1. Why "Let 45 People Go Now" Fails Both the Bank and the Business

Your adviser's recommendation to cut 45 roles (~21% of total headcount) immediately has three flaws:

1. **It worsens cash before February.** Under German law (§622 BGB), statutory notice periods run up to 7 months to month-end after 20 years of service. Combined with *Sozialplan* severance negotiations (if a *Betriebsrat* exists) and mandatory advance notification to the *Agentur für Arbeit* (§17 KSchG for >25 dismissals in a 60–499 person plant), cash goes *out* in Q1 2027 long before payroll drops.
2. **It cuts capability blindly.** In the Stuttgart region, experienced CNC machinists, mechatronics technicians, and application engineers take years to replace. Hollowing out the shop floor and the 14 apprentices jeopardises delivery and quality for the customers you *do* keep.
3. **It confuses a structural shift with a headcount target.** A bank renewing a credit line in February does not just want a lower wage bill; it wants proof that you have sized the *permanent* revenue gap, preserved liquidity now, and have a credible path to replace lost ICE-related volume.

## 3. Options Comparison (Ordered from Least to Most Harm)

| Option | Cash Impact & Timing | Operational / Human Impact | Risks & Legal Notes (Germany) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Owner draw, management pay, capex & non-payroll spend** | Immediate cash preservation (Weeks 1–4); zero severance cost | Signals fairness and shared sacrifice; protects shop-floor trust | Cannot close a large structural revenue gap alone, but essential to establish credibility with the bank and workforce |
| **2. Working capital, pricing & commercial pivot** (service, retrofits, non-EV/non-auto) | 3–9 months for new orders; 30–60 days for inventory/receivables release | Keeps skilled trades employed; builds long-term resilience | Requires sales discipline and engineering capacity; don't underprice just to fill machines |
| **3. Hiring freeze, natural attrition & redeployment** | Gradual (3–12 months); zero exit cost | Protects the 14 apprentices and core *Facharbeiter*; low disruption | Too slow on its own if one major customer's volume is permanently gone |
| **4. *Kurzarbeit* (short-time work) + paid upskilling** | Immediate payroll relief on idle hours | Preserves skills for cyclical recovery; use idle hours for maintenance/training | **Bridge only:** Germany's IAB warned in 2026 that *Kurzarbeit* delays needed change in a *structural* crisis. Requires *Betriebsrat* agreement (§87(1) no. 3 BetrVG) or individual consent |
| **5. Voluntary exits & early retirement (*Altersteilzeit* / *Aufhebungsverträge*)** | Medium-term savings; upfront cash cost for top-ups/severance | Preserves dignity and goodwill; avoids contested dismissals | Employer-initiated *Aufhebungsverträge* count toward §17 KSchG mass-dismissal thresholds; watch for losing scarce skills |
| **6. Targeted operational dismissals (*betriebsbedingte Kündigung*)** | Slowest cash benefit (up to 7 months notice under §622 BGB + *Sozialplan* costs) | High trust and knowledge loss if rushed; must cut *work*, not just people | Requires *Sozialauswahl* (§1(3) KSchG), *Interessenausgleich* & *Sozialplan* (§§111–112 BetrVG if >20 staff and *Betriebsrat* exists), and *Agentur für Arbeit* notice (§17 KSchG) |

## 4. What the Bank Needs to See in February

1. **A 13-week cash-flow forecast** showing you will not breach liquidity before spring, backed by concrete working-capital actions (inventory reduction, debtor collection, supplier terms).
2. **Leadership skin in the game**—owner's draw/dividends paused or cut first, alongside management bonuses.
3. **Honest separation of cyclical vs. structural demand**—showing *Kurzarbeit* only where volume can return, and a concrete commercial + capacity plan for the permanent EV-drivetrain loss.
4. **A single, well-costed headcount plan (if still needed)** that accounts for German notice periods and severance cash outflows, rather than an unrealistic assumption that 45 exits save cash on Day 1.
