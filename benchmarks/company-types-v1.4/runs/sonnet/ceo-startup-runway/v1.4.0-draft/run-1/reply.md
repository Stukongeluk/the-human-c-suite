**Why "30% this month" doesn't work as stated**

- **Swedish law won't allow it.** Under LAS, redundancies follow last-in-first-out within each collective agreement area, with only three exemptions. You can't choose who goes, so you'd lose your newest hires, who may be the ones carrying the new strategy. If you're bound by a collective agreement, MBL requires negotiating with the union before the decision. Notifying Arbetsförmedlingen of 14 redundancies is due at least two months ahead, and notice periods run one to six months. The saving starts in spring, and severance or notice pay lands first. Check this with employment counsel; I'm not giving legal advice.
- **The saving is smaller than it looks.** Take illustrative figures: €300k monthly revenue, €700k monthly costs, 14 people at about €104k loaded each. The saving is roughly €120k a month, and it only begins around month four.
- **Survivors leave.** Layoffs cost more in lost knowledge and departures than the spreadsheet shows. Your best people have options in Stockholm, and they'll start interviewing the day you announce.

**Options, in the order I'd try them**

| Option | What it does (illustrative) | Who carries it | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Do nothing | Cash runs out in month 11, with about €0.6M left in August | Everyone, late and abruptly | No |
| B. Spend and leaders first: vendors, cloud, office, paused hires, 20% exec pay cut, pricing and collections | About €44k a month, runway to month 14, about €0.95M in August | Leaders and suppliers | Yes |
| C. B plus about 7 roles (15%), via voluntary exits first | About €1.2M in August, and on these numbers a path to break-even | 7 people, treated decently | No |

On these numbers, the second seven roles buy about €0.25M of extra cash at the moment you raise. That is a real benefit, but it costs seven more people and more risk to delivery. Check that trade-off with your actual figures.

**What I'd do**

1. **Own it.** Tell the board and the team in plain words that the runway is leadership's responsibility, not the team's. Take the first and largest cut yourself, as a signal of trust. Then do B this month.
2. **Open the numbers to the team.** Share a monthly runway scenario with everyone. People who see the problem and the options can often find savings you can't, and this is how you earn the right to ask for sacrifice.
3. **Close the funding gap in parallel.**
   - Ask existing investors for a pro-rata bridge, such as a convertible loan or extension, sized to reach the raise. If your two investors want a 30% cut, ask whether they'll put money behind the alternative.
   - Look at venture debt, plus climate-specific grants and loans such as Vinnova, the Swedish Energy Agency, Almi and EU programmes.
   - Test a pre-emptive Series B conversation now, rather than in the summer.
4. **Set the trigger now.** Agree with the board that on a date such as 31 January, if runway is below about 12 months or revenue growth falls below a threshold, you do option C or D. If you must cut, do it once, deep enough not to repeat, tied to what you stop doing, with generous terms. Open the union or employee consultation early, fund coaching and training from the day of notice, as Sweden's job security councils do (TRR for white-collar private sector staff, subject to your agreement), and give a written plan to everyone affected on the same day.
5. **Stop work, not just people.** If you reduce headcount, decide which products and projects stop. Otherwise 34 people carry 48 people's work.

**Steelmanning your investors.** They may be right. Runway is the only thing that can't be negotiated, a cut done now is cheaper than one done in desperation, and 4% monthly growth is solid but not a Series B certainty. That's why I'd give them a dated trigger rather than a refusal. Ask them what they're afraid of. If it's "we run out of cash before the raise", the plan answers it; if it's "we don't trust the forecast", show them the monthly scenarios.
