Don't make the 30% cut this month. It buys far less than it looks like, it lands on the wrong people, and your real problem is not nine months of runway but arriving at a Series B with about one month left. Aim for 15 to 18 months of runway by early January, with a hard decision date if the plan falls short.

I have no access to your books here, so everything below uses stated assumptions. Replace them with your real numbers.

**Why 30% buys little**

Thirty percent of 48 is about 14 people. Assume payroll is roughly 65% of burn (my estimate) and burn is flat. Gross saving is then about 19% of monthly burn. But a Swedish collective redundancy is not instant: you must negotiate with any union before deciding (MBL), notify Arbetsförmedlingen at least two months ahead for 5 to 25 people, and pay notice of one to six months by tenure. Say the savings start in month 3.

- Cash used in months 1 to 3: 3 months of burn, leaving 6.
- After that, burn is about 0.81 of today's, so 6 / 0.81 = 7.4 months.
- Total: about 10.5 months, against 9 today. That is roughly 1.5 extra months (my estimate), before lost productivity, and your Series B is 8 to 9 months out at best.

There is a second problem. Under LAS, redundancies follow last-in-first-out within your unit, not your judgement of who matters (check with counsel whether your collective agreement or a negotiated list changes this; each employer may exempt three people). A 30% cut will mostly fall on your newest hires, often the people brought in to drive the growth your investors are paying for. Revenue growing 4% a month compounds to about 42% over nine months; cutting the team that produces it weakens the story you must tell in a Series B.

**What I would do instead**

| Step | What | Likely effect (estimates) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Leaders first | CEO and exec pay cut now (for example 20 to 30%), bonuses suspended, perks gone. Say plainly that the runway and the Series B timing are leadership's responsibility | Small in kronor, large in trust. It is also what gives you the right to ask others for anything |
| 2. Spend | Audit cloud, licences, vendors, office, travel. Chase receivables, offer annual prepay on contracts | Often 5 to 10% of non-payroll costs, plus cash timing |
| 3. Revenue | Pricing and annual prepay for your best-retaining customers; protect what drives the 4% | Each extra point of monthly growth matters more than most savings |
| 4. Non-dilutive money | Grants and loans for climate tech (Vinnova, the Energy Agency, EU programmes, EIB venture debt) | Months, not weeks, so apply now |
| 5. Investors | Ask the two investors who want a 30% cut to fund a bridge or extension tied to milestones. If they will not add capital, ask why they want you to shrink | The cleanest fix for the gap itself |
| 6. Focus and freeze | Stop low-value projects, freeze hiring, let attrition run; offer voluntary reduced hours or voluntary exits | A 10 to 15% payroll effect without forced exits |
| 7. One targeted reduction, only if needed | If steps 1 to 6 do not reach about 15 months of runway by a set date, do one decent round, planned with counsel and employee representatives | Last resort, once, with real transition support from day one of notice |

**The date that matters**

Because the process takes two to three months, a late decision costs you months of runway. Pick a tripwire now, for example mid-December: if the bridge, grants and cost levers do not show a credible path to 15 months, you start the consultation process in January, and the people affected already know why. Tell the whole team now, with real numbers: the problem, the options, the date. Nobody should learn this from a rumour.

**The investors' view, fairly put**

They are right that nine months is too short and that waiting for hope is not a plan. Cash discipline is not optional. Where I disagree is on the remedy: a large cut this month saves about 1.5 months at a high price, while the real fix is more time and more money. Show them your model of both paths, and ask what they would need to see to back the alternative.

**Seat check**

- Technology: protect whoever builds what customers pay for and whoever keeps the product reliable; check which cloud and tooling costs are truly waste.
- Product: look at which customers and features drive the 4%, and stop what does not.

**Five numbers that would change my answer**

1. Monthly net burn, and payroll as a share of it.
2. Gross margin and revenue retention (is the 4% new customers or expansion?).
3. Do you have a collective agreement or union members, and what are the tenures of your 48?
4. Whether the two investors, and others, will fund a bridge.
5. What your Series B investors need to see to close in summer.

This is not legal advice; involve Swedish employment counsel and any employee representatives before any decision.

What am I missing, and what would make this wrong? If your burn is mostly payroll and no bridge or grant is reachable, a smaller, earlier cut may be unavoidable. In that case do it once, and do it properly.
