Don't adopt the three measures as proposed. Take the board a counter-plan that closes the 7% gap in a different order, with the three measures reduced to what you can defend. My reasoning is below, so you can challenge it.

**Why these three measures are the wrong start**

They are solutions chosen before the problem was diagnosed. They also cut at the front line, where your quality and your reputation with insurers and municipalities sit.

- **Replacing 15% of district nurses.** The saving is small and the risk is large. As an illustration only: if you employ 100 district nurses and each swap saves about €12,000, that is roughly €180,000 a year. Against a gap of several million euros, that is a rounding error (please replace my numbers with yours). Fewer qualified nurses means less clinical judgement, more incidents and more complex clients passed on. Research on hospital nursing links leaner staffing with higher mortality and burnout (Aiken et al., JAMA 2002). Buurtzorg's teams of up to 12 nurses were found by KPMG (2015) to use fewer hours per client. You would also carry the legal and quality duties, including those of the Inspectie, with fewer qualified people.
- **Minute-by-minute visit registration.** You need to register visits for billing, and that is fine. Timing every visit per carer is different. It pushes carers to rush, it erodes trust, and it is a monitoring system that your works council (OR) must consent to under art. 27 WOR. The data protection rules (AVG) also apply. It saves nothing until it changes how work is done.
- **Central planning.** It moves decisions away from the people who know the clients and the streets. Travel time, continuity of carer and sickness cover often get worse. Staff turnover and agency cover then eat the saving.

**What I would do first**

1. **Test the gap (next 2 to 3 weeks).** Is the 7% on all contracts or on a few, such as Wmo tariffs? Is it final or still open to negotiation? Which cost lines drive it: pay increases under the cao, agency cover, absence, travel time, overhead? Use the finance system and the roster data, in aggregate only.
2. **Revenue and contracts.** Take a full-cost calculation to every insurer and municipality. Ask for indexation and for tariffs that reflect the cao. Ask for multi-year agreements. Sector bodies and your peers can strengthen your case. If a contract is structurally below cost, you need to know which, and decide honestly whether to keep it.
3. **Waste and toil.** Look at agency spend, unproductive travel time, overtime, cancellations, admin and double registration, and management layers. Let the teams find where care time is lost. In my experience this is where most of the money is, but I don't know your numbers yet.
4. **Natural attrition and a hiring freeze** on non-care roles, and redeploy before you recruit. Use team planning tools so that teams plan with software support rather than being planned from a central office.
5. **Leaders go first.** The board and you take the first and largest cut in pay, bonuses and perks, and say publicly that the result is leadership's responsibility. You can't ask the front line for sacrifice that you haven't made yourselves.
6. **Only then**, and only if the gap is still open: voluntary exits, then anything involuntary. That is a last step, and it must be done once and decently.

**What I'd keep from the board's ideas**

- Registration for billing and safety, designed with a group of carers, used at team level, and agreed with the OR before it is chosen.
- A shared back office for planning support, payroll and admin, with the teams keeping control of the schedule.
- A skill-mix review: not a target of 15%, but whether the right tasks go to the right level, with the nurses involved.

**Process and law**

With 650 staff you have an OR. Involve it now, with the real figures, while it can still change the plan. This is not legal advice: check with an employment lawyer and your employers' association, and check your cao. Mass dismissals (20 or more in an area in three months) bring extra rules.

**Assumptions and what would change my mind**

I assumed a non-profit with an OR and a care cao, and that the gap is real and structural. If your reserves are thin and the gap is truly 7% with no negotiating room, you will have to go further down my list, and I would show the board that arithmetic openly. If the numbers show that a skill-mix change saves far more than I expect without harming quality, I will update.

Two questions that would change my answer most: when do the new contracts start, and how much of the 7% is still open to negotiation? Second, what are your reserves, and what does the roster data say about agency and overtime?
