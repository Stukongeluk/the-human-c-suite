Don't ship it as specced. Say yes to the goal, no to a live individual leaderboard plus a weekly bottom-10% flag, and put a counter-proposal on the table: measure and fix what slows stops down, and pilot it before any rollout. Two numbers frame this. A bottom 10% exists by construction: of 2,400 drivers, 240 get flagged every week, however good the fleet is. And two thirds of your drivers (about 1,600) aren't your employees, so you would be steering other companies' staff through a ranking you can't properly defend.

**Why the spec fails**

- **Stops per hour mostly measures the route, not the driver.** Stop density, apartments versus houses, parcel size, access problems, traffic, failed deliveries and how the depot loaded the van all move it. A driver in the Amsterdam centre or on a rural Belgian route will rank differently from a driver in a dense suburb, whatever their effort. The weekly flag will mostly punish bad routes and bad draws.
- **It rewards the wrong behaviour.** People optimise the number: skipped carding, parcels dropped at the neighbours or left unsafely, rushed handovers, speeding, phone use in traffic. Failed first attempts, damage claims and complaints are the costs, and they land on you. Pair any speed measure with quality and safety, or don't show it.
- **A live leaderboard in a driver app is a safety question.** Real-time rank pressure on someone driving a van is a risk. Put it in front of your safety lead before anyone else.
- **Legal and relationship exposure (not legal advice; get counsel).** Weekly performance flags sent to subcontractors can look like you directing their employees, which matters for the employer relationship and for chain liability. Subcontractors' own works councils, where they have one, may have consent rights over monitoring systems, and your own works council likely does for anything that touches your people. Belgium has its own rules on monitoring workers. AI Act duties for systems that allocate tasks or monitor workers apply from December 2027, so design for them now. Check GDPR basis and a DPIA for individual-level tracking.
- **Effect on the supply side.** In a tight driver market, the best drivers leave first if they feel surveilled, and subcontractors who get weekly lists will start dropping people. That is turnover and training cost, and the 8% has to survive it.

**What to ask Operations first**

1. Where does the 8% come from? A pilot, a competitor, a model? Does it come from the bottom 10% improving, or from everyone speeding up? If it is the bottom decile, what do they believe is wrong with those drivers: effort, skill, or routes?
2. What does the data say about *why* time is lost? I'd expect big items outside the driver's control: queueing at the depot, loading order, address quality, access codes, no-one home, parking. I haven't seen your data, so I don't know. Ask for it, aggregated by depot and route, not by person.

**Counter-proposal**

- **Show drivers their own numbers, privately,** next to route context (density, parcel mix) and their own past. No ranking against colleagues, no live public board.
- **Report at depot and subcontractor level,** normalised for route difficulty, with first-attempt success, damage and complaints beside speed. Use it to find the depots and routes with the biggest gaps and fix their causes: routing, loading, address data, handheld speed.
- **Individual concerns go through the subcontractor's own manager,** based on a ride-along and a conversation, not a dashboard rank.
- **Pilot it:** three or four comparable depots get the new approach, matched depots don't, for 8 to 12 weeks. Agree up front the productivity target, the quality and safety guardrails (first-attempt success, damage, incidents, driver and subcontractor churn), and what result would make you stop. Involve works council representatives and a few subcontractors and drivers in designing it, not after.
- **Review on a fixed rhythm** and publish the results to Operations. If the pilot shows the ranking approach really wins on all guardrails, you will have changed your mind on evidence, and I would too.

**Steelman for Operations:** productivity matters, the margin is thin, and some drivers and subcontractors really do underperform. That is true. The disagreement is about the instrument, not the goal.
