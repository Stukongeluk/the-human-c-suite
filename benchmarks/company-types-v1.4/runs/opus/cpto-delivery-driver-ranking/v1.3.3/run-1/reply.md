Back the 8% goal but turn down this design. A weekly bottom 10% is 240 drivers, about 160 of them employed by someone else, and there is always a bottom 10%, even when every driver is doing fine. Offer Operations a depot pilot aimed at the same gain.

**Why the design won't deliver it**
- **Stops per hour mostly measures the route.** Density, flats versus farms, parcel size and traffic drive it, so the list will mostly name rural routes and new drivers.
- **Drivers will chase the number.** Expect faster driving, glances at a live leaderboard behind the wheel, and more "not home" cards. Redeliveries, complaints and crashes can eat the gain.
- **The law is involved** (not legal advice, so check with counsel). Dutch works councils must consent to systems that monitor performance (art. 27 WOR), and systematic monitoring of employees and their GPS needs a DPIA ([AP list](https://www.dirkzwager.nl/kennis/artikelen/definitieve-nederlandse-lijst-verwerkingen-waarbij-dpia-gegevensbeschermingseffectbeoordeling-verplicht-is-gepubliceerd)). In Belgium, telling subcontractors which of their staff to act on can count as exercising employer authority, which can make those drivers your employees ([FOD WASO](https://werk.belgie.be/nl/themas/arbeidsovereenkomsten/terbeschikkingstelling-van-werknemers)). The Parcel Delivery Act already presumes you liable when a direct subcontractor breaches drivers' working conditions ([PwC](https://www.pwclegal.be/en/news/parcel-delivery-act-introduces-improved-working-conditions-for-p.html)).

To be fair to Operations, showing rank raised warehouse pickers' output by about 7% in one German study ([Blanes i Vidal & Nossol, 2011](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/39267)). In another, removing rank feedback raised sales by 11% ([Barankay, 2012](https://repository.upenn.edu/bepp_papers/75)). Rankings help when the work is comparable, and routes aren't.

**What I'd do**
1. **Talk to the head of Operations one-to-one** before this reaches the exec team. Ask where the 8% comes from and what it fixes: cost per parcel, peak capacity or late deliveries.
2. **Find where the hours go, per depot and route:** depot loading and waiting, failed first attempts, bad addresses, route sequencing. Most are product fixes your teams own.
3. **Pilot in four depots for eight weeks against comparable depots.** Track stops per hour alongside first-attempt delivery, complaints, damage, incidents and driver turnover. Drivers see only their own trend on their own route: no leaderboard, nothing sent to subcontractors automatically.
4. **Bring the works councils and two or three subcontractors in now**, with the numbers, while they can still shape the design.
5. **Treat an outlier route as a question about the route** for the depot and subcontractor, not a list of names.

**A line you can use:** "I want the 8% too. Let's test in four depots how much of it comes from fixing routes and depots, before we put 240 names on a list every week."
