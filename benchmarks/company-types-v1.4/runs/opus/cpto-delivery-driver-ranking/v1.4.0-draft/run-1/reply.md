**Say no to the leaderboard and the weekly bottom-10% list, and yes to the 8%: offer Operations a way to get it that you can defend.** There is always a bottom 10%, however good everyone gets: 240 drivers named every week, about 160 of them to a subcontractor who can take their routes away.

**Why I'd push back**

- **It mostly ranks routes, not drivers.** Stops per hour depends on stop density, parcel mix, traffic and when the van was loaded. The bottom 10% will likely be rural routes, new drivers and whoever got the late load. Ask your data team how much of the spread comes from route, depot and day before anyone talks about drivers.
- **The quickest ways to raise the number are unsafe or cost customers:** speeding, skipped breaks, glancing at a live ranking while driving, a "not home" card instead of ringing. Every redelivery is a stop you pay for twice.
- **The legal floor (check with counsel; this isn't legal advice).** For drivers you employ in the Netherlands, a performance-monitoring system needs works council consent (article 27(1)(l) WOR) and, per the AP, [a DPIA](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2019-64418.pdf). In Belgium, telling subcontractors which drivers to act on can be read as exercising employer authority; under the [1987 law on hiring out workers](https://employment.belgium.be/en/themes/international/posting/working-conditions-be-respected-case-posting-belgium/hiring-out), those drivers can then be presumed your employees.

**Their fair point:** seeing your rank can lift output, by 6.8% in a [German warehouse](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/blanesiv/tournaments.pdf). But in a [randomised trial](https://repository.upenn.edu/bepp_papers/75), removing rank feedback raised sales by 11%. Neither flagged the bottom to anyone who could drop them.

**What to offer instead**

1. Within two weeks, show where the minutes go, by route and depot: depot waiting, stop sequencing, access problems, failed first attempts, app friction.
2. Fix the top two in the app and in route planning.
3. Pilot in a few depots against matched control depots, judged at depot level on stops per hour, first-attempt success, complaints and incidents, with a stop rule agreed up front.
4. Hold subcontractors to service levels per route and depot, not to lists of names. If drivers want personal feedback, make it private and route-adjusted, designed with them and the works council.

**First step:** a private conversation with the head of Operations before any wider meeting. A line you can use: *"I want the 8% as much as you do. Give me two weeks to show where drivers lose time, then let's pilot fixes in a few depots. I won't build a ranking that tells subcontractors whom to drop: it measures routes more than people, and the legal and safety risk lands on us."*

Two answers would change my advice: where does the 8% come from, and what do they expect subcontractors to do with the names?
