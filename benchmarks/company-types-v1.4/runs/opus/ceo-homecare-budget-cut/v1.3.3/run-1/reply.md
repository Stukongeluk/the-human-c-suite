**Don't adopt the three measures as a package.** Lead the diagnosis yourself and bring the supervisory board one plan within about eight weeks. Roughly, as an estimate: if your costs are around €36m, 7% is about €2.5m a year. Replacing 15% of district nurses saves perhaps €0.3m, an eighth of the gap. The other two save little and put at risk what keeps nurses with you.

The board is right that the gap must close; the trap is picking solutions before anyone knows where the 7% comes from.

| Measure | What it really does | Better version |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer district nurses | ~€0.3m. Insurers require enough HBO nurses for indication; nurses you lose are hard to win back | Skill mix per team from client needs, via natural turnover and level-2 helpers under nurse supervision. No dismissals |
| Minute app | Costs money and adds admin; under hourly billing, admin time is lost income. Needs works council consent (art. 27(1)(l) WOR) and likely a DPIA | Use existing care-record and planning data: worked versus billed hours per team, never per person |
| Central planning | Cheaper planner hours, but risks continuity (insurers want a fixed team per client) and the autonomy that keeps nurses | Pilot central rostering support in three teams that keep client allocation; compare continuity, travel, absence |

**What I'd do next**

1. **Talk to the chair privately first:** "The gap is real and closing it is my responsibility. Give me eight weeks to bring one plan that closes it, with your three proposals tested against the alternatives."
2. **Diagnose in four weeks:** cost per client hour against each tariff, absence, agency spend, worked versus billed hours, travel, overhead, and reserves to bridge 2027. Sector absence was 9.9% in Q1 2026; each point costs you roughly €0.3m in wages.
3. **Revenue before people:** negotiate with cost-price evidence. Municipalities must agree realistic tariffs and indexation (art. 5.4 Uitvoeringsbesluit Wmo 2015; VNG advises 3.59% for 2027), and the cao adds 3.6% from 1 June 2027.
4. **Bring in the works council, client council and nursing advisory council now,** with the real numbers, while they can still change the plan. Central planning likely needs works council advice (art. 25 WOR). Check the client council's Wmcz 2018 rights with counsel; this isn't legal advice.
5. **Go first:** freeze or cut your own and the management team's pay, as a signal, not the savings plan.

Two questions would change my answer: is the gap mainly price (tariffs below cost) or cost (absence, agency staff, unbilled hours)? And how much of 2027 can reserves bridge while structural fixes take effect?

Assumptions: about 0.7 FTE per person, €60k per FTE, staff around 75% of costs. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
