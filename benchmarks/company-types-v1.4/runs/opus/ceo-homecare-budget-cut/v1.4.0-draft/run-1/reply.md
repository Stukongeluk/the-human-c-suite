Don't adopt the three measures as a package; take six weeks to find where the 7% actually sits, then bring the board a plan. On my rough numbers, the most harmful measure, the nurse swap, closes under a tenth of the gap, and none of the three would save money in January anyway.

**The three proposals**

- **15% fewer district nurses.** Rough estimate for your controller to check: if a quarter of your 650 people are district nurses, 15% is about 25. At €10–15k a year less per FTE, that saves €0.2–0.3m against a gap of probably €2.5m or more. You'd lose your scarcest skill, and insurers require enough hbo-nurses for indication and coordination ([CZ's 2027 purchasing policy](https://zorginkoopbeleid.cz.nl/external/asset/download/project/f9ff770a-0425-0000-f337-a117fb0b90e7/name/Wijkverpleging2027_v12645.pdf), p. 21, October 2026). If teams find nurse work that carers can do, shift it through natural turnover.
- **Minute-by-minute app.** The minister, insurers, the NZa and providers agreed to end minute registration in district nursing by 2019, to trust nurses instead of controlling them ([Accountant.nl, December 2018](https://www.accountant.nl/nieuws/2018/12/minutenregistratie-in-wijkverpleging-verdwijnt/)). Use existing planning data, per team, to find travel and admin time.
- **Central planning.** The same CZ policy asks for a fixed team per client with as few different carers as possible (p. 22). Buurtzorg's teams plan their own work and used fewer hours per client ([KPMG 2015, via the Commonwealth Fund](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/case-study/2015/may/home-care-self-governing-nursing-teams-netherlands-buurtzorg-model)). Keep planning in the teams with better tools, and pilot anything central in two or three teams first.

**Where I'd look for the 7%**

1. Revenue first. For municipalities, the Wmo "reële prijs" rule (art. 5.4 Uitvoeringsbesluit Wmo 2015) bases prices on cao wages, travel, training and non-productive hours ([explanatory note](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/blg-775004.pdf)). Check the current text with counsel. With insurers, challenge their tariff build-up line by line.
2. Absence, agency staff and turnover: sector absence hit a record 9.4% in 2024 ([Intrakoop via ANP, June 2025](https://nieuws.nl/algemeen/minder-rode-cijfers-in-ouderenzorg-maar-verzuim-blijft-stijgen)).
3. Overhead, admin and travel time.
4. Reserves to bridge 2027, only if a lasting fix is in sight.

**First steps**

1. Talk to the chair privately before the full board meets: "I share the urgency and I own this gap. Before we take steps that are hard to undo, give me six weeks to show where the 7% sits, with three options, yours included."
2. Go first: freeze or cut your own pay and the board's fees, as a signal.
3. Bring the works council and client council in now, with the real numbers. The app needs the council's consent (art. 27 WOR) and central planning its advice (art. 25 WOR) anyway.

Two questions would change my answer: is the gap price or production caps, and with which funders? How much of your costs go to absence and agency staff?
