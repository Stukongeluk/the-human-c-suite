Don't sign up for "30% this month". Sign up this month for a plan that keeps you alive without the Series B, and let the size of any cut come out of that plan. Two numbers behind that. If payroll is about 70% of your spend, 30% of your people (14) is only about a fifth of your costs. And under Swedish rules a cut decided now stops payroll around March at the earliest, so after a decent package it banks under half of what "30% for nine months" suggests.

**Your investors are right about the urgency.** Waiting for the B is the worst option, and if a cut is needed it should come early, once, and deep enough not to repeat. But they picked the percentage before anyone sized the gap, and "this month" isn't legally possible in Sweden. Depending on your numbers, 30% could be too much or not enough.

**The options** (illustrative model; middle case where revenue covers 40% of monthly costs):

| Option | Runway, growth 4% / 2% a month | Who carries it | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wait for the B | 9 / 8 months | Everyone, if it slips again | Until cash runs out |
| 30% cut only (14 people, from March) | 13 / 10 months | 14 people, mostly the newest; survivors | No |
| Savings without layoffs (step 2) | 13 / 10 months | Leaders first, then everyone a little | Mostly |
| Savings + one 15% cut (7 people) | Reaches break-even / 13 months | 7 people, once | No |

The savings without layoffs buy as much runway inside your window as the 30% cut alone: they start in January and carry no severance. Neither is enough on its own.

People you'd need to cut *on top of* those savings to reach 18 months of runway:

| Revenue covers ... of monthly costs | Growth holds at 4% | Growth slows to 2% |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | ~11 | ~17 |
| 40% | ~2 | ~12 |
| 50% | None | ~6 |

Assumptions, all estimates: payroll 70% of costs; nine months of cash on today's plan; from January, non-payroll spend down 25%, leaders' pay cut, a few people on voluntary reduced hours, hiring freeze with 10% yearly attrition not replaced; any cut effective in March with two months' pay on top of notice.

**What I'd do in the next two weeks**

1. **Own it and go first.** Tell the board and the team plainly that the gap is leadership's: the plan depended on a Series B that has slipped. You and the exec team take the first and largest pay cut (20–30%), and bonuses go to zero. It's a signal of trust, not the savings plan.
2. **Start the fast, reversible savings now.** Freeze hiring except revenue-critical roles (honour accepted offers), cut non-payroll spend line by line, pull cash forward with annual prepayment and milestone billing, and offer voluntary reduced hours or unpaid leave. Contractors get the same notice and honesty as staff. If you don't claim it yet, Sweden's R&D deduction cuts employer contributions by 20% of pay for staff spending at least half their time on R&D ([Skatteverket](https://skatteverket.se/foretag/arbetsgivare/arbetsgivaravgifterochskatteavdrag/forskningsavdrag.4.8dcbbe4142d38302d7cb4.htm)): about SEK 275,000 a month for 25 engineers on SEK 55,000. Short-time work support probably doesn't fit: it needs temporary difficulties from outside events you couldn't foresee ([Skatteverket](https://www.skatteverket.se/foretag/arbetsgivare/stodvidkorttidsarbete.4.339cd9fe17d1714c0775230.html)).
3. **Protect the 4%.** That's about 60% a year and the heart of your B story. Ask your CTO and product lead which projects aren't needed for revenue or B milestones in the next 12 months, and stop them; stopping work is what makes any cut survivable.
4. **Build the real model this week:** monthly for 18 months at 4%, 2% and 0% growth, on gross profit if you sell hardware, with Swedish timing for any people change. Target 18 months on the 2% case, or "default alive": profitable on the cash you have ([Graham, 2015](https://paulgraham.com/aord.html)).
5. **Go back to your investors with a target, not a percentage.** Ask what their 30% is based on, then: "Here's the plan to 18 months on the conservative case. What would you put into an insider bridge?" In the middle case, a bridge of about two months of today's costs does the work of about a dozen redundancies if growth slows. The board decides the size on the model by mid-December.
6. **Tell the team before any cut is decided.** Share the runway and options at an all-hands, ask for ideas, and update the runway monthly.
7. **If a gap remains, make one cut, sized to the conservative case and tied to work you stop.** The Swedish floor (not legal advice; use employment counsel): MBL negotiation before you decide, even without a collective agreement if affected staff are union members; notice to Arbetsförmedlingen at least two months before reductions take effect for 5–25 people; one to six months' notice by length of service; selection by length of service per unit, with three exemptions, unless you agree a list with the union; nine months' re-employment priority ([Företagarna](https://www.foretagarna.se/driva-eget-foretag/hrpersonalfragor/uppsagning-pa-grund-av-arbetsbrist-for-dig-som-ar-arbetsgivare/)). Check who that would actually pick before agreeing any number. Package: TRR if your agreement covers it (bought outplacement if not), pay on top of notice, generous vested-equity terms and placement help, as in Airbnb's 2020 package ([Airbnb](https://news.airbnb.com/a-message-from-co-founder-and-ceo-brian-chesky/)).

**Human impact:** downsizing 1% was associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). The first to leave tend to be the people a B lead wants to meet.

**Measure monthly, shared with board and team:** runway on the 2% case, growth, burn multiple, regretted attrition, a pulse survey.

**Two questions that would change my answer:**
1. What share of monthly costs does revenue (or gross profit) cover, and does the nine months already include growth?
2. Would your existing investors bridge, and on what condition?
