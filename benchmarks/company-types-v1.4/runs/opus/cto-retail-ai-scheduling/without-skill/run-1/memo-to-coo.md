# AI workforce management: how we bank the saving without the blow-up

**To:** COO · **From:** CTO · **Date:** 20 November 2026

## Recommendation

Go ahead with AI-driven demand forecasting and scheduling. Don't sign the vendor's plan as it stands: schedules published three days ahead, shifts changed on the day, all 140 stores live by March. Instead: run the forecast in shadow mode in every store now, run a controlled pilot through Easter, make a go/no-go call in June, and roll out in waves so every store is live by mid-November 2027.

## Why not the plan as pitched

1. **It breaks our rules.** The supermarket cao requires the full roster to be published at least one full week ahead. If it isn't, staff only have to work their structural hours. On-call staff need 4 days' notice by law, or 24 hours under the cao for unforeseen factors, and if we cancel inside that window we still pay. Three-day publishing and same-day cuts don't save what the vendor says they will.
2. **The youth rules limit 15-minute shift building.** For 16 and 17-year-olds, school time counts as working time (maximum 9 hours a day), and no overtime or night work is allowed. Short calls are paid at a 3-hour minimum anyway.
3. **March can't be done safely.** We need works council consent first (art. 27 WOR), plus a DPIA. The December peak and change freeze leave about 12 working weeks for 140 stores, and Easter falls on 28 March. The cao negotiations are tense right now, and "AI cuts 12% of hours" would become the unions' campaign.
4. **The 12% is unverified.** In practice the hours would come out of students' flexible hours: about 840,000 a year, or roughly 1,000 students' jobs, assuming 15 hours a week on average. Unstable schedules drive turnover and cost sales; in Gap's randomised trial, stable schedules raised sales by 7% and productivity by 5%.

## Plan

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Now to January | Pilot contract plus option on the other stores at a locked price; forecast in shadow mode in all 140 stores; rules engine built and tested (working-time act, youth rules, WAB, cao, 2028 flex contracts); consent request to the OR; DPIA |
| February to May | Pilot in 8–12 stores, each matched to a control store; schedules published at least one week ahead; shifts at least 3 hours; open shifts offered on the day to volunteers; manager approves before anything is published |
| June | Go/no-go against thresholds we set now |
| June, then September to mid-November | Rollout in waves of 20–35 stores; all live before the peak freeze, before the AI Act high-risk rules (2 December 2027) and before the flex-work law (1 January 2028) |

## Go/no-go measures, against the control stores

- Labour hours per euro of sales
- Sales, on-shelf availability and queue times
- Staff attrition and sick leave
- Schedule stability (changes after publication, swings in each person's hours)
- Compliance and payroll errors, target zero

## What we ask of the vendor

- Part of the fee tied to measured savings
- Proven handling of Dutch cao and youth rules
- A written AI Act classification and documentation
- EU hosting and a data processing agreement
- Full data export and exit help
- An offline fallback for the store app

## Trade-off

If a March big bang went perfectly, it would deliver about twice the 2027 saving of this plan. That isn't the realistic alternative. The realistic risks are an OR veto, a rollback at Easter, or savings that disappear into turnover and empty shelves. The March deadline is the vendor's quarter, not ours.

## Decision needed

Agree to the pilot plus option structure, and agree the go/no-go thresholds with me before we sign.
