The number behind that: "90% accurate" can't mean that 9 in 10 flagged people commit fraud, unless at least 9% of all your recipients do. [Amsterdam's participation council](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/06/11/1118233/amsterdam-fair-welfare-ai-discriminatory-algorithms-failure/) puts fraud at about 3% of applications. If your rate is 3–5% and the vendor means plain accuracy, investigating the top 10% catches half the real fraud, and 75–85 of every 100 households investigated are innocent. (My estimate; your records give the real rate.)

**Why not this design**
- It has failed before. [Rotterdam](https://www.lighthousereports.com/suspicion-machines-methodology/) scored about 30,000 recipients and investigated the top 10%. Its own documents rated the model only 50% better than random, and it was shelved in 2021 over bias the city couldn't remove. Amsterdam's carefully built Smart Check was stopped in November 2023 for bias. A court struck down [SyRI](https://www.rechtspraak.nl/organisatie-en-contact/organisatie/rechtbanken/rechtbank-den-haag/nieuws/2020/02/syri-legislation-in-breach-of-european-convention-on-human-rights), which linked government data to profile welfare fraud, in February 2020.
- The law. Under the AI Act this is almost certainly high-risk from [2 December 2027](https://lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/09/council-of-the-eu-gives-ai-omnibus-final-green-light-102nbb1), seven months after your go-live. Debt-counselling and housing data bring it close to the [social-scoring ban](https://fpf.org/?p=248448), in force since February 2025. People shared that data to get help. Using it to rank suspects is a new purpose under the GDPR, and would deter people from seeking help. Not legal advice: have your FG check.

The council's aim is fair: fraud erodes support for the safety net, and investigators are scarce. The trap is taking a vendor's figure as evidence, and a score on every resident as the answer.

**Next steps**
1. Talk to the alderman privately before this reaches the college or council. A line you can use: "I want you to show results on fraud by [month]. I can't advise scoring every recipient: Rotterdam dropped that design, and most people it flags would be innocent. Here's what I can deliver instead."
2. Ask the vendor in writing for precision and recall at the top-10% cut-off, their test data, and error rates by nationality, age, gender, language and postcode.
3. Propose six months of:
   - counting how many recent investigations found fraud, honest mistakes or nothing;
   - fixing the main causes of wrong payments;
   - pointing investigators at explainable signals you already get, such as Inlichtingenbureau income matches, plus a small random sample to measure your true fraud rate.

   Involve the client council and works council from the start.
4. If a model still makes sense then, decide in 2027 with real data, a DPIA, bias tests and no debt-counselling data.
