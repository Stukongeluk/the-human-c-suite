Advise the council not to buy this system as specified, and not to go live in six months. Suppose 5% of recipients commit real fraud (an assumption; check yours). A model that flags nobody is then 95% accurate, and even a perfect model would fill its top 10% with at least as many innocent people as fraudsters. Offer a six-month test on your own data instead.

**Why**

- **"90% accurate" is the wrong number.** Ask the vendor for precision in the top 10% and error rates per group. Rotterdam ran this design on about 30,000 recipients. Its model was [barely better than random and flagged migrant mothers far more often than Dutch men](https://www.lighthousereports.com/suspicion-machines-methodology/), and the city dropped it in 2021.
- **The data.** Debt-counselling data is collected to help people. Using it to score them raises a GDPR purpose-limitation problem and deters people from seeking help early. The Commission's guidelines cite welfare fraud scoring on [data from unrelated contexts](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-the-eu-ai-act-unpacking-social-scoring-as-a-prohibited-ai-practice) as an example under the social-scoring ban, which has applied since February 2025.
- **Precedent and law.** SyRI was struck down in February 2020. DUO is [refunding €61 million to more than 10,000 students](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2024/11/11/gecontroleerde-oud-studenten-met-uitwonendenbeurs-krijgen-geld-terug) after risk checks that discriminated indirectly. Under the AI Act this system is almost certainly high-risk, with a fundamental-rights impact assessment required [from 2 December 2027](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/en/portugal/intellectual-property/art/digital-omnibus-ai-has-been-published).

The goal is fair, and selecting cases on relevant data is legitimate.

**The six months instead**

1. **Months 1–2, baseline:** hits per 100 investigations today, money recovered against cost, the share of honest mistakes, and a random sample for the real fraud rate.
2. **Prevent honest mistakes:** simpler change reporting and clearer letters. The [Wet handhaving sociale zekerheid](https://www.eerstekamer.nl/nonav/wetsvoorstel/36785_wet_handhaving_sociale), passed by the Senate on 27 October 2026, will let you skip fines for them.
3. **Months 3–6, shadow test:** test rules on your existing legal signals, and the vendor's model if you wish, on historical data against the random sample, measuring errors per group. Use no debt-counselling data, do the DPIA and a human-rights assessment (IAMA) first, and involve your DPO, investigators and client council.
4. **Month 6:** go or no-go, against stop criteria agreed now.

**First step:** a private talk with the alderman before the council meets. A line you can use: *"I want our investigators focused too, and in six months I can show you what works on our data. I can't put every resident on benefits on a ranked list built partly from debt-counselling data. That's what SyRI and Rotterdam were stopped for, and the headlines would carry your name."*

The trap is the deadline: six months is enough to sign a contract, not to test safely. Two questions would change my advice: is the council fixing money, capacity or trust? And what do you find per 100 investigations today?
