### The three traps to name clearly

1. **The "90% accurate" trap:** When true fraud is rare (say 2%), a trivial model that predicts *"no fraud"* is already 98% accurate. Until the vendor gives **precision, recall, false-positive rates by subgroup, and the full variable list** tested on real municipal data, "90% accurate" is a sales slogan, not evidence.
2. **The legal red lines** *(flag this for your FG/DPO and Juridische Zaken—this is not legal advice)*:
   - **Debt-counselling (*schuldhulpverlening*) data:** Residents share this voluntarily to get help. Repurposing it for fraud surveillance breaches GDPR purpose limitation (*doelbinding*) and scares people away from debt help, raising downstream municipal costs.
   - **SyRI & ECHR Art. 8:** Blanket cross-linking of welfare, debt, and housing data to score everyone mirrors the SyRI architecture struck down by the Hague District Court in 2020.
   - **EU AI Act (as of November 2026):** Public-benefit risk scoring is High-Risk (Annex III; enforceable **2 December 2027** under the 2026 Digital Omnibus, requiring a Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment, bias testing, and *Algoritmeregister* publication). Worse, public-authority "social scoring" from unrelated data has been **prohibited since February 2025**.
   - **Works Council (*OR*):** Major IT/process changes trigger advisory rights (Art. 25 WOR) and consent rights if the tool tracks investigators' work (Art. 27(1)(l) WOR).
3. **The six-month deadline:** A DPIA (plus possible *Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens* prior consultation), public procurement, FRIA, OR consultation, and bias audit realistically take **12–18 months**.
