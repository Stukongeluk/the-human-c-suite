### First, be honest about the two traps in front of you

1. **Your adviser's trap:** A 35% drop at two big customers looks like roughly a fifth of revenue, so a spreadsheet adviser cuts a fifth of headcount (45 people) to show the bank "decisiveness." That ignores German cash timing, *Sozialauswahl* (where you cannot select by performance and risk losing younger machinists who can run new lines), and the years it takes to rebuild skilled trades around Stuttgart.
2. **The family-legacy trap:** Your grandfather's record matters — family firms that avoid knee-jerk layoffs and run frugally weather downturns better (Kachaner, Stalk & Bloch, HBR 2012). **But your grandfather didn't face a permanent drivetrain shift removing part of the order book.** If you treat a permanent loss as temporary and run out of cash next year, you lose all 210 jobs. The real promise isn't "never change headcount under any circumstance" — it is never treating people as the first cost to cut, exhausting every other lever first, and if roles must end, doing it once and protecting the people.

And see the bank's side clearly: they aren't demanding 45 layoffs out of cruelty. They are afraid of lending into a family business that is in denial about the combustion-engine decline. Give them hard numbers and a credible cash plan, and they care about **liquidity and viability**, not a headcount trophy.

### What to do in the next 10 days (before you talk to the bank)

1. **Own the concentration risk and go first.** Letting two automotive suppliers dominate the order book is a leadership risk, not the shop floor's fault. Cut your own managing-director draw, freeze management bonuses, and suspend family dividends immediately. Put that on page 1 of the bank deck — it earns credibility with both the bank and your employees.
2. **Pull the real numbers — not guesses.** With your *Steuerberater* and ERP, split the 35% drop into **temporary vs. permanent** revenue and contribution margin; build a **13-week rolling cash forecast**; tally fast non-people cash (overdue receivables, excess inventory, energy, scrap/rework, external machining you can bring back in-house, deferrable CapEx); and pull **aggregated** HR figures (overtime, agency/temp staff, upcoming 2027–2028 retirements, tenure bands).
3. **Use every non-layoff lever first:**
   - Stop overtime, wind down temp/agency cover with honest notice, and freeze external hiring.
   - Review underpriced legacy parts and push high-margin service, retrofit, and spare-parts work to your installed base.
   - Use **Kurzarbeitergeld** *only* for the cyclical/temporary part of the drop — and note the timing: as of November 2026, the 24-month maximum duration runs until **31 December 2026** and falls back to **12 months from 1 January 2027** unless extended again. Use idle hours for cross-training toward non-ICE work.
4. **Size the structural gap honestly — and protect the 14 apprentices.** Once non-people savings, realistic non-automotive/EV pipeline, and 24-month natural retirements are counted, see what structural payroll gap actually remains. Close it first through redeployment, retraining, *Altersteilzeit*, and voluntary exits (*Aufhebungsverträge*). Only if a verified gap still threatens solvency should you do **one** right-sized compulsory reduction — cutting the underlying product lines alongside the roles and funding day-one transition support (such as a *Transfergesellschaft* and retraining). **Keep all 14 apprentices:** they are strongly protected after probation under German vocational training law (BBiG), and they are your skilled workforce five years from now.
5. **Bring in employment counsel and your works council now.** *(This is management advice, not legal advice — retain a Fachanwalt für Arbeitsrecht this week.)* Check whether you have an elected *Betriebsrat* or an applicable *Tarifvertrag*. If you have a *Betriebsrat*, share the real numbers *now* while they can still help shape the plan — *Kurzarbeit* needs their consent (§87(1) no. 3 BetrVG), and major operational changes require an *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan* (§§111–112 BetrVG). Also note that employer-initiated termination agreements count toward the §17 KSchG mass-dismissal threshold (>10% or >25 people in 30 days for your size).
