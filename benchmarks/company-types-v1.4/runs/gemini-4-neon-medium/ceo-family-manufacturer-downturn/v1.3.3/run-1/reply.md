### What to do this week

1. **Demand the math behind "45."** Ask your adviser to show total company revenue exposure to those two customers (35% off two customers is not automatically 21% of total capacity), the severance cash outflow, and when payroll savings would actually hit your bank account.
2. **Own the strategic risk and go first.** Over-reliance on two ICE automotive suppliers is a leadership responsibility, not the shop floor's. Cut your own pay and pause owner distributions before asking anyone else to sacrifice hours or roles.
3. **Protect the 14 apprentices publicly.** Under German law (BBiG), apprentices past probation are strongly protected from operational dismissal anyway—and in Stuttgart's labour market, they are your future shop floor. Make it clear their training continues.
4. **Bring the works council in now, with the real numbers.** If you have a *Betriebsrat*, share the bank's February deadline and the permanent EV shift at the problem stage—while they can still help design the mix of *Arbeitszeitkonten*, *Kurzarbeit*, retraining, and (if truly needed) voluntary *Altersteilzeit* or a *Transfergesellschaft*.
5. **Be honest with yourself about the structural floor.** If, after steps 1–5, permanent demand still cannot carry 196 regular roles, a smaller, well-funded voluntary or single-round reduction done decently—with transition coaching and retraining from day one, and cutting the corresponding legacy work—is far kinder than bleeding cash and endangering everyone.
