# Restructuring & Credit-Line Renewal Plan (Working Framework)

**Prepared:** November 2026  
**Target deadline:** Credit-line renewal, February 2027  
**Company profile:** Family-owned machine builder (Baden-Württemberg), 210 employees (196 regular staff, 14 apprentices)  
**Trigger:** 35% order reduction from two largest automotive-supplier customers, including a permanent volume reduction at one customer shifting to electric drivetrains.

> **Important note:** This document is a management and planning framework, not legal or financial-restructuring advice. German employment law, co-determination rules, and insolvency/restructuring obligations (including IDW S6 requirements) depend on your exact corporate structure, collective agreements (*Tarifverträge*), and whether a works council (*Betriebsrat*) is in place. Review all employment and bank steps with a German employment lawyer (*Fachanwalt für Arbeitsrecht*) and your tax/restructuring adviser before acting.

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Core Diagnosis

1. **Do not announce 45 involuntary dismissals now.** In Germany, cutting 45 long-tenure employees in November will not improve cash by February 2027—it will consume cash first through statutory notice periods (up to 7 months after 20 years of service under § 622 BGB), mandatory works-council negotiations (§§ 111–112 BetrVG), collective-dismissal waiting periods (§ 17 KSchG), and social-plan severance. It also risks losing key technical skills under statutory social selection (*Sozialauswahl*, § 1(3) KSchG).
2. **Separate the two problems.** You are facing two distinct issues that require different tools:
   - **A permanent structural loss** from the customer transitioning to electric drivetrains. Short-time work (*Kurzarbeit*) cannot fix this; Germany's Institute for Employment Research (IAB) warned in 2026 that relying on *Kurzarbeit* in a structural downturn delays necessary adaptation.
   - **A cyclical and transition gap** from the second customer's cut and the 12–18 months needed to win replacement orders in non-ICE or adjacent sectors.
3. **What the bank actually needs by February:** Not a panic headcount number, but a credible 13-week liquidity forecast, a 24-month turnaround plan showing how you match capacity to realistic post-ICE demand, visible owner commitment (leaders cutting their own draw first), and pre-agreed fallback triggers if orders stay weak.

## 3. Protecting the 14 Apprentices

- **Legal reality:** Under the German Vocational Training Act (*Berufsbildungsgesetz*, BBiG), apprentices past their probationary period cannot ordinarily be dismissed for operational reasons unless the business closes entirely.
- **Strategic reality:** In Baden-Württemberg's machine-building labour market, trained machinists and mechatronics technicians are scarce. Cutting or neglecting apprenticeships saves little cash and starves the business of the exact skills needed for the next decade.
- **Action:** State explicitly—to the bank, the works council, and the workforce—that all 14 apprenticeships continue, and align their training modules toward service, automation, and non-ICE applications.

## 4. Ten-Week Action Plan (November 2026 – February 2027)

### Weeks 1–2 (Late November 2026): Facts, Cash, and Leadership Signal
- **Build a 13-week rolling cash-flow forecast** and map exact revenue and contribution margin from the two automotive customers (separate permanent EV loss from potentially recoverable volume).
- **Challenge the "45 people" figure:** Ask the adviser to show the underlying capacity model, severance cash-outflow, notice-period timeline, and *Sozialauswahl* impact.
- **Leaders go first:** Document your own managing-director salary reduction, suspension of owner drawings/dividends, and elimination of management bonuses and non-essential perks. Assess whether family equity or a subordinated shareholder loan (*Gesellschafterdarlehen*) is feasible to strengthen the bank's confidence.
- **Engage specialist German employment counsel** (*Fachanwalt für Arbeitsrecht*) to verify whether a works council exists, which collective agreements apply, and the exact *Kurzarbeit* and § 17 KSchG thresholds.

### Weeks 3–4 (Early December 2026): Early Consultation & Co-Design
- **Bring the works council (*Betriebsrat*) in early** (or employee representatives if none exists) with the real numbers—while the plan can still be shaped. Share the bank deadline, the permanent EV shift, and the leadership cuts already taken.
- **Co-design the bridge:** Negotiate *Arbeitszeitkonten* drawdown, targeted *Kurzarbeit* for the transition period (noting that the 24-month maximum duration reverts to the statutory 12 months on 1 January 2027 unless extended by law), and paid retraining (*Qualifizierung*) for staff moving into service or new sectors.
- **Audit non-personnel spend and working capital:** Freeze non-essential capex, renegotiate supplier payment terms, collect overdue receivables, and review external contractors (giving honest notice if ending contracts).

### Weeks 5–7 (Late December 2026 – Early January 2027): Structural Sizing & Commercial Plan
- **Size the true structural gap:** After deducting non-personnel savings, natural attrition, and realistic 12–18 month service/retrofit/new-sector orders, calculate the exact permanent capacity gap (in hours and euros, not a rough headcount guess).
- **Design voluntary measures first:** If a permanent headcount reduction is still unavoidable, model *Altersteilzeit* (partial retirement), a voluntary exit programme with employer veto (*doppelte Freiwilligkeit*), and a *Transfergesellschaft* (providing coaching, retraining, and job-search support from day one).
- **Define what work stops:** If roles are reduced, explicitly stop the corresponding legacy product lines or low-margin work so remaining staff are not overloaded.

### Weeks 8–10 (Mid-January – February 2027): Bank Package & Decision
- **Deliver the bank restructuring pack** (see Section 5) at least 3–4 weeks before the February renewal date.
- **Hold a transparent all-hands briefing** (after works-council alignment) so staff hear the full picture from you—not through rumours.
- **If involuntary reductions remain unavoidable:** Execute them once, with full legal compliance (§§ 111–112 BetrVG, § 17 KSchG, § 1(3) KSchG), fair transition support from day one of notice, and in-person conversations led by direct managers and leadership.

## 5. What to Put in the Bank Pack

1. **Honest problem statement:** Acknowledge leadership's ownership of customer concentration and the permanent ICE-to-EV shift—no wishful thinking.
2. **13-week liquidity forecast & 24-month P&L/cash model:** Three scenarios (Base, Downside, Severe Downside) with clear covenant headroom.
3. **Owner contribution:** Quantified MD pay cut, paused distributions, and any subordinated shareholder support.
4. **Phased cost & capacity plan:** Immediate cash/working-capital actions, *Kurzarbeit* bridge for cyclical volume, and a funded, legally sound path (voluntary first, *Transfergesellschaft*, involuntary only if needed) for the permanent gap—showing *when* cash actually leaves and *when* savings actually arrive.
5. **Commercial diversification plan:** Concrete pipeline for service, spare parts, retrofits, and non-ICE customers, with conservative conversion assumptions.
6. **Pre-agreed milestones & triggers:** Monthly KPIs (order intake, cash balance, utilisation, sick leave, and regretted attrition) with agreed actions if the downside scenario hits.

## 6. How to Communicate (Core Principles)

- **Own the past:** *"We grew too dependent on two automotive customers. That is my responsibility as managing director, not yours."*
- **Tell the truth about the future:** *"One of those customers is moving to electric drivetrains and those volumes will not come back. Pretending otherwise would put all 210 jobs at risk."*
- **Show you went first:** State plainly what you and senior management have already cut before asking anything of the team.
- **Be honest about what you can and cannot promise:** Do not promise "zero layoffs forever" if the numbers cannot back it up. Promise that involuntary exits are the absolute last resort, that the 14 apprenticeships are protected, and that no one will be blindsided.

## 7. Key Legal & Statutory Checkpoints (Germany, as of November 2026)

- **Works council co-determination:** *Kurzarbeit* / temporary hours reduction requires works-council agreement (§ 87(1) no. 3 BetrVG). Major operational changes in companies with >20 employees require negotiating an *Interessenausgleich* and *Sozialplan* (§§ 111–112 BetrVG).
- **Collective dismissal notification (§ 17 KSchG):** In an establishment of 60–499 employees, dismissing 10% or more than 25 employees within 30 days (including employer-initiated termination agreements) requires advance notification to the *Agentur für Arbeit*. Procedural errors can void dismissals.
- **Social selection (§ 1(3) KSchG):** Operational dismissals require weighing tenure, age, maintenance obligations, and severe disability among comparable employees.
- **Statutory notice periods (§ 622 BGB):** Scale with length of service, up to 7 months to the end of a calendar month after 20 years.
- **Kurzarbeitergeld duration:** The extended 24-month maximum runs until 31 December 2026 and reverts to the statutory 12-month maximum from 1 January 2027 unless extended by legislation.
