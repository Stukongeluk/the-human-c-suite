### Why this feature will backfire
1. **Stops per hour measures the route, not just the driver.** Density, apartment stairs, traffic, commercial vs. residential stops, parcel size, and late depot loading explain much of the variance. Ranking individuals on raw speed punishes drivers on harder routes.
2. **Speed-only incentives create expensive side effects.** A live leaderboard in a delivery van is a road-safety distraction. Weekly bottom-10% flags drive shortcuts: doorstep drops, fake "not home" scans, damaged parcels, and higher driver churn. Any speed metric without paired quality and safety metrics gets gamed.
3. **Where did 8% come from?** Ask for the evidence behind it—and whether it is net of re-deliveries, damage claims, support tickets, and accident costs.
4. **Serious NL, BE, and EU legal exposure** *(not legal advice—check with your NL/BE employment counsel and DPO before any commitment)*:
   - **Netherlands works council (OR):** With ~800 employed drivers, a driver-app performance tracker is a monitoring system requiring **prior OR consent** under Article 27(1)(l) WOR.
   - **Belgium:** Individual monitoring triggers social-dialogue rules (Ondernemingsraad / CPBW) and strict GDPR proportionality/transparency rules (including CAO nr. 81 principles). Publishing a live named leaderboard to peers is very hard to defend under GDPR data minimisation in either country.
   - **Subcontractor co-employment risk:** Weekly flags telling subcontractors which individual drivers are in the bottom 10% look like exercising employer authority (*gezagsverhouding* / *werkgeversgezag*) over their staff—a major reclassification and liability trigger in both NL and BE.
   - **EU rules:** The **Platform Work Directive (2024/2831)**—whose transposition deadline is **2 December 2026**—tightens rules on automated monitoring and algorithmic management. Under the **EU AI Act**, AI-based worker evaluation is high-risk (Annex III); the employer duty to inform workers and representatives (Art. 26(7)) applies from **2 December 2027** after the 2026 Digital Omnibus, not yet, but national co-determination and GDPR apply today.

Come with a concrete, reversible plan to find real throughput gains in the **system**, not by squeezing individuals:

- **Ask the drivers and representatives early:** Bring employed drivers, subcontractor reps, and your works council/unions in at the problem stage. They already know which addresses, handoffs, and app steps waste 20 minutes a day.
- **Measure balanced outcomes at the depot and contract level:** Track stops per hour *alongside* first-time delivery success, claims/damage rate, and safety incidents. Manage subcontractors on **company-level SLA outcomes**, not individual driver blacklists.
- **Keep any in-app feedback private and helpful:** If you pilot driver-facing metrics at all, show a driver only their own route progress and tools that remove friction (better delivery notes, accurate ETAs)—never a peer ranking.
