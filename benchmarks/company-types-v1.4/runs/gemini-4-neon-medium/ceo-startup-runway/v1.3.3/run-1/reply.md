### Why "30% this month" doesn't work—commercially or legally

The investors are right about the problem, even if they are wrong about the prescription. If Series B starts in summer 2027 and takes 4–6 months to close, cash won't land until late 2027 or early 2028. Running out of cash saves nobody.

What they are missing is how Swedish employment law and cash timing actually work (this is not legal advice—confirm all of this with Swedish employment counsel before taking action; rules checked November 2026):

* **You cannot legally execute a 14–15 person cut "this month."** Under the Co-determination Act (MBL), you must negotiate with the relevant unions before deciding on major redundancies. Dismissing 5–25 people also requires notifying *Arbetsförmedlingen* (*varsel*) **at least 2 months in advance**.
* **You don't simply pick who stays.** Under the Employment Protection Act (LAS), selection follows length of service per operational unit unless you negotiate an agreed list (*avtalsturlista*) with the union, with only three statutory exemptions per employer. Notice periods run 1–6 months (typically 3 months in Stockholm tech).
* **The cash arrives late, while the damage arrives immediately.** Between consultation, notice periods, and accrued holiday payouts, a layoff started today saves almost no cash for the next **3–5 months**. Meanwhile, you risk losing institutional knowledge, slowing the milestones Series B requires, and triggering regretted departures among the people you need most.
