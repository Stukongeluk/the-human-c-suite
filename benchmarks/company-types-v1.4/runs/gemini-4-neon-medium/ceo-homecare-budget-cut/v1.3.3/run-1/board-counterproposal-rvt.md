# Board Memo: Closing the 7% Funding Gap Without Eroding Care Capacity

**To:** Supervisory Board (Raad van Toezicht)  
**From:** Director (Bestuurder)  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Counter-proposal to close next year's 7% structural funding shortfall  
**Status:** Draft for private discussion with the Chair prior to the full Board meeting

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Recommendation

**Recommendation:** Reject the three proposed measures (replacing 15% of district nurses, minute-by-minute visit tracking, and centralising planning) and approve a **three-week financial diagnostic followed by a 12-month recovery plan** targeting **6.0%–10.5% in structural margin improvement** through overhead reduction, contract and indication discipline, lower agency (ZZP) and sickness-absence costs, and team-led route and admin improvements.

The 7% shortfall between next year's insurer and municipal contracts and our cost base is serious and cannot be bridged by wishful thinking. The Supervisory Board is right to demand a concrete plan before reserves erode. Responsibility for our current cost structure sits with executive leadership, not with our nurses and carers. Accordingly, leadership will take the first step—freezing executive pay and non-essential management spend—before asking teams for change.

However, the three measures currently on the table are likely to **widen** the deficit in year one while damaging our clinical capability, triggering avoidable legal delays under the Dutch Works Councils Act (WOR), and driving scarce nurses to competitors or into agency work.

## 2. Assessment of the Board's Three Proposals

| Proposed measure | Intended saving | Hidden costs & operational risks | Legal & governance exposure (NL)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Replace 15% of district nurses with lower-qualified carers** | Lower average wage per care hour | District nurses perform Zvw indications, manage complex care, and build client self-reliance—reducing total hours needed per client. Cutting them shrinks complex-care capacity and increases remaining nurses' supervision load. Workforce reductions also trigger voluntary exits among remaining staff (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008 found a 1% cut associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year), pushing up expensive ZZP/agency backfill. Severance and notice periods consume much of year-one savings. | **WOR Art. 25** (Works Council advisory right, with Enterprise Chamber appeal). **WMCO** collective dismissal notification and union consultation if ≥20 roles are affected in one UWV area within 3 months (including employer-initiated settlement agreements). Statutory transition payments and 1–4 months' notice. |
| **2. Minute-by-minute visit-tracking app** | Higher billable utilisation (*declarabiliteit*) | Measuring minutes turns time into the target rather than client independence. It creates perverse incentives (stretching simple visits or rushing complex ones), adds administrative friction, and erodes trust—driving up sickness absence (*ziekteverzuim*). In a 650-person organisation, a 1–2 percentage point rise in absence wipes out theoretical utilisation gains. | **WOR Art. 27(1)(l)** gives the Works Council a **binding consent right (*instemmingsrecht*)** over systems suited to monitoring employee attendance, behaviour, or performance. GDPR/AVG proportionality also applies. (If AI is used for task allocation or monitoring, EU AI Act Art. 26(7) adds a duty to inform worker reps from 2 December 2027.) |
| **3. Move all planning to one central office** | Economies of scale and tighter rotas | Adds fixed overhead (central planners, coordination handoffs, software) immediately. Strips out local team knowledge of client needs, travel bottlenecks, and continuity of care—increasing unbillable travel time and client complaints. Public-sector trust reforms (e.g. City of Copenhagen; Vallentin & Thygesen, 2017) show that trust paired with a few clear outcome controls outperforms heavy central control. | **WOR Art. 25** (Works Council advisory right for major organisational restructuring). |

*\*Note: This is a management assessment, not legal advice. All labour-law, CAO VVT, and privacy implications must be verified with employment counsel and our Data Protection Officer before any formal decision.*

## 3. Alternative Plan: Closing the 7% Gap

Before altering frontline care roles, we should exhaust measures that protect clinical quality and nurse retention. All percentage ranges below are **preliminary estimates** to be validated against the general ledger, HR data, and contract files during a 3-week diagnostic.

| Lever | What changes | Estimated impact (% of total cost) | Time to cash | Reversibility | Lead |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **1. Overhead, real estate, IT & vendor spend** | Consolidate unused office space, cut idle software licences, pause external consultants, freeze non-care hiring, and freeze executive pay/perks. | 1.5% – 2.5% | 3 – 6 months | High | Finance & Director |
| **2. Contract & indication discipline** | Audit under-indicated complex clients (Zvw/Wmo), challenge below-cost tariffs jointly with regional peers/ActiZ, and decline or cap structurally loss-making municipal Wmo lots. | 1.5% – 2.5% | 6 – 12 months | Medium | Director & Contracting |
| **3. ZZP/agency reduction & sickness absence** | Replace high-cost ZZP hours via internal flex pools and stable rotas; introduce early, practical return-to-work support and workload adjustment (modelled on Norway's Inclusive Working Life agreement, where participating employers improved return-to-work after long-term absence; *Scand J Work Environ Health*, 2023). | 1.5% – 2.5% | 6 – 12 months | High | HR & Team Coaches |
| **4. Team-owned routes & admin removal** | Remove internal reports and forms nobody uses; tighten team catchment areas to cut travel time; give each team a clear team-level financial guardrail rather than individual tracking. | 1.0% – 2.0% | 3 – 9 months | High | Care Teams & Operations |
| **5. Natural attrition & team-led skill-mix (only if needed)** | If levers 1–4 leave a residual gap, adjust role mix gradually through natural attrition, voluntary retraining, and team-designed task sharing—avoiding forced exits. | 0.5% – 1.0% | 12 – 18 months | Low–Medium | HR, OR & Teams |
| **Total addressable range** | **Sum of levers 1–5** | **6.0% – 10.5%** | **3 – 18 months** | — | — |

**Bridge financing:** Because levers 2, 3, and 5 ramp up over 6–12 months, we should review our liquidity and reserve policy now to confirm how much of year one can be safely bridged while structural savings land—avoiding irreversible cuts made purely for calendar-year optics.

## 4. Governance, Works Council & Fair Process

Research on organisational change shows that fair process—engaging people early, explaining the reasoning behind decisions, and setting clear expectations—makes hard transitions succeed even when choices are tough (Kim & Mauborgne, 1997). In the Netherlands, it is also the fastest path because early co-design prevents formal WOR disputes.

1. **Involve the Works Council (OR) at the problem stage:** Share the exact 7% contract shortfall and underlying P&L with the OR immediately, before any formal proposal is drafted. Treat the OR as a co-design partner on levers 1–5.
2. **Co-design with district teams:** Present the financial reality openly to team representatives and ask each team to identify wasted admin, travel leakage, and safe task-sharing opportunities.
3. **No individual surveillance:** Commit explicitly to the OR and staff that we will not introduce minute-by-minute individual monitoring.

---

## 5. Balanced Metrics (Team-Level Only)

Every financial target will be paired with a quality and people counter-metric, reviewed monthly at the team and organisational level—never used to rank individual nurses.

| Financial / operational metric | Paired counter-metric (quality & people) | Decision it informs |
|---|---|---|
| Operating margin & cash runway (%) | Reserve buffer vs. covenant floor | Whether bridge reserves or lever 5 are required |
| Team-level billable share (%) & travel time | Client satisfaction & continuity (unique carers per client) | Catchment adjustments and admin removal |
| ZZP / agency spend (% of payroll) | Sickness absence (*ziekteverzuim*) & regretted attrition | Flex-pool staffing and workload balance |
| Indication accuracy & contract margin | Clinical escalations / unplanned hospitalisations | Tariff renegotiation and Wmo lot decisions |

---

## 6. Next 90 Days & Decision Gate

- **Weeks 1–3 (Diagnostic):** Finance, HR, and Contracting validate the exact euro gap per contract (Zvw vs. Wmo), ZZP spend, sickness absence cost, travel time, and overhead. Director holds a working session with the OR to share the numbers.
- **Week 4 (Board Decision):** Present the verified euro plan to the Supervisory Board with concrete targets per lever and reserve-bridge requirements.
- **Months 2–3 (Execution):** Lock in overhead cuts, remove redundant admin, launch team-level catchment and flex-pool pilots, and open contract/tariff discussions with insurers and municipalities.
- **Month 6 (Review Gate):** Compare realised margin, absence, ZZP spend, and client satisfaction against plan. Only if levers 1–4 fall materially short of closing the gap will we activate lever 5 with the OR.

---

## 7. Key Assumptions & What Would Change This Recommendation

**Key assumptions:**
- Our current reserves and cash flow can absorb a 6–12 month transition while structural levers take effect.
- At least 4–5 percentage points of the 7% gap sit in overhead, ZZP reliance, sickness absence, travel/admin waste, and under-indicated or loss-making contracts.
- Insurers and municipalities are not imposing an immediate liquidity covenant breach in Q1.

**What would change this recommendation:**
- If our cash runway is under 6 months and reserves cannot bridge the ramp-up, we would need faster, deeper non-care cuts and immediate discussions with lenders and insurers on continuity financing.
- If the diagnostic shows overhead, ZZP, and absence are already at sector best-in-class levels, a larger structural resizing of loss-making municipal contracts (and associated staff redeployment with the OR and unions) would be unavoidable.
