### Why the three proposals backfire

* **Replacing 15% of district nurses (*wijkverpleegkundigen*) with lower-qualified carers:** District nurses handle Zvw indications, complex care, and client self-reliance—often *reducing* total care hours per client. Cutting them shrinks complex-care capacity, overloads remaining nurses, and triggers voluntary exits among the people you want to keep (research shows a 1% workforce cut is associated with a 31% rise in voluntary turnover the next year), forcing expensive ZZP/agency backfill. Severance and 1–4 months' notice also eat year-one savings.
* **Minute-by-minute visit-tracking app:** Turning minutes into the target rewards logging time over helping clients stay independent, adds admin friction, and drives up sickness absence (*ziekteverzuim*). It also hits a hard legal wall: under **Article 27(1)(l) WOR**, your Works Council (*Ondernemingsraad*, mandatory above 50 staff) has a **binding consent right (*instemmingsrecht*)** over systems suited to monitoring employee attendance, behaviour, or performance.
* **Moving all planning to one central office:** Adds fixed overhead right when you need to cut it, strips out local knowledge of clients and routes, increases unbillable travel time, and hurts continuity of care. Both centralising planning and restructuring nursing roles trigger the OR's **advisory right (WOR Art. 25)**, and dismissing 20 or more people in one UWV area within three months (including employer-initiated settlement agreements) triggers **WMCO** notification and union consultation. *(Not legal advice—verify specifics and CAO VVT rules with employment counsel.)*
