# Closing the 7% Funding Gap Without Eroding Care Quality or Staff Retention

**To:** Supervisory Board (Raad van Toezicht)  
**From:** Director (Bestuurder)  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Status:** Discussion paper — prior to formal Works Council (OR) and Client Council consultation

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Recommendation

Next year's contracts with health insurers and municipalities leave a **7% gap** between contracted income and our projected costs. I take full responsibility for closing that gap so the organisation remains financially healthy and able to serve our clients. Doing nothing is not an option.

However, I recommend **rejecting the three proposed measures** (replacing 15% of district nurses with lower-qualified carers, introducing minute-by-minute visit tracking, and centralising all team planning). Together, they carry high legal, clinical and financial risks: they are likely to drive up sickness absence, agency spend and voluntary turnover, while delivering little net saving in Year 1.

Instead, I propose a **four-part recovery plan** to close the 7% gap over 12–18 months, using reserves only as a time-limited bridge if needed:

1. **Cut avoidable cost leaks first (est. 2.5%–4.0%):** Reduce sickness absence and reliance on agency/ZZP cover, trim non-care overhead and unused IT/office spend, and start with executive and management restraint.
2. **Close the contract and billing gap (est. 1.5%–2.5%):** Present full-cost budgets to insurers and municipalities, review under-claimed care complexity, and renegotiate or exit structurally loss-making non-core contracts.
3. **Improve team-level productivity through trust, not surveillance (est. 1.0%–2.0%):** Keep planning in the teams with better scheduling tools, remove unused admin, and track team-level direct-care ratios and route efficiency rather than individual minutes.
4. **Adjust skill mix gradually through natural attrition (est. 0.5%–1.0%):** Where local caseloads genuinely allow a different mix, evolve roles via attrition and team-led redesign — protecting the district nurses' clinical triage, care indication (*indicatiestelling*) and coaching capacity.

*(All percentage ranges above are estimates to be verified against our actual general ledger, HR/absence, agency and rostering data over the next 14 days.)*

## 2. Why the Three Proposed Measures Carry High Risk

| Proposed measure | Intended saving | Key operational, clinical & legal risks | Net Year 1 financial reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Replace 15% of district nurses with lower-qualified carers** | Lower hourly wage bill | District nurses drive clinical triage, care indication and client self-reliance. Diluting skill mix often increases total hours per client and burnout (Aiken et al., 2002; KPMG/Commonwealth Fund, 2015 on Buurtzorg). Involuntary cuts trigger **Art. 25 WOR** (OR advisory right), potentially **WMCO** (20+ exits in 3 months), notice periods (1–4 months), transition payments and survivor turnover (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). | **Low or negative in Year 1:** Severance, notice periods, recruitment and temporary agency cover eat early savings. |
| **Minute-by-minute visit registration app** | Capture unbilled minutes, tighten pace | Requires **OR consent under Art. 27(1)(l) WOR** (monitoring attendance/behaviour/performance). Raises GDPR/AVG proportionality issues (special-category health data). Destroys professional trust, adds admin toil, and accelerates sickness absence and exits in a tight nursing market. | **Uncertain to negative:** Software, rollout and higher absence/turnover likely outweigh recovered minutes. |
| **Move all planning to one central office** | Higher schedule utilisation | Adds central planner payroll and software overhead while losing local route and client knowledge. Breaks continuity of care, triggers **Art. 25 WOR** and Client Council consultation, and reduces schedule stability (Williams et al., 2018). | **Negative in Year 1:** Upfront central office and transition costs arrive before any utilisation gain. |

*Note: Legal references (WOR, WMCO, GDPR/AVG, CAO VVT) are flagged for governance awareness and must be verified with employment counsel before any formal decision.*

## 3. Side-by-Side Comparison of Paths

| Dimension | Board's 3-Measure Package | Recommended 4-Part Plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Estimated 12–18 month impact** | Uncertain; high risk of Year 1 net deficit from transition & turnover costs | **5.5%–9.5% combined levers** (illustrative estimate; exact baseline due in 2 weeks) |
| **Clinical & client impact** | Lower continuity of care, weaker clinical oversight, rushed visits | Preserves continuity and district-nurse triage; targets client self-reliance |
| **Staff & retention impact** | High risk of burnout, absence spike and nurse resignations | Involves teams in solving the gap; protects permanent jobs and autonomy |
| **Legal & co-determination risk** | High: OR consent block (Art. 27 WOR), Enterprise Chamber appeal (Art. 25 WOR), WMCO | Low-to-moderate: early joint design with OR and Client Council |
| **Reversibility** | Low: lost nurses and broken trust take years to rebuild | High: operational, contract and overhead measures can be adjusted quarterly |

## 4. Facts Needed in the Next 14 Days

Before locking in targets, Finance, HR and Operations will compile an aggregated, team-level baseline (no individual staff tracking) from our systems of record:

1. **Sickness absence & agency/ZZP spend:** Current absence rate by team, cost of agency/ZZP cover vs. permanent staff, and main drivers of long-term absence.
2. **Contract profitability:** Margin per insurer and municipality contract (Zvw and Wmo), including unbilled care coordination and travel time.
3. **Overhead & non-care spend:** Management layers, office leases, software licences, external consultancy and administrative rework.
4. **Team-level capacity & routes:** Aggregated direct-care ratio, travel time per route, and natural attrition rate by role over the past 24 months.
5. **Cash & reserves:** Available reserve buffer and any bank covenant thresholds for a 12-month bridge if needed.

---

## 5. Governance, Leadership & Timeline

- **Leadership goes first:** Before asking teams for restraint, we will freeze non-essential management hiring, review executive and management perks/consultancy spend, and cut unnecessary internal reporting.
- **Early involvement:** Share the real 7% funding numbers with the Works Council (OR), Client Council and team representatives *before* finalising the plan, inviting them to co-design operational savings.
- **30-day milestone:** Return to the Supervisory Board with verified baseline numbers, concrete savings per workstream, and a joint risk review.
- **Quarterly scorecard (money + people):** Track monthly cash/margin, agency spend, sickness absence, voluntary turnover, continuity of care and client satisfaction side by side — so we catch any hidden harm early.
