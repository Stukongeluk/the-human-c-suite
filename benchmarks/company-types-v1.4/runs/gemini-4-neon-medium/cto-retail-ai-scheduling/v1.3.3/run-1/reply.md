### Why the March plan breaks (and why the 12% won't survive contact with reality)

Start by backing the COO's actual goal: grocery margins are thin, labour is the largest controllable store cost, and better demand forecasting is worth having. The problem is the vendor's mechanism and timeline, not the COO's objective.

1. **Dutch law and the CAO erase the vendor's core saving** *(not legal advice—confirm exact clauses with HR and employment counsel; checked November 2026)*:
   - **Same-day shift cuts still cost you wages.** Under the *Wet arbeidsmarkt in balans* (WAB, Art. 7:628a BW), on-call workers (*oproepkrachten*) get 4 days' notice; cancelling or changing a shift inside that window means you still owe the originally scheduled hours. Even where the *CAO Levensmiddelenbedrijf* shortens notice to **24 hours** for *hulpkrachten/erbijbaners* in unforeseen cases, same-day adjustments remain payable.
   - **Publishing 3 days ahead breaches CAO norms.** The *CAO Levensmiddelenbedrijf* requires full schedules at least **7 days** in advance (rostered days off 14 days ahead), and flexible contracts under 15 hours/week carry a **3-hour minimum pay per call-up**—which limits 15-minute slot slicing.
   - **Mandatory Works Council consent.** With ~9,000 staff, your OR has a statutory consent right (*instemmingsrecht*) over scheduling systems (**Art. 27(1)(b) WOR**) and systems suited to monitoring attendance, behaviour, or performance (**Art. 27(1)(l) WOR**). Bypassing them lets the OR nullify the rollout.
   - **GDPR & EU AI Act.** Automated scheduling affecting income requires a DPIA and human oversight under **GDPR Art. 22**. Under the **EU AI Act (Annex III)**, AI for task allocation or worker evaluation is high-risk; the employer duty to inform worker reps (Art. 26(7)) applies from **2 December 2027** (moved by the 2026 Digital Omnibus, so not yet in force—but national WOR already applies, and you shouldn't buy a tool that fails compliance nine months after launch).

2. **Hidden operational and technical costs**:
   - **Student attrition.** Students choose the supermarket that lets them plan around exams and lectures. Three-day schedules and same-day changes will push turnover up in a tight labour market, eating the labour savings in recruiting and training.
   - **140-store payroll risk in ~14 weeks.** Connecting POS, HRIS, time-and-attendance, and Dutch CAO payroll rules (youth scales, evening/Sunday allowances, *Arbeidstijdenwet* breaks) by March leaves no time for parallel payroll runs. One broken payslip cycle for 9,000 people will cost far more than a quarter's delay.
