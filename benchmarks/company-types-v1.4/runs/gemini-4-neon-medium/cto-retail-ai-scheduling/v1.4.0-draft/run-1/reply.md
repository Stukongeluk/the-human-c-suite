### The trap—and the real problem worth solving
Your COO is right to care about labour efficiency: in grocery retail, labour is the largest controllable cost on thin margins, and better demand forecasting around promotions, weather, and local peaks can genuinely cut both quiet-hour overstaffing and checkout bottlenecks. **The trap is treating a legal, operational, and people change as a 14-week software rollout built around a vendor's brochure number.**

### Why the March proposal breaks in practice
1. **Dutch law and co-determination (flag for HR and employment counsel, not legal advice):**
   - **Works Council (OR):** Mandatory from 50 employees (you have 9,000). Under **Article 27(1)(l) WOR**, the OR has a **consent right (*instemmingsrecht*)** over systems suited to monitoring attendance, behaviour, or performance—and over working-hours/rostering rules (**Article 27(1)(b) WOR**), alongside advisory rights (**Article 25 WOR**). Pushing 140 stores live by March leaves no credible path for OR consent and risks nullification.
   - **On-call notice and paid cancellations (WAB, since 2020):** On-call staff must be called at least **four days ahead** or may refuse, and shifts cancelled or altered inside that window **must still be paid** (a CAO can shorten the four days to no less than 24 hours; check your Supermarket CAO with HR). Same-day cuts either pay for hours not worked—erasing the 12% saving—or breach statutory/CAO rules.
   - **Direction of travel:** The *Wet meer zekerheid flexwerkers* passed the Senate in July 2026 (intended effective date **1 January 2028**, with an exception for pupils' and students' side jobs). Under the **EU AI Act (Annex III)**, AI used for task allocation or worker monitoring is high-risk; the employer duty to inform workers and representatives (**Article 26(7)**) applies from **2 December 2027** (moved from August 2026 by the 2026 Digital Omnibus), well inside this tool's lifespan, and GDPR requires a DPIA and human oversight now.
2. **Store-floor arithmetic:** Students juggle lectures, exams, and other jobs. Three-day notice and same-day cuts drive no-shows, sickness absence, and turnover—while 15-minute slots ignore deliveries, cold-chain restocking, and queue waves, turning "saved hours" into empty shelves and lost sales. High-performing grocers (such as Mercadona and Costco, studied by Zeynep Ton) keep labour competitive through cross-training and modest slack, not quarter-hour rationing.
3. **Delivery risk across peak:** It is late November. December (Sinterklaas, Christmas, New Year's) is your busiest period; store managers and IT cannot safely integrate POS, HRIS, and payroll across 140 stores—nor validate forecast accuracy on your own data—before March.
