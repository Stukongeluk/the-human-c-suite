Plan Q1 as a small rebrand plus a protected customer-fix stream, not a whole-organisation rebrand. NPS has dropped 24 points (+28 to +4) while three complaints have stayed open for months. A new name won't fix slow invoicing, and customers will notice that quickly.

**Take the rebrand at its real size.** After a merger, one name and one story can be right. The must-have part is small: the name in the app, legal entities, domain redirects. The design system and the full marketing site can be phased. Together they don't need every product and engineering person for a quarter.

**A Q1 shape you could propose**
- **Fix first (largest share of capacity):** invoicing speed, the accounting integration and the top missing reports. Each gets one owner, one customer measure (invoice time, integration error rate, report usage or ticket volume) and a date.
- **Rebrand core (a few dedicated people):** name, domain, redirects, legal. Phase the design system and marketing site to Q2, starting with the screens customers use most.
- **Slack:** don't plan any team at 100%. Domain migrations produce surprises.

Let the teams size both streams before you commit numbers. I can't give you the split without your capacity data: team availability, unplanned-work share and past throughput. Those, plus churn reasons and the revenue tied to the three complaints, are what will convince leadership.

**Handling "leadership decides, teams execute"**
Don't argue with the slogan. Use its own logic: "any decision beats no decision" fits choices that are cheap to undo. A one-quarter freeze on customer fixes is hard to undo, because churned customers don't come back with the new logo. So decide quickly on the cheap, reversible parts (the name, the domain) and give teams a week to bring their analysis on the expensive parts.

**First step: talk to the CEO privately, before any wider forum.** Something like: "I'm not questioning the rebrand. I want it to land. Here's what NPS and the three open complaints put at risk, and here's a plan that delivers the rebrand's core in Q1 and fixes what customers are asking for. What would make you comfortable with that?" Ask what problem the full-scope rebrand is solving and what would change their mind. They may be under pressure from the merger, the board or an investor, and you'll want to hear which.

Bring one page: the NPS trend, the revenue or churn exposure, the two-stream plan and a review date in week six. If the churn numbers are bad, that review is where you reopen the split.
