**Plan Q1 around the three customer problems, and shrink the rebrand to its must-have core instead of making it the whole quarter.** NPS fell from +28 to +4 (24 points) while invoicing, the accounting integration and reports sat open for months. A new name won't fix that, and the churn from it shows up in revenue two or three quarters later, after the rebrand is "done".

You can do this without defying the CEO. "Any decision beats no decision" is right for choices that are cheap to undo. Moving the entire org onto the rebrand for a quarter is a big, hard-to-reverse bet, so it deserves a short look at the evidence first. Offer him the rebrand as a decision, with a smaller scope, instead of a reversal.

**What I'd propose for Q1**

1. **Fix the top three complaints first, as named outcomes with owners.** Invoicing speed (e.g. p95 time to generate an invoice), accounting-integration success rate, and the missing reports (the top two or three that customers actually asked for). Each needs a number the team agrees on after looking at its own data, not one I set from here.
2. **Keep the rebrand's must-have part small.** After a merger, one name and one story can be right. The must-have part is the name in the app, the legal and billing entities, a holding marketing page, and redirects. Phase the rest: roll out the new design system gradually through tokens, logo and colours, and migrate components as screens get touched anyway, instead of rebuilding everything.
3. **Treat the domain move as the risky piece.** It touches SSO, email deliverability, API endpoints, webhooks, customer IP and domain allow-lists and SEO. Run old and new in parallel for months, and don't cut over in the same quarter as the integration fix, because the accounting integration is the thing customers already distrust.
4. **Protect reliability and slack.** Leave room for incidents and debt in the plan. No plan that only fits with overtime.

**Capacity split.** As a starting hypothesis for the teams to estimate against, not a commitment: roughly 60-70% on the customer problems, 15-20% on the must-have rebrand, and the rest on reliability and slack. Each team estimates its own work, then you see what fits. Whatever doesn't fit moves to Q2 with a date, and you show leadership that trade-off in plain numbers.

**How to take it to the CEO.** Go privately first, before any wider meeting, with one page: the NPS drop, the open complaints and what they cost (revenue at risk, churn reasons, support volume). Then:

> "I want the rebrand to land well. Two-thirds of the org on it for a quarter puts the customers we're already losing at risk. Can I bring a plan where the name, domain and site go live on schedule in a minimal form, and the rest is phased while we fix invoicing, the integration and reports? I'll bring the teams' estimates by [date]."

Steelman his side: after a merger, two brands and two products confuse customers and sales, and a clear new identity can speed up integration. That is a real argument for the must-have scope, just not for all of it. "Leadership decides, teams execute" also loses something: the people closest to customers and code can see which rebrand items are cheap and which break integrations. Ask for their analysis before the plan is final, then tell them what you changed because of it.

Also check whether your works council (ondernemingsraad) has an advice right on a change like this, and involve it early. That isn't legal advice, so confirm with HR or legal.

**Measure monthly:** invoice p95 time, integration success rate, report usage, support tickets on the three issues, NPS and churn among affected accounts, plus a quick team pulse on workload.

**Key assumptions:** the complaints are as widespread as the NPS drop suggests, the three fixes are feasible within a quarter, and the CEO will listen to a smaller-scope option. A fourth cause for the NPS fall, such as onboarding or support after the merger, would change the plan.

Two questions would sharpen this: how much revenue sits with accounts that raised these complaints, and has anyone asked the teams how long the rebrand and the fixes would each take?
