I wouldn't take the three moves to the board as agreed: take a plan that fixes customers first, keeps the rebrand but halves its Q1 scope, runs one evidence-based restructure from Q3, and keeps junior roles. The reason is the buyer: in 18 months they will price churn, NPS and key-person risk, and an NPS drop from +31 to +6 with rising churn is the first thing they will find.

The board paper is in `board-plan.md`, written for the board and owners. Figures I don't have (revenue, churn rate, contractor and junior counts, EBITDA target) are marked [●] for finance to fill in before the meeting.

**What the plan says**

1. **Customers first.** Rank the top churn and NPS causes with evidence, give each an owner and a date from January, and gate every other change on those measures. A new name does not fix an unhappy customer.
2. **Rebrand in two steps.** In Q1 only the essentials: name, legal entities, invoices, redirects, and a clear customer letter on what stays the same. Visual identity, product names and portals follow in waves, each only when that segment's red accounts are under control.
3. **One restructure, effective Q3 2027 at the earliest.** This would be your fourth. Before it, write down what the first three failed to solve. Brief the works council (Betriebsrat, if there is one) at the problem stage; §§111-112 BetrVG require an Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan negotiation, and counsel should check the §17 KSchG thresholds. Don't run it alongside the rebrand rollout, because the same managers carry both.
4. **Juniors stay, redesigned. Contractors end one by one where the work has gone,** with proper notice, not as a blanket rule. Juniors are among your cheapest people and the source of the seniors a buyer will ask about. Training contracts are legally protected; get counsel's view first.
5. **Profit from other levers first:** duplicated tools, licences, offices and vendors across four companies, one price book, stopping low-value work, a hiring freeze with exceptions, then redeployment and voluntary exits. Involuntary cuts only if a gap remains, once.
6. **Leaders go first.** The NPS fall happened on this leadership group's watch, and the paper says so. Tie executive bonuses to retention and NPS as well as EBITDA, and review leadership perks before any frontline measure.

**The honest trade-off.** The three moves show more profit sooner on paper. My plan gives less in 2027 and a retention trend a buyer can believe. The paper shows that gap rather than hiding it. Ask your sell-side adviser how run-rate savings from completed actions are treated, because that is the one thing that could change my timing of the restructure.

**Illustration, not your data:** 60 roles at €70,000 saves €4.2m. On €120m revenue at a 60% contribution margin, losing about 6% of revenue erases that, before severance and backfill. Finance should replace these numbers with real ones.

**Before the board:** speak to the CFO and each leader who agreed the three moves, one by one, then to the owners' lead. They agreed to something, and they should hear the reasons from you first, not in the boardroom.

**Two questions that would change my answer:**
- What do churned and at-risk customers say is the reason? If it is price or product, not service, the plan shifts.
- What did the previous three restructures set out to fix, and did they?
