# Board paper: protecting customers and earnings through to the sale

**Prepared by:** the CEO · **For decision at:** next week's board meeting · **Status:** draft; figures in [brackets] are to be filled from our own systems before the meeting

## 1. Decision requested

The board is asked to approve an 18-month plan with one order of work:

1. **Fix the customer problems first.** NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year and churn is rising. A buyer will test exactly this in due diligence, so it comes before everything else.
2. **Rebrand in two steps, not one.** The essentials (name, legal entities, invoices, web redirects) move in Q1. The rest follows in waves, each started only when customer measures hold.
3. **One restructure, designed on evidence and with the works council.** It removes management layers, but only after we have named what the last three restructures failed to solve. Earliest effective date: Q3 2027.
4. **Keep junior roles and redesign them. End contractor roles one by one, where the work has gone**, not as a blanket rule.
5. **Reach the profit target through integration savings, pricing and focus first.** Headcount reductions only if those fall short, once, and after the works council has been consulted.

The leadership team agreed three moves: rebrand in Q1, a fourth restructure, and ending all contractor and junior roles. This paper keeps the rebrand and the restructure but changes their timing and scope, and replaces the third move. The reasons follow, so the board can challenge them.

## 3. What a buyer will check

In 18 months a buyer pays for retained customers, recurring revenue and a team that can run the business without a few key people. Their due diligence will look at:

- churn and net revenue retention by cohort, and whether the trend is improving;
- NPS or equivalent, and the reasons behind it;
- key-person dependency, attrition of experienced staff and the age profile of the engineering and service teams;
- whether recent savings are real or have moved into rehiring, contractors or lost revenue.

A cost cut that lifts this year's profit but shows up as churn in the data room gets discounted, or questioned. Our aim is the opposite: earnings a buyer believes, with a retention trend that is clearly improving from the third quarter of 2027.

## 5. The three moves, adjusted

### 5.1 Rebrand

A single name after four acquisitions makes sense. The cost is that it competes for the same people who must fix customer problems, and a new name does not fix an unhappy customer.

- **Q1 (must-have only):** choose the name, register legal changes, update invoices, contracts, email and web redirects, and a plain customer letter saying what changes and what stays the same (contacts, prices, service levels).
- **Q2 to Q4:** visual identity, product names, portals and sales material in waves, by customer segment. No wave starts while that segment's top issues are open or while its accounts are flagged red.
- **Cost:** €[●]m, of which the must-have part €[●]m. We will report the actual against this monthly.

### 5.2 Restructure

This would be our fourth. Before we change the structure again we write down what the first three set out to solve and why they did not. The proposal then rests on spans and layers data (managers per team, decisions that need three approvals), not on a target number of managers.

- **Design (Q1 to Q2):** data on layers and spans; interviews with affected teams; options, including leaving roles in place and giving them different work.
- **Works council:** where the companies have one, we brief it at the problem stage with the real numbers and negotiate a reconciliation of interests and a social plan (§§111-112 BetrVG) before any decision. Counsel to confirm how many establishments exist, whether a group works council is involved, and whether the thresholds for mass-dismissal notification (§17 KSchG) are reached. Social selection (Sozialauswahl) applies to operational dismissals; performance rankings do not replace it.
- **Effective date:** Q3 2027 at the earliest, so each affected person hears first from their own manager, with notice, paid training and a job-search offer from the first day.
- **Not at the same time as the rebrand rollout.** The same managers carry both.

### 5.3 Contractors and junior roles

Ending all of them is the part we recommend against.

- **Junior roles:** these are among the cheapest people we employ and the source of tomorrow's seniors and of the people who can maintain our systems in three years. Removing them saves little and removes the pipeline a buyer will ask about. We redesign them instead: pairing, reviewing and testing, owning small systems, and a deliberate share of entry-level roles in workforce planning. Training contracts (Ausbildung, dual study) are protected by law and need counsel before any decision.
- **Contractors:** for each, we record the work, rate, end date, what only they know and whether the work still has to be done. Where the work has ended, we do not renew, giving the notice the contract requires. Where the work is permanent and critical, we offer employment, which also reduces classification risk. Counsel reviews any long-running arrangements. Ending income is still ending income; we treat contractors with the same notice and honesty as staff.
- **Illustration only (assumptions, not our data):** if 60 roles cost €70,000 each fully loaded, the saving is €4.2m. On €120m revenue with a 60% contribution margin, each point of extra churn costs €0.72m of contribution, and the saving would be erased by about 6% of revenue lost. Severance, rehiring and the premium paid to seniors who absorb the work come on top. Finance will replace these with our real figures.

## 7. Leadership first

The NPS fall and the churn happened under the leadership of this group, and the responsibility is ours, not the teams'. We therefore propose:

- executive bonuses for FY2027 tied to retention, NPS and the integration milestones as well as EBITDA;
- executive perks and the discretionary bonus pool reviewed before any frontline measure;
- leaders' pay, as a first and visible step, held back until the first customer milestone is met; this is a signal, not a savings plan.

## 10. Risks, assumptions and what would change this plan

- **A buyer values run-rate profit more than the trend.** If our sell-side adviser tells us otherwise, we revisit the timing of the restructure, not the customer plan.
- **Savings from integration and pricing may arrive later than headcount savings.** The gap in 2027 earnings is real; we show it in the plan rather than hiding it.
- **Churn may be caused by product or price, not service.** The data in section 2 decides.
- **Our assumptions:** a works council exists at least in some companies; no collective-dismissal thresholds are met by the first steps; the owners accept sequencing if retention is visible. Each is to be confirmed.
- **Not legal advice.** Employment counsel in Germany confirms the works council process, notice periods, the treatment of contractors and training contracts before any decision.

**What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?** We ask the board, and the leadership team, to challenge in particular the timing of the restructure and the level of the profit target.
