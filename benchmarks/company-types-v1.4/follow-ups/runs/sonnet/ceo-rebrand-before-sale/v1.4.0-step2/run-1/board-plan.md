# Board paper: Protecting customers and value before the sale

**For decision at:** next board meeting · **Decision owner:** CEO · **Status:** draft for discussion; figures marked [to fill] come from our own systems before the meeting

## 1. What we ask the board to decide

1. Make retained customers and recurring revenue the group's first priority for the next 18 months, with named owners, measures and dates (section 5).
2. Approve a minimal rebrand in Q1 (name, legal entities, redirects, contracts), with the rest phased behind the customer fixes (section 4).
3. Replace the planned fourth restructure with a diagnosis first. Any structural change goes to the works council as a proposal, not a finished decision (section 4).
4. Replace the blanket end of contractor and junior roles with a role-by-role review, reported at the next board meeting (section 4).
5. Agree that executive pay and bonuses carry the first cost of missed results, and that bonuses are tied to retention and customer measures as well as profit (section 7).

## 2. The problem in one sentence

Customers are leaving: NPS fell from +31 to +6 this year and churn is rising. A buyer in about 18 months will price retained customers, recurring revenue and capable teams, so the plan that raises profit before the sale is the one that stops customer loss first.

Responsibility sits with this leadership team, not with the people who serve customers. The group was assembled from four acquired companies in two years, and the integration has not yet delivered one customer experience. This paper is written on that basis.

## 4. Options

The three moves the leadership team agreed are Option A. Option B is the recommendation. Option C is the baseline.

| | A. The three agreed moves as planned | B. Customers first, then targeted change (recommended) | C. Do nothing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rebrand | Full rebrand of all four companies in Q1 | Must-have part in Q1: name, legal entities, redirects, contract and invoice templates. Product and customer-facing rebrand phased by renewal cycle, after the top churn causes are being fixed | None |
| Restructure | Fourth restructure to remove layers | Diagnose first: which problem did the last three not solve? Then fix decision rights and layers where customers wait on handoffs, designed with employees and the works council | None |
| Contractors and juniors | End all roles | Review role by role: keep scarce skills, convert long-serving contractors where the law and the work call for it, end only roles that add no value, with notice and support. Keep a junior intake and redesign the roles | Unchanged |
| Profit effect | Visible in the P&L within 2 to 3 quarters (estimate) | Slower at first; larger and more durable through retained revenue, fewer duplicated costs and a cleaner story at due diligence (estimate, to be sized) | Churn erodes revenue |
| Customer effect | Four disruptions at once while NPS is falling | Fixes the top complaints first; rebrand lands on a calmer base | Churn continues |
| People effect | Third and fourth wave of uncertainty for 900 people, plus loss of contractors and the junior pipeline | One clear plan, shared early; some roles change | None directly |
| Reversibility | Low: lost knowledge and lost people do not come back | Mostly reversible steps first; irreversible moves taken once, on evidence | High |
| Main risk | Churn accelerates just as the buyer's diligence looks at it; regretted leavers; legal challenge | Profit gain arrives later than the owners hoped; needs discipline to hold priorities | Falling NPS becomes lost revenue |

### Why not Option A

- **A rebrand does not fix an unhappy customer.** A new name on the same experience gives customers a reason to re-evaluate their supplier at the moment they are already dissatisfied. Contracts, integrations, invoices and legal entities all change, and each is a chance to lose someone.
- **A fourth restructure needs a stated reason.** Every restructure costs productivity, creates new handoffs and takes out people who know how things work. If three have not solved the problem, the problem has probably not been defined.
- **Ending all contractor and junior roles is a blunt tool.** Juniors are among the cheapest people in the company, so the saving is small and the pipeline cost is large: no juniors now means no seniors in a few years. Contractors may hold scarce skills or customer knowledge. Both need a role-by-role view, and contractors still lose income, so they get proper notice and honesty.
- **Survivors leave too.** One study found that downsizing a workforce by 1% was associated with a 31% increase in voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor and Nyberg, 2008). A buyer examining key-person risk will see that.
- **Fund-owned businesses vary widely.** Research on buyouts (Davis et al., NBER 2019) finds very different employment and productivity outcomes by deal type; the better ones come from operational improvement, not from cost-out timed before the exit.

### Steelman: the owners' and the CFO's view

The owners want profit up and a clean exit, and every quarter of delay costs something. A single brand lowers marketing and admin cost and makes the group easier to sell as one asset. Four sets of overlapping managers are expensive and slow. Contractors are often the most flexible cost. These points are right in part, and Option B keeps them: one name in Q1, layers fixed where they cost customers time, contractor spend reviewed line by line. The disagreement is about order and method, and about which numbers the buyer will check.

What stops or waits: any new initiative that does not serve the top customer problems or the exit-value measures in section 6.

## 6. Measures the board will see every month

Customers: gross and net revenue retention by company, NPS by company and segment, top-20 account health, time to resolve incidents.
People: regretted attrition, attrition by tenure cohort, internal fill rate, junior intake, time to productivity, a short anonymous pulse ("decisions that affect me are explained").
Money: EBITDA, cash, covenant headroom, one-off programme costs, savings realised against savings planned.

We report on teams and companies. We do not rank individuals.

## 7. Leadership accountability

- The leadership team, including the CEO, is responsible for the fall in NPS and the integration so far.
- Executive bonuses for the next period are tied to retention, NPS and delivery measures as well as profit, and executive pay and bonuses carry the first cost of any shortfall. [Board to set the amount.]
- Cost programmes that affect staff follow this order: spend and duplication, pricing, stopping low-value work, automation of toil, a hiring freeze with natural attrition, redeployment and reskilling, voluntary exits. Involuntary reductions are considered only if the earlier steps cannot close the gap, once, with a fair social plan.

## 8. People and legal

This is not legal advice. German counsel should review before any announcement.

- Works council: it exists only where employees have elected one, so confirm which entities have one. Where there is one, major operational changes go through the reconciliation of interests and social plan process (§§111-112 BetrVG). Monitoring or analytics tools need its agreement (§87(1) no. 6 BetrVG). The council is involved at the proposal stage, while its input can still change the plan.
- Collective dismissals above the thresholds in §17 KSchG need advance notification to the employment agency, and termination agreements the employer initiates count towards them.
- Operational dismissals need a fair social selection under §1(3) KSchG. Performance rankings are not a selection method.
- Contractors: check classification and temporary-agency rules before ending or converting roles, and give notice in line with contracts and good practice.
- Four entities may have different contracts, collective agreements and consultation bodies. Counsel maps them before the plan is final.
- Communication: managers first, then each affected person from their own manager, then everyone, with decided and open items stated separately. Support (training, job search help) starts on day one of notice.

## 9. Risks, and what would change our mind

| Risk | Early warning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Profit gain is slower than owners expect | Savings realised below plan by month 6 | Add spend and pricing levers; revisit the contractor review; board discussion with owners |
| Customer fixes drift into a new programme | No measurable NPS or churn movement by month 6 | Narrow to the top two problems; escalate to the CEO |
| Key people leave anyway | Regretted attrition above baseline | Retention conversations, clarity on roles, stop further change |
| Works council or legal challenge | Advice not sought in time | Slow the timeline, not the consultation |

We would change this recommendation if:
- churn analysis shows customer loss comes mainly from price or a competitor offer rather than service and integration;
- the covenant or cash position leaves no room for the slower path (then we say so plainly and take the next steps in the order of section 7);
- the diagnosis of the last three restructures shows a structural problem that needs fixing sooner.
