Don't take the three moves to the board as agreed. Take this instead: protect customers first, do only the must-have part of the rebrand in Q1, diagnose before a fourth restructure, and review contractor and junior roles one by one rather than ending them all. A buyer in 18 months will price retained customers, and NPS falling from +31 to +6 with churn rising is the number their due diligence will open with. Stacking a rebrand, a restructure and the end of contractor and junior roles on top of that, in one year, puts the exit multiple at risk to raise one year's profit.

The full board paper is in board-plan.md in the outputs folder. Its main points:

- **The problem, in owners' terms.** The goal is a higher sale price, not just higher profit before it. Retained revenue, customer satisfaction and capable teams are what a buyer checks. I wrote it that way so the owners can agree to it.
- **Rebrand.** One name makes sense after four acquisitions. Q1 covers the name, legal entities, redirects and contract templates only. Product and customer-facing changes follow by renewal cycle, once the top churn causes are being fixed. A new name does not fix an unhappy customer, and it gives dissatisfied customers a reason to re-evaluate you.
- **Fourth restructure.** Before it goes ahead, name what the first three failed to solve. If nobody can, the problem was never defined. Fix handoffs and layers where customers actually wait, and bring the works council in at the proposal stage.
- **Contractors and juniors.** Ending all of them saves little and costs a lot. Juniors are cheap, and no juniors now means no seniors later. Contractors may hold knowledge or scarce skills. Review role by role, with notice and support for anyone whose role ends.
- **Where profit comes from instead.** Duplicated tools, vendors, offices and cloud across four companies, pricing and discounts, stopping low-value work, and a hiring freeze with natural attrition. I have not sized these because I have no finance data. The paper leaves those cells open and does not invent numbers.
- **Leaders first.** The paper says the NPS fall and integration so far are leadership's responsibility, ties executive bonuses to retention as well as profit, and has executive pay carry the first cost of any shortfall.
- **Germany.** Confirm which of the four entities have a works council. Restructuring, monitoring tools and any larger dismissals trigger consultation and notification duties (BetrVG, KSchG). This is not legal advice, so have German counsel review it before anything is announced.

The case for the leadership team's plan is real. The owners want speed, one brand is easier to sell, and four layers of overlapping managers do cost money. The paper keeps those parts and changes the order and the method.

The plan is thin on facts you will have and I do not: revenue, EBITDA and covenants, churn by company and segment, why customers leave, how many contractors and juniors there are and what they do, what the earlier restructures aimed at, and whether works councils exist. The board will ask about all of these, so fill them in before the meeting. Two weeks is enough.

2. Is there a covenant or cash constraint that rules out the slower path? If so, tell me and I will rework the order of steps.
