Don't approve the leaderboard or the weekly bottom-10% list as designed. Back the goal instead: go after the 8% with route, depot and app fixes, tested in a few depots after peak.

Two numbers for the table:

- A bottom 10% always exists: 240 named drivers every week, about 160 of them subcontractors' staff, however well everyone drives.
- If weekly rank is mostly noise, 99.6% of drivers get flagged at least once a year. If it's mostly route, you flag the same rural routes and new drivers every week. Either way you rank routes and luck, not effort.

**Why not**

- **Stops per hour mostly measures the route:** stop density, flats versus houses, parcels per stop, van loading order, route familiarity.
- **It gets gamed, and the bill lands elsewhere:** "not home" scans, parcels left at doors and faster driving lift the number and raise failed deliveries, complaints and accidents. Drivers you can't easily replace leave, and their replacements start at the bottom.
- **The legal floor:** in the Netherlands a driver ranking needs works council consent (article 27(1)(l) WOR) and likely a DPIA; Belgium has its own consultation rules. Named lists for subcontractors to act on look like you exercising employer authority over their staff, which can turn subcontracting into labour hire (NL) or make you the deemed employer (BE). I couldn't check current sources here, so have counsel confirm.

Operations has a point: the cost pressure is real and drivers want to know how their day went. Private feedback can help; public ranking with consequences is where it turns.

**What to offer**

1. **Find where the minutes go**, from the app's scan and GPS data aggregated by depot and route type: time at the stop, searching the van, failed attempts, taps per delivery. Fix the top three.
2. **A private view for drivers** of their own trend, designed with drivers and the works council.
3. **Subcontractors get outcomes per route and depot** (on-time, first-time delivery, complaints), not names. They manage their own people.
4. **A fair test:** 4–6 depots against comparable ones for 8–10 weeks from January, stops per hour paired with first-time delivery, complaints, incidents and turnover, criteria agreed upfront.

**First step:** a private conversation with the head of Operations before any wider meeting. Ask what the 8% is based on and which problem it solves: cost per parcel, peak capacity or late deliveries.

A line you can use: "I want the 8% too. I don't think a ranking gets us there without paying in failed deliveries, accidents and drivers we can't replace. Let's find where the minutes go first, and if your version wins a fair test, I'll say so."
