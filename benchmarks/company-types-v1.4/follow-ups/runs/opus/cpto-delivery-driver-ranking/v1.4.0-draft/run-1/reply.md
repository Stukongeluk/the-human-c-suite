Say no to the live leaderboard and the weekly bottom-10% list, and yes to the productivity goal by another route. A bottom 10% of 2,400 drivers is 240 names a week, about 160 of them at subcontractors, however well everyone drives. And stops per hour depends heavily on the route: density, parcels per stop, depot loading.

Why not this way:

- **Safety.** A live ranking in an app used in traffic rewards speeding, skipped breaks and glances at the screen. One serious accident outweighs the gain.
- **It ranks routes, not effort.** The list will mostly name whoever drew the hardest routes, and it invites gaming: easy stops first, failed deliveries pushed to tomorrow.
- **The law.** Your Dutch works council has a consent right on systems that monitor performance (art. 27(1)(l) WOR), Belgian employee representatives must be consulted on new technology (CAO 39), and monitoring like this needs a DPIA under the GDPR. Sending named flags to subcontractors means directing their staff, which in Belgium can count as prohibited hiring-out of workers and make you their employer. I couldn't verify sources today, so check this with employment counsel in both countries; it isn't legal advice.
- **Who carries it:** subcontracted drivers with the thinnest buffer, who can't see or challenge your data.

The trap is treating a route and depot problem as driver effort. Operations' goal is fair; it's the public ranking and the forced flag that do the damage.

What to offer instead:

1. **Find where the hour goes.** Split each route's time from TMS and scan data: driving, doorstep, failed deliveries, depot waiting, time in our app. Have analytics size it before anyone promises a date.
2. **Fix our share first:** route sequencing, loading, address quality, scan steps in the app.
3. **Give drivers their own route-adjusted numbers** against their own past, visible only to them and never while driving, designed with drivers.
4. **Report to subcontractors per depot,** pairing stops per hour with first-time delivery, damage, incidents and complaints. Individual concerns stay with the driver's own manager.
5. **Pilot in a few depots against comparison depots for eight weeks,** after works council and DPIA, with 8% as the target.

Start privately with the head of Operations, before any wider meeting. A line you can use: "I want the 8% too, but not by naming 240 drivers a week on a number that may mostly measure their route. Let me take the route data first and come back with where the hours go and a pilot we can measure."

Two questions that would change my answer: where does the 8% come from, and was it measured on routes like ours? And what should it fix: cost per stop, peak capacity or late deliveries?
