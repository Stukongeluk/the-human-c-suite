**My advice: don't adopt the three measures as proposed. Close the gap first through price, care you deliver but aren't paid for, and the costs that don't touch care (agency staff, absence, turnover, overhead), and keep only the sound core of each board idea, piloted with your teams.** On my rough numbers, 7% is about €2.4 million. Replacing 15% of your district nurses would save about a tenth of that, while carrying most of the risk to clients and to recruitment.

**The trap:** all three were chosen before anyone diagnosed the 7%, and all three take cost out of the work of the people who deliver care, which is where your quality and recruitment are decided. The board is right that the gap is real and that doing nothing isn't an option, and each of its levers has a sound version.

**First, spend 3–4 weeks finding out what the 7% is made of**
- **Price:** do your tariffs keep up with CAO VVT wage increases? For Wmo work, municipalities must pay a price that reflects real costs, including CAO wages (the "reële prijs" rules). Bring municipalities and insurers a full-cost calculation.
- **Unpaid care:** care above revenue ceilings, and time delivered but never declared.
- **Hidden staff costs:** agency and self-employed cover, overtime, absence and turnover. By my estimate, each percentage point of absence is worth about €0.3 million a year.
- **Overhead:** offices, procurement, IT and management layers.

**The board's three measures**

| Measure | Likely saving | Main risks | Better version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Replace 15% of district nurses with carers | About €0.2–0.3m if roughly 150 of your staff are district nurses; less if contracts pay by skill level | Fewer nurses still carry all assessments (indicatiestelling) and complex care. In European hospitals, each 10% more professional nurses in the mix was associated with 11% lower odds of death (Aiken et al., *BMJ Quality & Safety*, 2017; hospital data). Nurses you let go walk to a competitor, and dismissals mean UWV, transition payments and a duty to redeploy | Each team sets its skill mix task by task. Fill some nurse vacancies with carers on a paid training route to nurse. No dismissals; quality limits agreed in advance |
| An app that registers every visit to the minute | Nothing by itself; licences and registration time cost money | Stopwatch care and lost trust. Needs works council consent (art. 27(1)(l) WOR) and a data protection impact assessment | Registration that removes admin and catches undeclared care. Team-level reports only, no location tracking, designed with nurses |
| Move all planning to one central office | Some travel and planning time; uncertain | More different faces per client, lost local knowledge, less say over rosters and so more turnover, plus a new overhead office. KPMG (2015) found Buurtzorg's self-managing teams used fewer hours per client, though total costs per client were about average | Planning stays in teams, with a shared route tool and a central flex pool for absence cover. Pilot in 2–3 teams |

**What I'd take back to the board**
1. **Own it.** Say plainly, to the board and staff, that the gap is management's to solve: management signed these contracts, and the nurses didn't set the prices. You and the management team take a visible pay cut for 2027 and invite the supervisory board to lower its fees. It won't close the gap; it makes the rest credible.
2. **Revenue.** Reopen talks with full-cost figures and claim undeclared care. Cap or exit contracts that lose money on every hour, with a careful handover so no client is left without care.
3. **Costs away from the client.** Turn agency and self-employed hours into your own staff, invest in absence prevention and retention, cut overhead and freeze hiring outside care.
4. **Bridge rather than break.** If reserves allow, cover part of 2027 from reserves with a two-year path to balance, instead of forcing irreversible cuts into one year.
5. **Pilot the board's ideas, decided with people.** Bring the works council (OR), the client council and the nurses in now, with the real numbers, while they can still change the plan. The planning and skill-mix changes need the OR's advice (art. 25 WOR); the app needs its consent.
6. **No forced exits of care staff.** If a gap remains, use natural turnover and redeployment.

**Review monthly with the board:** euros closed per measure and agency spend; absence, turnover and vacancies; and care quality: incidents, falls, pressure injuries, hospital admissions, the number of different carers per client and client satisfaction. Agree in advance which numbers would stop or reverse a pilot.
