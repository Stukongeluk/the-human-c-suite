Advise against buying this system, and offer the alderman a six-month plan for the same, legitimate goal: fewer wrongful payments, and investigators working where they find something. Scoring every recipient is the wrong route.

**Why**

- **"90% accurate" says almost nothing.** Illustration: 3,000 households on welfare, 3% committing fraud. A model that flags nobody is 97% accurate. A model that is 91% accurate can still send investigators to 300 households of whom 237, four in five, did nothing wrong.
- **We have seen this before.** In February 2020 the Hague District Court struck down SyRI, which linked government data to flag fraud risk. Rotterdam's welfare model, stopped in 2021, turned out to score women, parents and people with weaker Dutch as riskier. The childcare benefits scandal wrongly accused more than 20,000 families.
- **The law.** Scoring benefit recipients is high-risk under the AI Act (obligations from December 2027, when this would be running) and needs a DPIA before go-live. Using debt-counselling data for enforcement may come close to the Act's ban on social scoring, in force since February 2025. People gave that data to get help; use it against them and fewer will ask for help early.
- **Six months doesn't fit.** Tender, DPIA, legal checks, linking three systems and bias tests take 12–18 months by my estimate, if it can be done lawfully at all.

**Offer instead, within six months**

1. **Baseline (months 1–2):** the hit rate of today's investigations per source (tips, Inlichtingenbureau signals, random checks), and how much recovered money was intent versus mistakes. A small random sample gives the real base rate.
2. **Fix the front door (months 1–6):** make changes in income or household easy to report, and act on the signals you already receive with explainable rules about a fact, not a score about a person.
3. **Decision gate (month 6):** decide whether any model would beat this. If so: no debt-counselling data, bias tests including proxies like postcode, a random control group, the client council and investigators involved, an independent audit, and a person deciding, with reasons residents can challenge.

**First steps**

- Talk to the alderman privately before this reaches the council. A line you can use: *"I want to help you show the council less fraud and a safety net people trust. This system takes us where SyRI and Rotterdam ended up. Give me six months and I'll bring numbers that hold up in court and in the papers."*
- Ask the vendor in writing what "90%" measures, on whose data, the hit rate in the top 10%, results per group and which variables.
- Bring in your data protection officer and legal counsel now; this isn't legal advice.
