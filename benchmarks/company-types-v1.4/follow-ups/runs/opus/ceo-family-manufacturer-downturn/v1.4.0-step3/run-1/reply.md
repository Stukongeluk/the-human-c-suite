**Don't let 45 people go now.** Split the 35% drop into the part that will come back and the part that won't. Bridge the first with short-time work (Kurzarbeit); shrink for the second over 18–24 months through retirements, voluntary exits and new work. Take the bank a plan with scenarios and triggers, not a headcount number. On my rough assumptions (below), 45 dismissals save about €3.4M a year from 2028 but only about €0.5M net in 2027, because consultation, notice and the social plan come first.

**Why "45 now" is the wrong first move**

- **It's a number before a diagnosis.** Only one customer says its cut is permanent.
- **It isn't fast.** 45 of 210 (21%) means a mass-dismissal notification (§17 KSchG), an enforceable social plan (§112a BetrVG) and notice of up to seven months (§622 BGB).
- **You'd lose the wrong people.** Social selection protects long-serving staff (§1(3) KSchG), so mostly younger skilled workers would go, the ones you need for the next 20 years.
- **Survivors leave too.** In one study, a 1% workforce cut went with 31% more voluntary quits the next year. Apprentices can't be dismissed for business reasons after probation (§22 BBiG), and shouldn't be.

**What I'd do**

1. **This week: cash and the bank.**
   - Build a 13-week cash forecast and 2027 base and downside cases.
   - Ask the bank exactly what it needs: a "Sanierungskonzept" may mean an IDW S 6 report, which takes weeks.
   - Free up cash (down payments, inventory, receivables) and ask whether the Bürgschaftsbank Baden-Württemberg could guarantee part of the line.
   - If the forecast shows a gap without the line, see restructuring counsel now.
2. **Go first, visibly.** Suspend your dividend and drawings, and cut your own and your family's pay before anyone else's. Say it plainly to the bank and to staff: "We became too dependent on two customers. That happened on my watch, and fixing it is my job."
3. **Bring the works council in now, if you have one** (and IG Metall, if you're bound by its agreement), with the real numbers, while they can still change the plan. Kurzarbeit needs their agreement anyway (§87(1) no. 3 BetrVG). Then tell all staff the situation and the options before anything is decided.
4. **Bridge the temporary part with Kurzarbeit from January.** It is legally only for a drop that will pass, and holiday and working-time balances go first (§96 SGB III).
   - By my estimate it saves roughly 70% of the cost of idle hours from the first month, with no severance; you still pay the social contributions.
   - For your people, a 30% cut in hours costs roughly 10–12% of net pay.
   - The 24-month extension ends on 31 December 2026, so plan for 12 months unless it's renewed.
5. **Shrink for the permanent part at human speed.**
   - Freeze hiring; end agency and fixed-term contracts with proper notice.
   - Use retirements, partial retirement, voluntary packages and moves into service, retrofit and new customers.
   - Qualifizierungsgeld (§82a SGB III) pays a wage replacement during retraining; with under 250 staff it needs 10% affected and a works agreement.
   - If by mid-2027 leavers clearly won't close the gap, a transfer company comes before any dismissal (Transferkurzarbeitergeld: up to 12 months with training and job search, §111 SGB III). Any dismissals after that: one round, under a social plan.
6. **Fix the real problem: two customers carry too much of your business.** Set a target for their share of revenue and grow service and spare parts. Ask your engineers which machines fit other sectors, and ask the customer going electric what its new lines will need.

**About your grandfather.** His record is worth protecting, but not at the cost of the company. You may not be able to promise that nobody ever leaves. You can promise that you go first, that you decide with your people rather than about them, and that nobody leaves without support.

**Two questions that would change this**

- What share of revenue do these two customers make up, and how much of each one's cut is permanent?
- Without the credit line, how many weeks of cash do you have?

**My assumptions,** to replace with your payroll and HR figures: €75k fully loaded per person; a 45-person gap; dismissals effective around July 2027, with a social plan of 0.75 monthly salary per year of service (six years on average) plus €0.15M advisory costs; 12 unreplaced leavers a year; 10 voluntary exits mid-2027 at €30k each.

**What would change my mind:** your adviser is right if most of the drop is permanent and cash can't carry everyone through 2027, even with Kurzarbeit. Then one well-prepared reduction is the responsible choice: through a transfer company, with a generous social plan.

This isn't legal advice: check the process with employment counsel, or Südwestmetall if you're a member.
