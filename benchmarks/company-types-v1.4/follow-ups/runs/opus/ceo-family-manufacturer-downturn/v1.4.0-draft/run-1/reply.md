**Don't let 45 people go now. Go to the bank within weeks, not in February, with a staged plan: the family and leadership first, bridge the part of the drop that will pass, shrink only the part that won't, and agree in advance what would trigger job cuts.** Started today, a 45-person cut would save little or even cost cash in 2027, the year the bank is looking at.

**Why not 45 now**

- 45 is 21% of your people. If the two customers are about 60% of revenue, 35% off them is also 21%, so I suspect the 45 is volume scaled to headcount. Yet only one calls its drop permanent, and you need engineers and sales people to replace that revenue.
- It's slow and costly. 45 crosses the mass-dismissal threshold (§17 KSchG), and with a works council a reconciliation of interests and a social plan come first. My rough estimate (€75k a year per person, exits July to September 2027, a social plan of 0.5 to 1 month's pay per year of service, 12 years' average service): about €3.4m a year saved from 2028, but between +€0.3m and −€1.6m in 2027. Swap in your payroll figures.
- Social selection goes by length of service, age, family duties and disability, not by who you need, so it tends to hit younger staff with the newest skills.
- The people who stay leave too: a 1% downsizing was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor and Nyberg, 2008).

**What I'd do instead**

1. **Leadership goes first, and says so.** Relying on two automotive customers was a leadership choice, the family's and now yours, not the shop floor's: say so plainly to the bank and your people. Suspend owner distributions, cut your own pay and management bonuses, and decide what the family can put in (a shareholder loan, a sale-and-leaseback of the building). Banks expect owners to contribute before they do.
2. **Cash now:** down payments on new orders, inventory, receivables, outside spend. Bring subcontracted work back in-house, end overtime, freeze hiring.
3. **Bridge the temporary part with Kurzarbeit,** for the work tied to the customer that should recover; the employment agency won't fund a permanent loss. It needs your works council's agreement (or each employee's, without one) and overtime accounts used first, and from 1 January the maximum falls back from 24 to 12 months unless the government extends it again ([4. KugBeV, 17 December 2025](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/kugbev_4/BJNR1520A0025.html)). Train in the idle hours.
4. **Shrink the structural part at human speed:** retirements and partial retirement (how many of your 210 reach pension age by 2028?), voluntary exits, and redeployment into service and new work. Qualifizierungsgeld pays 60 to 67% of net pay while people retrain for structural change; you pay the course.
5. **Replace the revenue.** The customer going electric still needs equipment for electric drivetrains: are you on that list? Then sectors that buy similar machines (energy, medical, rail, defence) and service on your installed base.
6. **Keep the 14 apprentices.** They're your skilled workforce in 2030, and largely protected from dismissal after probation anyway.
7. **Agree a trigger with the bank.** If order intake by mid-2027 is below a level you set together, one round of redundancies sized to the remaining gap, done once and decently: a social plan above the minimum and a transfer company (up to 12 months of pay support, with training and job search from day one). The bank gets certainty; nobody loses their job to a guess.

**Your grandfather's record.** What's worth keeping is the reason behind it: people trusted the firm to stand by them. Don't promise "no layoffs" unless you can keep it. Promise what you can keep: everything else first, the family first, and anyone who does leave gets real help to the next job.
