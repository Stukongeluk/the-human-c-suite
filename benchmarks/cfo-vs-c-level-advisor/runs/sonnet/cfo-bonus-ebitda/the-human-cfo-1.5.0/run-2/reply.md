I can draft it, but I wouldn't pay 100% on adjusted EBITDA with restructuring excluded. I'd pay 40% on EBITDA with restructuring left in, and 60% on retention, cash, people and quality. The full draft plan is in plan.md in this folder.

**Why not as briefed**
- You've restructured three years running, so it's a recurring cost, not a one-off. A bonus that excludes it pays managers for the savings and hides the costs. Cutting roles raises the bonus even when the programme never pays back.
- A buyer's diligence will test the add-backs and the churn behind them. Repeated reorganisations and cost-out before a sale are a known weak spot in PE exits. Paying 100% on that number rewards what diligence penalises.
- For a healthcare software business, a bonus on profit alone also pays for underspend on security, quality and support, which is where the exit value and the clinical risk sit.

**What the draft proposes**

| Measure | Weight |
|---|---|
| Bonus EBITDA (restructuring included; genuine one-offs only, pre-approved) | 40% |
| Gross and net revenue retention | 20% |
| Cash conversion | 10% |
| Regretted attrition and engagement | 15% |
| Product quality, clinical safety and security | 15% |

- Threshold 50%, target 100%, cap 150%, with a gate on audited, reconciled numbers.
- 30% of any payout is deferred over three years, with malus and clawback.
- Savings count only net of what they cost.
- No award for headcount reduction as such, and no funding by cutting jobs, training or security.
- If results disappoint, executive bonuses are cut first, and the plan says so in advance.
- Covenant and sale-process EBITDA can keep their own definitions. Only the bonus definition changes.

**If the board resists**
The fallback in the plan is a capped exclusion for a board-approved programme, limited to cost that the documented savings pay back within 12 months. Anything above the cap is charged. It's a compromise, not my recommendation.

**Steelman for the board:** EBITDA is the simple, lender-recognised measure, aligned to the exit multiple, and a wider scorecard is harder to administer. My answer is that the scorecard measures what the multiple actually rests on, and the plan keeps the lender definition separate.

**Caveats**
The percentages, thresholds and the 30% deferral are illustrative placeholders. I haven't seen your ledger, loan agreement or baselines. The illustration of the restructuring effect uses made-up numbers (1.5m cost, 1.0m in-year saving). Counsel should check bonus, clawback and consultation rules in each country, and auditors and lenders the definitions. Nothing here is legal or tax advice.
