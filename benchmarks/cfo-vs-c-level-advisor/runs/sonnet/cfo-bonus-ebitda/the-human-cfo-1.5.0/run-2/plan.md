# 2027 Management Bonus Plan (draft for Remuneration Committee)

Status: draft for board discussion. All figures marked (illustrative) are placeholders to be replaced from the ledger and the 2027 budget. Not legal, tax or accounting advice; employment counsel, the auditors and the lenders' counsel should review before adoption.

## 1. Purpose
Reward management for building a company a buyer will pay for: retained customers, recurring revenue, a capable team and clean, reconciled earnings. Pay for results that are still there after the exit diligence.

## 2. Who participates
Executive team and direct reports, plus a small broader pool (target: management group of roughly 25-35 people, to be confirmed). Eligibility and target percentages per role are set by the Remuneration Committee. No individual figures are modelled here.

## 3. Scorecard

| Measure | Weight | Threshold (payout 50%) | Target (100%) | Stretch (cap 150%) | Why it is here | Gaming risk and guard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bonus EBITDA (defined in section 4) | 40% | 90% of budget | 100% | 110% | The owners' value driver and the covenant base | Restructuring and other recurring costs stay in; savings count only net of their cost |
| Gross revenue retention and net revenue retention (ARR) | 20% | budget minus 2 pts | budget | budget plus 2 pts | A buyer pays for retained recurring revenue; diligence tests churn | Measured on contracted ARR from the billing system, cohort definitions fixed in advance; no credit for discounts that buy renewals below floor price |
| Cash conversion (operating cash flow / bonus EBITDA) | 10% | 70% | 80% | 90% | Profit that is not collected is not profit | Receivables ageing reviewed; no stretching of small suppliers |
| Regretted attrition and engagement | 15% | regretted attrition at 1.5x prior-year rate | prior-year rate or better | 25% lower than prior year | A restructuring-heavy company loses the people who know the product | Regretted = rated high performers and key-skill roles, rated before the year starts, reviewed by HR, not by the bonus recipients; engagement from an independent survey |
| Product quality, clinical safety and security | 15% | no severity-1 incident unresolved beyond SLA | budget targets for uptime, defect escape rate and audit findings closed | per budget | Healthcare software: a safety or data incident destroys value fast | Incident classification set by an independent owner (quality/compliance), not the business line; any reportable data breach or patient-safety event caused by negligence zeroes this block |

Payout is linear between points. Total payout is capped at 150% of target.

Gate: no payout on any block if the audited accounts are qualified or restated for the year, or if the reconciliation in section 4 is not signed off by the CFO and reviewed by the Audit Committee.

## 4. Definition of Bonus EBITDA
Starting point: reported (audited, accounting-standard) EBITDA. Every adjustment is named, explained and reconciled in a table at year end.

Permitted adjustments: genuinely non-recurring items (for example a disposal gain or loss, a named acquisition's deal fees, a court settlement) that did not occur in the prior two years and are not reasonably likely in the next two, approved by the Remuneration Committee before the year-end result is known.

Not permitted: restructuring and severance. The company has restructured three years running, which makes it a recurring cost of how the business operates, not a one-off. Restructuring costs therefore stay inside Bonus EBITDA. The lenders' covenant EBITDA and the sale-process "adjusted EBITDA" may follow their own definitions; the bonus plan does not.

Fallback if the board will not accept full inclusion (a compromise, not my recommendation): a cap on excluded restructuring of 1% of revenue (illustrative), only for a board-approved programme, with the exclusion applying only to the part of the cost that the programme's documented savings pay back inside 12 months, and with the saving counted in the bonus year only after being measured in the ledger. Anything beyond the cap is charged.

Illustration (made-up numbers): reported EBITDA 10.0m; restructuring cost 1.5m in the year, saving 1.0m in-year (6 months of a 2.0m run-rate). If restructuring is excluded: Bonus EBITDA = 10.0 + 1.5 = 11.5m, of which the saving contributes +1.0m, and the cost is invisible. If included (this plan): the programme nets to minus 0.5m in the bonus year, and pays off only if it genuinely pays back later. Under the excluded definition, a restructuring that never pays back still raises the bonus; under this plan it does not.

## 5. Funding rules (no cuts to fund the payout)
- No bonus is funded by cutting jobs, training, security, maintenance or product quality. Budgeted spend on training, security and technical-debt work is a floor; going below it reduces Bonus EBITDA credit by the shortfall (the underspend is added back for the calculation).
- No award, in any form, for headcount reduction as such.
- Any redundancy programme in 2027 needs a written business case (net savings by quarter, severance, notice, consultation, rehiring risk, customer risk) approved before launch, and legal review in each country.

## 6. Deferral, malus and clawback
- 70% of any payout in cash in the year after performance; 30% deferred and paid in three equal annual instalments (illustrative), subject to continued service and to the checks below. A rolling deferral gives a multi-year effect.
- Malus on unpaid deferred amounts and clawback for 3 years on paid amounts if results are restated, if a measure was misreported, or if the result was achieved at the expense of the long term (for example churn that arrives after cuts to support, or a security incident traced to underspend).
- Subject to local employment and data law; counsel to confirm enforceability per jurisdiction.

## 7. When results disappoint: leaders first
If the company is below threshold or must take cost action, the executive team's bonus and then base-pay adjustments are reduced before any frontline reduction is proposed. This is stated in advance here, not decided afterwards. Executives who participate in a redundancy decision have no personal bonus tied to its savings.

## 8. Governance and reconciliation
- Remuneration Committee owns the plan; the CFO prepares the numbers; the Audit Committee (or auditors) reviews the reconciliation. Executives do not set their own targets or adjustments.
- Targets set once, at budget approval; changed only by Committee decision recorded in minutes, with reasons.
- Annual disclosure to participants of the full reconciliation from reported to Bonus EBITDA.
- Review after 2027 with the retention, attrition and incident data, to fix what proved gameable.

## 9. Open items
1. Reported EBITDA, revenue and covenant definitions (need ledger and loan agreement).
2. Jurisdictions of employees (consultation and bonus-law rules differ).
3. Current attrition, retention and incident baselines to set thresholds.
4. Exit timing, which changes the weight of the deferred part and any transaction-linked incentive (e.g. MIP) that already exists.
5. Whether any existing management equity plan overlaps with this plan.
