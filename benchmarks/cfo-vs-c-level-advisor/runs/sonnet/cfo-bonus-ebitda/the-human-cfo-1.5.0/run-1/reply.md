I'd draft the plan so that restructuring costs stay inside the bonus EBITDA, and I'd put EBITDA at 50% of the scorecard instead of 100%. Three restructurings in three years is a cost of running the company, not a one-off. A plan that pays 100% on EBITDA with that excluded rewards the next cut and leaves the bill (churn, attrition, security debt) for the buyer's due diligence to find. The full draft is in `bonus-plan-2027.md`; here is the shape.

**Scorecard (proposed weights, to be calibrated to the 2027 budget)**

| Measure | Weight |
|---|---|
| Bonus EBITDA (restructuring included) | 50% |
| Gross and net revenue retention (ARR basis) | 15% |
| Cash conversion | 10% |
| Product quality, security and clinical-safety record | 15% |
| Regretted attrition and engagement | 10% |

Each measure has a threshold, target and stretch, plus a named way it could be gamed and the guard against it. Payout is capped at 150% of target.

**How EBITDA is defined**
- Reported EBITDA is the starting point, and every adjustment is reconciled next to it.
- Restructuring costs reduce Bonus EBITDA. Lenders and the exit model can keep their own add-back definition, reported side by side. It just isn't used for pay.
- Freeze the capitalised-development policy at the 2026 basis, since capitalisation is the easiest way to lift EBITDA in software.
- No bonus for headcount reduction as such. A cut counts only net of its costs, and a leader's payout is reduced if a programme's actual 12-month saving falls short of its cost.

**Payout and protection**
- 75% cash, 25% deferred over two further years. Unvested amounts accelerate at an exit only if the retention and quality gates were met, so a pre-sale cost cut isn't rewarded.
- Malus and clawback with a three-year look-back, for results bought at the expense of customers, security or the team.
- Leaders first, written in advance. Below threshold, executive bonuses go to zero before any involuntary reduction is proposed.

**The board's best argument, and a fallback**
Management shouldn't be discouraged from restructuring the board asks for, and lenders and buyers value adjusted EBITDA. That is a fair point. If the board won't accept a flat "stays in", the draft offers a narrow exception: a named, board-initiated programme, the first of its kind in two years, that recovers its cost from net savings within 18 months (checked at 12), approved and disclosed by the Remuneration Committee. Three consecutive years of restructuring would not qualify. Treat this as the fallback, not the recommendation.

**Questions that change the numbers**
1. Which country or countries are the participants employed in? Employment law, works council rights and bonus rules differ, so I'd want counsel to review before this goes anywhere.
2. What did the last three restructurings cost, and how much of the planned saving appeared in actual EBITDA? That tells us whether the exclusion has been hiding a pattern.
3. When is the expected exit window? It sets the vesting and acceleration terms.
4. Is the 2027 budget already built on further cost reduction?

All weights, thresholds and ranges are my placeholders, not derived from your data, which I haven't seen. Tax and accounting treatment of the deferral needs your advisers. Nothing here is legal, tax or investment advice.
