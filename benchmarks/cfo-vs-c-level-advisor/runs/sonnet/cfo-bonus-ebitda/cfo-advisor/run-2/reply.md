## The Plan Draft: 2027 Management Bonus Plan

**1. Purpose.** Reward delivery of the 2027 budgeted EBITDA, in a way that holds up to sponsor scrutiny, lender covenant tests and an exit diligence process.

**2. Participants.** Executive team and direct reports, plus named senior managers. 🔴 Assumed: roughly 25 to 40 people out of 350. Not the whole company.

**3. Target bonus.** Percent of base salary by level (e.g. CEO and C-suite 50-100%, VPs 30-40%, directors 20-25%). 🔴 Assumed: these are market-typical placeholders, so I need your actual grid.

**4. Performance metric: 100% Adjusted EBITDA for FY2027.**
- Measured **before** bonus expense, to avoid the circularity of the bonus lowering its own metric. The payout curve is calibrated to the budget with the bonus pool included.
- Measured on a **cash-cost basis for capitalized software**: capitalized development above a pre-agreed budgeted level is added back into the measurement. Otherwise EBITDA can be lifted by capitalizing more.
- Revenue measured under the audited policy in force at plan start. No changes to revenue recognition or deferred revenue treatment count toward the plan.

**5. Payout curve (percent of budgeted adjusted EBITDA).**

| Result vs budget | Payout (% of target bonus) |
|---|---|
| Below 90% | 0% |
| 90% (threshold) | 50% |
| 100% (target) | 100% |
| 115% or above (cap) | 150% |

Linear interpolation between points. 🟡 Calibrate the thresholds against the sponsor's variance history: if budget EBITDA has landed within +/-5% in recent years, a 90% threshold is generous. Note the steep slope below target (5 payout points per 1% miss), which creates an incentive to manage results at year end.

**6. Restructuring treatment (the key change from "excluded as usual").**
- A **restructuring allowance is set in the budget** and approved by the board before the year starts. Costs up to the allowance are excluded.
- Any cost **above the allowance is charged** against plan EBITDA, or alternatively spread over 24 months.
- Any restructuring **not in the approved plan** requires board approval and is excluded only if the program pays back in under 18 months on the board-approved savings case.
- Savings are counted in EBITDA as they occur. Management should not collect the savings while the cost is excluded.

**7. Fixed list of permitted adjustments** (agreed now, nothing added mid-year without compensation committee and sponsor approval): board-approved M&A and financing costs, sponsor monitoring fees, non-cash equity compensation, FX at budget rates, and one-off legal settlements over a stated size. Nothing else.

**8. Quality gate and modifier (so "100% EBITDA" cannot be earned by cutting the future).**
- **Gate:** no payout above target if gross revenue retention is below a floor set in the budget (e.g. the 2026 level less 2 points), or if a Severity-1 security or compliance event (for healthcare software, HIPAA or data-protection related) occurs from a control failure.
- **Modifier (+/-10% of the earned payout):** the compensation committee can adjust for regretted attrition, delivery of committed product milestones and customer satisfaction.
- This keeps the plan 100% EBITDA-driven in mechanics but protects against the cuts that raise EBITDA this year and hurt next year's ARR.

**9. Governance.** Compensation committee (sponsor + independent director) approves actual results and all adjustments. Finance prepares the calculation, and the auditor-reviewed reconciliation from reported to plan EBITDA is attached to the payout paper. Clawback for restatement or misconduct. Payout 100% cash, with the executive tier paid 50% after year-end and 50% 6 months later conditional on continued employment. 🟡 Staged payment is optional but common in sponsor-owned companies.

**10. Legal and HR.** Employment, tax and (if you have EU staff) works council consultation need to be checked by counsel and HR before launch. I have not assumed a jurisdiction.

## Why I'd Push Back on the Original Wording

- **It's not one-off.** Three years in a row means the next restructuring is already in the base case for a lender or buyer. Exit diligence usually challenges recurring add-backs, so an adjusted EBITDA that excludes them can lose credibility at sale. 🟢 Standard practice in quality-of-earnings reviews.
- **It rewards the wrong thing.** Management earns savings from the restructuring but never carries its cost. The easiest way to hit EBITDA is cutting headcount, R&D or customer success, and for a 350-person software company with retention-driven ARR that is how you win the year and lose the next.
- **Each new restructuring makes people trust the plan less.** If the same managers are cutting their own teams and being paid on it, expect regretted attrition among the people you least want to lose. 🟡 Medium confidence: this is judgment, not your data.
- **Single-metric plans are gamed.** 100% on one number invites pulling spend into next year, delaying hires, deferring renewals' costs and capitalizing more. The fixed adjustment list, the capitalization rule and the gate address this.
