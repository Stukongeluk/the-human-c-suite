**CFO: Budget approach for the board (opex -8%)**

**Bottom line**

Don't take a flat 8% per cost centre to the board. At 68% payroll it works out as roughly a 8% headcount reduction (about 48 people) done as a restructuring, and it lands unevenly. Take a target-driven budget instead. It reaches the same -8% by lever and protects the capacity that earns your margin. Confidence: medium. I don't have your actual numbers, so the figures below use stated assumptions.

**Why the blanket cut is the wrong tool for this company**

1. **It cuts revenue capacity along with cost.** In a B2B services firm much of the 68% is billable staff, and in the P&L that is partly cost of sales, not opex. If the opex definition includes billable people, an 8% cut there removes revenue. With a thin margin, losing EUR 1 of billable gross margin costs you more than the EUR 1 you saved. [ASSUMED: I don't know how you classify billable staff; this is the first thing to check.]
2. **It punishes efficient teams and rewards padded ones.** Everyone gets the same percentage whatever their return.
3. **In the Netherlands, payroll cuts are slow and structured.** Dismissal needs UWV permission, or a court route for personal grounds. Notice periods run one to four months, and a transition payment is due. If 20 or more roles go within three months in one UWV region, you must notify unions and UWV under the collective redundancy rules. The works council (OR) has a right of advice on the plan, so it must be involved early. The afspiegelingsbeginsel (proportional selection by age bracket within exchangeable roles) works across the company, not per cost centre, so "each cost centre cuts 8%" does not match how selection is actually done. [ASSUMED: general rules, not legal advice. Confirm with employment counsel and your CAO if one applies.]
4. **One-off costs.** [ASSUMED] About EUR 30-40k all-in per exit (transition payment, notice, process) gives roughly EUR 1.4-1.9m one-off for 48 exits. Payback is about 5-6 months, but it hits cash and the P&L in year one. A better mix avoids most of it.

**Recommended approach: tiered, with the cut in the order of least damage**

- **Tier 1, non-payroll first (-12%, about EUR 2.9m).** Procurement renegotiation and vendor consolidation, software and licence audit, travel, external hiring agencies, facilities, discretionary marketing and events with no measurable pipeline. Quick, reversible, no works council. Confidence: medium (no spend data yet).
- **Tier 2, hiring freeze and managed attrition.** If attrition is around 12% (about 72 leavers a year, [ASSUMED]), about 6 a month, you can retire 35-40 roles in roughly six months with no dismissals. Backfill only roles with a business case, mainly billable roles where utilisation is above target. Confidence: medium; depends on real attrition and where it falls.
- **Tier 3, flex and variable pay.** Reduce subcontractors and ZZP capacity where internal utilisation is slack, and set the bonus pool to results. This covers the year-one timing gap in the cheapest way.
- **Tier 4, targeted structural change only for what is left.** Remove roles in low-value activities, chosen on value rather than on cost centre. Only start the formal OR and UWV process if Tiers 1-3 miss the target. Decide this by a set date (below).
- **Protect:** billable delivery capacity, key account owners, and sales or delivery roles with a proven return. Cutting these costs more than it saves.
- **Revenue side, because the margin is thin:** check utilisation, rate indexation at renewal, and write-offs. A 1-point utilisation gain on EUR 51m payroll is worth about EUR 0.5m, so it can reduce how much you cut.

**How to act**

1. Get actual opex by cost centre and nature, with billable vs non-billable staff and 24-month attrition by function. Finance, week 1.
2. Agree the baseline and the in-year vs run-rate definition with the CEO. CFO, before the board pre-read (week 1).

**Risks**

- If Tiers 1-3 only deliver about 70% of plan, the gap is about EUR 1.8m. A fixed checkpoint (say end of Q1) with a pre-agreed trigger for Tier 4 keeps that a plan rather than a crisis.
- Freezing hiring in billable functions can lower revenue. Exempt roles with utilisation above target.
- Announcing a company-wide percentage cut can trigger voluntary departures of your best people, who find jobs easily, while the people you would rather lose stay.

**Your decision**

- Option A: Blanket -8% as the CEO proposes. Simple to communicate; likely causes a revenue loss, a legal process of about 48 exits, and a spread that does not follow value.
- Option B (recommended): Tiered target of -8% with Tier 4 only on a trigger. Slightly more work to explain; keeps margin and delivery intact and avoids most one-off cost.
- How to put it to the CEO: keep the simple message ("-8%, total"), and change only how we get there. The single number stays; the allocation follows value, not equal shares.

**Weakest point of my recommendation:** it relies on attrition and procurement savings that I have not seen data for. If attrition is low (say 6%) or the non-payroll base is mostly fixed contracts, Tier 4 becomes necessary sooner.
