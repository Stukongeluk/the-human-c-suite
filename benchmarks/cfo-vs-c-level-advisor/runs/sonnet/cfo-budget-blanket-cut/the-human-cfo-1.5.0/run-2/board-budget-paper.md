# Budget next year: reach 8% lower opex as an exit run-rate through attrition, a zero-based non-payroll review and leaders' pay, and gate any job reductions on evidence

Draft for the CFO's board paper. Every figure below is an illustrative estimate built from the four facts we have (600 people, payroll 68% of opex, thin positive margin, target 8%). Replace the baseline with ledger and payroll aggregates before the paper goes out; the structure and formulas hold.

## 1. The target and why

**Board ask:** opex 8% lower next year. **CEO's method:** every cost centre cuts 8%.

Before choosing a method, the board paper should state what the 8% is for. These give different answers:

| If the real aim is... | Then the number that matters is... | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Margin | Operating result as % of revenue | Revenue levers (price, utilisation) count, and cutting billable capacity can lower the result |
| Cash or covenant headroom | Cash and covenant ratio by quarter | Working capital and the timing of payments count |
| A fixed opex figure | Opex in the budget year | Savings that land after the year do not count; one-off costs may |

**Illustrative baseline (EUR m, to be replaced with actuals):**

| Item | EUR m | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Opex | 66.0 | estimate: 600 people at about EUR 75k loaded, plus non-payroll |
| Payroll (68%) | 44.9 | given |
| Non-payroll (32%) | 21.1 | derived |
| 8% target | 5.3 | derived |
| Revenue | 68.8 | estimate, consistent with a margin of about 4% |
| Operating result | 2.75 | estimate |
| Loaded cost per FTE | 74.8k | payroll / 600; includes holiday allowance, 13th month where applicable, pension and employer charges |

If revenue holds, an 8% opex cut moves the margin from about 4% to about 12%. That is a large step for a services business; ask whether the board means 8% of opex or a margin target, and whether it counts restructuring costs inside the 8%.

## 2. Why not 8% in every cost centre

Payroll is 68% of opex. Cutting every cost centre by 8% means either (a) cutting about 48 of 600 jobs (8% of payroll, about EUR 3.6m a year), or (b) asking non-payroll to cut about 25% to avoid it, which nobody believes. It also cuts equally in the places that earn revenue (delivery, sales), the places that protect the company (security, compliance, finance controls, training) and the places with real waste. And it lands in the wrong year.

**What Dutch process does to the timing** (counsel to confirm):

- Board decision mid-December. The works council has an advisory right on a reorganisation (art. 25 WOR); its advice must come before the decision is final, which usually takes weeks, not days.
- Dismissal for business reasons goes through UWV (or settlement agreements, which count towards collective-dismissal thresholds when the employer initiates them). Collective dismissal of 20 or more people in one UWV region within three months also needs notification to UWV and the unions (WMCO) before the decision is final.
- Notice periods run one to four months by service, and the statutory transition payment applies (one third of monthly salary per year of service).
- Selection follows the exchangeability / age-cohort rule (afspiegelingsbeginsel), so you do not choose who goes by performance, and the cohort mix tends to remove younger, cheaper people first.
- Result: first exits land around the end of Q2, so even a decision in December delivers about half a year of savings, against full-year one-off costs.

## 3. Options, on the same basis

All options below carry their own one-off, cover, attrition and control costs. Savings are in EUR m, by quarter. "Opex effect" is the change in opex including one-offs; "Result effect" also deducts contribution lost on revenue.

### Option C: do nothing

Opex unchanged, one-off cost zero, no board target met. Natural attrition continues and is backfilled. The result stays at about EUR 2.75m and is exposed to any revenue dip. This is the baseline the others are measured against, not a recommendation.

## 4. Levers outside the opex number (not counted towards the 8%)

These improve margin or cash and should be in the board paper, but they are not savings against the opex target. Each is sized as a labelled estimate.

| Lever | Size | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Collect receivables 5 days faster | EUR 0.9m cash, one-off | revenue 68.8m / 365 x 5 days; estimate |
| Rate indexation on renewing contracts (2% on about 60% of revenue) | about EUR 0.8m a year result | estimate; check contract clauses and customer risk |
| Utilisation +1 point on billable staff, redeploying bench capacity | about EUR 0.5m a year | estimate |
| Owners' distributions held until the result recovers | protects cash; not an opex effect | board decision |

If the board's aim is margin, these deserve more weight than a further cut. They are not netted into option A or B, and neither option uses them to claim the 8%.

## 5. Protected, stopped and waiting

- **Protected, with the cost of cutting written next to it:** training and certification (billable skills), security and compliance, finance controls, customer-facing delivery capacity, and the works council and HR capacity needed to run this properly.
- **Stops or waits:** discretionary travel, low-return internal projects, renewals of tools below a usage threshold, consultant spend without a named outcome, any new role outside the exceptions list.
- **Exceptions to the freeze:** roles with signed demand, regulated roles, and critical single points of knowledge; approved by the CFO and the delivery director in a fixed weekly slot.

## 6. Headcount and people

- Flow plan: about 78 expected leavers (estimate), about 47 not replaced, about 31 replaced. Internal moves first: a vacancy is offered to people on the bench or in over-supplied teams before external hiring.
- Reskill and redeploy where utilisation is low; this is a revenue and a retention lever.
- Leaders first: leaders take the first and the largest cut (above). Bonus design moves to a balanced scorecard (profit defined honestly, customers, people, quality) with part deferred over three years.
- Short-time work is available only for calamities in current Dutch rules; check whether the crisis scheme bill has passed before relying on it. Do not plan on it.
- Tell the works council early, while the numbers can still change. A voluntary, honest conversation before the formal advice request is quicker than a contested one.

## 7. The gate (the part that keeps the plan honest)

Review on a fixed date: **end of Q1 and end of Q2.**

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Q1 run-rate savings below 3.5m annualised | Widen the zero-base review, tighten freeze exceptions, review leaders' pay again |
| Revenue falling more than 3% below plan | Revisit the freeze exceptions and which billable roles to hold |
| End of Q2 still more than EUR 1m below the 8% exit path | Open a decision on a voluntary exit scheme as a first tranche, with works council advice. Cost per exit about EUR 25-40k, payback about 6-12 months at EUR 75k loaded, so it does not help the budget year |
| A further step needed | Involuntary reductions only once, with the full process (works council, UWV, WMCO if 20 or more), a social plan, and no rehiring for 12 months |

## 8. Indicators (reviewed monthly, reported quarterly)

Money: opex vs budget by line, run-rate savings delivered vs plan, cash, utilisation, DSO. People and customers: regretted attrition, time to fill exceptions, engagement pulse, customer retention and delivery quality incidents. Savings are not booked until they appear in the ledger.

## 9. Risks and sensitivities

| Assumption | If wrong | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Attrition 13% | At 9%, 31 not replaced instead of 47 | in-year freeze saving falls about EUR 0.6m |
| Non-payroll 10% | At 6% instead of 10% | about EUR 0.7m less in-year |
| Cover cost 15% | At 30% | about EUR 0.3m less in-year |

## 10. Three-week plan to the board meeting

1. **Week 1:** pull payroll aggregates (headcount, attrition by team, loaded cost), the ledger by cost centre and vendor, contracts above a threshold; replace the baseline. Start the hiring freeze with exceptions now; it is reversible.
2. **Week 2:** zero-base sessions with each cost-centre owner on non-payroll lines; HR data on attrition and exceptions; check legal timing with employment counsel; brief the works council chair informally.
3. **Week 3:** finalise model, pre-mortem with the management team, agree the paper with the CEO before the board.

## 11. Key assumptions and the question for you

Assumptions: the baseline above; attrition 13%; non-payroll waste of 10%; cover and attrition costs. This is not legal, tax or accounting advice; Dutch employment counsel and the auditor should confirm the process, timing, the treatment of one-off costs and the labelling.

**What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?**

Steelman for the CEO's version: it is simple, visible and fair-looking, and a hard number forces every manager to act. Option B keeps that discipline through named owners, a freeze, a gate date and a number each owner signs, but it does not make the places that create revenue and protect the company shrink in step with the places that don't.
