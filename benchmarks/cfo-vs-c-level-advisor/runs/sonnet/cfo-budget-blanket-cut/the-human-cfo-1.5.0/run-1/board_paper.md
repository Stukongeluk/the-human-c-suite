# Budget 2027: reach the 8% opex reduction as a run-rate by Q4, without a blanket cut and with no redundancies in the base plan

Draft for the board meeting. **Every euro figure below is an estimate** scaled from the three facts I was given (600 people, payroll 68% of opex, thin positive margin). Replace the inputs in the ledger and payroll aggregates before the paper goes out. Amounts are EUR thousands unless stated. Not legal, tax or investment advice; employment-law and works-council steps need counsel.

## 1. The target and why

| Item | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Opex base | 75,000 | 600 people x 85 loaded cost = 51,000 payroll, which is 68% of opex |
| Non-payroll opex | 24,000 | 32% of opex |
| 8% target | 6,000 | 8% x 75,000 |
| Revenue and operating profit | 78,000 / 3,000 (about 4%) | assumed "thin" margin |

Two things the board should see before choosing a method:

- **8% of opex is twice today's operating profit (est.).** If revenue holds, it would lift margin from about 4% to about 11%. That is a bigger ambition than "thin margin" suggests. If the real aim is a covenant, a cash number or a valuation, say which and by when, because it changes the plan.
- **The baseline matters.** If "8% lower" means below this year's actual cost, then wage indexation (est. 3.5% on payroll, 2.5% on other costs, about 2,400) widens the real gap to about 8,400. If it means 8% below the draft 2027 budget, the gap is 6,000. This paper uses 6,000.

## 2. Why not 8% off every cost centre

Steelman first: it is simple, looks fair, needs no negotiation about who is "important", and signals seriousness. Those are real advantages, and the plan below keeps the simplicity: one company-wide number, every centre owns a plan, and each centre's plan is reviewed against the same four tests. What it does not keep is the flat percentage.

- **It lands on people.** Payroll is 68% of opex, so about 4,080 of a flat cut is payroll, roughly 48 FTE, plus 1,920 of other costs cut without asking which are worth keeping.
- **In a services company, payroll is the product.** Assuming 70% of staff are billable, each billable FTE carries about 186 of revenue against 85 of cost. Cutting delivery capacity by the same 8% as a back-office team cuts revenue, not only cost.
- **It cannot land in the year.** In the Netherlands a reorganisation needs the works council's advice before the decision is final (art. 25 WOR). Collective dismissal of 20 or more people in a UWV area within three months involves UWV and the unions (WMCO), and employer-initiated settlement agreements count towards the 20. Add notice periods of one to four months, transition payments, and the age-cohort selection the dismissals procedure requires. The board can approve an intention in three weeks; it cannot approve savings that arrive in Q1.
- **It rarely sticks.** Research cited by McKinsey found only about 10% of cost programmes showed sustained results after three years. A 1% downsizing was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor and Nyberg, 2008); I do not extrapolate that to 8%, and use a modest +3 points in the model.

## 3. The two options side by side (year 1, by quarter, estimates)

### Option A: blanket 8% (payroll and non-payroll)

| Line | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Year 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Payroll saving, from actual exit dates (48 FTE, run-rate 4,080) | 0 | 153 | 714 | 1,020 | 1,887 |
| Non-payroll 8% across the board (run-rate 1,920; contracts lag) | 120 | 360 | 480 | 480 | 1,440 |
| Transition payments, legal, outplacement (about 20 per exit) | 0 | -384 | -576 | 0 | -960 |
| Revenue at risk (60% of exits billable; 25% of lost capacity becomes lost revenue) | 0 | -50 | -234 | -334 | -618 |
| Extra voluntary attrition (+3 points, 18 leavers at 0.5x loaded cost to replace) | 0 | -153 | -306 | -306 | -765 |
| **Net P&L effect** | **120** | **-74** | **78** | **860** | **984** |

Year 2: about 4,300 run-rate after continuing lost revenue (1,337) and rehiring about 20% of exits (about 370). Not 6,000. Break-even: if more than about 65% of lost billable capacity turns into lost revenue, year 1 saves nothing. Even at 0% the net is only about 1,600.

### Option B: targeted plan (recommended)

| Lever (owner, confidence) | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Year 1 | Run-rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Zero-base non-payroll lines: vendors, licences, subcontractors, travel, office, consultants. 12.5% of 24,000 (range 10-15%). CFO with budget owners; medium | 112 | 375 | 638 | 750 | 1,875 | 3,000 |
| Renegotiation and early-exit fees (5% of run-rate) | 0 | -150 | 0 | 0 | -150 | 0 |
| 2. Do not backfill 35 of about 72 expected leavers (12% attrition, est.), chosen by workload, non-billable first. COO and HR; medium | 93 | 279 | 465 | 651 | 1,488 | 2,975 |
| Revenue at risk from lever 2 (25% of the 35 billable; 25% translates) | -13 | -38 | -63 | -89 | -203 | -406 |
| 3. Stop low-value work: a named stop list of internal projects and reports, about 1% of opex. CEO and ExCo; low | 0 | 75 | 150 | 188 | 412 | 750 |
| 4. Leaders first: all variable pay for about 15 leaders suspended for the year (660), plus 10% off fixed pay from Q2 (264, needs each person's consent). Remuneration committee; high, but **temporary** | 165 | 231 | 231 | 231 | 858 | 0 |
| **Net P&L effect** | **358** | **772** | **1,420** | **1,730** | **4,280** | **6,319** |

Year 1 closes about 71% of the target (4,280 of 6,000). **The Q4 exit run-rate, excluding the temporary leaders' lever, is 6,319, so the 8% is reached as a run-rate by the end of year 1.** The in-year gap is about 1,720.

### Option C: do nothing

Net 0 against the target; wage indexation (est. 2,400) takes margin from about 4% to about 1%, and the board target is missed.

### Why B beats A on the same basis

Year 1 net is about 4,280 versus 984, and year-2 run-rate is about 6,300 versus 4,300. B also costs less in one-offs (150 versus 960) and puts no one through an involuntary exit. Both options carry my own estimates, and the largest B assumption (attrition and the non-payroll yield) is the one most worth testing in the next three weeks.

## 4. Closing the 1,720 in-year gap, in order

1. **Agree the target as a Q4 run-rate** (6,319) with an in-year P&L figure of about 4,300. This is a board decision, and it is the honest one.
2. **Pricing and margin, outside opex:** each 1% net rate increase is worth about 780 of revenue at the same cost. Check rate cards against indexation, unbilled hours and unapproved discounts. Each point of billable utilisation is capacity worth about 4 FTE (est.). Neither counts towards the opex target; both count towards the margin the board really cares about. Not sized here beyond that, so not counted.
3. **Voluntary exits and redeployment, in reserve.** A voluntary window in H1 yields little in-year (the transition payment comes first) and settlement agreements count towards the WMCO threshold of 20. Keep it as the trigger action, not the base plan.
4. **Involuntary reductions are not in the plan.** They would not close the gap in-year either (Option A nets 984), and they stay a last resort with their own consultation path.

## 5. What we protect, and why

Client-facing delivery capacity (it is the revenue), training and certification budgets, security and tooling that keeps delivery running, and onboarding capacity for backfilled roles. Cutting any of these carries a future cost (lost revenue, rehiring at 0.5x to 2x salary per Gallup, security incidents); if the board wants any of them cut, the future cost goes next to the saving.

## 6. What stops or waits

The stop list under lever 3 (to be named by ExCo before the board meeting; without names, lever 3 drops to 0 and the gap grows by 412 in year 1), non-critical subscriptions and consultants, discretionary travel, unfilled non-billable roles until the freeze exceptions are agreed, and leadership variable pay.

## 7. Headcount

Flows, not a percentage: expected leavers about 72 (est.); 35 not backfilled, 37 refilled. Freeze exceptions for billable roles with signed demand, security, and legal or regulatory roles. Redeployment first for anyone in a stopped activity. Net headcount about 565 by year end (est.). Because this changes work organisation, the works council gets the plan while it can still change it, and counsel confirms the advice-right and hiring-freeze implications.

## 8. Leaders first

Leadership variable pay is suspended for the year and any fixed-pay reduction is voluntary and uniform at the top. The CFO and CEO are in the 15. The remuneration committee defines the 2027 scorecard so that the profit measure is not the only one: customer retention, regretted attrition, delivery quality and risk sit next to it, partly deferred over three years, with malus. No one is paid for headcount reduction.

The two biggest unknowns are the non-payroll yield and the real attrition rate. Both can be measured from the ledger and payroll aggregate in week 1.

## 10. What would change this budget (triggers)

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Q1 tracked savings below 60% of plan | widen freeze to all non-billable roles; second zero-base pass |
| Mid-year covenant or cash headroom below the agreed floor (to be set with the bank) | early conversation with the bank; voluntary window; then, only if still needed, a once-only, decently run reduction |
| Utilisation falls below plan or regretted attrition above 15% | release backfill for billable roles |
| Revenue drops more than 3% against plan | re-run the whole budget, not one line |

## 13. Indicators we track quarterly

Money: net savings versus plan by lever, operating margin, cash, covenant headroom. People: regretted attrition, time to fill, utilisation, overtime, engagement pulse. Customers: retention and delivery quality. Review: a standing quarterly item with the same layout each time.
