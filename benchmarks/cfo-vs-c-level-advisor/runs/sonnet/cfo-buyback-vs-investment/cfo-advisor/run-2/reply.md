# Recommendation to the board: buyback vs. reinvestment

## Bottom Line

Recommend neither request in full. Fund the COO's €120m, but stage it behind gates. Pair it with a smaller buyback of about €100m, sized to close the EPS gap rather than to satisfy the activist's headline number. Do not authorise €300m.

The two asks together total €420m, which is more than the €400m you hold. Taking both in full is not an option. Doing the buyback first would leave about €100m, and the COO's plan would then have to be funded from operating cash flow or debt. Net cash is also not free cash. A retailer's net cash includes seasonal working capital, and consumer electronics peaks hard in Q4.

Proposed allocation:

| Use | € m | Cash left after |
|---|---|---|
| Starting net cash | 400 | 400 |
| COO programme, staged (about 70 committed, about 50 gated) | 120 | 280 |
| Buyback, executed in tranches | about 100 | about 180 |
| Retained as liquidity and flexibility | about 180 | |

The right buyback size depends on your share count and price, which I don't have. Treat the €100m as a placeholder to be re-sized by the first calculation under "How to Act" below.

## What (with confidence)

1. 🟢 **€300m buyback plus €120m investment is €420m against €400m of net cash.** Both in full is arithmetically impossible without debt or drawing the cash that funds operations.
2. 🟡 **A buyback much smaller than €300m can probably close a gap of "a few cents".** Illustration, using my assumptions. Suppose a 2-3% EPS gap, a 3% pre-tax yield on cash (about 2.3% after tax) and a P/E of about 12.5. Each 1% of shares retired then adds roughly 0.7% to EPS. Closing the gap needs about 3-4% of shares. If your market cap is about €2bn, that is roughly €60-80m. A €300m buyback would retire about 15% of shares and overshoot the gap several times over. The overshoot is what the activist is really asking for, and it is not needed to hit guidance.
3. 🟡 **The COO's €120m probably does not sit entirely on the balance sheet.** Store-staff training and some platform costs (planning, data migration, run costs) are normally expensed, not capitalised. Only qualifying development spend can be capitalised under IAS 38. If, say, €30-40m is expensed this year, the EPS gap gets bigger, not smaller. Check with the auditors before the board meets.
4. 🔴 **I have not seen the COO's business case.** I don't know the expected incremental gross profit, the payback period, how much e-commerce share you would gain, or the execution risk. Platform rebuilds overrun often. I am recommending funding on conditions, not endorsing the numbers.
5. 🔴 **I don't know your gross cash, debt maturities, lease obligations or seasonal working-capital swing.** The size of the safe liquidity floor depends on them.

## Why

- **EPS guidance is the weakest reason to buy back shares.** If the justification is "it gets us to guidance", that is a financial-engineering motive. Regulators and investors scrutinise buybacks timed to hit an EPS target, especially when executive pay depends on EPS. Disclose that link to the board and have the remuneration committee review it. A miss of a few cents, explained credibly, costs far less than starving the business for years.
- **The strategic risk of doing nothing is larger than the EPS risk.** Consumer electronics retail is under pressure from online competitors and manufacturers selling direct. If the e-commerce platform and service model are the response, then paying out 75% of net cash and leaving about €100m would signal that management expects shrinkage. Staged funding keeps you able to act.
- **Both choices have to clear the same hurdle.** A buyback earns about the earnings yield on the shares (roughly 8% in my example) and is easy to reverse. The investment must earn more than your cost of capital, which I'd estimate at 8-9%, after execution risk. If the COO cannot show that, the investment fails and the buyback wins. If they can, it wins.
- **Cash returned cannot be recalled.** A staged programme lets you return more later if the gates are failed, and it costs nothing to wait on the second tranche.
- **The activist is a stakeholder, not the board.** Their €300m is an opening position. A €100m programme, a stated capital-return policy and a credible cash floor is usually a negotiable outcome, and it reads better to the other shareholders.

## How to Act

1. **Re-size the buyback this week.** Compute it from your actual share count, price, cash yield and the real EPS gap. Include the EPS drag from expensed investment spend (point 3 above). That gives the minimum buyback that hits guidance.
2. **Stress-test the liquidity floor.** Take the Q4 inventory peak, supplier payment terms, covenants and lease obligations, then model a downside case (sales down 10%, margin down 100bp). Set a minimum cash level and do not breach it in that case. My working assumption is that a floor of about €150-200m would be the starting range.
3. **Make the COO bring a gated case.**
   - Tranche 1 (about €70m): core platform rebuild plus the service model piloted in a minority of stores.
   - Tranche 2 (about €50m): released only if pre-agreed pilot KPIs are met, such as online conversion, NPS, service attach rate and gross profit per store against matched control stores.
   - Require a named owner, a monthly cost-to-complete report and a stop rule on overruns.
4. **Do not fund either with debt.** Borrowing to buy back shares, or to cover an investment you cannot afford from cash, removes the safety margin that is your main advantage.
5. **Execute the buyback in tranches** (for example, quarterly), within your authorisation and market-abuse rules, with the remaining amount decided at each review. Do not announce a €300m programme.
6. **Reset guidance in the same announcement.** Present: reaffirmed or lightly adjusted EPS, the investment case with milestones, and a capital-return framework (for example, "excess cash above €X will be returned"). A few cents of explained miss is better than a hit achieved with a larger buyback and a weakened business.
7. **Engage the activist before the announcement**, with the capital-allocation framework and the cash-floor logic, not just the number.

## Your Decision

The decision for the board is a choice between three paths:

- **A. Recommended: staged €120m plus about €100m buyback.** About €180m of cash remains. Guidance is roughly met or slightly missed, and the e-commerce plan is funded with gates.
- **B. Activist path: €300m buyback, COO plan cut or deferred.** EPS is hit and then exceeded, with about €100m left. I would only support it if the board concludes the COO's case is weak. It is hard to reverse.
- **C. Invest fully, minimal buyback.** The strongest long-term case if the COO's numbers hold, but it breaches guidance by more than a few cents and invites a harder activist campaign.

What would change my recommendation: a COO case that cannot show a return above about 9%, which moves me toward B; or a liquidity stress test showing less than €150m of true surplus, which means shrinking both asks.

Confidence: 🟡 medium on the structure and direction. 🔴 the specific euro amounts need your share count, price, expensing split and liquidity data before they go to the board.
