# Recommendation to the board: buyback vs. platform investment

## Bottom Line

Recommend a split, not a choice: **approve the €120m investment, staged and gated, and approve a smaller buyback of about €100-150m. Do not approve €300m, and do not size the buyback to hit EPS guidance.**

The two asks total €420m against €400m of net cash. Doing both in full takes you to net debt before any working-capital swing. That settles the main question: both asks cannot be met in full.

Confidence: 🟡 medium. Direction is solid. The exact buyback size depends on three numbers I don't have (see "Your Decision").

## Why

**1. €300m plus €120m does not fit.** A retailer's cash swings with the season. A year-end or post-Christmas net cash figure can overstate what is free in Q3, when you build inventory. If the €400m is a seasonal peak, the real headroom is smaller. 🔴 I have assumed a floor of about €150m. Replace it with your treasury's intra-year minimum, including leases, pensions and covenants if they are not already in "net cash".

**2. A buyback is the wrong tool for the EPS gap, and it is a poor reason to do one.**
- Illustration, with assumed inputs (market cap €2.0bn, P/E 15, 3.3% pre-tax yield on cash, 25% tax), 🔴:
  - A €100m buyback retires 5% of the shares.
  - It costs about €2.5m of after-tax interest income, which is about 1.9% of net income.
  - Net EPS accretion is roughly +3.3%.
- A miss of "a few cents" is probably 2-4% of EPS. A buyback of about €100m therefore closes it. Another €200m beyond that buys EPS you do not need.
- The harder point is that buying back stock to cover an operating miss improves the number and does nothing for the business. Analysts and the activist will see that. It also sits badly with the market-abuse and disclosure rules around buyback programmes. Take legal advice on timing, and keep the programme's stated purpose a capital-return decision, not a guidance fix.

**3. The investment may widen the EPS gap this year, not close it.**
- Training and the service-model change are expensed immediately. The platform build is mostly capitalised and amortised, so its cost arrives later.
- Ask the COO for the P&L phasing. Without it you cannot tell the board what year-1 EPS looks like. 🔴 My guess is that the expensed part is a meaningful share of the €120m.
- This is why the guidance conversation has to happen with the board now. It should not be improvised at Q3 results.

**4. The investment has to earn its place.** The €120m should not be funded on faith.
- Hurdle: a return above your cost of capital. At an assumed ~9% WACC, 🔴, it needs about €11m a year of after-tax incremental profit, or roughly €14-15m pre-tax.
- In consumer electronics, margins are thin and online price competition is intense. The real question is whether the platform and service model protect gross profit and share. Cost savings alone will not carry the case.
- Ask the COO for: incremental revenue and margin by year, the online/store mix, the service-attach rate, payback (I would want 4 years or less), and the downside if the platform migration slips. I have none of this data. The case is unproven until the COO supplies it.
- The cost of not investing also belongs in the case. If online share is already eroding your stores, doing nothing is not free.

**5. The activist's real interest is returns, and you can meet it.** €120m reinvested plus ~€130m returned is about €250m, or about 60% of the cash pile put to work within 12 months. That is a credible capital-allocation story, and more defensible than either extreme.

## How to Act

1. **Present three options to the board** with a clear recommendation:
   - A: €300m buyback, defer the investment. It breaches the floor if the investment follows.
   - B: €120m investment, no buyback. It is defensible but is likely to invite a harder activist campaign.
   - C (recommended): staged €120m plus €100-150m buyback.
2. **Stage the investment.** Release about half now. Release the rest at gates tied to measurable outcomes: platform milestones on time and on budget, conversion and attach-rate targets. Set the gates with the COO before the vote.
3. **Run the buyback as an on-market programme over about 12 months**, not an accelerated front-loaded one. That keeps flexibility if the investment overruns or trading weakens.
4. **Reset guidance deliberately.** If EPS is still a few cents short, say so and explain the investment. Do not paper over it. Present the buyback as a capital-return decision, not the fix.
5. **Set a capital-allocation framework** the board can state publicly: a minimum liquidity floor, a priority order (reinvest where returns beat the hurdle, then return surplus), and a commitment to review excess cash again after the investment gates are passed. Offer that to the activist as the path to further returns.
6. **Test the downside before the vote.** Run a case where sales fall ~10% and the investment overruns ~20%. Confirm you stay above the floor in that case.

## Your Decision

Three things would change my numbers. Please confirm them before the board pack goes out:

1. **Is €400m net cash a seasonal peak or the year-round minimum, and are leases and pensions inside it?** This sets the floor and how much buyback you can afford. If the floor is higher than €150m, cut the buyback toward €100m or below.
2. **What is the real EPS gap, and what does the €120m do to it in year 1?** If the investment widens the gap, the board needs to accept a guidance reset. A bigger buyback is not the answer.
3. **Does the COO's business case clear the hurdle?** If it does not, trim the investment to the part that does, rather than funding it in full or dropping it.

My recommendation: take option C to the board. It does not choose between the activist and the COO. It sets a floor, stages the risk, and keeps the guidance conversation honest.

Assumptions flagged 🔴 are mine and not your data: market cap and P/E, cash yield, tax rate, WACC, the €150m floor, and the expensed share of the investment. The directional case does not depend on them, but the sizes do.
