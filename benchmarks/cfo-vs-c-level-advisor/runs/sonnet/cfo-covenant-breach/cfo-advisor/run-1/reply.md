# CFO - Leverage covenant breach and the 10% job-cut announcement

## Bottom line
Do not announce the job cuts before the bank meeting. Go to the bank first, with a costed plan and a request for a waiver or covenant reset. Keep redundancies as a conditional measure, not the headline. Confidence: medium-high. I have not seen your numbers, so treat the sizing below as illustrative.

## What
- **The gap is small, and that changes the answer.** 3.6x against 3.5x means you need about 2.9% more LTM EBITDA, or about 2.8% less net debt. On an illustrative EBITDA of EUR 10M and net debt of EUR 36M, that is roughly EUR 0.3M of EBITDA or EUR 1.0M of net debt. [VERIFIED: arithmetic, 3.6/3.5]
- **Layoffs are the wrong tool for a gap this size.** Cutting 10% of 260 means about 26 people, and in the Netherlands that is a collective dismissal. You would need to notify the unions and UWV (20 or more dismissals in 3 months in one region), get the works council's advice (OR, WOR art. 25) before the decision is taken, and obtain a UWV permit. Realistically that takes 3-5 months. The cuts will therefore barely touch LTM EBITDA at the end of Q2, while the restructuring costs (transitievergoeding of one-third of a monthly salary per year of service, notice periods, legal fees) may land in the same period. Whether those costs can be added back depends on your facility's EBITDA definition. [ASSUMED: timeline and costs from general Dutch practice; confirm with your employment lawyer]
- **Announcing early creates risk.** It can breach your information and consultation duties to the OR, and it triggers attrition among the people you most want to keep. It can also lead credit insurers (Atradius, Coface, Allianz Trade) to cut supplier limits, which squeezes cash further. The bank is unlikely to be reassured by a headline number with no costing behind it. [ASSUMED: general pattern, not specific to your lenders]
- **You have better levers for 3-5% of EBITDA or net debt.** Working capital is the first place to look, especially inventory, customer deposits, and supplier terms. After that: deferring capex, suspending dividends and owner withdrawals, a subordinated shareholder loan or equity cure (if the agreement allows it and the loan is excluded from net debt), a hiring freeze, not renewing temporary and flex contracts, and overtime limits. A sale-and-leaseback of property is a further option, but check how leases are treated in the covenant definition. [ASSUMED: none of these has been sized against your balance sheet]
- **Terms of the facility matter more than anything else.** Check four things this week: (1) the exact definition of net debt and EBITDA, (2) any equity-cure right, (3) any duty to notify the bank of an expected breach, and (4) the test dates. This is a forecast breach, not a default yet, and banks respond much better to early disclosure than to a surprise. [VERIFIED: standard loan-document practice; your documents may differ]

## Why this matters
A forecast breach handled early usually costs a waiver fee and a margin step-up. A badly handled one can lead to tighter terms, more reporting, or the bank taking control of the process. Cutting 26 jobs is irreversible, slow, and expensive, so it should not be your first move on a 2.9% gap.

## How to act
1. Pull the facility agreement and covenant definitions, and rebuild the Q2 test on the contractual definition -> CFO + legal -> this week.
2. Build a 13-week cash forecast and a base, downside and upside covenant forecast to Q2 and Q3 -> CFO + financial controller -> within 7 days.
3. Size the levers above (cash release and EBITDA effect, with timing) and select enough to close the gap with a buffer of at least 0.3x -> CFO + COO + sales -> within 10 days.
4. Call your relationship manager before the review, tell them you expect a tight Q2 test, and say you will bring a plan. Do not let it be a surprise in the meeting -> CFO -> this week.
5. Brief the owner, and tell the OR confidentially that a restructuring is a contingency; prepare the formal advice request so you can move quickly if needed -> CFO + HR + legal -> before the bank meeting.
6. Bring to the bank: the forecast, the measures with amounts and dates, a request for a waiver or covenant reset (headroom for Q2-Q4), and a trigger-based contingency (for example, "if Q3 EBITDA is below X, we start the formal collective redundancy process") -> CFO -> review date.

## Risks
- If the working-capital and cost levers do not close the gap, you may still need job cuts. The contingency plan, with triggers, handles that without the premature announcement.
- If the owner announces anyway, tell them plainly what it risks: OR procedure violations, loss of key staff, credit-insurer reactions, and a bank that sees a company in a hurry.
- If the covenant definition counts shareholder loans or leases as debt, the cure options change. Check before you pitch them.

## Your decision
- **Option A:** Plan-first approach: engage the bank early, ask for a waiver or reset, show the lever package, and hold redundancies in reserve with triggers. Trade-off: it needs 3 weeks of disciplined work, and the bank may charge a fee or margin step-up.
- **Option B:** Announce 10% cuts now. Trade-off: it signals commitment but is slow, expensive, legally exposed, and does little for the Q2 ratio.
- **Recommendation:** Option A. If the owner wants a visible gesture, a hiring freeze and a temporary cut in owner and management pay or withdrawals show seriousness at no legal risk.
