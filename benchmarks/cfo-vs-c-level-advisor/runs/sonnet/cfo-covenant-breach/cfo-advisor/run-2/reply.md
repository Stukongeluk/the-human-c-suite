# CFO: Q2 leverage covenant breach (3.6x vs 3.5x), bank review in 3 weeks

## Bottom line

Do not announce the 10% cut before the bank meeting. Go to the bank first, with a covenant plan that asks for a waiver or reset and shows the cash levers and an equity cure you control. The breach is small enough to fix with those. The layoffs would not fix it by Q2, could make the Q2 number worse, and are not something you can lawfully announce as a done deal in three weeks. Confidence: medium-high. I have not seen your facility agreement or your numbers.

## What

1. **The gap is small.** Leverage is net debt / EBITDA. At 3.6x against 3.5x you are about 2.8% over on net debt, or EBITDA is about 2.9% short. [VERIFIED: arithmetic from your figures]
   - Illustration, assuming LTM EBITDA of EUR 10m, which is my assumption and not your number: net debt of EUR 36m. You need roughly EUR 1.0m less net debt, or EUR 0.29m more EBITDA, by the Q2 test date.
   - Scale this to your real EBITDA. Each 0.1x is 1% of EBITDA times 10. The cure is a seven-figure cash move at most, not a restructuring.

2. **Layoffs fix the wrong period.** The test is almost certainly on LTM (trailing twelve month) EBITDA at 30 June. [ASSUMED: check your definitions]
   - 26 roles at an assumed EUR 55k fully loaded is about EUR 1.4m a year. That is about EUR 0.12m a month once they have left.
   - Dutch dismissal lead times (see 3) mean few of them are gone before June. At best you bank 1-2 months of savings by the test date, roughly EUR 0.1-0.25m. That is less than the EUR 0.29m you need in the illustration.
   - Severance and notice costs hit first. Transition payments (about 1/3 of a month's salary per year of service) plus notice, at an assumed EUR 15-25k per person, is about EUR 0.4-0.65m. Unless your agreement lets you add back restructuring costs, that lowers LTM EBITDA. Paid in cash, it also raises net debt. Both push leverage up. [ASSUMED: tenure and pay mix]
   - The savings are real for H2 and for 2027. They do not cure the Q2 test, and the bank will see that.

3. **You cannot "announce" this in three weeks.** [Employment lawyer must confirm; this is my understanding of Dutch law]
   - 26 dismissals is 10% of 260. Twenty or more in a three-month period in one UWV region is a collective dismissal under the WMCD. You must notify the unions and UWV, and consult the unions before applying for a dismissal permit on economic grounds.
   - With 50+ employees you have a works council (OR). Under art. 25 WOR it has a right of advice on a collective dismissal, and you must ask for that advice before the decision. Announcing the decision first is a procedural breach and can poison the whole procedure.
   - Selection follows the afspiegelingsbeginsel (proportional by age cohort within exchangeable functions). You cannot pick the people you want to lose, so the cut will not be as targeted as the owner imagines.
   - Check the furniture-industry CAO and any existing social plan for extra obligations.

4. **An announcement before the meeting reads as panic, not as discipline.** Relationship banks want a credible plan and early notice. A headline number with no procedure behind it invites the questions you cannot answer yet: which roles, what cost, what savings by when, what happens to capacity and orders. It also leaks to customers, suppliers, credit insurers and your best people, who are the ones with other options.

5. **There are faster, cheaper levers.** Most are cash levers because the debt side of the ratio responds quickest. [ASSUMED: applicability to your business]
   - Working capital: inventory (raw material, WIP and finished goods in a furniture business), debtor collection, supplier terms, deposits on custom orders. A 5-day improvement in the cash conversion cycle on EUR 60-80m revenue frees roughly EUR 0.8-1.1m.
   - Capex deferral and a hold on dividends, owner draws and discretionary spend until the covenant is safe.
   - Hiring freeze and not renewing temp or flex contracts. This costs nothing in severance and is quick.
   - Owner equity cure or a subordinated shareholder loan. Check whether your agreement treats these as equity or as EBITDA cure. A family owner who wants to "show we're serious" can do it with money, which the bank will value more than headcount.
   - Sale-and-leaseback or asset-backed financing on property or machinery, if you own it (slower, and it may add lease costs that affect EBITDA).
   - Margin and price actions: surcharges, mix, scrap and waste.

## Why this matters

If you breach without having talked to the bank, it can reprice, impose fees and tighter reporting, or move you to its special asset management team. A waiver requested in advance for a 0.1x miss, with a funded plan, typically costs a fee and maybe a margin step-up. Trust is the real asset, and an unplanned cut announced for show spends it. Layoffs also cost cash up front, which could make a 0.1x problem bigger.

## How to act

1. Read the facility agreement this week. Pull the exact definitions of EBITDA and net debt (add-backs, IFRS 16 or lease treatment, cash netting), the test dates and period, cure rights, any duty to notify of an anticipated breach, and what a waiver or amendment requires. Owner: CFO, with your lawyer. Deadline: 3 days.
2. Build the covenant model on your real numbers. Base, downside and upside to Q2, plus Q3 and Q4 headroom. State the EBITDA and net debt change needed, and the sensitivity of the 3.6x to one or two drivers. Owner: CFO and FP&A. Deadline: 5 days.
3. Launch the cash levers now and track them weekly: inventory, collections, supplier terms, capex and dividend freeze, hiring freeze. Set a target of EUR (net debt cut needed x 1.5) so you have a buffer. Owner: COO and finance. Deadline: start in week 1.
4. Talk to the owner before the bank: show the numbers above, the equity cure option and why an announcement now hurts. Ask what he or she will put up. Owner: CFO. Deadline: this week.
5. Pre-wire the bank. Call your relationship manager before the review, not at it. Present the forecast, the 0.1x gap, the levers already in motion, and a request: a waiver or reset of the Q2 and Q3 tests, or a headroom amendment, plus agreed monthly reporting. Owner: CFO and owner. Deadline: week 2, before the formal meeting.
6. Prepare a contingent restructuring plan that is not announced. Scope what a 10% cut would really mean (roles, costs, timing, savings), have an employment lawyer map the WMCD, OR and UWV route, and agree a trigger for starting it, for example if cash levers miss target by end of month 1. Present it to the bank as a contingency. Owner: CFO, HR and lawyer. Deadline: before the meeting.
7. Communicate internally in a controlled way: tell the OR early that the company is watching its covenant, so that if cuts come later, the advice process starts from trust. Owner: CEO or owner. Deadline: after step 4.

## Risks

- The 3.6x is a forecast. If orders or margin weaken further, the gap grows and you may need both a waiver and the cuts. That is why step 6 prepares the cuts without announcing them.
- The bank may refuse a waiver or ask for fees, tighter terms, or security. Have the owner's cure ready and know your walk-away terms.
- If the facility has a duty to notify early, being quiet is itself a default risk. This is the other reason to read the agreement first.
- Your lawyer may find that restructuring costs are not an add-back in your definition, which makes layoffs worse for Q2 than shown here.
- Working-capital pushes can damage customer and supplier relations if overdone, particularly for squeezing small suppliers.

## Your decision

Option A: Go to the bank with a waiver request, the cash levers and an owner equity cure, with layoffs prepared as a contingency. Trade-off: you give up the symbolic gesture and must show hard numbers.
Option B: Announce 10% cuts first. Trade-off: no Q2 relief, severance costs up front, legal exposure, damage to staff and customers, and a weaker position with the bank.
Recommendation: Option A. The bank cares about covenant headroom and credibility. Both are cheaper to buy with cash, equity and early honesty than with 26 jobs.
