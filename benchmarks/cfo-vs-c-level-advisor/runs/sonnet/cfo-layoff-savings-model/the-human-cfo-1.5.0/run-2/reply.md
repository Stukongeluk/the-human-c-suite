I built the model, and I would not put "120 redundancies = €9 million in 2027" in the board pack: on realistic Dutch and German timelines the 120 exits save about €2.9m gross in 2027, cost about €4.9m to execute, and leave 2027 net cash around €3.5m **negative**. The €9.0m only appears from 2028, at roughly €7.3m net.

The €9m is 120 × €75,000, which only holds if all 120 people leave on 1 January with no costs. The announcement is next month, so consultation, notice and severance push the savings out and pull the costs forward.

- Cumulative cash turns positive around mid-2028.
- If severance is provided for at announcement (Q4 2026, subject to your auditors), 2027 P&L is about +€1.0m and 2026 takes a charge of about €4.6m. Either way, 2027 is nowhere near €9m.
- Revenue at risk is not in the table because I have no data. The 2028 net saving is wiped out if the cuts cost roughly €29m of revenue a year (at an assumed 25% contribution margin).
- No single assumption closes the gap. Best realistic case (exits a quarter earlier, lighter German social plan, no extra attrition, less backfill): 2027 gross €5.1m, net cash about +€0.5m. The Sensitivities sheet has all cases.

So the board can plausibly be offered about €4m in 2027 and a €9m+ annual run-rate by the end of 2027, with a decision point on involuntary exits after the first quarter's results. Cutting only 60 to 80 people involuntarily would still not deliver €9m in 2027. If the board truly needs €9m of in-year profit, that is a revenue, pricing or covenant conversation, not something a headcount number can supply.

**Things I'd flag before the CEO announces anything**
- Announcing a fixed number before consultation is a legal risk. Dutch works council advice (art. 25 WOR) must be requested while the decision can still change, and UWV/union notification (WMCO) applies at 20+ dismissals in one UWV area. German works councils, where they exist, negotiate an Interessenausgleich and social plan, and §17 KSchG notification applies. Employer-initiated settlement agreements count towards the thresholds.
- Germany: dismissals need a social selection (age, tenure, dependants), not performance ranking, which limits who can go.
- Spending on survivors matters: research links downsizing to higher voluntary turnover afterwards (Trevor & Nyberg 2008), and replacing a leaver costs half to two times salary (Gallup). I used the low end.
- This is not legal or tax advice; employment counsel and your auditors should check the severance rules, the provisioning date and the notification duties.

**Inputs I assumed, because I was not given them (all in yellow on the Assumptions sheet).** 50/50 NL/DE split, 12 years average tenure, gross salary at 78% of loaded cost, German social plan at 0.75 months per year of service, NL transition payment at the statutory one third, and the exit timing by country. Real HR data will move the answer, so please replace them first.

**Two questions that would change the numbers most:**
1. What is the €9m protecting: a covenant, an EBITDA guidance figure or a margin target, and is it measured on 2027 in-year or run-rate?
2. What is the NL/DE split of the 120, their tenure and the collective agreements that apply, and is there a German works council?

What am I missing, and what would make this wrong? In particular, if part of the 120 sits in a site that is closing or a contract already lost, the timing and the revenue-at-risk picture change, and I would model that site separately.
