I've built the model (`redundancy_savings_model.xlsx`, one sheet, three scenarios, live formulas). The main finding is that the board pack can't say "120 redundancies deliver €9m of savings in 2027". €9m is the annual run-rate. In-year 2027 delivery is much lower, and the one-off costs are large.

**The arithmetic**

120 × €75,000 = €9.0m a year. That only holds if every role is gone on 1 January, every euro of loaded cost disappears with the person, and nobody is backfilled. None of that will be true. If the announcement is next month, consultation comes first, then notice periods, so most exits fall in the middle or second half of 2027.

| EUR m | Downside | Base | Upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline run-rate (120 × €75k) | 9.0 | 9.0 | 9.0 |
| Net full-year run-rate after leakage | 6.1 | 6.9 | 7.7 |
| **2027 in-year saving** | **1.4** | **2.8** | **4.4** |
| One-off costs (severance, legal, outplacement) | 6.3 | 5.2 | 4.6 |
| 2027 net effect if costs land in 2027 | -4.9 | -2.4 | -0.2 |
| Payback on one-offs (months of run-rate) | 12 | 9 | 7 |
| Cumulative net, 2027 + 2028 | 1.3 | 4.5 | 7.5 |

In the base case, 2027 delivers about 31% of the €9m target. The full-year saving of roughly €6.9m only arrives in 2028. To hit €9m in-year in 2027 at the same timing, you would need about 390 roles, not 120.

**Assumptions behind the numbers**

I had no site data, so the drivers are my assumptions. They are in blue on the sheet and should be replaced with real figures before this goes to the board.
- **Country split:** 60% Germany, 40% Netherlands (72 and 48 roles).
- **Avoidable share of loaded cost:** 85% in the base case. Allocated overhead, vehicles, IT and space do not go away with the head.
- **Backfill and contractor leakage:** 10% in the base case.
- **Months of 2027 saved per role:** Germany 4 and Netherlands 6 in the base case. Germany is slower because of works council negotiation of the Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan, plus notice periods. Paid notice is not a saving.
- **Severance:**
  - Germany: 0.75 months of gross pay per year of service, 12 years' average tenure.
  - Netherlands: 0.5 months per year, 10 years' average tenure.
  - Both are multiplied by 1.2, 1.0 or 0.9 for the three scenarios.
  - Gross pay is the loaded cost divided by 1.25.
- **Other one-offs:** about €6k per role in the base case.

The severance and tenure inputs are the biggest swing factors. Please replace them with HR's actual tenure and age data by site.

**Points to put in front of the CEO and board**

1. **Reframe the headline.** Present it as "€6–8m annual run-rate from 2028, €2–4m in 2027, after about €5m of one-off cost". Presenting €9m for 2027 invites a miss that the board will see in the first half-year.
2. **The timeline is the main driver.** Each month earlier that exits happen is worth roughly €0.5m in 2027 in the base case. Voluntary or settlement-based exits in the Netherlands, and an early agreement with the Betriebsrat in Germany, move the number most.
3. **Announcement and consultation.** In both countries the works bodies (Betriebsrat in Germany, ondernemingsraad in the Netherlands) generally have to be consulted before the decision is final. Collective-dismissal notification rules (Germany's Massenentlassungsanzeige, the UWV route in the Netherlands) also apply at this scale. Announcing 120 redundancies as a done deal next month creates legal and timing risk. Please confirm the sequence with employment counsel in both countries before the date is fixed.
4. **Accounting timing.** Under IFRS (IAS 37), a restructuring provision is recognised once a detailed plan is announced. Announcing in December could put most of the €5m of costs into FY2026. If so, 2027 would show the savings without the costs. The cash still leaves in 2027, and the board should see both views.
5. **If €9m in 2027 is a hard commitment,** this headcount action alone will not deliver it. The gap is about €6m in the base case. Other levers would need to be sized next to it, such as hiring freeze and attrition, contractor and agency spend, and non-people costs. I have not modelled these.
6. **Risks not in the numbers:** lost productivity and attention among those who stay, regretted attrition of people who are not on the list, and customer or safety impact in industrial service delivery. Before the number is final, check that the 120 roles are tied to a workload and demand analysis and not just a cost target. Cuts that are not matched to work removed are the main source of backfill.
