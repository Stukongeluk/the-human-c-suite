# Redundancy savings model: 120 roles, DE/NL, FY2027

## Bottom line

The model does not support "€9 million of savings in 2027". €9.0M is the annualised run-rate (120 x €75,000), and it only becomes the 2027 P&L number if all 120 people leave on 1 January 2027. That cannot happen if the announcement is next month, because both countries require consultation before anyone can be exited. My base case gives about **€4.3M of gross in-year saving, against about €4.7M of one-off cost, so roughly break-even in 2027 (-€0.4M)**, and about **€8.6M run-rate from 2028**. I would not put the €9M-in-2027 figure in the board pack. Confidence in the structure is high; confidence in the exact euro figures is medium until HR, legal and the works councils supply the inputs marked ASSUMED below.

## 1. Why €9M in 2027 does not follow

- Run-rate: 120 x €75,000 = €9.0M a year, or €750k per month once all 120 have left. [VERIFIED: your inputs]
- In-year saving = headcount x €75k/12 x months actually off payroll in 2027. The question is the average exit date, not the announcement date.
- Announcing in December starts consultation. It does not end employment. Consultation, notice periods and (in the Netherlands) the UWV route all sit between the announcement and the first euro saved. [ASSUMED timing, see section 3; legal detail to be confirmed by counsel]
- What is needed to hit €9M in 2027 on the same €75k:
  - average exit 1 April (9 months saved): 160 roles
  - average exit 1 July (6 months saved): 240 roles
  - average exit 1 October (3 months saved): 480 roles, i.e. about a third of the group

## 2. The model

**Formulas**

- Gross in-year saving = 120 x €75,000 / 12 x months off payroll in 2027
- Net recurring saving = gross x share of cost genuinely avoidable (allocated overhead, equipment and facility costs often do not go away) x (1 - backfill/contractor leakage)
- One-off cost = 120 x [severance / social-plan cost + other costs per head]
- 2027 net = net recurring saving - one-off cost (cash view)

## 3. What drives timing in Germany and the Netherlands (to confirm with counsel)

- Germany: works council (Betriebsrat) consultation and an Interessenausgleich / Sozialplan negotiation, mass-dismissal notification to the employment agency where thresholds are met, and statutory notice periods that lengthen with tenure. Senior, long-tenure staff can have notice of several months.
- Netherlands: works council advice right under the WOR, then a collective redundancy filing and dismissal permit from the UWV, then notice periods. Statutory transition payment applies, and social plans typically add to it.
- Both: selection criteria and social-selection rules, protected groups, and the risk of challenges that push exits later. A works council that is announced to rather than consulted is the most common cause of slippage. [ASSUMED from general practice, not verified for your sites]
- The base case exit date of 1 July is my reading of a December announcement followed by about three to four months of consultation and then notice. It could easily be later in Germany.

## 4. Accounting and board-pack points

- A restructuring provision is typically recognised when a detailed plan is announced to those affected (IAS 37 / IFRS; check the equivalent under your GAAP). If you announce in December 2026, much of the one-off cost may land in **FY2026**, while the savings sit in FY2027. Show the P&L and cash views separately so the board does not read FY2026 as saved cost.
- Notice periods and any garden leave are paid. Savings start on the exit date, not the notice date.
- Exclude from the savings line: backfills, contractor cover, overtime to cover lost capacity, and any revenue lost if the affected roles touch billable or customer-facing delivery. For an industrial services group, the revenue side of the roles needs to be checked before the cost side is credited. [ASSUMED risk, not quantified]

## 5. What I recommend the board pack shows

1. Run-rate saving of about €8.5M a year from 2028 (range €7.3M to €9.0M), not €9M in 2027.
2. A 2027 in-year range of roughly -€4.3M to +€3.6M net, base about break-even, with one-off cost of about €4.7M (range €3.1M to €6.2M).
3. The three levers that close the gap to €9M in 2027, with their own sizing, rather than a bigger headcount number:
   - a hiring freeze and not backfilling natural attrition (for 1,400 staff, even 8% annual attrition is about 112 leavers a year, which is a similar-sized lever)
   - contractor and overtime reductions
   - non-people cost (procurement, sites, subcontracting)
4. Decision triggers agreed in advance: for example, if the average exit date slips beyond 1 September, or one-off cost exceeds €5.5M, the plan returns to the board.

## Risks

- Anchoring risk: if the CEO announces "€9M in 2027" externally or to the banks and the model delivers about €4M, the shortfall becomes visible in Q1 2027 reporting. Fix the number before the announcement, not after.
- Covenant and liquidity: €4.7M of cash cost lands mostly in 2027 before the savings arrive. Check headroom against any bank covenants. [ASSUMED, no facility data provided]
- Attrition of people you want to keep: voluntary leavers after an announcement are usually the most employable, and backfilling them erodes the saving.

## Your decision

- Option A: present the corrected framing (run-rate about €8.5M, 2027 about break-even) and keep 120 roles. Cost: a less attractive headline for 2027.
- Option B: keep the "€9M" headline, but pair 120 roles with about 120 more FTE-equivalents of non-redundancy levers (freeze, attrition, contractors) to cover the 2027 gap. Cost: more moving parts and more delivery risk.
- Recommendation: Option A with the section 5 levers added as a separately tracked 2027 bridge. It keeps the redundancy number defensible and the 2027 target honest.
