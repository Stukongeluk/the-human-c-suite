# 2027 Management Bonus Plan: draft for the board

**Status:** draft for discussion, November 2026. Nothing here is decided until the board approves it.
**Owner:** CFO, for the remuneration committee.
**Figures marked *(illustrative)*** are placeholders. Finance replaces them with the 2027 budget and the 2024–2026 actuals before the plan is approved.

---

## 1. Recommendation

Keep adjusted EBITDA as the largest measure (50%, plus 10% on cash conversion) and as the gate for the executive team, but **define it with restructuring costs kept in**, and put the other 40% on what a buyer of a healthcare software business pays for: retained recurring revenue, service quality and safety, and the people who hold the know-how. Defer part of the executive payout and add malus and clawback.

Why restructuring stays in: we have restructured in each of 2024, 2025 and 2026. A cost that comes back every year is a cost of running the business. If the plan excludes it, a restructuring's savings raise the bonus and its costs never reach it. In the illustration in section 5, a mid-year restructuring **raises the payout from 75% to 100% of target while reported EBITDA falls by €0.8m** *(illustrative)*.

The bonus cost at target is the same as under the plan as briefed: target bonus opportunities stay unchanged. A cap of 150% of target is assumed in every option *(illustrative; set to the current plan's cap)*.

## 2. Why not 100% on adjusted EBITDA with restructuring excluded

1. **It makes restructuring free for the people who decide on it.** The saving counts toward the bonus from the day people leave; severance, notice, advisers and the backfill never do. A one-year plan also never sees the second-year costs: rehiring, ramp-up, lost know-how and customers who notice.
2. **It pays a bonus on EBITDA a buyer may not pay for.** A buyer's quality-of-earnings review will ask whether a charge that recurred three years running is really one-off. If an add-back of €1.5m a year *(illustrative)* is stripped out at a 12–16x multiple *(illustrative)*, that is €18–24m of enterprise value that existed only on paper.
3. **The label doesn't pass the test listed companies face.** For US-listed companies, SEC rules forbid calling a charge non-recurring, infrequent or unusual in a non-GAAP performance measure when a similar charge occurred within the prior two years or is reasonably likely within the next two (Regulation S-K Item 10(e); SEC interpretation C&DI 102.03). We are not bound by this, but it is the right test, and a US IPO or a US-listed acquirer would have to apply it.
4. **A profit-only bonus pays for cutting what keeps the business healthy.** In a survey of 401 senior financial executives, 78% said they would give up economic value to smooth earnings, and many said they would cut discretionary spending such as R&D and maintenance to hit an earnings target (Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal, *Journal of Accounting and Economics*, 2005). In a healthcare software company, that discretionary spend includes the security, maintenance and quality work that keeps hospitals' and care providers' systems running.

**The case for the board's version, fairly put:** EBITDA is what the fund's return and the debt are measured on, a single number is simple and hard to argue with, and soft measures can be gamed. This draft answers that by keeping EBITDA as the largest weight and the executive gate, and by taking every other measure from a system of record (billing, monitoring, the incident process, the HR system), with definitions fixed before the year starts.

## 4. The plan (option C)

### 4.1 Purpose and participants

The plan rewards the management team for building profit, cash and a business that keeps its customers, its systems and its people, over more than one year. Participants: the executive team and the senior managers listed in Schedule 1. Target bonus opportunities as a percentage of base salary are unchanged from 2026 (Schedule 1). Performance period: 1 January to 31 December 2027.

### 4.2 Scorecard

Thresholds, targets and stretch levels are fixed by the remuneration committee by 31 January 2027 from the approved budget, and are not changed during the year except under section 4.3 or 4.9.

### 4.3 Definition of Bonus EBITDA

**Starting point:** reported operating profit under the group's accounting policies, plus depreciation, amortisation and impairment, **after** the cost of this plan at its actual payout level (the plan funds itself).

**Permitted adjustments (the complete list):**
1. Costs of the owner's transactions: acquisition, refinancing and exit-process costs (advisers, due diligence) and sponsor monitoring fees.
2. Acquisitions and disposals during 2027: the target is restated pro forma, so management is neither paid nor penalised for the deal itself.
3. Changes in accounting standards imposed from outside: neutralised so the result is measured on the budget's basis.

**Not permitted (these stay in):**
- **Restructuring, reorganisation, redundancy and related adviser costs** (see the rule below).
- Any cost category that occurred in two or more of the last three years: it is recurring by definition.
- Ordinary-course litigation and settlements, SLA credits and penalties to customers, and the cost of responding to security incidents.

**Restructuring rule:**
- Restructuring costs stay in Bonus EBITDA.
- If the board approves a restructuring **in advance**, with a business case showing its full costs and savings by quarter (severance, notice and consultation time, advisers, backfill, rehiring, revenue at risk), the target moves by the planned cost **and** the planned in-year saving. Management is then measured on delivering that plan: better than plan helps the bonus, overruns and late savings hurt it, and the decision itself neither pays nor costs.
- A restructuring not approved in advance hits Bonus EBITDA in full.
- Any restructuring already planned for 2027 goes into the budget, and so into the target, at its full cost.

**Guards for a software P&L:**
- **Capitalisation:** development cost is capitalised at the budgeted share of R&D spend for bonus purposes. Capitalisation above that share is treated as an expense in Bonus EBITDA.
- **Protected lines:** information security, clinical safety and quality assurance, training and development, and platform maintenance are counted at the higher of actual and budget, so underspending them cannot lift the bonus. Their 2027 budgets are listed in Schedule 2.
- **Revenue:** contracts with non-standard terms (unusual prepayment, side letters, discounts beyond approval levels) are reviewed by the committee before revenue from them counts.

**Reconciliation:** every quarter, the board pack shows reported EBITDA, covenant EBITDA (as defined in the credit agreement, which may keep its own add-backs) and Bonus EBITDA side by side, with each adjustment named and explained. The year-end calculation is checked by the auditors under agreed-upon procedures before payment.

### 4.4 Gate for the executive team

If Bonus EBITDA is below threshold (90% of target), **the executive team receives no 2027 bonus on any measure.** Other participants can still earn the non-financial measures (retention, quality and safety, regretted attrition), capped at target, worth at most 40% of their target bonus, so the managers below the executive team keep a reason to protect customers and quality in a hard year.

### 4.5 Quality and safety circuit breaker

The committee reviews, and may reduce payouts on all measures to as low as zero, if in 2027:
- a reportable breach of personal data involving patient or customer data occurs and a control failure was a cause;
- a serious patient-safety incident occurs to which the company's software contributed, as classified by the company's clinical risk management process;
- a certification or regulatory approval the products need is suspended or lost.

The review asks whether management decisions (for example, under-resourcing security or quality) contributed. It is not a penalty for bad luck. Reasons are recorded and reported to the board.

### 4.6 Deferral

- **Executive team:** one-third of any payout is deferred, half released 12 months and half 24 months after the main payment, each subject to the malus review in 4.7.
- **Other participants:** any payout above target is deferred 12 months, subject to the same review.
- **On an exit:** deferred amounts vest at completion, after a malus review before completion, unless the transaction terms roll them into a buyer's plan.
- If the executive team also holds equity in a management incentive plan, that remains the main long-term element; the board can lighten the deferral accordingly, but should not remove it.

Deferred pay has tax rules in most countries (for example Section 409A in the US); the deferral terms need tax advice before approval.

### 4.7 Malus and clawback

Unpaid and deferred amounts can be reduced (malus), and amounts paid can be recovered for two years after payment (clawback), where:
- results are restated or found to be materially wrong;
- there was misconduct by the participant;
- results are later shown to have been achieved by deferring costs or risks (protected spend cut, security or maintenance work postponed, revenue pulled forward) that then caused a loss.

Enforceability depends on the jurisdiction and on participants' contracts; employment counsel to confirm.

### 4.8 Leaders go first, and no bonus for cutting jobs

- If the quarterly forecast shows Bonus EBITDA below threshold, the executive team's bonus accrual is released **before** any cost action affecting staff is proposed, and the board sees both in the same paper.
- No measure rewards headcount reduction. A saving from any 2027 redundancy counts only net of all its costs, and only from each person's actual leaving date.

### 4.9 Governance

- The remuneration committee owns the plan, fixes targets by 31 January 2027 and approves the year-end result.
- Committee discretion applies in both directions, with written reasons reported to the board. Definitions are not changed after the year starts.
- The full scorecard is reported to the board every quarter, alongside the EBITDA reconciliation in 4.3.
- Payment of the non-deferred part follows the audited 2027 accounts (expected April 2028).
- Joiners: pro rata from start date. Leavers: as set out in the existing plan rules and contracts (counsel to confirm).

## 5. Illustration: how a 2027 restructuring is treated

*(All figures illustrative, € millions.)* Target Bonus EBITDA €14.0m. Without any restructuring the year comes in at €13.3m (95% of target). A restructuring effective 30 June costs €1.5m one-off, saves €2.0m a year gross (€1.0m in the second half of 2027), and needs €0.3m of contractors and overtime for work that still has to be done. Payout curve as in 4.2.

| 2027 case | Reported EBITDA | EBITDA used for the bonus | Target | % of target | Payout on the EBITDA measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No restructuring | 13.3 | 13.3 | 14.0 | 95.0% | 75% |
| Restructuring, plan as briefed (cost excluded) | 12.5 | 14.0 | 14.0 | 100.0% | 100% |
| Restructuring, recommended plan, not approved in advance | 12.5 | 12.5 | 14.0 | 89.3% | 0% (executive gate applies) |
| Restructuring, recommended plan, approved in advance with its costs and savings | 12.5 | 12.5 | 13.2 | 94.7% | 73% |

Under the plan as briefed, the restructuring lowers reported EBITDA by €0.8m and raises the EBITDA used for the bonus by €0.7m, taking the payout from 75% to 100% of target. Under the recommended plan, a restructuring approved in advance leaves the payout roughly where it would have been (73% against 75%), so the decision is made on its merits; an unplanned one costs the bonus. In the plan as briefed, EBITDA is the whole bonus; in the recommended plan it is half of it.

## 7. Assumptions, sensitivities and what would change this

- **Assumed:** a target EBITDA of about €14.0m and a restructuring of about €1.5m a year, roughly scaled to a 350-person healthcare software company; a 12–16x exit multiple. The direction of every conclusion holds at other sizes; the amounts don't.
- **Most sensitive:** how often restructuring recurs (if 2027 has none, the cost of keeping it in is zero), and the exit timing (an exit in 2027–2028 argues for shorter deferral and more weight on retention and quality).
- **What would change the recommendation:** evidence that the 2024–2026 restructurings were each caused by events outside management's control and are not expected again would weaken the case for keeping restructuring in, though the pre-approval route already covers a genuinely necessary programme.

This is not legal, tax or investment advice; employment counsel, tax advisers and the auditors need to review the plan before the board approves it.
