I've drafted the 2027 plan with EBITDA still the biggest measure (50%, plus 10% on cash conversion), but with restructuring costs kept in and the other 40% on customer retention, service quality and safety, and regretted attrition. After three restructurings in three years, restructuring is a cost of running the business. In the illustration below, a mid-year restructuring under the plan as briefed lifts the payout from 75% to 100% of target while reported EBITDA falls by €0.8m.

The full draft for the board is in **2027-management-bonus-plan-draft.md**: options, scorecard, the bonus EBITDA definition, gates, deferral, malus and clawback, and a worked example.

**Why not 100% on adjusted EBITDA with restructuring excluded**

It makes restructuring free for the people who decide on it: the savings count toward their bonus and the costs never do.

| 2027, illustrative (€m) | Reported EBITDA | EBITDA used for bonus | Payout on EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|
| No restructuring | 13.3 | 13.3 (95% of €14.0m target) | 75% |
| Mid-year restructuring, plan as briefed | 12.5 | 14.0 (100%) | 100% |
| Same, recommended plan, not approved in advance | 12.5 | 12.5 (89%) | 0%, executive gate applies |
| Same, approved in advance with its costs and savings | 12.5 | 12.5 against a target moved to 13.2 (95%) | 73% |

*Assumptions: €1.5m one-off cost, €2.0m a year gross saving (€1.0m in the second half), €0.3m of contractors for work that still has to be done; payout 50% at 90% of target, 100% at target, 150% at 110%.*

- **Exit value.** A buyer's quality-of-earnings review will question a "one-off" that recurred three years running. Stripping a €1.5m add-back at 12–16x takes €18–24m off the value *(illustrative)*, after the bonus on it has been paid.
- **The label test.** For US-listed companies, SEC rules forbid calling a charge non-recurring if a similar one occurred in the prior two years (Regulation S-K Item 10(e), C&DI 102.03). You aren't bound by that, but a US IPO or a US-listed buyer would be.
- **What a profit-only bonus pays for.** Of 401 senior financial executives surveyed, 78% would give up economic value to smooth earnings, and many would cut R&D and maintenance to hit a target (Graham, Harvey & Rajgopal, 2005). Here, that's the work that keeps customers' clinical systems running.

**The board's case, fairly put:** EBITDA is what the fund's return and the debt are measured on, and one number is hard to argue with. So EBITDA stays the largest weight and a hard gate: below 90% of target, the executive team gets nothing on any measure. The other measures come from systems of record, defined before the year starts. If the board wants EBITDA to drive the whole pool, option B in the draft has the honestly defined EBITDA fund 100% of it, with a 0.8–1.2 modifier for retention, quality and people. I could live with that; I wouldn't recommend the plan as briefed.

**What's in the draft**

- **Restructuring stays in.** If the board approves one in advance, with full costs and savings by quarter, the target moves by both, so management is measured on delivery and neither paid nor penalised for the decision. An unplanned one hits the bonus in full.
- **A short, closed list of exclusions:** the owner's deal, refinancing and exit costs, sponsor fees, and acquisitions restated pro forma. Any cost category that occurred in two of the last three years counts as recurring.
- **Guards for a software P&L.** Development capitalised at the budgeted rate; security, clinical safety and QA, training and platform maintenance counted at the higher of actual and budget, so cutting them can't lift the bonus.
- **No measure rewards headcount reduction.** A 2027 redundancy saving counts only net of its costs, and only from the date each person leaves.
- **The long term.** One-third of the executive payout deferred over two years; malus and clawback for two years after payment; a circuit breaker for a patient-data breach caused by a control failure, a serious software-related patient-safety incident, or a lost certification.
- **Leaders first.** If the forecast falls below threshold, the executive bonus accrual is released before any cost action affecting staff is proposed.

The bonus cost at target is unchanged. I've assumed a 150% cap in every option; set it to your current plan's cap.

**Before it goes to the board**

- Replace every *(illustrative)* number with the 2027 budget and 2024–2026 actuals; I scaled €14.0m EBITDA and a €1.5m restructuring to a 350-person healthcare software company.
- Covenant EBITDA can keep its own add-backs; the board pack shows reported, covenant and bonus EBITDA side by side, reconciled.
- Employment counsel: new bonus terms may need participants' consent, and in some countries (the Netherlands, Germany) employee representatives have a say on pay systems. Tax advisers for the deferral, auditors for the year-end calculation. This isn't legal or tax advice.

**Two questions that would change the numbers**

1. What did the 2024, 2025 and 2026 restructurings cost, what did each save net, and how much came back as rehiring, contractors or churn? If the savings held, the pre-approval route covers a fourth. If they leaked, the board has a bigger question than the bonus.
2. Is an exit planned for 2027 or 2028? If so, I'd shorten the deferral to completion and weight retention and quality more, since diligence will check them.
