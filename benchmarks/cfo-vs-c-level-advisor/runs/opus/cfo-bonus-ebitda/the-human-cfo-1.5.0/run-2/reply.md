I've drafted the plan with adjusted EBITDA as the largest measure, but restructuring costs stay in it. Restructuring has happened in each of the last three years, so it's a running cost. Excluding it would pay management more for every cut. In an illustration with estimated figures (€17.5m EBITDA, a €1.0m bonus pool at target), an unbudgeted 20-role restructuring in mid-2027 would lift the payout under the board's version from 100% to about 126% of target. In that same year, reported EBITDA would fall by €1.1m. Under my draft, the payout stays at 100%.

The full draft, written for the remuneration committee, is in **2027-management-bonus-plan-draft.md**. It includes the rules, the scorecard, the EBITDA definition, a reconciliation template and the illustration.

**What the draft does**

- **Scorecard (adds to 100%):**
  - Bonus EBITDA 50%
  - cash conversion 10%
  - gross revenue retention 15%
  - service quality and security 10% (uptime against SLAs, severity-1 incidents, keeping your security certification)
  - people 15% (regretted attrition and an anonymous engagement survey, company-wide aggregates only)

  Every measure comes from a system of record.
- **Bonus EBITDA definition:** reported EBITDA with a closed list of five adjustments.
  - Restructuring is budgeted at a realistic level, so the target is honest. If restructuring goes as planned, the payout is the same as under the board's version.
  - If the board approves a restructuring outside the budget, its costs and its 2027 savings both come out. Management neither gains nor loses from a cut.
- **Guards against the easy routes to a profit-only bonus:**
  - The development-cost capitalisation rate is fixed at budget.
  - Underspend on training, security, maintenance and clinical quality doesn't count towards Bonus EBITDA.
  - No measure targets headcount.
- **Gates:**
  - Below 90% of the EBITDA target, the financial part pays nothing and the total is capped at 20% of target.
  - No bonus at all if a covenant is breached or needs a shareholder cure.
- **Deferral, malus and clawback:** 30% of any payout is deferred over two years and paid at completion on an exit, after a malus review. Malus and clawback apply, including when roles are cut and then refilled within 12 months.
- **Leaders first:** if 2027 falls behind plan, stopping the bonus accrual is the first saving. It comes before any proposal touching non-management jobs or pay.

**Why not 100% adjusted EBITDA with restructuring excluded**

The plan as proposed excludes a recurring cost but counts the savings it produces. That makes every restructuring pay the people who propose it, whether or not it pays the company. It also pays for cutting training, security and maintenance: in the illustration, €0.5m less of those adds about 14 points of payout.

In the owners' own terms, expect a buyer's quality-of-earnings review to treat three years of restructuring as recurring. At around €2m a year (estimate), that's €20–30m of headline value at 10–15x EBITDA. The adjusted number claims it, and a buyer won't pay for it. The bonus would be paying on value that isn't there at exit. US-listed companies may not call a charge non-recurring when a similar one occurred in the prior two years (SEC Regulation S-K Item 10(e)). You aren't bound by that rule, but diligence teams apply the same logic.

The board's case is fair. EBITDA is what lenders test and buyers price, and one number is simple. Management also shouldn't lose bonus over a restructuring the owners want. The draft keeps 60% on financial measures, uses hard data for the rest, and neutralises any restructuring the board approves outside the budget.

There's also a people reason for the 15% weight. After a 1% downsizing, voluntary turnover was 31% higher the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008), and after three rounds in three years, key-person risk is something a buyer will look at.

If the board wants to stay closer to its ask, the draft includes a minimum-change option: 100% EBITDA with restructuring in. It removes the reward for cuts, but leaves the training, security and capitalisation routes open and gives no weight to customers or people.

A line you could use with the board: *"EBITDA stays at the centre. We just stop paying bonus on costs we'll have to explain away in due diligence."*

**Before approval** (not legal or tax advice)

- **Counsel:** whether existing contracts promise a bonus on another basis, whether employee representatives must be consulted on a pay-scheme change, and whether malus, clawback and leaver terms can be enforced.
- **Tax adviser:** treatment of the deferral.
- **Lenders:** confirm the covenant EBITDA definition is untouched.
- **Auditors:** agreed-upon procedures on the calculation.

**Two questions that would change the numbers**

1. What did restructuring cost in 2024–2026, and is one already planned for 2027? The answer sets the budgeted amount and the target.
2. How much do participants hold in the management equity plan, and when do you expect to exit? If the equity already pays heavily on exit EBITDA, the annual bonus should lean further towards retention, quality and people.

Key assumptions: all figures are estimates scaled to 350 people: about €70m revenue, a 25% margin, around €2m of restructuring a year and a €1.0m pool at target.
