# 2027 Management Bonus Plan: draft for the board

**Status:** draft for discussion by the board and its remuneration committee. Rules are written to be adopted as they stand. Figures marked *(est.)* are illustrations scaled to a company of about 350 people. They are not company data. Replace them with general ledger, payroll, billing and HR figures before targets are set.

## 1. Recommendation

Keep adjusted EBITDA as the plan's largest measure, but define it honestly and balance it:

- **60% financial:** Bonus EBITDA 50%, cash conversion 10%.
- **40% on what a buyer will check in due diligence:** gross revenue retention 15%, service quality and security 10%, people 15%.
- **Restructuring costs stay in Bonus EBITDA.** We restructured in 2024, 2025 and 2026, so it is a running cost, and the 2027 budget and target include a realistic amount for it. Any restructuring the board approves outside the budget is neutralised both ways: its costs and its 2027 savings both come out, so the bonus neither gains nor loses from it.
- **30% of any payout is deferred** over two years. Malus and clawback apply, and the management bonus is the first saving if 2027 falls behind plan.

**Why it matters, in figures (est.):** under the plan as proposed (100% adjusted EBITDA, restructuring excluded), an unbudgeted 20-role restructuring in mid-2027 raises the payout from 100% to about 126% of target. In the same year, reported EBITDA falls by €1.1m. Under this draft the same restructuring leaves the payout at 100%. Separately, if restructuring keeps averaging around €2m a year, expect a buyer's quality-of-earnings review to treat it as recurring. That is roughly €20–30m of headline value at 10–15x EBITDA that the "adjusted" number claims and a buyer is unlikely to pay for.

## 2. Options considered

| | A. As proposed | B. Minimum change | C. Recommended (this draft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design | 100% adjusted EBITDA; restructuring excluded as one-off | 100% EBITDA; restructuring in; board-approved unbudgeted restructuring neutralised | 60% financial (EBITDA and cash), 40% customers, quality and security, people; restructuring in; deferral, malus, clawback |
| What it pays for | EBITDA growth from any source, including cuts whose costs are excluded | Profit growth, honestly measured | Profit growth that comes with retained customers, reliable service and a stable team |
| Effect of an unbudgeted 20-role cut mid-2027 (est.) | Payout 100% → 126% of target | No change if neutralised (65% if not) | No change if neutralised (82% if not) |
| Effect of cutting €0.5m from training, security and maintenance (est.) | Payout +14 points | Payout +16 points (lower base) | No effect (protected-line rule) |
| Effect of capitalising €1.0m more development cost (est.) | Payout +29 points | Payout +32 points | No effect (rate fixed at budget) |
| Due-diligence view | Adjustment likely challenged: restructuring in each of 2024–2026 | Clean | Clean, plus evidence on retention, quality and key people |
| Simplicity | Highest | High | Moderate: five measures, all from systems of record |

"Do nothing" means rolling over the current approach, which is option A.

**The board's case for A, taken seriously:** EBITDA is what lenders test and what buyers price. A single measure is simple and hard to argue with. Non-financial measures can be soft. And management shouldn't lose bonus over a restructuring the owners asked for. This draft keeps EBITDA dominant at 60% financial. It uses only measures that come from systems of record (billing, monitoring, HR system, a third-party survey). It neutralises any restructuring the board approves outside the budget, so management is neither punished nor rewarded for it.

### 3.3 Definition of Bonus EBITDA

**Starting point:** reported EBITDA in the audited 2027 accounts, under the accounting policies and development-cost capitalisation rate used in the 2027 budget.

**Stays in, with no adjustment:** restructuring and reorganisation costs (severance, paid notice, outplacement, advisers, retention payments), recruitment and onboarding, contractors covering removed roles, integration of past acquisitions, ordinary-course legal costs, and the cost of this bonus.

**The only permitted adjustments** (a closed list; each is shown in the reconciliation in section 4):

1. Transaction costs of acquisitions, disposals and refinancing initiated by the shareholders. If a business is acquired, the committee decides at completion, in writing, whether its EBITDA is excluded or the target is raised for it.
2. Fees paid to the shareholders.
3. **Board-approved restructuring outside the budget:** when the board approves such a restructuring in writing with a business case, its 2027 costs are added back *and* its 2027 savings are removed. The savings are the fully loaded cost of the affected roles from each exit date to 31 December, taken from payroll aggregates. The 2028 target is then set on the new cost base.
4. Changes in accounting policy or capitalisation rate compared with the budget are neutralised.
5. **Protected lines:** an underspend against budget on training, information security, platform maintenance and quality or clinical safety is added back as a cost. The exception is an underspend the committee accepts, in writing, as a genuine efficiency with the same output.

Nothing else is adjusted. In bonus and board reporting, no item is called "one-off" or "non-recurring" if a similar item occurred in the prior two years or is reasonably likely in the next two. This follows the test the SEC applies to US-listed companies (Regulation S-K Item 10(e); C&DI 102.03), which we adopt as good practice. Bonus EBITDA is separate from the EBITDA definition in the credit agreement. This plan does not change covenant reporting.

### 3.4 Gates

- **Financial gate:** if Bonus EBITDA is below threshold, the financial measures pay nothing. The non-financial measures then pay at most 50% of their target value, so the maximum is 20% of target bonus.
- **Covenant gate:** no 2027 bonus is paid if a financial covenant is breached or a shareholder cure is needed.
- **Serious-incident gate:** after a reportable personal-data breach with a likely high risk to the people affected, or a software-related patient-safety incident reportable to a regulator, the committee reviews the payout and may reduce it by up to 100%.

### 3.5 Payment and deferral

- 70% of the payout is paid after the 2027 accounts are signed *(expected Q2 2028)*.
- 30% is deferred and paid in two equal parts, after the 2028 and the 2029 accounts are signed. The deferred amount is paid in cash, or in management equity plan instruments if the shareholders prefer and the tax treatment allows.
- Deferred amounts stay subject to malus until paid.
- **On an exit:** unpaid deferred amounts are paid at completion, after a malus review.
- **Leavers:** good leavers keep deferred amounts on the normal schedule. Bad leavers lose unpaid amounts. Definitions to be agreed with counsel.

### 3.6 Malus and clawback

- **Malus** (reduction before payment, including of deferred amounts) applies for:
  - material misstatement;
  - misconduct;
  - a serious breach of risk, data-protection or safety rules;
  - a serious incident under 3.4;
  - results later shown to have been achieved at the long term's expense, for example protected spend cut, roles removed and refilled (including with contractors) within 12 months, or customer commitments missed to defer cost.
- **Clawback** (recovery after payment) applies for three years after payment, for misstatement or misconduct. Whether it can be enforced depends on employment law and contracts; counsel to confirm.

### 3.7 Leaders first

- If the 2027 forecast shows savings are needed to meet plan, covenants or cash needs, the first saving is the management bonus. Accrual stops before any proposal to reduce non-management jobs, hours or pay.
- No part of the bonus is earned for headcount reduction as such. No measure targets headcount or payroll.
- If compulsory redundancies happen in 2027, the committee reviews the management payout. It records in writing why any payout is fair in that light, and shares that reasoning with participants.

### 3.8 Governance

- The remuneration committee approves targets before 31 January 2027, and participants receive the rules in writing at the start of the year.
- The CFO prepares the calculation from systems of record. The auditors perform agreed-upon procedures on it, and the committee approves it.
- Committee discretion is **downward only**, apart from the defined adjustments in 3.3. Every use of discretion is recorded with its reasons.
- Quarterly, the board pack shows Bonus EBITDA next to reported EBITDA, management adjusted EBITDA and covenant EBITDA, along with every measure on the scorecard.

## 5. Illustration (est.)

All inputs are estimates for a company of about 350 people, to be replaced with company data:

- revenue €70m (about €200k per employee; plausible range €50–90m);
- EBITDA before restructuring €17.5m (25% margin; range 20–30%);
- budgeted restructuring €2.0m (range €1–3m);
- target bonus pool €1.0m.

**The unbudgeted cut:** 20 roles at €90k fully loaded cost each, with exits effective 1 July. It costs €2.0m one-off, saves €0.9m in 2027 and €1.8m a year after that. Reported EBITDA goes from €15.5m to €14.4m (−€1.1m). In the C rows, the other measures are assumed on target.

| Definition | Target (€m) | Actual (€m) | Achievement | EBITDA payout | Total payout | Pool (€m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. As proposed: restructuring excluded | 17.50 | 18.40 | 105.1% | 125.7% | 125.7% | 1.26 |
| B. 100% EBITDA, restructuring in, cut not neutralised | 15.50 | 14.40 | 92.9% | 64.5% | 64.5% | 0.65 |
| C. Recommended, cut not neutralised | 15.50 | 14.40 | 92.9% | 64.5% | 82.3% | 0.82 |
| B or C, board-approved cut neutralised | 15.50 | 15.50 | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% | 1.00 |

If restructuring simply happens as budgeted, A, B and C all pay 100%. The honest definition costs management nothing when restructuring is planned for openly. It only removes the reward for unplanned cuts.

## 6. To check before approval

This draft is not legal or tax advice.

- **Employment counsel:**
  - existing contracts or letters that promise a bonus on a different basis;
  - whether employee representatives (for example a works council) must be consulted on a change to a pay scheme;
  - whether malus, clawback and leaver terms can be enforced.
- **Tax adviser:** treatment of deferred cash, and of any payment in management equity plan instruments.
- **Lenders:** confirm the credit agreement's EBITDA definition and reporting are unaffected.
- **Auditors:** scope of the agreed-upon procedures.
- **Shareholders:** how this plan sits next to the management equity plan, and the expected exit timing.
- **Data:**
  - actual restructuring costs for 2024–2026;
  - 2027 restructuring already planned;
  - three years of gross revenue retention, availability, incident and attrition history to set thresholds.

## 7. Open questions for the board

1. What did restructuring cost in 2024, 2025 and 2026, and what is already planned for 2027? The answer sets the budgeted amount and the target.
2. How much do participants hold in the management equity plan, and when is an exit expected? If the equity plan already pays heavily on exit EBITDA, the annual bonus should lean further towards retention, quality and people.

**Key assumptions:** the figures in section 5 are estimates. Restructuring is assumed to continue at roughly the 2024–2026 rate unless the board decides otherwise. Billing, monitoring and HR data are assumed reliable enough to set thresholds. If the engagement survey doesn't exist yet, start with retention and attrition in 2027 and add engagement in 2028.
