I've drafted the plan: **2027-management-bonus-plan-draft.md**. It pays 100% on EBITDA, as the board asked. I changed one thing from your brief, and it should be raised with the board before the plan goes further.

**Restructuring can't be treated as one-off in this plan.** You've restructured in each of the last three years, so it is now a recurring cost of running the business. Excluding it "as usual" causes two problems.

1. **It pays management for another restructuring.** Under that treatment the costs are added back, but the savings still count towards the bonus. Here is a worked example from the draft, with illustrative figures in millions:
   - Target EBITDA is 24.0, and a restructuring costs 3.0 and saves 2.0 in-year.
   - Reported EBITDA falls to 23.0.
   - Adjusted EBITDA rises to 26.0, which is 108% of target, so the bonus pays **183% instead of 100%**.
   - The quickest route to a maximum payout becomes a fourth restructuring. In a 350-person healthcare software business, that also carries retention, product-quality and compliance risk.
2. **It works against the exit.** A buyer's quality-of-earnings review will probably treat a cost that recurs every year as normal operating cost. Every unit of recurring cost presented as one-off overstates the EBITDA you can sell by the same amount, multiplied by the exit multiple. The bonus should pay on the EBITDA a buyer will actually pay for.

**What the draft does instead (section 5.2):**
- **Restructuring counts as an operating cost by default.** The 2027 target already includes any restructuring the board has approved for the year.
- **The board can designate a genuinely exceptional programme as bonus-neutral.** It has to do this when it approves the programme, before any money is committed. The costs are then excluded and the target rises by the programme's in-year savings, so a sound long-term decision doesn't hurt the bonus and a weak one doesn't pay it.
- **Guardrails apply:** no designation after the fact, a cap at [5]% of target, a tight definition of qualifying costs, and the exclusion is reversed if an eliminated role is refilled.
- **Pay at budget doesn't change.** The target is set on the same basis as the measurement, so delivering budget still pays 100%. This isn't a pay cut, which matters for how you present it to the management team.

If the board still wants full exclusion, Annex B has a fallback clause with minimum safeguards. The safeguard that matters most is raising the target by the restructuring savings.

**The rest of the plan:**
- **Single metric:** 100% EBITDA, with no individual modifier.
- **Payout curve:** threshold [95%] pays [50%], target pays 100%, and maximum [110%] pays [200%], capped and interpolated in a straight line.
- **Self-funding:** EBITDA is measured after the cost of the bonus itself.
- **Closed list of adjustments,** each applied whether it raises or lowers payouts:
  - shareholder fees and deal costs are excluded;
  - M&A is made bonus-neutral by adjusting the target;
  - the capitalisation rate for development is locked at budget, so capitalising more R&D can't boost EBITDA.
- **Costs that stay in:** breach, regulatory and SLA costs. Management owns those.
- **Malus:** the committee can reduce payouts, including to zero, for a patient-safety incident, a health-data breach or a regulatory sanction. This keeps the 100% EBITDA weighting while covering the main risk of a single-metric plan in healthcare.
- **Standard terms** for leavers, change of control (with a likely exit in mind), clawback and payment timing.
