**CFO: 2027 Management Bonus Plan**

**BOTTOM LINE**
The draft plan is attached. It pays 100% on adjusted EBITDA, as the board asked, with one change: restructuring costs count. You've restructured three years running, so these costs aren't one-off. If we exclude them, the plan pays management for the savings and never charges them the cost. Confidence: high on the restructuring point, medium on calibration (target percentages and the payout curve are placeholders).

**WHAT**
- **Restructuring isn't one-off here** (based on your three-year history; we haven't seen the amounts yet). The clearest public test is the SEC's rule for listed companies: a cost can't be called non-recurring if a similar one occurred in the prior two years or is likely within the next two ([Reg S-K Item 10(e), Deloitte summary](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/accounting/sec/sec-reporting-interpretations-manual/roadmap-non-gaap-financial-measures/chapter-4-non-gaap-measures-that/4-7-performance-measure-prohibitions)). That rule doesn't bind a private company, but your history fails it three times over.
- **Excluding it makes cuts look far better in the bonus maths than in the P&L** (illustrative figures, not yours). Say 30 roles go in Q1 at $100k fully loaded cost each, against a $20M EBITDA target:
  - In-year savings are $2.25M and the one-off costs $1.2M, so the real gain is $1.05M.
  - Under the board's wording, management is credited with the full $2.25M. Payout goes to 143% of target, against 120% with the cost counted.
  - Repeat that every year and the bonus never sees the cost.
- **It also pays on a number a buyer won't pay for** (illustrative multiple). A buyer's quality-of-earnings review at exit will treat about $1.2M a year of recurring restructuring as a normal running cost. At 15x, that's $18M of enterprise value that the adjusted EBITDA figure overstates.
- **The draft keeps EBITDA at 100% of the formula and adds three gates that can only reduce payout:** cash conversion, gross revenue retention, and security and compliance incidents. In healthcare software you can hit one year's EBITDA by cutting support, security or collections. The bill comes later and is bigger.
- **The rest is standard PE practice:**
  - a closed list of adjustments, fixed before 1 January and applied the same way to one-off gains as to one-off costs
  - capitalised development held at the budgeted rate
  - a self-funding curve: 0% below 95% of target, 100% at target, capped at 150% at 110%
  - payment after the audited accounts, with malus and clawback

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
As asked, the plan makes a fourth restructuring free for the people deciding on it, and pays out on profit the sponsor can't sell. With the cost counted, management is still rewarded for any restructuring that pays back within the year, just on the net figure.

**RISKS**
- Management will push back: "you're penalising us for restructuring you asked for." The draft answers this. When the board directs a restructuring outside the budget, the target moves by its net cost, so the bonus is neutral. If that rule is dropped, the pushback is fair.
- The restructuring allowance in the budget could be set at a hopeful number rather than a real one. The draft sets it at the higher of the three-year actual average and the 2027 plan.
- If the strategy is buy-and-build, integration costs recur by design. Each deal's business case carries its own capped integration budget instead of a general exclusion.
- Three restructurings in three years is a finding in itself. Each round costs more than severance: people you wanted to keep leave, knowledge goes with them, and in healthcare software customers' clinical operations feel it. The board should know what drove each round before the bonus plan makes a fourth easier.

**YOUR DECISION**
- Option A: exclude restructuring, as in prior years. Simplest, and what management expects. Pays on gross savings, overstates the EBITDA that matters at exit, and invites costs to be reclassified as restructuring.
- Option B: count restructuring, with a budgeted allowance in the target and board-directed programmes neutralised net. Gives an honest number and still rewards net savings. Needs a credible allowance and one hard conversation with management.
- Option C: exclude restructuring only up to a board-approved cap. Limits the damage, but still pays on gross savings up to the cap and still calls a recurring cost one-off.
- Recommendation: **B**. It keeps the board's metric and its 100% weighting, and the bonus pays on the same EBITDA a buyer will pay for.
