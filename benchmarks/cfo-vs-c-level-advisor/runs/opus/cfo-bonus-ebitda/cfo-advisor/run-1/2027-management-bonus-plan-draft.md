## 2. Purpose

The plan rewards the management team for profitable performance in 2027. It pays on the EBITDA figure a buyer's quality-of-earnings review would also accept at exit, so the team gains when the owners gain.

## 3. Eligibility and bonus opportunity

- **Joiners and role changes:** paid pro rata by days, if the person was in the role for at least [3] months of 2027.

## 4. Performance measure: Bonus EBITDA

### 4.1 Definition

Bonus EBITDA = audited 2027 operating profit + depreciation + amortisation, plus or minus **only** the adjustments in 4.3, on the accounting basis in 4.5.

Bonus EBITDA is measured **after** the cost of this plan and of all other bonus and commission plans. That makes the plan self-funding: any payout above target has to be earned on top of its own cost.

### 4.2 Restructuring and reorganisation costs

Restructuring costs are **included** in Bonus EBITDA. The plan does not describe them as one-off, because the company has had restructuring costs in each of 2024, 2025 and 2026.

**(a) Allowance.** The 2027 budget, and so the target, includes a restructuring allowance of $[X]. It is set at [the higher of the 2024–2026 average actual cost and the cost in the 2027 plan]. Spending within the allowance does not cut payout against target. Spending above it does. Spending below it raises Bonus EBITDA, like any other saving.

**(b) Programmes the board directs.** Sometimes the board will direct a restructuring that is not in the budget. When it approves one, RemCo adjusts the target by the programme's net effect in the year: in-year costs minus in-year savings, both taken from the approved business case. The programme then neither raises nor lowers the payout. This adjustment goes into the board minutes when the board approves the programme, not at year-end. The run-rate savings go into the 2028 budget.

**(c) Restructuring that management starts itself** during the year, without a board direction, counts in full, both costs and savings. Management is rewarded only if the savings exceed the cost within the year.

**(d) What counts as restructuring cost**, for tracking against the allowance:

- severance and notice pay
- legal and advisory fees
- outplacement
- retention bonuses
- contractor or temporary backfill
- duplicate running costs
- onerous leases
- costs of any programme labelled "transformation"

Because restructuring is counted either way, calling an operating cost restructuring does not change its treatment.

### 4.3 Permitted adjustments: closed list, applied the same way to gains and costs

1. **Sponsor costs:** monitoring fees, reporting or advisory work the sponsor requests, and refinancing costs.
2. **Transaction costs** of board-approved acquisitions, disposals and the exit process: advisers, due diligence, banking fees. **Integration costs are not excluded.** Each acquisition's business case carries its own integration allowance, treated like 4.2(a).
3. **Acquisitions and disposals:** the target is adjusted by the acquired or disposed business's budgeted EBITDA for the period owned, as set out in the approved deal case.
4. **Non-cash share-based payment charges** from the management incentive plan (MIP).
5. **Changes in accounting standards or policies:** Bonus EBITDA is measured on the 2027 budget basis.
6. **Gains and losses on disposal of fixed assets**, revaluations [and unrealised FX on intercompany balances].

**Any other adjustment** must meet three conditions:

- The CFO proposes it and RemCo approves it **in the quarter the item arises**, not at year-end.
- It is shown in the quarterly reconciliation (Appendix B).
- It is applied the same way to gains as to costs. One-off gains are removed just as one-off costs are added back. Examples of one-off gains: provision releases, insurance recoveries, legal settlements in our favour.

### 4.4 Costs that are not adjusted (non-exhaustive)

These stay in Bonus EBITDA:

- restructuring and reorganisation costs (see 4.2)
- integration costs
- retention and sign-on bonuses
- recruitment fees
- consultant and contractor costs
- system migrations
- bad debts
- SLA credits and penalties paid to customers
- ordinary-course litigation
- the cost of responding to a data breach
- regulatory fines

### 4.5 Accounting basis

- Audited [IFRS / US GAAP] accounts.
- **Capitalised development:** Bonus EBITDA uses the budgeted capitalisation rate of [x]% of R&D spend. Capitalising more than budgeted does not raise Bonus EBITDA, and capitalising less does not lower it.
- FX translated at budget rates.
- Revenue recognition policy as at the budget date.

### 4.6 Target setting

- Target = 2027 board-approved budget EBITDA, on exactly the definition in 4.1 to 4.5. That means it includes the restructuring allowance (4.2(a)) **and the full-year run-rate savings of restructurings completed in 2024–2026**, so those savings are not paid for a second time.
- Target: $[20.0]M (placeholder).

## 5. Payout curve

| Bonus EBITDA as % of target | Payout as % of target bonus |
|---|---|
| Below 95% | 0% |
| 95% | 50% |
| 100% | 100% |
| 105% | 125% |
| 110% or more | 150% (cap) |

The curve is straight-line between these points. Between 95% and 100% of target, each point of EBITDA is worth 10 points of payout. Between 100% and 110%, each point is worth 5.

The range is narrow because EBITDA at a mature, PE-owned software company is fairly forecastable. RemCo and the CHRO should calibrate it against how confident the budget is.

## 6. Gates: these can only reduce payout

| Gate | Measure | Threshold | Effect if missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash conversion | (Bonus EBITDA − capex − capitalised development − increase in net working capital) ÷ Bonus EBITDA | [Budget − 10 points] | Payout capped at 100% |
| Customer retention | Gross revenue retention on ARR at 1 January 2027 (excluding expansion) | [Budget − 2 points] | Payout capped at 100% |
| Security and compliance | Any of: a material breach of health or personal data that must be reported to a regulator; a regulatory sanction; loss or qualification of a certification customers require by contract [e.g. ISO 27001, SOC 2]; a patient-safety incident linked to our product | Any occurrence | RemCo may reduce payout by up to 100%, taking into account whether management decisions or underinvestment contributed |

**Why the gates are there:** a single-year EBITDA plan can be hit by cutting what EBITDA does not see, such as support, security, product quality and collections. In healthcare software, the cost of those cuts usually arrives later and larger. The gates do not change the 100% EBITDA weighting. They only stop a payout from being earned by hollowing out the business.

## 7. Governance

- The CFO calculates Bonus EBITDA. The CFO is also a participant, so the RemCo chair signs off the final figure and every adjustment.
- Each quarter, RemCo receives a reconciliation from reported EBITDA to Bonus EBITDA (Appendix B), including restructuring spend against the allowance and the status of each gate.
- [Optional] The auditor performs agreed-upon procedures on the final calculation.
- RemCo discretion runs **downward only**. The only upward changes are the pre-agreed target adjustments in 4.2(b) and 4.3. RemCo minutes every use of discretion, with its reasons.

## 8. Payment, leavers, change of control

- **Payment:** within 30 days of the audited 2027 accounts being signed, expected [April 2028], through payroll and subject to tax.
- **Leavers:**
  - A participant must be employed, and not under notice, on the payment date.
  - Good leavers (redundancy, ill health, death, retirement, [other cases at RemCo's discretion]) receive a pro-rata payout at the normal time, based on actual results.
  - Bad leavers forfeit the bonus.
  - A participant whose role is removed in a restructuring is a good leaver.
- **Change of control during 2027:** [Option 1: measured on year-to-date Bonus EBITDA against the phased budget at completion, and paid pro rata at completion. / Option 2: the buyer takes on the plan for the full year.] RemCo decides before 1 January 2027 and aligns the choice with the MIP's exit and leaver terms.

## 9. Malus and clawback

Malus applies before payment. Clawback applies for up to [2] years after payment. Either can be used in these cases:

- a material misstatement of results
- a calculation error
- gross misconduct
- conduct by a participant that causes serious reputational or regulatory harm

Recovery is net of tax where local law permits. Legal counsel confirms enforceability in each country where participants are employed.

## 10. Communication

- Every participant gets a one-page letter by [15 January 2027]. It sets out their target %, the Bonus EBITDA definition, the payout curve, the gates and the quarterly reconciliation.
- Tell participants directly that restructuring is now counted, and explain why. This change is the most likely to draw pushback. The answer to "you're penalising us for what you asked us to do" is rule 4.2(b): restructuring the board directs is neutral for the bonus.

## Appendix A: Worked example (illustrative figures, not company actuals)

**Assumptions:**

- Target Bonus EBITDA of $20.0M.
- Target bonus pool of $1.2M (about 30 participants at an average target bonus of $40k).
- Without any restructuring, the business delivers exactly on target.
- In Q1, 30 roles are removed. Their average fully loaded cost is $100k a year, and the exits take effect on 1 April.

**Restructuring economics in 2027:**

- In-year savings: 30 × $100k × 9/12 = **$2.25M**
- One-time costs: severance and notice of 30 × $100k × 4/12 = $1.0M, plus advisers and outplacement of $0.2M = **$1.2M**
- Net in-year effect: $2.25M − $1.2M = **$1.05M**

**Payout under each treatment:**

| | Board's version (restructuring excluded) | Recommended: management starts it | Recommended: board directs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bonus EBITDA before the cost of above-target bonus | $22.25M | $21.05M | $21.05M |
| Target | $20.00M | $20.00M | $21.05M (raised by the net $1.05M) |
| Bonus EBITDA after self-funding | $21.73M | $20.81M | $21.05M |
| Bonus EBITDA as % of target | 108.7% | 104.0% | 100.0% |
| Payout as % of target bonus | 143.3% | 120.2% | 100.0% |
| Bonus pool | $1.72M | $1.44M | $1.20M |

**How self-funding works out.** Above target, payout = 100% + 5 × (EBITDA ÷ target − 1). Each payout point above 100% costs 1% of the $1.2M pool, and that cost comes out of Bonus EBITDA. This gives EBITDA = gross − 1.2 × 0.25 × (EBITDA − 20), so EBITDA = (gross + 6) ÷ 1.3:

- Board's version: (22.25 + 6) ÷ 1.3 = 21.73
- Recommended: (21.05 + 6) ÷ 1.3 = 20.81

**What this shows:**

- Under the board's version, the programme adds 43 points of payout. Its real in-year effect is $1.05M, but management is credited with $2.25M because the $1.2M cost never shows up.
- With restructuring counted, management still earns 20 points, but only on the net figure.
- If the board directs the programme, the bonus is neutral and the savings go into the 2028 target.

**The exit view.** Suppose restructuring of about $1.2M recurs every year. That is 6% of $20M EBITDA. A buyer's quality-of-earnings review will treat it as a run-rate cost. At an illustrative 15x multiple, $1.2M × 15 = **$18M** of enterprise value that the adjusted EBITDA overstates. Replace these figures with your actual three-year average and your entry multiple.

## Appendix C: Alternative wording if the board chooses Option A or C

**Option A (as originally asked):** "Board-approved restructuring costs are excluded from Bonus EBITDA."

**Option C (capped):** "Board-approved restructuring costs are excluded up to $[cap]. Costs above the cap are included."

**Minimum safeguards under either option:**

1. Only programmes the board approves with a written business case qualify. Nothing qualifies retrospectively at year-end.
2. Only severance and notice pay, legal and advisory fees, and outplacement are excluded. Retention bonuses, backfill, contractors, consultants and "transformation" costs stay in.
3. The 2027 target includes the full-year run-rate savings of the 2024–2026 programmes.
4. RemCo sees the reconciliation every quarter.
5. The plan text does not describe these costs as one-off or non-recurring.
