# 2027 Management Bonus Plan: Draft v0.1 for the Remuneration Committee

**Status:** Draft for discussion, prepared 21 November 2026. It is not an offer or a contractual commitment until the board approves it and participant letters go out.

## 2. Purpose

The plan rewards management for growing durable, cash-backed EBITDA in 2027, meaning the earnings a future buyer will pay for, while the product stays safe and compliant and customers stay.

## 3. Eligibility and target bonus

3.1 **Participants:** the CEO, the executive leadership team and senior leaders named by the committee. Illustration: about 35 people.

3.2 **Target bonus as a % of base salary:** CEO [ ]%, executive leadership [ ]%, senior leaders [ ]%. The CHRO benchmarks these against PE-backed software peers before the committee meeting. Indicative ranges, not yet benchmarked: CEO 50-100%, executive leadership 30-50%, senior leaders 15-25%.

3.3 **No double-dipping:** a leader on a 2027 sales commission plan either stays out of this plan or has a target reduced by [ ]%, as the committee decides.

3.4 **Joiners:** paid pro rata by days employed. Anyone joining after 30 September 2027 is in the 2027 plan only at the committee's discretion.

## 4. Performance measure: Plan EBITDA

4.1 **Starting point:** EBITDA from the audited 2027 consolidated financial statements. That is operating profit before depreciation, amortisation and impairment, under the accounting policies used in the approved 2027 budget.

4.2 **Permitted adjustments.** This list is exhaustive:
- a. Third-party transaction costs of board-approved M&A, refinancing or exit processes: adviser fees, vendor due diligence, data room. Integration costs are not adjusted.
- b. Monitoring fees and directors' fees paid to the sponsor.
- c. Non-cash share-based payment expense from the sponsor's management equity plan.
- d. Acquisitions and disposals made in 2027. When it approves the deal, the committee chooses one of two treatments: remove the acquired or sold business's EBITDA from actuals, or adjust the target by the 2027 EBITDA in the board-approved deal model.
- e. Exceptional items that meet the test in 4.4.

4.3 **Always included (never adjusted):**
- Restructuring and reorganisation costs: severance, notice pay, outplacement, retention bonuses, lease exit costs
- Consultants on cost, efficiency or transformation programmes
- Duplicate running costs, recruitment and backfills
- Ordinary-course litigation and settlements
- Costs of data-breach and security-incident response and remediation
- Customer credits, SLA penalties and bad-debt expense
- FX gains and losses

4.4 **Exceptional-item test.** The committee may approve an adjustment only if all of the following are true:
- (i) No charge of a similar nature was booked in 2025 or 2026. This mirrors the two-year test the SEC applies to public companies' non-GAAP measures ([Regulation S-K Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B)](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/accounting/sec/sec-reporting-interpretations-manual/roadmap-non-gaap-financial-measures/chapter-4-non-gaap-measures-that/4-7-performance-measure-prohibitions)).
- (ii) A similar charge is not reasonably likely in 2028 or 2029.
- (iii) The item was outside management's control, or the board directed it against management's recommendation.
- (iv) The item is above [$250K] on its own.
- (v) The CFO has documented the item and the committee has approved it in writing.

The total of exceptional adjustments is capped at [5%] of target Plan EBITDA. Anything above the cap goes to the full board with a written rationale.

4.5 **Symmetry rule:** an adjustment that removes a cost also removes the in-year benefit linked to it (savings or revenue), and the other way round.

4.6 **Accounting-policy freeze.** The effect of any change in accounting policies or estimates made in 2027 is reversed for Plan EBITDA. In particular:
- **Capitalised development:** if capitalised development cost goes above [the budgeted amount / the budgeted % of total R&D spend], the excess counts as an expense in Plan EBITDA. Capitalising more lifts EBITDA without changing cash.
- **Provision and accrual releases:** releases of provisions and accruals created before 2027 are excluded. They are earnings from earlier years.
- **Other estimates:** revenue recognition judgements and the bad-debt provisioning method stay as they are in the budget.

4.7 **Self-funding:** Plan EBITDA is measured after accruing the full cost of this plan, including employer social charges.

## 5. Restructuring allowance and target

5.1 **Target Plan EBITDA** = 2027 budget EBITDA after a restructuring allowance of [$ ]. The allowance is set at the average actual restructuring cost for 2024-2026, which the CFO confirms. Illustration: $2.0M.

5.2 **Within or over the allowance:** restructuring below the allowance raises Plan EBITDA and restructuring above it lowers Plan EBITDA. Nothing is adjusted.

5.3 **Board-approved strategic programme:** the board may approve, during 2027, a restructuring programme that is not in the budget, based on a written business case with a payback of more than 12 months. In that case the committee may, at the time of approval and not at year-end, exclude the programme's costs **and** raise the target by the in-year savings in the business case. Management is then neutral on the decision, which rests on the business case. Savings above the business case help participants, and shortfalls hurt them. No retrospective approvals.

5.4 **Participants who lose their role:** a participant whose role is eliminated in a restructuring is a good leaver (section 9).

## 6. Payout curve

| Plan EBITDA as % of target | Payout as % of target bonus |
|---|---|
| Below 92% | 0% |
| 92% | 50% |
| 96% | 75% |
| 100% | 100% |
| 105% | 125% |
| 110% or more | 150% (cap) |

Payouts are interpolated in a straight line between points. **Calibration:** if the 2027 budget is a stretch above the current forecast, 92% is a reasonable threshold. If the budget is conservative, raise the threshold to [95%].

## 7. Underpins

Underpins are conditions, not weightings. EBITDA still sets 100% of the amount, and underpins can only lower a payout.

7.1 **Compliance and patient safety (malus).** The committee assesses whether management action or inaction was a material cause if any of these happens in 2027:
- (a) a personal-data breach that has to be notified to a regulator under applicable law, such as HIPAA or GDPR
- (b) a regulatory enforcement action or sanction, or the loss of a certification the business relies on, such as ISO 27001, SOC 2 or medical-device software clearance where it applies
- (c) a serious patient-safety incident linked to the product

Management action or inaction includes under-investment in security, QA or compliance. If it was a material cause, the committee may reduce payouts for the responsible participants by up to 100%.

7.2 **Cash conversion.** Cash conversion is operating cash flow before interest and tax divided by Plan EBITDA. If it is below [80%], or below the budgeted conversion minus 10 points if that is lower, payouts are capped at 100%.

7.3 **Customer retention.** Gross revenue retention (GRR) is the share of start-of-year recurring revenue still billed at year-end, without counting upsell. If GRR is below [budget GRR minus 2 points], payouts are capped at 100%. This stops support, implementation or product quality being cut to lift EBITDA.

## 8. Calculation, payment and in-year reporting

8.1 **Pool at target** = sum of each participant's base salary × target %. Illustration: 35 × $70K average = $2.45M.

8.2 **Individual payout** = target bonus × the payout % from section 6, after underpins. There is no individual performance modifier, in line with the board's single-measure design. [Optional: a ±20% individual modifier that nets to zero across the pool, if the committee wants one.]

8.3 **Reconciliation:** the CFO prepares a reconciliation from audited EBITDA, through each adjustment (amount, rationale, approval date), to Plan EBITDA. The auditors run agreed-upon procedures on it before payment.

8.4 **Quarterly reporting:** each quarter the CFO reports to the committee:
- the forecast Plan EBITDA
- reported EBITDA alongside it
- every adjustment proposed so far

There should be no new adjustments at year-end.

8.5 **Payment:** in cash through payroll after the audited accounts are signed, no later than [31 March 2028].

---

## 9. Leavers, joiners and change of control

9.1 **Good leavers** include redundancy (restructuring included), ill health, death and retirement, plus others at the committee's discretion. They receive a pro rata bonus to their leaving date, paid at the normal time on actual performance.

9.2 **Other leavers** forfeit the bonus if they leave before the payment date.

9.3 **Exit or change of control in 2027:**
- The bonus is pro rata to completion, based on performance to the latest month-end against the phased budget, as the committee determines.
- Transaction costs are treated under 4.2a.
- The bonus is coordinated with the management equity plan so nothing is counted twice.

---

## 10. Malus and clawback

The committee may reduce an unpaid bonus (malus) or recover a paid bonus within two years of payment (clawback) if:
- Plan EBITDA was materially overstated, for example through misstatement, misclassified costs, or an adjustment later found not to meet section 4
- there was misconduct
- an underpin event from section 7 comes to light after payment

This is subject to local law. Employment counsel confirms enforceability in each participant's jurisdiction.

---

## 11. Governance

11.1 The committee interprets the plan, and its decisions are final.

11.2 After 31 March 2027 the 2027 terms change only under 4.2d or 5.3, or with full board approval.

11.3 The plan is discretionary. Pension and holiday-pay treatment follow local law, which counsel confirms.

11.4 For the 2028 plan the committee reviews whether restructuring is still recurring and resets the allowance.

## 12. If the board keeps restructuring excluded (Option A minimum safeguards)

If the board excludes restructuring as in prior years, replace section 5 with all of the following:

- a. **Narrow definition.** Only severance, notice, outplacement and lease exit costs for a programme the board approved in advance are excluded. Consultants, retention bonuses, backfills and duplicate running costs stay in.
- b. **Prospective approval.** There is no restructuring add-back unless the board approved the programme before any costs were incurred.
- c. **Cap.** Total excluded restructuring is no more than [3%] of target EBITDA.
- d. **Symmetry.** The target goes up by the in-year savings in each excluded programme's business case.
- e. **Transparency.** Every quarter the CFO shows the board reported EBITDA next to Plan EBITDA, plus the cumulative restructuring add-backs since 2024.

With all five in place, Option A works much like Option B with more paperwork. Without them, the incentive problem in section 13 stays.

## 13. Worked example (illustration only)

**Assumptions** (no company figures were supplied, so replace them with actuals):

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| 2027 revenue | $70M (350 FTE × $200K revenue per FTE) |
| Budget EBITDA before restructuring | $17.5M (25% margin) |
| Normal restructuring (2024-26 average) | $2.0M |
| Target pool | $2.45M (35 participants × $70K) |
| Extra restructuring scenario | 25 roles × $100K loaded cost = $2.5M a year in savings; one-off cost $1.2M ($1.0M severance plus $0.2M advisers and legal) |

**The two plan designs:**
- **Option A (board's version):** restructuring excluded, target $17.5M.
- **Option B (this draft):** restructuring included, target $17.5M − $2.0M allowance = $15.5M.

| Scenario | A: Adj. EBITDA, % of target, payout, pool | B: Plan EBITDA, % of target, payout, pool | Effect on company 2027 EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Budget delivered, normal $2.0M restructuring | $17.5M, 100.0%, 100%, **$2.45M** | $15.5M, 100.0%, 100%, **$2.45M** | Budget |
| 2. Extra restructuring done 31 March (9 months of savings = $1.875M) | $19.375M, 110.7%, 150%, **$3.68M** | $16.175M, 104.4%, 121.8%, **$2.98M** | +$0.675M |
| 3. Extra restructuring done 30 September (3 months of savings = $0.625M) | $18.125M, 103.6%, 117.9%, **$2.89M** | $14.925M, 96.3%, 76.8%, **$1.88M** | **−$0.575M** |
| 4. $1.0M of ordinary cost labelled "restructuring" | $18.5M, 105.7%, 128.6%, **$3.15M** | $15.5M, 100.0%, 100%, **$2.45M** | None (a relabel) |
| 5. Restructuring overruns the allowance by $1.0M | $17.5M, 100.0%, 100%, **$2.45M** | $14.5M, 93.5%, 59.7%, **$1.46M** | −$1.0M |

**How to read it:**
- **Scenario 1:** in the base case both designs pay the same, so Option B does not cut management's expected bonus.
- **Scenario 2:** a restructuring that genuinely pays back in-year raises the pool in both designs. Option A also pays for the $1.2M cost management never bears: the pool rises $1.225M under A against $0.53M under B.
- **Scenario 3:** a late-year restructuring cuts company EBITDA by $0.575M in 2027. Under Option A it **raises** the bonus pool by $0.44M, and under Option B it lowers the pool by $0.57M. A restructuring like this may still be right on a multi-year view. Section 5.3 covers that case, with the decision made by the board on a business case rather than driven by the bonus.
- **Scenarios 4 and 5:** under Option A, labelling costs as restructuring and overspending on restructuring cost management nothing.

**Valuation effect:** a recurring $2.0M add-back at a 10-15x EBITDA multiple is $20-30M of enterprise value ($24M at 12x) that a buyer's quality-of-earnings review could strike out. Transaction advisers already ask whether a company that restructures every year can call those costs exceptional ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.nl/en/insights-en/advisory/adjusted-ebitda-the-seller-vs.-buyer-tug-of-war)).

**Simplification:** to keep the table readable, the example ignores the fact that above-target bonus accruals themselves reduce Plan EBITDA (section 4.7). Including that effect slightly lowers the above-target payouts in both designs and does not change the comparison.

## 14. Known limitations

- **Baseline quality.** The allowance is only as honest as the 2024-26 baseline and the 2027 budget. A padded budget makes any plan easy.
- **Root cause.** The plan does not fix why restructuring keeps recurring. If the budget can only be hit by cutting, even Option B rewards cutting. The root-cause review that comes before target-setting deals with this.
- **Judgement in the underpins.** The compliance underpin needs the committee to judge cause. The triggers are defined so that judgement is limited to that one question.
