# CFO: 2027 management bonus plan

## Bottom line

I've drafted the plan at 100% on EBITDA, as the board asked, with one change: restructuring costs count against EBITDA instead of being excluded. You've restructured in each of the last three years, so those costs are recurring, not one-off. If the plan excludes them, management earns more bonus for cutting jobs, even when the cut loses the company money in 2027. Confidence: high on the principle; the figures below are illustrative.

## What

- **Restructuring isn't one-off here.** [Verified: you told me it has happened three years running.] For public companies, the SEC does not allow an adjusted measure to strip out a charge labelled non-recurring if a similar charge occurred in the prior two years ([Reg S-K Item 10(e)(1)(ii)(B), via Deloitte](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/accounting/sec/sec-reporting-interpretations-manual/roadmap-non-gaap-financial-measures/chapter-4-non-gaap-measures-that/4-7-performance-measure-prohibitions)). That rule doesn't bind a private company, but buyers, lenders and auditors will measure you against it. [High]
- **The exclusion makes restructuring free for the people who decide on it.** In the worked example, a late-year restructuring costs $1.2M and saves only $0.6M in 2027, so company EBITDA falls by $0.575M. Under the board's version the bonus pool still rises by $0.44M; with costs included, the pool falls by $0.57M. Relabelling $1.0M of ordinary cost as "restructuring" would add $0.7M to the pool. [Math verified; inputs assumed]
- **It inflates the number the sponsor will sell.** If the recurring add-back is about $2M a year, then at 10-15x EBITDA that is $20-30M of enterprise value a buyer's quality-of-earnings review could strike out. (That review is the buyer's accountants testing which EBITDA adjustments are real.) Advisers already ask whether a company that restructures every year can call the cost exceptional ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.nl/en/insights-en/advisory/adjusted-ebitda-the-seller-vs.-buyer-tug-of-war)). [Medium; amounts assumed]
- **The fix keeps the 100% weighting.** Set the 2027 target after a restructuring allowance equal to your 2024-26 average. When management delivers the budget, both versions pay exactly 100%. Overspending the allowance costs management money, and relabelling costs gets them nothing. A strategic programme the board approves can still be excluded, but only if its in-year savings are taken out too. [Recommendation]
- **Healthcare software needs guardrails a pure EBITDA plan lacks.** On its own, an EBITDA plan rewards cuts EBITDA can't see: security, compliance, QA, support, and capitalising more development cost. The draft adds an accounting-policy freeze and three underpins. Underpins are conditions that can only lower a payout, never weightings. The three are a compliance and patient-safety malus (a power to reduce bonuses), a cash-conversion floor and a customer-retention floor. [Medium]

## Why this matters

As written, the board's plan would pay cash bonuses on EBITDA your next buyer is unlikely to pay for. It would also give management a reason to run a fourth restructuring in 2027. Each repeat of that cycle costs you staff, product knowledge and customer trust, in a regulated market where all three are hard to rebuild.

## How to act

1. Pull restructuring costs for 2024-2026 and every add-back paid out under past bonus plans, then set the 2027 allowance at the three-year average. **Owner:** CFO. **By:** Friday 4 December 2026.
2. Check the EBITDA definition and the caps on add-backs in your credit agreement. The bonus definition should be no looser than your lenders'. **Owner:** CFO with General Counsel. **By:** 4 December 2026.
3. Find out why restructuring keeps recurring before the 2027 target is locked. Is the budget set above what the cost base can deliver, or do costs regrow after each cut? **Owners:** CEO, CFO and CHRO. **By:** Friday 11 December 2026.
4. Take the attached draft to the remuneration committee with Options A and B below, with target-bonus percentages and leaver terms benchmarked. **Owner:** CHRO. **By:** the committee meeting before the 2027 budget is signed off, no later than Friday 18 December 2026.
5. Have employment counsel review the malus, clawback and leaver clauses for each participant's jurisdiction, then send participant letters. **Owners:** General Counsel and CHRO. **By:** Friday 29 January 2027.

## Risks

- **The board may read this as management protecting its bonus.** In the base case both versions pay the same 100%. They differ only when restructuring goes beyond budget or costs are relabelled. Open with that table (section 13 of the draft).
- **Vague underpins become a negotiation every year.** The draft defines each trigger, and the committee only judges whether management caused it.
- **Bonuses in a year of rewarded job cuts will land badly.** If the plan rewards a cut and management are then paid, expect it to hurt trust with the employees who remain. It will also come up in exit diligence, where attrition gets examined.
- **Neither version fixes a budget that can only be hit by cutting.** That is what action 3 is for.

## Your decision

- **Option A: the board's version as written.** 100% adjusted EBITDA with restructuring excluded. It's simple and familiar. But it pays for cuts that cost management nothing, and it keeps a disputed add-back in the equity story. If you choose it, adopt all five minimum safeguards in section 12 of the draft: prior approval, a cap, symmetry, a narrow definition and quarterly transparency.
- **Option B: this draft.** 100% Plan EBITDA, with restructuring counted against an allowance, plus the underpins. Same weighting, and the same payout when the budget is delivered, without the incentive to cut. The cost is one more definition to agree.
- **Recommendation: Option B.** The board gets exactly the single-measure plan it asked for, measured on EBITDA a buyer will accept.
