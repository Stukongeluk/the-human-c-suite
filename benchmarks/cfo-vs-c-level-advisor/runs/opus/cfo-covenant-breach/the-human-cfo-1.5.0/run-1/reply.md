Don't announce the cuts before the bank meeting. You're 0.1x over the limit, which is about 3% of net debt or 3% of LTM EBITDA. A lawful 10% cut saves at most a month or two before the 30 June test and costs about €0.56m up front, so it leaves you at 3.6x if the bank lets you add back the costs, and at about 4.2x if it doesn't. Close the gap with cash and working capital, go to the bank before the review with a plan and a request for headroom, and put the family's contribution on the table first.

The figures are illustrative (€4.0m LTM EBITDA, €14.4m net debt), so swap in your own. The ratios scale. The full plan, model and bank-pack outline are in **bank-review-plan.md**.

**Why the cut doesn't fix June**

- **Timing.** 26 people is over the collective-dismissal threshold of 20: notify UWV and the unions (WMCO), and ask the works council for advice (art. 25 WOR) while it can still change the decision. Then the UWV procedure or settlement agreements, then one to four months' notice. People leave around June, so the 30 June LTM figure sees about €0.13m of a €1.5m annual saving.
- **The cost comes first.** Transition payments, advisers and outplacement come to about €0.56m (estimate, assuming 12 years' average service). Once announced, your auditor will probably want a restructuring provision, which hits test-period EBITDA unless the agreement allows an add-back. In cash, the cut is €0.38m negative at 30 June.
- **Announcing first is a legal risk.** A decision announced before the works council has advised can be challenged at the Enterprise Chamber. The bank will read that as a governance risk, not as resolve.
- **The wrong people leave.** The first to go after an announcement are the joiners, upholsterers and CNC operators who can find work tomorrow. One study found a 1% downsizing went with 31% more voluntary quits the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008).

| Option | Ratio | Net debt vs forecast | Dismissals |

If EBITDA comes in 7.5% lower, it's 3.95x doing nothing and 3.53x with the plan. That's why you also need the bank.

**The next three weeks**

1. **Talk to the owner privately first.** They're right that the bank wants to see you're serious. The cut just doesn't show it in the ratio. A line you could use: *"I want the bank to see we're serious too. The cut doesn't get us under 3.5 by June, it costs half a million first, and announcing it before the works council advises could get it blocked. Let's show them a plan that does get us under, with the family's commitment behind it."*
2. **Call the relationship manager this week** and flag the forecast yourself. Breaches the bank hears about late are how firms end up in special asset management (bijzonder beheer).
3. **Read the facility agreement with your finance lawyer:** the EBITDA and net debt definitions (add-backs, caps, cash netting), the test basis and dates, and whether there's an equity cure.
4. **Build the 13-week cash forecast and a covenant model per test date,** base and downside, with each lever's owner and landing week.
5. **Levers, counting only the low end** (estimates; 3.22x is after the plan's own fees and a stock write-down):
   - Inventory down 7.5–12.5%: €0.6–1.0m less net debt
   - Receivables collected 3–5 days faster: €0.37–0.62m
   - Non-safety, non-maintenance capex moved past June: €0.3–0.6m
   - Energy, logistics, services and material terms: +€0.10–0.25m EBITDA
   - Price up 1–2% on new orders from February: +€0.05–0.15m
   - Hiring freeze with exceptions, and agency hours matched to demand with agreed periods honoured: +€0.05–0.15m
   - Not on the list: paying small suppliers late or dressing up the 30 June balance sheet.
6. **Owners and leaders first:** no dividend until leverage is below about 3.0x, 2026 management bonuses deferred, and a family standby commitment sized to the downside (about €0.1m holds 3.5x, about €1.2m holds 3.2x).
7. **Ask the bank for headroom:** ideally a reset to about 4.0x for the 2027 tests, stepping back down, or else a one-off waiver. Offer monthly reporting, milestones and the dividend lock-up in return, and say plainly that the inventory release and capex deferral are one-offs: they buy June, and later tests depend on EBITDA. Expect a fee and perhaps a margin step-up (estimate: €75k plus €35–70k a year), well below the cut's €0.56m.
8. **Tell the works council about the financial position now,** confidentially if necessary. Any later structural step goes far faster with people who saw the numbers early.
9. **Agree a dated trigger:** if January–February actuals track the downside, or order intake runs 10% below plan for three months, open a structural review with the works council in March, voluntary routes first. A trigger, a date and an owner are what "serious" looks like to a bank.

**Two questions that would change this**

- Is the shortfall a temporary dip in orders, or structural (a lost customer, lasting overcapacity)? If structural, a smaller, properly consulted restructuring may still be needed, for 2028 rather than as a signal for June.
- What does the agreement allow in add-backs and equity cures? That decides whether the downside is a negotiation or a cheque from the family.

Key assumptions: the illustrative balance sheet, exits no earlier than May, and a bank that values a credible plan over a headline. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
