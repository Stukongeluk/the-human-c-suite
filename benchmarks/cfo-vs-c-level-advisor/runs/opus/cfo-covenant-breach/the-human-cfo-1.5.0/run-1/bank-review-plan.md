# Leverage covenant: plan for the bank review (draft)

**Recommendation:** don't announce job cuts before the bank meeting. Close the 0.1x gap with working capital, capex timing, spend and pricing; go to the bank before the review with that plan, the family's contribution and a request for headroom; and agree a dated trigger for a structural review if the first-quarter numbers track the downside. On the illustrative figures below, the plan takes the 30 June 2027 test from 3.60x to about 3.22x. A 10% cut, run lawfully, leaves it at 3.60x, or 4.16x if the bank won't let you add back the restructuring costs.

Status: draft for the CFO and the owner, 21 November 2026. **Every figure is an illustrative estimate for a Dutch furniture manufacturer with 260 employees.** Replace them with your ledger, ERP and payroll figures before anything goes to the bank. The ratios scale, so the conclusions hold unless your mix is very different.

---

## 1. The arithmetic

Assumed, base case at the 30 June 2027 test: LTM EBITDA €4.0m, net debt €14.4m, so 3.60x against a limit of 3.50x. Downside: EBITDA 7.5% lower (€3.7m), net debt €0.2m higher (€14.6m), so 3.95x.

| To reach | Lower net debt by | or raise LTM EBITDA by |
|---|---|---|
| 3.50x (the limit) | 2.8% (€0.40m) | 2.9% (€0.11m) |
| 3.20x (about 9% headroom) | 11.1% (€1.60m) | 12.5% (€0.50m) |

Rule of thumb at these figures: every €100k of net debt is 0.025x, and every €100k of EBITDA is about 0.09x.

Check in the facility agreement: how EBITDA is defined (add-backs for exceptional or restructuring costs, and any cap), how net debt is defined (cash netting, leases, shareholder loans), whether the test is LTM, the test dates, and whether there is an equity cure and how often it may be used.

## 2. Options at the 30 June 2027 test

Same basis for every option. "Add-back" means the bank lets restructuring costs be excluded from covenant EBITDA.

| Option | Base case | Downside | Net debt vs forecast at 30 June | Dismissals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | 3.60x | 3.95x | 0 | 0 |
| 10% cut alone | 3.60x with add-back, 4.16x without | 3.93x with, 4.60x without | +€0.43m | 26 |
| Plan in section 4, low end | 3.22x | 3.53x | −€1.16m | 0 |
| Plan in section 4, high end | 2.76x | 3.00x | −€2.11m | 0 |
| Plan (low end) plus the cut | 3.23x with add-back, 3.72x without | 3.52x with, 4.11x without | −€0.73m | 26 |

Even on the most optimistic dismissal timetable (everyone out by 1 May) and with full add-back, the cut alone reaches 3.46x: about what a 7% inventory reduction does, without the €0.56m of one-off costs.

**Do nothing** isn't free either: a reported breach usually means a waiver fee, a higher margin, tighter terms and a real chance of being moved to the bank's special asset management (bijzonder beheer), where you lose control of the timetable.

## 3. The 10% cut, net and by quarter

26 roles (above the threshold of 20 in one UWV work area within three months, so the collective dismissal rules apply if they are at one site).

Realistic timetable, if you start properly now: works council advice request in December (art. 25 WOR, at a point where the advice can still change the decision); WMCO notification to UWV and the unions, with a one-month wait before UWV handles dismissal requests unless the unions confirm they were consulted; UWV procedure or settlement agreements; then notice of one to four months by length of service, minus the time the UWV procedure took (at least one month remains). Average exit about 1 June 2027. Announcing a decision before the works council has advised invites an appeal to the Enterprise Chamber, which can order it reversed: that delays everything further.

Assumptions: fully loaded cost €58k per FTE; average monthly salary for the transition payment €3,600; average service 12 years, so a transition payment of 4 months' salary each (one-third of a month per year of service); legal and advisers €80k; outplacement and transition support €4k per person; 4 roles refilled within 18 months if demand recovers (recruiting and ramp-up at 1x salary of €45k, the low half of Gallup's half-to-two-times range); extra voluntary leavers costed at €0.2m over 18 months. One study found a 1% downsizing went with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, *Academy of Management Journal*, 2008). I have not extrapolated that to 10%; the estimate here is deliberately modest.

What this shows:
- **The cut costs cash before the test date** (−€0.38m cumulative at 30 June) and only pays back in Q4 2027.
- **The cost lands in the test period.** Once the plan is announced, your auditor will probably require a restructuring provision (check with them), so the €0.56m hits LTM EBITDA at 30 June unless the agreement allows an add-back.
- **Break-even:** the table assumes the work goes away with the people. If you have to buy back about half of those hours as overtime or agency work, the first full year after the exits nets to roughly zero.
- Not in the table: lost know-how, delivery reliability and the firm's name as an employer in the region, in a tight market for joiners, upholsterers and CNC operators.

## 4. The plan: what closes the gap by 30 June

Low end counts toward the plan; high end is upside. The leverage effect of each lever is shown separately at base-case figures; the combined effect is computed together in section 2.

| Lever | Effect by 30 June (estimate) | Leverage effect | Owner | Confidence | Human impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inventory down 7.5–12.5% of an assumed €8.0m: slow movers, raw materials bought to plan, finished goods to order | −€0.60m to −€1.00m net debt | −0.15x to −0.25x | Operations and purchasing | Medium | None; protect safety stock and delivery times |
| Receivables 3–5 days faster (from 45 days on €45m revenue): invoice on time, settle disputes, enforce terms | −€0.37m to −€0.62m | −0.09x to −0.15x | Finance and sales | Medium-high | None |
| Capex timing: move non-safety, non-maintenance, non-quality projects past 30 June | −€0.30m to −€0.60m | −0.08x to −0.15x | CFO and operations | High | None now; it's a deferral, the cash goes out in H2 |
| Spend and purchasing: energy, logistics, external services, material terms | +€0.10m to +€0.25m LTM EBITDA | −0.09x to −0.21x | Purchasing | Medium | Low; renegotiate before cancelling |
| Price 1–2% on new orders from February, where value and the market allow | +€0.05m to +€0.15m | −0.04x to −0.13x | Sales | Low-medium | Volume risk; watch order intake weekly |
| Hiring freeze with exceptions for critical roles; agency and temporary hours matched to demand, agreed periods honoured | +€0.05m to +€0.15m | −0.04x to −0.13x | HR and line managers | Medium | Agency workers have the thinnest buffer: give notice and consider them for vacancies |
| Plan's own costs: fees, advisers, a possible margin step-up, stock clearance write-down | +€0.11m net debt, −€0.09m EBITDA | — | CFO | — | — |
| **Plan total (low end)** | **−€1.16m net debt, +€0.11m EBITDA** | **3.60x → 3.22x** | | | **No dismissals** |

Not in the plan: stretching payments to small suppliers or window-dressing the balance sheet at 30 June. The bank sees it, and it only moves the problem to 1 July and onto people with less room than you.

**Be honest with the bank about what recurs.** The inventory release and the capex deferral are one-off; they buy time for June, they don't fix EBITDA. The later 2027 test dates depend on the EBITDA levers sticking and on understanding why EBITDA fell. That is what the trigger in section 7 is for.

## 5. Owners and leaders first

- No dividend or other distribution on the 2026 result until leverage is back below about 3.0x. Every €100k not paid out is 0.025x.
- Management bonuses for 2026 deferred until the covenant is back below 3.25x; owner-director fees reduced for the same period. Small in euros, large in what it tells the bank and the workforce.
- A standby commitment from the family, sized to the downside: about €0.1m holds 3.5x in the downside with the plan at its low end, and about €1.2m holds 3.2x. Equity, or a subordinated shareholder loan if the agreement counts it as equity; check with your finance lawyer.

## 6. What to ask the bank

- **The ask:** reset the leverage covenant to about 4.0x for the 2027 test dates, stepping back to 3.5x by mid-2028; or, second best, a one-off waiver of the 30 June 2027 test.
- **What you offer:** monthly reporting with the 13-week cash forecast; the lever plan with milestones; the dividend lock-up; the family's standby commitment; the contingency trigger.
- **What it costs (estimate; check with the bank):** an amendment fee and advisers of about €75k, and possibly a margin step-up of 0.25–0.5% while leverage is above 3.5x (about €35–70k a year on €14m). Compare that with the cut's €0.56m of one-off costs.

## 7. Contingency trigger

Agree it now and put it in the bank pack. Suggested: if January–February actuals put LTM EBITDA on track for €3.8m or less, or order intake runs more than 10% below plan for three months, start a structural review in March:

- Share the numbers with the works council at the problem stage, while it can still change the plan.
- Look at capacity, product range, shifts and sites before roles.
- If the dip is temporary: reduced hours and training rather than exits. The Dutch short-time work scheme (werktijdverkorting) currently covers only calamities; check whether the crisis scheme bill (Wet personeelsbehoud bij crisis) has passed.
- If roles must go: voluntary routes first (early retirement, voluntary exit), then one properly consulted round with a decent package and transition support from the day of notice. Savings from that would land from Q4 2027, which is why the trigger has to be early.

Brief the leadership team: no cuts announced, one consistent message. |
| 8–12 Dec | Bank pack. Dry run with the owner, including a pre-mortem: "the bank said no; why?" The meeting. |

**Bank pack (about eight pages):** one-page summary (where we are, why, what we are doing, what we ask); drivers of the shortfall (volume, margin, cost, working capital); 13-week cash forecast, base and downside; covenant model per test date; lever plan with owners, dates and confidence; owners' and leaders' contribution; the request and what we offer; contingency triggers.

**If staff ask in the meantime:** "We're working on a plan with the bank. No decisions about jobs have been made, and the works council is involved." Don't promise "no job losses, ever" unless it's true.

## 10. Assumptions, and what would change the recommendation

- **Assumed:** a calendar financial year with the test on 30 June 2027; one site; the illustrative balance sheet above; a works council in place (mandatory at your size); a bank that values a credible plan over a headline.
- **What would change it:** if the shortfall is structural (a lost key customer, lasting overcapacity), a smaller, properly consulted restructuring may still be needed: for 2028, decided with the works council, not announced for the bank. If the agreement already allows generous add-backs and an equity cure, the downside becomes a cheque from the family rather than a negotiation. If working capital is already lean, the gap moves to EBITDA and the bank reset becomes the main lever.

This is not legal, tax or financing advice. Run the WOR and WMCO steps past employment counsel, the covenant terms past your finance lawyer, and the provisioning question past your auditor.
