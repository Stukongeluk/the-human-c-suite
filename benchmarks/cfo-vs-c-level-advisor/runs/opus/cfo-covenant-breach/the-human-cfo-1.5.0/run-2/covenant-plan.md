# Q2 2027 leverage covenant: closing the gap without announcing job cuts

*Draft for the owner and management team, and the basis for the bank pack. Every figure below is an illustrative estimate scaled to a 260-person furniture maker. Replace each one with ledger, ERP, payroll (in aggregate) and loan-agreement data before anything goes to the bank.*

## 1. Recommendation

Don't announce job cuts before the bank review. Close the 0.1x gap by 30 June with working capital, capex timing, pricing, spend, a hiring freeze and contributions from the owner and management. Ask the bank for a covenant reset as a backstop against the downside, and put a trigger-based capacity plan in the pack instead of an announcement.

For the owner, the long-term case is the firm's name in the region and the next generation. Family firms that plan for the next generation borrow less, avoid layoffs and hold up better in downturns (Kachaner, Stalk & Bloch, 2012). Showing the bank that the family's own money goes first fits that better than a cut announced to make a point.

## 2. The size of the gap

Leverage = net debt / EBITDA. Check the definitions in the facility agreement: usually EBITDA over the last twelve months and net debt on the test date, with specific add-backs.

Rules of thumb that hold at any size:
- **3.6x to 3.5x:** EBITDA up 2.9%, or net debt down by 10% of annual EBITDA.
- **3.6x to 3.3x** (headroom worth having): EBITDA up 9.1%, or net debt down by 30% of annual EBITDA.

Illustration (estimate: EBITDA €4.0m, so net debt €14.4m at 3.6x):

| Target leverage | EBITDA increase needed (net debt unchanged) | Net debt reduction needed (EBITDA unchanged) |
|---|---|---|
| 3.5x | +€114k | −€400k |
| 3.3x | +€364k | −€1.2m |
| 3.0x | +€800k | −€2.4m |

How sensitive the forecast is: EBITDA 3% higher gives 3.50x. EBITDA 3% lower gives 3.71x, 5% lower gives 3.79x and 10% lower gives 4.00x. A 0.1x breach is within normal forecast error, so the bank will judge the downside, not the point estimate.

The working-capital levers in C are available whether or not you cut, so they are no argument for the cut. B has to stand on its own numbers, and before 30 June it doesn't.

EBITDA gains count twice: they raise EBITDA and, as cash, lower net debt (pre-tax simplification).

Basis for each estimate:
- **Receivables:** revenue of about €45m (estimate, about €170k per employee), so one day of receivables is about €123k.
- **Inventory:** about €6.5m (estimate, about 14% of revenue across raw materials, work in progress and finished goods).
- **Capex:** only growth capex that can wait.
- **Pricing:** 1% on new orders from February, allowing for the order-to-delivery lag and some lost volume.
- **Spend:** 1–2% of non-payroll spend renegotiated over five months. Renegotiate before cancelling.
- **Hiring freeze:** natural attrition of 5–8% a year means 6–10 leavers by June. About half are left unfilled, with exceptions for safety and scarce skills.
- **Bonus:** management bonus accrual for the first half of 2027.

Not counted, all upside: any dividend or owner distribution planned before 30 June (stopping it lowers net debt euro for euro), and an owner equity contribution or equity cure if the agreement allows one.

Rules for the plan: the working-capital gains must be real and sustained, not 30 June window dressing, because the bank sees July too. Don't pay small suppliers later to fund it.

## 5. Option B costed: a 10% cut of 26 FTE

**Assumptions (estimates):**
- Fully loaded cost of €55k per FTE (range €50–62k), so a gross saving of €1.43m a year.
- Transition payment: one third of a monthly salary (€3.6k including holiday allowance) per year of service, at an average of 10 years. That is €12k per person and €312k in total.
- Legal, advisory and the UWV and union process: €75k.
- Outplacement and transition support: €2.5k per person, €65k in total.
- Total one-off cost: €452k.

**Realistic timeline (verify with employment counsel):**

| Step | Timing |
|---|---|
| Advice request to the works council; WMCO notification to UWV and unions | Mid-December 2026 |
| Works council advice (typically 4–8 weeks) | Late January–February 2027 |
| WMCO waiting period: one month after notification, unless the unions declare they have been consulted and agree | In parallel |
| UWV permit procedure, or settlement agreements | February–April 2027 |
| Notice of one to four months by length of service, less the procedure time, at least one month | April–July 2027 |
| **Exits** | **May–July 2027; central case 1 June** |

**Effect at the 30 June test:**

| Exit date | Saving before 30 June | Net debt change at 30 June | Leverage, costs added back | Leverage, costs not added back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 May (best case) | €238k | +€214k | 3.45x | 3.86x |
| 1 June (central) | €119k | +€333k | 3.58x | 4.02x |
| 1 July | €0 | +€75k | 3.62x | 4.08x |
| 1 June, plus a €200k social plan top-up | €119k | +€533k | 3.63x | 4.31x |

"Not added back" assumes the restructuring provision is booked when the plan is announced. Under both Dutch GAAP (RJ 252) and IFRS (IAS 37), announcing a detailed plan to those affected generally creates the obligation, inside the test period. In the 1 July row the transition payments fall after the test, but the provision is already in EBITDA. Confirm with the auditor.

**Hidden costs not in these numbers:**
- Work that still has to be done (overtime, agency staff).
- Orders lost if delivery slips.
- Extra voluntary turnover: a 1% downsizing has been associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008).
- Rehiring scarce trades when demand returns: Gallup estimates one-half to two times annual salary per replacement.

**Later:** on twelve months to 31 December 2027, with costs added back and a fifth of the saving lost to hidden costs, B would bring leverage to about 3.0x, if the drop in volume is permanent. So the real question for B is whether the drop is structural. If it is, plan the capacity change properly, with the works council, and time it to the trading rather than to this meeting. Natural attrition of 5–8% a year (13–21 people) reaches 26 fewer people in about 15–24 months if most leavers aren't replaced.

## 6. What goes to the bank

1. **Own the forecast first.** If the bank was last told you would stay inside the covenant, say so and explain what changed.
2. **The diagnosis:** what moved leverage to 3.6x (lower EBITDA from orders or margin, or higher debt from capex, inventory or a distribution), the order book trend and the margin bridge.
3. **The 13-week cash forecast,** base and downside, and a monthly bridge to 30 June and the next two test dates.
4. **The plan:** section 4, each lever with its owner, date and confidence.
5. **What the owners and leaders contribute:** a dividend lock-up until leverage is below about 3.0x, management's 2027 bonus given up, and any equity contribution.
6. **The ask:** a reset of the Q2 and Q3 2027 tests to, say, 4.0x, back to 3.5x from Q4 2027, or a one-off waiver for Q2. In return: monthly reporting and the rolling forecast. Cost estimate on a facility of about €15m: an amendment fee of 0.10–0.25% (€15–38k), plus a margin step-up of 0.25–0.50% for six months (€19–38k), plus the bank's legal costs. Terms vary widely, so ask.
7. **Triggers and the actions they set off:**

| Trigger (reviewed monthly) | Action |
|---|---|
| The forecast shows leverage above 3.4x at 30 June even with the plan | A second round of working-capital and spend actions; talk to the owner about a contribution |
| Order intake more than 10% below plan two months running | Open a works council consultation on capacity: extend the freeze, voluntary hours reductions, a voluntary leavers scheme |
| The downside persists into Q2 with no recovery in the order book | Put a capacity proposal to the works council: done once, with a social plan, and support funded from the day of notice |

## 8. Legal and governance checks (not legal advice; confirm with counsel and the auditor)

- **Facility agreement:**
  - The EBITDA and net debt definitions, and the add-backs allowed.
  - Any equity cure.
  - Information undertakings: you may already have to notify the bank of a foreseeable breach.
  - Cross-default, and the consequences of a breach.
- **Works Councils Act (WOR):**
  - The works council has an advisory right on a reorganisation or a significant reduction in activity (art. 25).
  - Advice must be requested while it can still materially influence the decision.
  - The works council can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (art. 26).
  - The works council is bound by confidentiality on information shared in confidence.
- **Collective Dismissal Notification Act (WMCO):**
  - Applies to 20 or more dismissals in one UWV work area within three months.
  - Notify UWV and the unions before the decision is final.
  - Settlement agreements the employer initiates count towards the 20.
- **Dismissal for economic reasons:**
  - A UWV permit is needed.
  - The reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) applies by age group within interchangeable roles, so the company can't choose who goes.
  - Transition payments are due, and the notice periods above apply.
- **Collective agreement:** check for reorganisation and social plan clauses.
- **Short-time work:** the current Dutch scheme (werktijdverkorting) covers only calamities outside normal business risk. Check whether the broader crisis scheme bill (Wet personeelsbehoud bij crisis) has passed before relying on reduced hours with public support.
- **Auditor:** the timing of any restructuring provision, and how working-capital actions are presented.

## 9. Assumptions, and what would change this

**Assumptions (all estimates):**
- Revenue €45m and EBITDA €4.0m.
- Net debt €14.4m and a facility of about €15m.
- Inventory €6.5m.
- 26 FTE at €55k fully loaded, with an average of 10 years' service.
- Exits around 1 June.

**What would change the recommendation:**
- **Working capital and capex are already squeezed in the forecast.** The plan is then smaller, and the reset matters more.
- **The drop in volume is structural,** for example a major customer lost. Capacity then has to come down, through a works council process started soon, but still not announced as a decision before the bank meeting.
- **The facility agreement already requires notice of a foreseeable breach.** Then tell the bank now.
- **The bank says it wants headcount action.** Ask which measure it needs; it is almost always leverage and cash. Then show it the table in section 5.
