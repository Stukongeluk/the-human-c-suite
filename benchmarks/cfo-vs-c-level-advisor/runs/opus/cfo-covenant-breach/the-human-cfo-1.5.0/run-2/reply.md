**Don't announce the cuts before the bank meeting.** They won't fix the Q2 test, and they make the meeting harder. The gap is small: getting from 3.6x to 3.5x takes about 3% more EBITDA, or net debt lower by about 10% of one year's EBITDA (around €0.4m if your EBITDA is near €4m). Working capital, capex timing, spend and pricing can close that by 30 June without dismissals. A covenant reset is your backstop if trading gets worse.

**Why the cuts don't solve this one**

- **They land too late.** 26 dismissals at one site fall under the collective dismissal rules (WMCO): notify UWV and the unions, consult, wait at least a month, get the UWV permit or settlement agreements, then give one to four months' notice. Realistically people leave between May and July 2027. My estimate: €0–240k saved before 30 June against about €450k of one-off costs (transition payments, legal, outplacement), more with a social plan. At the test date that leaves you at about 3.6x if the loan agreement allows restructuring add-backs, and about 4.0x if it doesn't, because announcing a plan generally books the restructuring provision at once (ask your auditor).
- **Announcing it as decided breaches the works council's advice right.** Its advice must be sought while it can still change the decision (art. 25 WOR), and a cut announced as already decided can be challenged at the Enterprise Chamber, which can order it reversed.
- **It costs you the people you need.** Your best joiners, upholsterers and machine operators find new work fastest. A 1% downsizing has been linked to 31% more voluntary turnover the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008).

The owner is right that the bank has to see you're serious. To a bank, serious means coming early, owning the numbers, a plan that lands by June and the owner's own money going first, not a works council dispute added to the file.

**The next three weeks**

1. **This week, a private conversation with the owner,** before anything is said internally. A line you could use: *"I agree the bank has to see we're serious. But a cut announced now saves almost nothing before June, the transition payments come first, and announcing it before the works council has advised puts the whole decision at risk. Give me two weeks to bring you a plan that closes the gap by June, with our own money going first. If it can't, we'll know why, and we'll do the harder thing properly."*
2. **Read the facility agreement with counsel:** the EBITDA and net debt definitions, allowed add-backs, any equity cure, and whether you must already notify the bank of a foreseeable breach. Then call your relationship manager before the review. No surprises.
3. **Build the bridge to 30 June,** with a 13-week cash forecast underneath, base and downside. Illustrative sizes for a company your size (estimates; replace with your figures):

| Lever | Effect by 30 June |
|---|---|
| Receivables 2–3 days faster (large customers, disputes, invoicing on time) | €250–370k |
| Inventory 3–5% lower (slow movers, buying to orders) | €200–330k |
| Growth capex moved past June (maintenance and safety protected) | €150–300k |
| Price or surcharge on new orders from February, net of lost volume | €50–120k |
| Purchasing, energy, freight, consultants | €40–80k |
| Hiring freeze with exceptions; overtime and agency hours matched to demand | €40–80k |
| Management's 2027 bonus stopped | €30–60k |

Net of the plan's own costs, that gets you to about 3.3x (low case) or 3.05x (central). Make the working-capital gains real, not 30 June window dressing: the bank sees July too. And don't fund it by paying small suppliers later.

4. **Owners and leaders first.** Stop any dividend or owner distribution until leverage is back below about 3.0x, and tell the bank. An owner contribution or equity cure, if the agreement allows one, buys headroom euro for euro. Management, you included, gives up its 2027 bonus.
5. **Ask for a reset as the backstop.** If EBITDA comes in 10% below forecast, even the plan leaves you at about 3.7x. Ask for, say, 4.0x for the Q2 and Q3 tests in exchange for monthly reporting, the 13-week forecast and a dividend lock-up. I'd estimate €35–75k in fees and margin on a facility of about €15m, a fraction of the restructuring cost.
6. **Brief the works council now,** in confidence. They'll hear about it anyway, and bringing them in early is what makes a harder step credible later.
7. **Put a trigger in the pack, not an announcement.** For example: if order intake stays more than 10% below plan for two months, you open a works council consultation on capacity, starting with a longer freeze, voluntary hours reductions and voluntary leavers. The bank sees the owner's resolve without an irreversible step. With natural attrition of 5–8% a year (13–21 people), not refilling most leavers gets you close to 10% fewer people in 15–24 months.
