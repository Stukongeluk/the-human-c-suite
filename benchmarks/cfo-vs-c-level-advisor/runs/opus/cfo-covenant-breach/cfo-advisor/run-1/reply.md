**CFO: Q2 leverage covenant and the proposed 10% job cuts**

**BOTTOM LINE**
Don't announce the cuts before the bank meeting. They do almost nothing for the Q2 test and could make it worse. Announcing them before your works council has given its advice breaks Dutch law. And the bank will be persuaded far more by a costed covenant plan, a request for headroom and the owner visibly sharing the burden.
Confidence: high on the direction. Medium on the figures, which are illustrative until you put your actuals in.

**WHAT**
- **The gap is small.** To get from 3.6x to 3.5x, LTM EBITDA has to rise 2.9% or net debt has to fall 2.8% [VERIFIED: your figures]. On an illustrative €5.0m EBITDA and €18.0m net debt, that is +€0.14m EBITDA or −€0.5m net debt [ASSUMED]. If Q2 means June 2027, five of the twelve months in the test window (July to November) are already booked. Only December to June can still move.
- **Cuts barely reach the Q2 test.** Dismissing 20 or more people within three months in one work area is a collective dismissal. UWV and the unions must be notified in advance, the unions consulted and the works council (OR) asked for advice. Then come the UWV procedure and notice periods, so exits land around Q2 at best. In the best case you reach about 3.48x, with no headroom. If your facility agreement doesn't add back restructuring costs, the provision falls in the test period and you end up at 3.8–4.0x [ASSUMED: €55k loaded cost per FTE, about €0.45m one-off costs; VERIFIED: the process steps].
- **Announcing first is a legal problem, not just a tactical one.** At 260 staff you must have an OR. A reorganisation that changes staffing needs the OR's advice while that advice can still influence the decision. If you skip that, the OR can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer), which can order the decision withdrawn. UWV has also treated dismissal-permit applications filed before the OR was consulted as premature [VERIFIED: AWVN, Dirkzwager].
- **Faster levers close the gap without severance.** These are inventory, receivables, phasing capex, a price or material surcharge, a hiring freeze and fewer flex workers. In the illustrative case, a cautious package gets you to about 3.37x and a mid-sized one to about 3.09x [ASSUMED: lever sizes; replace them with yours].
- **What a bank reads as "serious":** telling them early, a covenant forecast that shows the downside, enough room left on your facilities, a costed plan with owners, and the shareholder sharing the pain (for example by pausing dividends). A headline about headcount answers none of these [MEDIUM: general bank practice].

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
A small forecast breach that you flag early, with a credible plan, is usually settled with a waiver or covenant reset and a fee. A surprise, or a plan that looks improvised or ends up in court with your own OR, is what gets a company moved to the bank's special-assets team (bijzonder beheer). There, pricing, control and your options all get worse.

**HOW TO ACT**
1. Hold the announcement and agree with the owner that the bank gets a plan, not a headline → you and the owner → this week, before anything reaches staff.
2. Call your relationship manager to flag the forecast and put the covenant on the review agenda. Read the facility agreement with your finance lawyer: EBITDA add-backs, how net debt is defined, whether there is an equity cure (shareholder money that counts towards the test) and what you are obliged to notify → you → end of week 1.
3. Start the no-regret levers now and size each one month by month inside the test window → you, operations and sales → start in week 1, numbers in the pack by the end of week 2.
4. Send the bank pack 3–5 working days before the meeting. It contains:
   - the covenant forecast for each test date, downside first
   - a 13-week cash flow and the room left on your facilities
   - the lever plan
   - the owner's contribution
   - a specific ask

   → you and your controller → end of week 2 or early week 3.
5. Test the order book for structural overcapacity. If there is some, prepare a properly sized proposal for the OR's advice after the meeting, with an employment lawyer → HR and operations → findings by the day of the meeting.

**RISKS**
- **Forecast error.** A 3% EBITDA miss is the whole gap, and a 10% miss takes you to 4.0x. Ask for a reset with headroom that covers the downside, for example 4.0x for the next two or three tests, stepping back to 3.5x. Don't settle for a one-off waiver at the line.
- **Quarter-end window dressing.** The bank can see suppliers being stretched at 30 June, and it costs you their goodwill. Go for working-capital gains you can hold.
- **You don't choose who leaves.** In a UWV dismissal for economic reasons, the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) selects by age group and length of service. Skilled makers and upholsterers can go, and they are hard to rehire when demand returns.
- **If the decline is structural, the owner is partly right.** Some capacity may have to go. The answer then is one properly sized restructuring through the OR, not a 10% figure picked to impress the bank.

**YOUR DECISION**
- **Option A: announce 10% cuts before the meeting.**
  - It shows action.
  - But it gives no covenant benefit by Q2 and possibly a bigger breach.
  - It creates legal exposure with the OR, the unions and UWV.
  - It costs about €0.4–0.5m in cash and loses skills.
- **Option B: go to the bank with a funded plan and a reset request.**
  - Bring the owner's contribution, start the hiring freeze and flex reduction now, and review capacity.
  - That review leads to a proper OR process only if the numbers call for it.
  - It is less dramatic and means more work in three weeks, and it asks the owner to put something on the table.
- **Recommendation: Option B.** It fixes the test on terms the bank accepts and keeps a restructuring available, done properly, if you really need one.
