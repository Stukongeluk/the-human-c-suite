# Q2 leverage covenant: plan for the bank review

This plan is for the CFO and the owner, and is meant to be used in the three weeks before the bank review.

**How to read the figures.** Only the 3.6x forecast, the 3.5x limit and the 260 employees come from you. Every euro amount below is an illustrative assumption: €50m revenue, €30m cost of goods sold, €5.0m LTM EBITDA and €18.0m net debt, which gives 3.6x. Replace them with your actuals. The method stays the same.

## 1. Covenant arithmetic

Leverage = net debt at the test date ÷ EBITDA over the twelve months to the test date (LTM). Check the exact definitions in your facility agreement (section 5).

| Target | EBITDA needed (net debt flat) | Net debt needed (EBITDA flat) |
|---|---|---|
| 3.5x (at the line) | +2.9% (+€0.14m) | −2.8% (−€0.50m) |
| 3.25x (about 7% headroom) | +10.8% (+€0.54m) | −9.7% (−€1.75m) |
| 3.2x (about 9% headroom) | +12.5% (+€0.63m) | −11.1% (−€2.0m) |

The percentages follow from your figures [VERIFIED]. The euro amounts use the illustrative base [ASSUMED].

**Sensitivity, downside first, with net debt flat at €18.0m:**

| LTM EBITDA vs forecast | Leverage |
|---|---|
| −10% | 4.00x |
| −5% | 3.79x |
| Forecast | 3.60x |
| +5% | 3.43x |

A €1m build-up in working capital before 30 June takes you to 3.8x on its own.

**What this means:** your forecast leaves no headroom at all, and the gap is smaller than normal forecast error seven months out. The bank will test the downside, so show it first.

**What can still move in the test window.** If your Q2 ends on 30 June 2027, July to November 2026 are already in the LTM figure. Only December to June, seven months, can still change.
- An EBITDA lever counts only for the months it is live. For example, a €100k-a-year saving that starts on 1 February adds €42k to the Q2 LTM figure.
- A net-debt lever counts in full at the test date.

**The owner's own lever comes on top.** Every €0.5m of distributions not paid before 30 June lowers leverage by about 0.1x (€17.5m ÷ €5.0m = 3.5x). Subordinated shareholder support, or an equity cure if the agreement allows one, is stronger still. It is also the clearest signal of commitment the bank can get, but only if the family can afford it without strain.

**Don't window-dress.** Banks look at average and peak debt, not just the 30 June figure. Paying suppliers late at quarter-end is visible to them and damages supplier relationships. Book only working-capital gains you can hold.

## 3. The 10% cut (26 people), honestly

### The Dutch process if you start it properly now

1. Prepare the business case and the proposed decision (voorgenomen besluit): 2–4 weeks.
2. Ask the OR for its advice at a point where it can still influence the decision [VERIFIED: AWVN]. The OR often takes several weeks, sometimes with an outside expert [ASSUMED: typical duration].
3. If the decision departs from the OR's advice, you must wait at least one month before carrying it out, unless the OR waives the wait [VERIFIED: Rendement].
4. Notify UWV and the unions in advance and consult the unions, because this is 20 or more dismissals within three months in one work area [VERIFIED: UWV]. A one-month waiting period then applies to several routes, including settlement agreements and dissolution by the court. It falls away if the unions declare in writing that they were consulted [VERIFIED: UWV].
5. The UWV dismissal-permit procedure takes weeks. In that route, the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) decides who leaves, by age group and length of service, not you.
6. Notice periods run 1–4 months depending on length of service.
7. Pay the statutory transition payment: one-third of a month's salary per year of service. Add whatever your CAO or a social plan requires on top.

**Realistic exit dates:** if you start properly in December, roughly April to July 2027 [ASSUMED].

### Effect on the Q2 test

The scenarios assume:
- 26 FTE × €55k loaded cost = €1.43m a year, or €119k a month
- one-off costs of about €0.45m: transition payments of about €0.30m (average 10 years of service at €3.5k a month), plus legal, advisory and social-plan costs

All of these are [ASSUMED].

| Case | Exits | Restructuring cost in covenant EBITDA? | Q2 leverage |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (best) | 1 May, severance paid | Added back | 3.48x |
| B | 1 May, severance paid | Not added back | 3.80x |
| C | 1 July, provision booked, not yet paid | Added back | 3.60x |
| D | 1 July, provision booked, not yet paid | Not added back | 3.96x |

The savings figures also ignore lost output. If the orders are there, revenue falls with capacity and the real saving is smaller.

**The accounting point.** Announcing a detailed restructuring plan to the people affected usually triggers a restructuring provision. That puts the cost in the quarter of announcement and the savings later [MEDIUM: confirm the treatment under your reporting framework with your auditor].

**If it is announced before the OR's advice:**
- The OR can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer) in Amsterdam. The court can order the decision withdrawn, in whole or in part, and can prohibit you from carrying it out [VERIFIED: Dirkzwager].
- UWV has treated permit applications filed before the OR was consulted as premature [VERIFIED: Dirkzwager, 2016 article; check the current rules].
- Failing to meet the collective-dismissal obligations can lead a court to reverse the dismissals [VERIFIED: Rijksoverheid].

The result could be months of delay and a public dispute with your own employees while the bank is watching.

## 4. If a restructuring really is needed

Run a capacity analysis by department against the order book and the demand outlook.
- **If the downturn is temporary:** use the levers in section 2, the hiring freeze and natural turnover.
- **If overcapacity is structural:** size one restructuring properly, once. Two rounds do more damage than one well-judged one. Take it through the OR's advice, the collective-dismissal notification and union consultation, and a social plan. Tell the bank it is being prepared, not that it has been decided.

Briefing the OR early on the financial position, in confidence, builds the trust you will need if that day comes.

## 5. Facility agreement checklist (with your finance lawyer, in week 1)

- **Net debt:** which debt counts, whether netted cash is capped, whether leases count (and whether accounting rules are frozen at signing), guarantees, and whether subordinated shareholder loans are excluded.
- **EBITDA:**
  - Is it LTM?
  - Which one-off items can be added back, and is there a cap?
  - Are restructuring costs added back?
  - Are run-rate savings counted pro forma?
- **Test dates:** when each test falls and when the compliance certificate is due.
- **Equity cure:**
  - Does the agreement have one?
  - Does the money count as EBITDA or reduce net debt?
  - How much is allowed, and how often?
- **Information duties:** whether you must notify a default, or a likely default, and what budget and forecast deliveries are required.
- **Consequences of a breach:**
  - event of default
  - cross-default into leases and other facilities
  - margin ratchets
- **Other covenants:** solvency or guarantee-capital ratio, interest cover, capex limits and dividend restrictions.

## 6. The bank pack (send 3–5 working days before the meeting)

1. **One page:** where we are, why, what we are doing and what we are asking for.
2. **Leverage bridge** from the last test to the Q2 forecast.
   - EBITDA drivers: volume, price, materials, wages under the CAO, energy.
   - Net-debt drivers: capex, working capital, distributions.
3. **Covenant forecast for each test date over the next 4–6 quarters**, in downside, base and upside cases, each with and without the actions.
4. **13-week cash flow and the room left on your revolving credit facility.** Show that liquidity is not the problem. If it is, that becomes the headline.
5. **Action plan:** for each lever, its euro effect, timing, owner, confidence and the KPI you will track. Offer the bank monthly reporting on it.
6. **The shareholder's contribution.**
7. **The ask.**
   - Preferred: a covenant reset calibrated on your downside case, for example 4.0x for Q2 and Q3 2027, 3.75x for Q4 and 3.5x from Q1 2028.
   - Fallback: a one-off waiver.
   - Expect a fee, possibly a higher margin, tighter reporting and perhaps a dividend restriction. Negotiate these.
8. **Workforce.** The hiring freeze and flex reduction are already in place and a capacity review is under way. Any restructuring will go through the OR and the collective-dismissal process. Never present cuts as decided.

## 8. Putting it to the owner

- "You're right that the bank needs to see we're serious. To a credit committee, serious means a plan it can test, early warning and a shareholder who shares the burden."
- "The cut doesn't move the Q2 number. It may make it worse, it costs about €0.45m in cash, and announcing it before the OR has advised can be reversed in court."
- "The strongest signal comes from you: pause distributions and, if the family can afford it, offer subordinated support."
- "We keep the restructuring option. If the analysis shows structural overcapacity, we do it properly and size it once."
- "Our name in the region matters. Staff and dealers will read a sudden 10% cut as distress, and the first to leave voluntarily are usually our best people."

## 9. Critic's pre-screen

- **Weakest point:** the levers have to deliver within seven months. Working-capital programmes often land later than planned, and a price increase can cost volume in a weak furniture market.
- **Missing perspective:** whether demand has fallen structurally. If it has, the bank will expect a structural answer, and Option B buys time rather than solving the problem.
- **If this is wrong, the cost is:** a Q2 breach anyway. With a reset agreed in advance, that costs a fee and margin. Without one, it is an event of default.
- **Proceed:** yes, with the reset request as the safety net and an honest capacity analysis.
