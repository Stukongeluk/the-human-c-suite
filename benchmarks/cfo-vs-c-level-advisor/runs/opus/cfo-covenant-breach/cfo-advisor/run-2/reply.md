**CFO: the Q2 covenant and the proposed 10% cut**

---

**BOTTOM LINE**

Don't announce job cuts before the bank meeting. On realistic Dutch timing, a 10% cut puts more cost into the Q2 test than it saves. It also risks breaching the works council's right to give advice, and a bank reads an unprepared headline as panic. Close the 0.1x gap with net-debt and EBITDA levers you control. Ask the owner to put his own money behind the plan. Then go to the review with a tested forecast and a pre-agreed fallback.

Confidence: medium-high. The direction is solid. The sizes depend on your facility definitions and your actual numbers, which I don't have.

**WHAT**

- **The gap is small.** Going from 3.6x to 3.5x means cutting net debt by 0.1 x EBITDA (2.8%) or raising EBITDA by 2.9%. With an illustrative EUR 4.0M EBITDA and EUR 14.4M net debt, that's EUR 0.4M of net debt or EUR 114k of EBITDA. Comfortable headroom (3.2x) needs EUR 1.6M of net debt reduction. [Formula verified; numbers assumed]
- **A 10% cut (26 people) mostly misses the Q2 test.** The run-rate saving is about EUR 1.5M a year. But the one-off cost of about EUR 0.5M (transition payments, settlements, advisers) is booked when you announce. With a proper process, people leave between mid-May and August, so the test captures 0-2 months of savings. Result at Q2: 3.6-4.1x. It only gets you under 3.5x if your facility allows restructuring add-backs *and* people are gone by the end of April. [Medium: timing; Assumed: costs]
- **Announcing first is legally exposed.**
  - 26 people is a collective dismissal: 20 or more within 3 months in one region. You must notify UWV (the employee insurance agency that issues dismissal permits) and the unions in advance, and there's a one-month waiting period.
  - The works council (OR) must be asked for advice while it can still change the decision. If it isn't, the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer) can order the decision withdrawn, and UWV refuses premature permit applications.
  - Selection follows the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel), so you can't choose who leaves. [Verified: UWV, Rijksoverheid, Dirkzwager, KHN]
- **Levers you control can close the gap by 30 June.** Inventory down 10 days, DSO (days sales outstanding, how fast customers pay) down 5 days, capex moved to Q3, fewer agency hours where volume has fallen, a hiring freeze, price and procurement. Fully delivered, that's EUR 1.4M of net debt plus EUR 0.5M of EBITDA. At 50% delivery you land at about 3.15x; at 25%, about 3.37x. [Assumed sizes; math in the doc]
- **The bank is weighing credibility and shareholder support, not a headcount number.** What counts: an early warning, a forecast that survives a bear case, dated measures with owners, monthly reporting, and the owner's own money at stake. [Medium: how lenders assess a tight covenant]

**WHY THIS MATTERS**

A tight covenant you flag early, with a plan, is a routine amendment conversation with your relationship manager. A surprise breach, or a plan that falls apart in a works council dispute, is how companies end up in special asset management (bijzonder beheer). There, the bank sets the price and starts steering decisions.

**HOW TO ACT**

1. **Rebuild the covenant test from the facility agreement.** Check the exact definitions of net debt and EBITDA, the add-backs for one-offs, the LTM (last twelve months) basis and test date, any equity cure right, and the other covenants (DSCR, solvency). Run base, bear and bull cases. → CFO + your own finance lawyer → **Wed 25 Nov**
2. **Call the relationship manager before the review.** Something like: "Our forecast shows tight headroom at the Q2 test. We'll bring a plan to the review." No surprises. → Owner + CFO → **Fri 27 Nov**
3. **Put the self-help plan in writing.** Each lever gets an owner, a euro amount, a date and monthly tracking. Only structural changes count; no quarter-end window dressing. → Operations, purchasing and sales leads, coordinated by CFO → **Fri 4 Dec**
4. **Secure the owner's commitment.** No dividend or management fee until headroom is at least 0.3x, plus a standby subordinated shareholder loan (e.g. EUR 0.5M) to use as a cure. He attends the review. → Owner → **Fri 4 Dec**
5. **Finish the bank pack and do a dry run with the owner.** The pack includes the fallback ask: "If Q2 lands between 3.5x and 4.0x, a one-off reset or equity cure applies, and we report monthly." In parallel, run a structural capacity review. If the order book shows lasting overcapacity, prepare a proposal for the works council, sized to the order book rather than to a signal. → CFO (pack, by **Tue 8 Dec**); Owner + HR + CFO (go/no-go on consultation by **Fri 15 Jan 2027**)

**RISKS**

- **Bear case.** If EBITDA comes in 5% below forecast and only a quarter of the plan lands, you're at about 3.54x. Triggers: if the end-March forecast is above 3.4x, draw the owner's loan. If it's still above 3.5x after that, file a formal reset request by 30 April, before the test, not after.
- **My euro sizes aren't your numbers.** If your stock and DSO are already lean, the self-help total shrinks and the fallback ask becomes the main event.
- **Rumours of cuts reach credit insurers.** They cut the credit limits your suppliers have on you, the suppliers shorten terms, and your working capital and net debt rise. Skilled staff also tend to leave first.
- **Cutting agency hours still means people losing work.** Do it deliberately. It's also the order the law expects.
- **Not peer-verified.** Have your employment lawyer and HR confirm the legal and timing points, and check your cao (collective labour agreement) for reorganisation clauses, before anything is said internally.

**YOUR DECISION**

- **Option A: announce 10% cuts before the meeting.** It looks decisive. But the cost lands inside the test and the savings mostly after it, it invites a legal challenge, and the bank sees a headline rather than a plan. Q2 likely 3.6-4.1x.
- **Option B: self-help plan, owner support and a pre-agreed fallback at the review.** The structural review runs in parallel and goes through the works council if needed. Q2 about 3.15-3.4x at 25-50% delivery.
- **Option C: ask for a waiver only.** The least effort now, but the weakest position: the bank sets the terms and the price.

**Recommendation: B.** It fixes the number that's actually tested and keeps a capacity decision available on its merits for the Q4 and 2028 tests. It also lets the owner show seriousness the way a bank values most: his own money behind a plan that holds.
