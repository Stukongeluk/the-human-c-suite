**CFO: 2027 budget approach for the board's 8%**

**BOTTOM LINE**
Accept the board's 8% as one company-wide number (about €5.8M of run-rate opex), but don't spread it evenly. In a thin-margin services business, a cut of 8% per cost centre removes billable people who earn more than they cost. It buys a fraction of the profit the board expects and probably lowers 2027 EBIT.

Confidence: High on the direction. Low on the euro amounts until your actuals replace my illustrative inputs.

**WHAT**
- **Cutting a busy consultant loses you money.** You save their cost but lose the revenue they bill. On illustrative numbers (€75M revenue, 4% EBIT, €81.6k loaded cost per FTE, 80% billable), an average-utilised billable FTE brings in about €142k against about €82k of cost. Cutting one costs you about €60k of EBIT. A cut pays only where utilisation is below about 42% (loaded cost ÷ (rate × available hours)). [VERIFIED: your 600 FTE and 68% payroll. ASSUMED: the rest] High on the logic, Low on the amounts
- **Blanket 8% means about 48 people out (about 38 of them billable), plus 8% off every non-payroll line.** Run-rate EBIT improves by −€0.2M to +€1.4M, not the €5.8M the board is picturing. In 2027 itself, exits only take effect around July and cost about €1.2M in one-offs, so EBIT ends up about −€0.2M. [ASSUMED] Medium
- **A targeted plan reaches the same 8% (€5.8M run-rate) and keeps revenue intact:**
  - move about €2.0M of subcontracted work onto your own bench;
  - reduce billable capacity only where demand is structurally below break-even (−12 FTE);
  - take support functions down about 12% (−14 FTE, mostly by not backfilling leavers);
  - zero-base discretionary non-payroll spend (−10%).

  2027 EBIT rises by about €3.4M, or about €1.5M if only half of it delivers. [ASSUMED: bench is about 6% of billable capacity, 12% attrition] Medium
- **Two definitions decide whether the target is reachable at all.**
  - Baseline: CPB expects business labour cost per employee to rise 4.4% in 2027. So "8% below 2026 actual" is €8.5M (11.4%) from the do-nothing budget, not €5.8M.
  - Timing: no Dutch redundancy takes effect on 1 January. Hitting 8% on full-year 2027 opex with a blanket cut would need about 107 people out by July.

You must notify UWV and the unions and wait one month. Selection follows the afspiegelingsbeginsel (proportional selection by age group and length of service), not performance or utilisation. The works council must also be asked for advice while the decision is still a proposal. [VERIFIED: Rijksoverheid, UWV, AWVN. Your employment lawyer to confirm the route] High

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
With EBIT at about €3M, the gap between the two approaches (about €3.6M in 2027 on these numbers) is bigger than your entire current profit. The wrong allocation method can take you from a thin margin to zero while the board still sees a lower opex line.

**HOW TO ACT**
1. Walk the CEO through the break-even rule on your own numbers and agree a joint proposal. Keep his simplicity by framing it as "one envelope, three rules" → CFO → Wed 25 Nov
2. Build the baseline:
   - 2026 forecast by cost centre and the 2027 do-nothing budget;
   - payroll split billable/support;
   - utilisation and bench by practice;
   - subcontractor spend by skill, and contract end dates.

   → FP&A lead → Fri 27 Nov
3. Collect three inputs → CRO/COO, CHRO, GC → Fri 27 Nov:
   - the 2027 demand plan by practice;
   - which subcontract roles your bench can take over;
   - attrition and age/tenure by function, plus the legal route and timing for the works council.
4. Hold budget-holder sessions, then an exec calibration where the CEO puts each cost centre in a tier: Protect (0–3%), Standard (8%) or Harvest (12–20%). Every lever gets a confidence rating → CFO and CEO → Thu 3 Dec
5. Send the board pre-read and pre-brief the chair. It covers the definitions, option A vs B, phasing, one-offs, the works council process and the draft resolution → CFO and CEO → Mon 7 Dec

**RISKS**
- **The biggest lever is unverified.** Insourcing is €2.0M, a third of the plan, and it depends on a skills match between bench and subcontractors that nobody has checked. If it fails, the plan is about €1–2M short. Name the backups in the paper: passing the wage increase through to client rates (€0.75M per 1%), an H1 discretionary freeze, and a cap on merit pay above the CAO.
- **Attrition slows when people sense cuts.** If it drops from 12% to 8%, not backfilling delivers about a third less in support functions.
- **A leak would hit your best people first.** If "8% everywhere" gets out before the plan is final, the most employable consultants in your busiest practices leave first.

**YOUR DECISION**
- **Option A: blanket 8% per cost centre.** Fast and simple to announce. About 48 exits under the collective-dismissal rules, revenue falls with capacity, and 2027 EBIT is roughly flat to negative.
- **Option B: 8% as one company-wide envelope, allocated by cost driver.** Billable teams are budgeted from demand; support and non-payroll get tiered targets. It takes two more weeks of analysis and a harder conversation in the exec team. It keeps revenue, needs about 5–10 forced exits, and 2027 EBIT rises by about €3.4M.

Recommendation: Option B, presented jointly with the CEO. Define the board target as €5.8M of run-rate opex reduction by the Q4 2027 exit (about 5% on full-year 2027), with an EBIT floor as a guardrail.
