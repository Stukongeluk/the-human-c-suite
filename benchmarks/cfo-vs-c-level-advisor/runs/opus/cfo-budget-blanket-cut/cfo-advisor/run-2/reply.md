## CFO: FY2027 budget approach for the 8% opex target

**BOTTOM LINE**

I wouldn't take "every cost centre cuts 8%" to the board. Take one company-level target instead, with a tight definition, and deliver it through targets that differ by cost centre:

- Protect revenue capacity.
- Cut overhead and non-payroll hardest.
- Use attrition before redundancies.
- Agree a second tranche up front, sized at a Q1 review.

In my illustrative model this hits the same 8% exit run-rate as the flat cut. It gives about €2.5M more FY2027 EBIT, with fewer than half the forced exits.

Confidence: high on the direction, because it follows from your 68% payroll share and thin margin. Medium to low on the euro figures until we load actuals.

**WHAT**

- **A flat 8% is mostly a people cut.**
  - 68% × 8% means 5.4 of the 8 points come from payroll. On an assumed €80M opex base, that is about 48 FTE at today's cost, about 36 of them billable.
  - To leave payroll untouched, non-payroll would have to fall 25%. So payroll will contribute. The question is which payroll, and how.
  - Basis: the share is derived from your numbers. The FTE count assumes ~€91k loaded cost and 75% billable. Confidence: high on the share, medium on the FTE count.
- **In services, payroll is revenue capacity.**
  - A utilised consultant costs about €91k and brings in about €185k.
  - The flat cut's EBIT effect is: €6.4M − (billable FTE cut × €185k × the share of them who were actually billed in demand).
  - If half of them were billed, the flat cut adds €3.1M of EBIT. If your practices are capacity-constrained, it adds roughly nothing. A targeted cut of the same €6.4M adds about €6.0M.
  - Basis: derived, plus assumptions. Confidence: medium.
- **The real cut is bigger than 8%.**
  - CPB expects cao wages to rise 3.8% in 2027 ([MEV 2027 via AWVN](https://www.awvn.nl/nieuws/mev-2027-wat-werkgevers-ervan-moeten-weten/)). With step increases and premiums I've modelled payroll drift at 4.5%.
  - Do nothing and the margin falls from about 4.0% to about 2.7%.
  - If the board means 8% below 2026 actual, that is an 11.5% (€9.5M) cut against what 2027 would otherwise cost. Under the flat rule that is about 72 FTE (12%), not 48.
  - Confidence: medium.
- **Dutch process sets the calendar, and it ignores cost-centre lines.**
  - Works council advice (WOR art. 25) has to come before the decision is fixed.
  - 20 or more exits in one UWV region within three months triggers the collective-dismissal notification (WMCO).
  - UWV selects by function category and age band (afspiegeling), with flex workers going first. It does not select by cost centre.
  - Forced exits realistically land around June, so FY2027 only captures about 7/12 of the saving, less one-offs.
  - Basis: assumed until employment counsel validates it. Confidence: medium.
- **No option gets FY2027 P&L opex 8% below 2026.**
  - The options land between −1.3% and −2.7% including one-offs.
  - Getting to −8% within the year would take about €17M of run-rate cuts, 21% of the base.
  - What can be delivered is a Q4-2027 exit run-rate 8% below 2026, plus an EBIT floor.
  - Confidence: high on the direction.

**WHY THIS MATTERS**

At a thin margin, cutting revenue-generating people can cancel the saving. And if the board leaves the meeting expecting −8% in the 2027 P&L, every option misses, and you'll be explaining that in Q2. Settle the definition in this meeting.

**HOW TO ACT**

The pre-read goes out about five days before the board, so you have roughly 10 working days.

1. **Align with the CEO first** → you → day 1.
   - Agree to bring the board options rather than a fixed flat cut.
   - Present the tiers as the CEO's simplicity with better maths: still one number per cost centre, with three tiers deciding the number.
2. **Start a vacancy freeze now, with a weekly exception board that backfills only against demand** → CEO, you, CHRO → day 2.
   - It is reversible and needs no board approval.
   - It starts the attrition clock in December.
3. **Build the 2027 no-action baseline and the cost cubes** → FP&A, HR, procurement → day 4.
   - Headcount by cost centre × billable/indirect × contract type × tenure and age band.
   - Utilisation and demand by practice.
   - Non-payroll by vendor, with contract end dates.
4. **Collect inputs from owners and HR** → cost-centre owners, delivery heads, CHRO with employment counsel → day 7.
   - Each cost-centre owner sends an options menu: what 5%, 10% and 15% look like, and what each costs in revenue, clients and people.
   - Delivery heads send demand against capacity per practice.
   - CHRO and counsel map the works council route, WMCO exposure and cost per exit.
5. **Finalise and send** → you, FP&A → days 8–10.
   - Model options A, B and C on actuals.
   - Agree the recommendation with the CEO.
   - Pre-wire the board chair.
   - Send the pre-read.

**RISKS**

- **Attrition falls in a soft market.** If it drops from 12% to 7%, Option B delivers about €5.6M of run-rate instead of €6.9M. The second tranche exists to cover this.
- **Approving a specific reorganisation before the works council advises** invites an art. 25 challenge. Frame structural measures as an envelope and an intent, subject to advice.
- **The targeted numbers only hold if the slack is real.** That means bench in low-demand practices, about 12% of room in non-payroll, and overhead attrition. The day-7 menus and practice data will prove or kill this. If they don't show it, the gap to the flat cut narrows.
- **Not yet checked by the people who own the data:**
  - headcount and attrition (CHRO)
  - the legal route (counsel)
  - utilisation and demand (delivery heads)
  - price indexation (commercial)

**YOUR DECISION (for the board)**

| Illustrative | A: flat 8% per cost centre | B: tiered, no forced exits | C: B + structural tranche |
|---|---|---|---|
| FTE change (forced exits) | −72 (~45) | −40 (0) | −60 (~20) |
| Q4-2027 exit run-rate opex vs 2026 | −8.0% | −4.8% (−8.4% vs no-action 2027) | −8.1% |
| FY2027 opex vs 2026, incl. one-offs | −2.7% | −1.3% | −2.3% |
| FY2027 EBIT (margin) | €4.2M (5.1%); €0.8M if practices are capacity-constrained | €6.0M (7.1%) | €6.7M (7.9%) |
| Trade-off | Simplest, but cuts without regard to revenue; WMCO certain | Least disruption, but misses a 2026-based 8% and depends on attrition | Hits the board's number with better EBIT; needs works council advice and ~20 exits |

**Recommendation: Option C.** Run it as B from today. Design the structural tranche in Q1 and size it at the Q1 review against the remaining gap, up to about 20 FTE and subject to works council advice. If the board accepts the 2027 no-action baseline as the base, B alone likely does it.
