# FY2027 Opex Programme: Budget Approach for the Board (draft v1)

**How to read this.** All euro figures come from an illustrative model. It assumes a €80M opex base (600 FTE × ~€91k loaded cost ÷ 68% payroll share) and a 4% EBIT margin. Replace these with actuals in week 1. The conclusions depend on ratios that follow from your own numbers, not on the €80M. Formulas are in section 11. Totals are computed before rounding.

Tags: **[YOURS]** = from your brief. **[DERIVED]** = arithmetic on your numbers. **[SOURCE]** = external. **[ASSUMED]** = my assumption, to replace.

---

## 1. Decisions the board is asked to take

1. **How "8% lower" is measured.** This covers the base year, P&L versus exit run-rate, the treatment of one-off costs, and an EBIT floor.
2. **The approach.** The proposal is tiered, driver-based targets instead of a uniform 8% per cost centre.
3. **A contingent second tranche.** The board sets its envelope and the Q1 review that sizes it. Any structural measure is subject to works council advice.

## 2. What "8% lower" means: settle five definitions first

| Question | Reading 1 | Reading 2 | Difference (illustrative) | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 8% below 2026 actual → **€73.6M** | 8% below 2027 no-action baseline → **€76.5M** | €2.9M ≈ 30 FTE | Board's call. Show both. |
| Measure | FY2027 P&L | Q4-2027 annualised exit run-rate | FY captures roughly 45–60% of run-rate | Exit run-rate, plus an FY EBIT floor |
| One-offs (exit costs, contract break fees) | Inside opex | Reported separately | €0.3–1.2M depending on option | Separately, but fully disclosed |
| Objective | Opex only | Opex plus EBIT floor | A cut that loses revenue can hit opex and still miss profit | Add the EBIT floor |
| Scope | All operating cost, incl. billable staff | Overhead only | Changes the whole revenue argument | Confirm. This document assumes billable staff are included. |

If the board's "opex" excludes direct delivery cost, section 4.2 loses most of its force. The drift, timing and legal points still hold, and the tiers get simpler.

## 3. The 2027 no-action baseline

| €M | 2026 [ASSUMED] | 2027, no action | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payroll | 54.4 | 56.8 | +4.5%: CPB cao wage growth of 3.8% for 2027 [SOURCE], plus step increases/promotions and the higher Aof premium [ASSUMED] |
| Non-payroll | 25.6 | 26.3 | +2.7%, the CPB inflation forecast for 2027 [SOURCE] |
| **Opex** | **80.0** | **83.1** | **+3.9%** |
| Revenue | 83.3 | 85.4 | +2.5% price indexation [ASSUMED] |
| **EBIT (margin)** | **3.3 (4.0%)** | **2.3 (2.7%)** | Margin falls 1.3 pts with no action |

- With no price indexation at all, 2027 EBIT is about **€0.2M (0.2%)**. Pricing belongs in this conversation even though it isn't opex.

## 4. Why a flat 8% per cost centre is the expensive way to hit the number

### 4.1 It is mostly a people cut [DERIVED]
- 68% × 8% = **5.4 of the 8 points** come from payroll.
- At 2026 cost, that is €4.35M ≈ **48 FTE**. At 75% billable, about 36 of them are billable.
- Measured against 2026 budgets with 4.5% drift, each cost centre needs **12% fewer people**. That is **72 FTE** in total: 54 billable and 18 indirect.
- Leaving payroll untouched would require non-payroll to fall 25%:

| Non-payroll cut | Payroll cut still needed |
|---|---|
| 10% | 7.1% |
| 15% | 4.7% |
| 20% | 2.4% |
| 25% | 0% |

Payroll will contribute. The question is *which* payroll, and *how*.

### 4.2 In services, payroll is revenue capacity [DERIVED + ASSUMED]
A utilised consultant costs about **€91k** and brings in about **€185k** of revenue. The ratio of roughly 2:1 follows from your 68% payroll share and thin margin, with 75% billable assumed.

The EBIT effect of a flat €6.4M cut (2026 scale) = €6.4M − (36 billable FTE × €185k × *u*). Here *u* is the share of the removed billable capacity that was actually billed, in practices where demand exists.

| *u* | 0 (all bench) | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1.0 (capacity-constrained) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EBIT effect of flat cut | +€6.4M | +€4.7M | **+€3.1M** | +€1.4M | **−€0.3M** |

For comparison, a targeted cut of the same €6.4M nets about **+€6.0M**. It takes non-payroll −12%, overhead −15% and billable −14 FTE from bench-heavy practices at *u* = 0.15.

### 4.3 It ignores where the slack is
- A cost centre that is 95% payroll has to cut people. One that is 30% payroll can cut vendors.
- Capacity-constrained practices lose revenue under a flat cut.
- Bench-heavy practices that could cut 12–15% only cut 8%.

### 4.4 Dutch rules don't follow cost-centre lines (detail in section 9)
UWV selection uses afspiegeling (proportional selection) by function category and age band across the location, not by cost centre or performance. Flex workers in the same category go first. A per-cost-centre headcount number may not be executable as designed.

### 4.5 Timing
- Forced exits need works council advice, a collective-dismissal notification and notice periods, so they realistically land **around June**.
- FY2027 then captures about 7/12 of the saving, less one-offs.

### 4.6 Second-order costs (not in the numbers)
- **Regretted attrition.** The most marketable people leave first once a flat cut is announced.
- **Lost capacity before exit.** People released from work during notice stop billing while still on payroll.
- **Rehiring when demand returns.** Recruiting fees of 15–25% of first-year salary, plus ramp time.
- **Client continuity** on key accounts.

## 5. Recommended approach: one company number, three tiers, driver-based

| Tier | What's in it | How the budget is set | Indicative target | Main levers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Protect** | Capacity-constrained delivery practices; sales/BD with qualified pipeline; account managers on top clients | Demand-driven. Backfill against signed or weighted demand. | ~0% | Utilisation discipline, rate/indexation |
| **Right-size to demand** | Delivery practices with bench; delivery support | FTE = forecast billable hours ÷ (available hours × target utilisation) | ~−11% FTE in these practices | Non-backfill; swap contractors for own bench staff |
| **Cut hard** | Overhead/G&A payroll; all non-payroll | Zero-based for discretionary non-payroll; attrition plus spans and layers for overhead | Overhead ~−11% FTE; non-payroll −12% (stretch 15%) | Freeze, vendor consolidation, housing, IT seats, travel/events, advisors |

**Rules for cost-centre owners.** This keeps the CEO's simplicity: one number per manager.
1. Every cost centre gets one number, derived from its tier.
2. Delivery budgets come from the demand formula, not from a percentage.
3. No backfill without exception-board approval. The board meets weekly: CFO, COO and CHRO.
4. Discretionary non-payroll is zero-based: every line is re-justified.
5. Every owner submits an options menu showing what 5%, 10% and 15% would look like, and what each costs in revenue, clients and people.

## 7. Options for the board

| €M (illustrative) | No action | **A: flat 8% per cost centre** | **B: tiered, no forced exits** | **C: B + structural tranche** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FTE change (forced exits) | 0 | −72 (~45) | −40 (0) | −60 (~20) |
| Q4-2027 exit run-rate opex | 83.1 | 73.6 | 76.2 | 73.5 |
| vs 2026 / vs no-action | +3.9% / 0 | **−8.0%** / −11.5% | **−4.8%** / −8.4% | **−8.1%** / −11.6% |
| FY2027 opex vs 2026, incl. one-offs (excl.) | +3.9% | −2.7% (−4.3%) | −1.3% (−1.6%) | −2.3% (−3.4%) |
| One-offs | 0 | 1.2 | 0.3 | 0.85 |
| FY2027 EBIT (margin) | 2.3 (2.7%) | **4.2 (5.1%)** at *u*=0.5; 0.8 (1.1%) at *u*=1 | **6.0 (7.1%)** | **6.7 (7.9%)** |
| Exit run-rate EBIT (margin) | 2.3 (2.7%) | 6.7 (8.4%); 1.6 (2.1%) at *u*=1 | 8.5 (10.1%) | 10.9 (12.9%) |
| Downside case | — | Capacity-constrained practices: EBIT below no-action | Attrition 7%: −26 FTE, exit opex −3.1%, FY EBIT 5.3 | Tranche 2 must grow if B under-delivers |
| Works council advice / WMCO | — | Yes / certain | No (budget info only) / no | Yes / possible at ~20 in one region |

Modelling notes:
- The gap between A and B/C comes mainly from *who* is cut. *u* is 0.5 for a blind cut, versus 0.15 for B/C's bench-targeted reductions (0.3 for tranche-2 billable).
- Every option assumes revenue only falls where capacity is cut. Any wider demand softness in 2027 comes on top in all of them.

**What no option does: FY2027 P&L opex 8% below 2026.** That would take €9.5M of in-year savings. At about 55% in-year realisation, that means roughly **€17M of run-rate cuts, 21% of the base**, which would go deep into revenue capacity. The deliverable version is an exit run-rate target plus an EBIT floor.

## 8. Recommendation and draft board resolution

**Recommendation: Option C, run as B from today.**
- Design tranche 2 in Q1.
- Size it at the Q1 review against the gap remaining to the exit run-rate target, up to about 20 FTE, subject to works council advice.
- If the board accepts the 2027 no-action baseline as the base, B alone likely delivers.

**Draft resolution.** The board:
1. Sets the FY2027 cost target as a **Q4-2027 annualised exit run-rate opex of ≤ €[73.6]M** (8% below 2026). FY2027 EBIT must be **≥ €[x]M**, set at the *downside* case of the chosen option, not the base case. One-off costs are reported separately.
2. Endorses a **tiered, driver-based approach** (Protect / Right-size to demand / Cut hard) instead of a uniform percentage per cost centre.
3. **Authorises management to prepare structural measures** of up to ~20 FTE for works council advice in Q1, to be sized at the Q1 review against the remaining gap. This is an envelope and an intent, not a final decision on specific roles.
4. Asks for **monthly reporting** on secured run-rate savings, the headcount bridge, utilisation by practice, revenue versus plan, and EBIT versus the floor.

## 9. Dutch people and legal path [ASSUMED: CHRO and employment counsel to validate before the pre-read goes out]

- **Works council (OR).**
  - A reorganisation or significant reduction of activities needs the OR's advice (WOR art. 25). The request must come early enough for the advice to have real influence.
  - If you decide against the advice, implementation waits one month and the OR can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber.
  - The OR is also entitled to the budget (art. 31a).
  - Implication: the board approves the target and envelope, and management then requests advice on concrete measures.
- **Collective dismissal (WMCO).**
  - The rule applies when the employer intends to end ≥20 contracts within one UWV region within three months. Settlement agreements at the employer's initiative count toward the 20.
  - You must notify UWV and the unions and consult the unions.
  - UWV waits one month before handling dismissal applications.
- **Selection.**
  - Economic dismissals via UWV follow afspiegeling per category of interchangeable functions per location, across age bands (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55+). Within each band, the shortest service goes first.
  - Flex workers in the category go before permanent staff.
  - Selection is not by cost centre and not by performance. Most firms use settlement agreements (VSOs) with a social plan, but the afspiegeling outcome is the reference point.
- **Cost.**
  - The statutory transition payment is 1/3 of monthly salary per year of service, capped. At ~6 years' tenure that is about €12k here.
  - Social plans usually add to it, plus legal and outplacement costs.
  - The model uses €20–35k per exit, €27.5k in the base case.
- **Timing.**
  - Notice runs 1–4 months by tenure. UWV procedure time may be deducted, but at least 1 month remains.
  - Fixed-term contracts of ≥6 months need written notice of (non-)renewal at least 1 month before the end date (aanzegging).
- **Pay.**
  - No unilateral salary cuts. These need consent, or a written unilateral-change clause plus a compelling interest.
  - Variable pay follows the plan rules.
  - Lease-car and mobility policy are employment conditions, so change them at renewal.

## 10. Governance after approval

- **Monthly dashboard:**
  - Secured versus planned run-rate by lever.
  - Headcount bridge: leavers, non-backfilled, exceptions approved.
  - Utilisation by practice.
  - Revenue versus plan.
  - One-offs to date.
  - EBIT versus the floor.
- **Q1 review (end of March, reported to the April board).** Tranche 2 is sized at the remaining gap to the exit target. Escalate if:
  - secured run-rate is below €2.5M by 31 March,
  - annualised voluntary attrition is below 9%, or
  - year-to-date revenue is more than 3% below plan.
- **Guardrails:**
  - If Protect-tier utilisation stays above 85% for two months, release backfills.
  - Watch regretted attrition in in-demand skills.
  - Watch revenue per billable FTE.
  - Track client health on the top-20 accounts.

| Assumption | Value used | Status | Replace with |

| 2026 opex | €80.0M | [ASSUMED] | Actual / latest forecast |

| Payroll drift 2027 | 4.5% | [SOURCE] cao 3.8% + [ASSUMED] incidental and premiums | CAO plus HR step-increase model |
| Non-payroll inflation | 2.7% | [SOURCE] CPB | Contract indexation |
| Price indexation | 2.5% | [ASSUMED] | Contract clauses, rate plan |
| Voluntary attrition | 12% normal / 7% weak market | [ASSUMED] | HR trailing 12 months |
| Non-backfill rate (Cut / Right-size tiers) | 90% | [ASSUMED] | Exception-board policy |
| *u* (share of removed billable capacity that was billed) | 0.5 flat; 0.15 targeted; 0.3 tranche 2 | [ASSUMED] | Utilisation by practice |
| Non-payroll in-year realisation | 75% (60% for stretch) | [ASSUMED] | Contract dates |
| Forced exits effective | 1 June (A), 1 July (C) | [ASSUMED] | Counsel timeline |
| Cost per forced exit | €27.5k (€20–35k) | [ASSUMED] | Social plan, tenure data |

**Formulas**
- Loaded cost per FTE = payroll ÷ FTE.
- Revenue per billable FTE = revenue ÷ billable FTE.
- Flat-cut EBIT effect = € cut − (billable FTE removed × revenue per billable FTE × *u*).
- No-action 2027 opex = payroll × (1 + drift) + non-payroll × (1 + inflation). Add planned hires if your draft budget includes them.
- Real gap versus 2026 = no-action 2027 opex − 0.92 × 2026 opex.
- Payroll cut needed = (8% − non-payroll share × non-payroll % cut) ÷ payroll share.
- Delivery FTE needed = forecast billable hours ÷ (available hours per FTE × target utilisation).
- In-year share of a saving that starts in month *s* = (13 − *s*) ÷ 12.

## 13. Critic pre-screen

- **Weakest point.** B and C assume slack exists where we intend to cut: bench in low-demand practices, about 12% of room in non-payroll, and overhead attrition near 12%. With 7% attrition and non-payroll delivering 8%, B gives about **€4.5M**, not €6.9M. Tranche 2 then roughly doubles and moves toward A's forced-exit count.
- **Missing perspective.** The squeeze is as much a pricing problem as a cost problem: a 3.8% cao increase against whatever indexation you can achieve. B2B clients also often cut services spend in the same cycle. An opex programme without a price and indexation plan addresses half the problem.
- **If wrong.** A shortfall of €2–2.5M in run-rate shows up at the Q1 review. Tranche 2 then starts later, more of the benefit slips into 2028, and FY2027 EBIT lands nearer A's.
- **Proceed:** yes, with these risks noted. The week-2 practice data must confirm the slack before the recommendation is final.
