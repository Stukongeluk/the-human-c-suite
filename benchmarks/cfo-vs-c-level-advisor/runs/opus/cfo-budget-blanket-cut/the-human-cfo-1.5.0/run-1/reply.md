Don't take "8% per cost centre" to the board. Take a ranked plan instead. On an illustrative base it brings 2027 opex below this year despite a 3.8% wage rise and earns more 2027 profit than the flat cut (about €3.8m against €3.1m), with no dismissals where the flat cut needs about 27. It also sets a review in April where the board decides, on evidence, whether the remaining gap justifies more.

The trap is that with payroll at 68% of opex, a flat 8% lands on people, and in a services firm the people are the revenue.

The board paper draft is in `board-paper.md` and its model in `budget_model.py`. Every number is an estimate on an illustrative base (€75m opex, about €85k loaded per person, 4% margin, 70% billable). Swap in your ledger figures and the tables recompute.

**What the board should see (mid case; ranges are in the paper)**

| | 8% per cost centre | Ranked plan |
|---|---|---|
| 2027 opex vs 2026 | −0.5% | −0.4% |
| Run-rate opex reduction | 8.4 pts | 6.2 pts |
| 2027 profit effect | +€3.1m | +€3.8m |
| Run-rate profit effect | +€6.2m | +€5.9m |
| One-off costs | €0.8m | €0.2m |
| Dismissals | ~27 | 0 |

1. **Neither option gets 2027 opex 8% below 2026.** [CPB expects cao wages to rise 3.8% in 2027](https://www.awvn.nl/nieuws/mev-2027-wat-werkgevers-ervan-moeten-weten/), so doing nothing takes opex up 3.4% (+€2.6m). If the board means 8% below this year's actual, the real gap is about 11% of opex.
2. **In the Netherlands, dismissals cost money first and save it late.**
   - Works council advice, then the WMCO procedure (20+ exits in one UWV region), then 1–4 months' notice: exits land June–August.
   - After transition payments, settlement costs, extra attrition and lost revenue, the dismissals alone net about −€0.4m in 2027.
   - If more than one or two of the roughly 19 billable people let go had work nobody else can absorb, they cost money in 2027.
   - The reflection principle (*afspiegelingsbeginsel*) decides who goes, so you can't aim the cut at idle capacity.
3. **From 2028 the flat cut earns about €0.3m a year more on mid assumptions.** That's what 27 jobs buy, and it depends on utilisation. Over 2027–28 combined, the ranked plan is ahead in all three cases I ran. Decide the residual in April, with utilisation data.

**The approach for the board**

- **Fix the target's purpose.** Ask the board to confirm what the 8% is for: margin, cash, a covenant or the owners' return. Propose a target of €6.2m run-rate profit improvement (the value of 8% of opex), with 2027 opex at or below 2026.
- **Leaders first.** The executive team, the CEO and you included, takes 10% off fixed pay and skips 2027 indexation; senior leaders skip indexation too. 2027 leadership bonuses pay only if financial, client and people measures are all met. About €0.35m: mostly a signal, but it makes the rest credible.
- **Zero-base the €24m of non-payroll spend:** vendors, licences, cloud, offices at lease breaks, consultants, travel. Put your own bench people on contractor work, ending engagements at contract end with notice. At 8–14%, that's €1.2–2.4m in 2027.
- **Manage headcount through flows.** Expect about 72 leavers. Refill billable roles against signed demand, internal candidates first. Leave about 20 overhead roles unfilled, and stop their work so it doesn't land on those who stay.
- **Price and leakage:** indexation clauses enforced at renewal, discount approval, unbilled hours, write-offs. At a 4% margin, a wage rise you don't pass on eats most of your profit; each 1% of price is worth about €0.8m.
- **Protect** training, junior intake, quality, security and capacity in growing accounts.
- **April review.** If signed non-payroll savings are below €2m run-rate, or a practice has a structural bench where demand won't return, bring a targeted plan for that practice: redeployment first, then a voluntary scheme, with the works council in from the start. Never a percentage.
- **Cash, separately:** five fewer debtor days is worth about €1.1m, without paying small suppliers later.

**The CEO**

His instinct has merit: a flat number is easy to explain, feels fair and gives every manager ownership. Keep that. Every cost-centre owner still gets a number, a date and a measure, but the number comes from the levers.

See him privately before anything reaches the chair; the board should see one plan from the two of you. You could say: "I want us to hit the board's number in a way that still stands in 2028. Let me show you what 8% per cost centre does to our revenue and our 2027 profit."

**Three weeks**

- **Week 1:** the CEO conversation; ask the chair what the 8% is for. Pull the ledger by cost centre and vendor, payroll aggregated by cost centre (no names), utilisation and bench by practice, attrition, the renewal and lease calendar, and indexation clauses.
- **Week 2:** a first zero-base pass with cost-centre owners; account leads on renewals; leadership pay to the supervisory board. Brief the works council: it's entitled to the budget anyway, and early beats late.
- **Week 3:** replace my estimates, run a pre-mortem with the executive team ("it's 2028 and this failed: why?"), pre-wire the chair.

Have employment counsel check the WOR and WMCO steps before anything touches jobs; this isn't legal advice.

**Two questions that would change the numbers most**

1. What is the 8% for, and 8% below what: 2026 actual or the 2027 budget?
2. What are utilisation and bench by practice?

Key assumptions: 4% margin, 12% attrition, 8–14% non-payroll savings, and 30% of the billable people cut having demand others can't absorb. I've also assumed ownership doesn't change the case; if a fund owns you, I'd reframe it as value at exit. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
