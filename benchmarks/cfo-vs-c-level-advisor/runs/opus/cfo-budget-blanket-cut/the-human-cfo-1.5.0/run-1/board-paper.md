# Budget 2027: close the gap with a ranked plan, not 8% per cost centre

Draft for the board · Prepared by the CFO · Status: **draft. Every amount is an illustrative estimate** on a base scaled to what we know (600 people, payroll 68% of opex, thin positive margin) until it is replaced with ledger, payroll, CRM and HR data. Assumptions: Appendix A. Model: `budget_model.py` (rerun with real inputs; all tables below come from it and reconcile).

Amounts in € millions. "pts" = percentage points of 2026 opex. Ranges run cautious to favourable; the headline is the mid case.

---

## Recommendation

Close the 2027 gap with a ranked plan rather than 8% per cost centre: leaders first, a zero-based review of non-payroll spend, headcount managed through leavers and internal moves, and pricing discipline. Then decide in April, on evidence, whether anything more is needed.

On the illustrative base, this plan:
- brings **2027 opex to €74.7m**, 0.4% below 2026, despite a 3.8% cao wage rise;
- lifts **2027 operating profit by €3.8m** (range €2.1–5.8m), against €3.1m (range €0.7–5.2m) for the 8% cut;
- needs **no dismissals**, where the 8% cut needs about 27 (19–36).

The 8% cut delivers more opex reduction from 2028 (8.4 pts against 6.2 pts) and, on mid assumptions, about €0.3m a year more profit. That gap is real, and section 4 shows what it costs. Taken over 2027 and 2028 together, the ranked plan is ahead in every case we ran.

## Decisions requested

1. **Confirm what the 8% is for and what it is measured against.** We propose a target of **€6.2m run-rate operating profit improvement**, the value of 8% of opex, together with **2027 opex at or below 2026**.
2. **Approve the ranked plan**, with an owner and a date for each lever (section 3).
3. **Approve the leaders-first measures** (section 9).
4. **Approve the list of protected investments** (section 6).
5. **Set the April 2027 review**, and the triggers that would bring a further decision to the board (section 12).

## 1. The target and why

**What is at stake.** Our margin is thin but positive. On the illustrative base, opex is €75.0m, revenue €78.1m and operating profit €3.1m (4%). The board's 8% is a solution. The underlying problem is presumably margin resilience, a cash, covenant or owner's-return requirement, or a mix of these. Which one it is decides which levers count. Pricing improves margin but not opex. Working capital improves cash but not profit.

**The base matters as much as the percentage.** CPB forecasts cao wages rising 3.8% and inflation 2.7% in 2027. Doing nothing therefore takes opex to **€77.6m (+3.4%, +€2.6m)**.

| Interpretation | Reduction needed vs 2027 "do nothing" | pts of 2026 opex |
|---|---|---|
| A. 8% below the 2027 budget baseline | 6.21 | 8.3 |
| B. 8% below 2026 actual | 8.59 | 11.4 |

No option on the table, the 8% cut included, gets 2027 opex 8% below 2026. Under interpretation B, what the board can choose is the route to a healthier margin, and how quickly the company gets there.

**The biggest threat to next year's margin is the wage rise.** On 68% of our costs, a 3.8% rise adds about €1.9m to payroll. If it isn't passed into rates, it takes most of a €3.1m profit. Every 1% of price we realise is worth about €0.8m.

## 2. Why not 8% per cost centre

The case for it is real. It is simple, it looks fair, every manager owns a number, and it avoids a fight over who is protected. We keep those strengths: every cost-centre owner still gets a number, a date and a measure. Applied flat, though, it does the following:

- **It lands on people.** Delivery cost centres are mostly payroll, so 8% there can only come from headcount. Payroll down 8% means about 48 roles. After leavers who aren't replaced, about 27 of them (19–36) would be dismissals.
- **In a services firm, the people are the revenue.** About 70% of those roles are billable. Each billable person whose work others can't absorb brings in about €186k against a cost of about €88k. Losing that person reduces profit.
- **Dutch timing pushes the saving into the second half.** Works council advice comes first (art. 25 WOR). Then comes notification to and consultation with UWV and the unions, because 20 or more exits in one UWV region within three months counts as a collective dismissal (WMCO). Settlement agreements count towards the 20. Then UWV permits or settlement agreements, then 1–4 months' notice. Realistically, exits land between June and August 2027. Transition payments (one third of a month's salary per year of service) and settlement costs come first.
- **We can't aim it at idle capacity.** In UWV dismissals, selection follows the reflection principle (*afspiegelingsbeginsel*) within interchangeable roles and age groups. Managers don't choose. A flat cut takes a cross-section of busy and idle people alike, including juniors.
- **It cuts the efficient and the bloated alike**, training included, and it rarely lasts. One line of research found only 10% of cost programmes still showing results after three years (cited by McKinsey).
- **People we want to keep leave next.** A 1% workforce reduction has been associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). We have modelled a much smaller effect: 1–3 extra points of attrition.
- **It can't be undone.** Every other lever in this paper can be adjusted quarter by quarter.

"Do nothing" holds headcount flat. Its profit falls by up to €2.6m unless rates rise with wages.

**What the dismissals add on their own** (8% option, mid case):

| | 2027 | 2028 onwards |
|---|---|---|
| Payroll saved (exits 1 July) | 1.19 | 2.38 |
| One-off costs (transition payments, settlement premium, legal, outplacement, programme) | −0.79 | – |
| Extra voluntary attrition | −0.27 | −0.27 (2028) |

| **Net** | **−0.40** | **1.06** |

**Break-even.** In 2027 the dismissals lose money in the cautious case whatever happens to revenue. In the mid case, if more than one or two of the roughly 19 billable people let go had work nobody else can absorb, they cost money in 2027. From 2028 they pay only while fewer than about 11 of the 19 had real demand behind them. Whether any targeted reduction makes sense turns on one number: utilisation by practice. We will bring it in April.

## 6. What we protect, and why

- **Training budget and paid learning time.** Skills are what we sell. A flat 8% would cut them too.
- **Junior and trainee intake.** Without juniors today we have no seniors in five years. Juniors are also the cheapest capacity on growing accounts.
- **Quality, information security and the upkeep of core systems.** Client contracts and our reputation depend on them. If any of these is ever cut, its future cost will be written next to the saving.
- **Client-facing capacity in growing accounts.** This is where the revenue in this plan comes from.

## 7. What stops or waits

This list comes from the cost-centre review; owners confirm it in week 2.

- Internal projects without a business case or an owner.
- Reports, approval steps and meetings that nobody uses.
- Discretionary consultants.
- New tools that don't replace an old one.
- Non-essential travel and events.
- Office space beyond hybrid occupancy, at the next lease break.
- Non-billable external hires without executive approval.
- Accounts or services that lose money after a pricing fix are reviewed for exit at renewal.

When a leaver isn't replaced, their work stops or is redesigned. It does not land on the people who stay.

## 8. Headcount

- **Leavers.** We expect about 72 (60–84) at 10–14% attrition. This is an estimate; we will replace it with our attrition by quarter.
- **Billable roles.** These are refilled against signed or highly probable demand, with internal candidates and the bench first.
- **Overhead roles.** About 20 (12–28) are not refilled where the work stops. A weekly exceptions panel approves critical hires.
- **No dismissals are in this plan.** If the April review shows a structural surplus in a specific practice, we will bring a targeted plan for it. That means redeployment and retraining first, then a voluntary scheme, with the works council involved from the moment we define the problem. Involuntary exits come only as a last resort, and then once, decently, with support from the day of notice.

## 9. Leaders first

The result is the executive team's responsibility, so the executive team moves first:

- **Executive team, including the CEO and CFO:** fixed pay 10% lower for 2027 and no indexation.
- **Senior leaders:** no 2027 indexation.
- **2027 leadership bonuses:** paid only if financial, client and people measures are all met. No element rewards headcount reduction.

The saving is about €0.35m. Next to the payroll it is small. Its value is that the rest of the plan becomes credible to the people asked to carry it.

## 10. Works council, law and process

- **Works council.** With 600 employees we have one, and its information rights include the budget. We will brief it on this approach before the board meeting and share the real numbers while they can still change. Its view on workload and stopped work is free risk analysis.
- **Legal steps if jobs are affected.** Any step that affects jobs triggers works council advice (art. 25 WOR). Twenty or more exits in one UWV region within three months also trigger the WMCO procedure. Employment counsel reviews any such step before it is taken.
- **Advisers.** Tax and accounting treatment of any provision or settlement is checked with our auditor.
- **Status of this paper.** It is a financial recommendation, not legal advice.

## 12. What would change this budget: the April 2027 review

We report monthly. The board reviews the plan in April, using these triggers.

- **Signed or contracted non-payroll savings below €2.0m run-rate.** We run a second review round and bring options.
- **Renewals realising less than 0.5% above plan.** We revisit pricing and the account mix.
- **Utilisation in a practice below its target for two quarters with no pipeline.** We bring a targeted plan for that practice (section 8), not a percentage.
- **Margin or cash ahead of plan.** We release protected spend first, then frozen roles.

## 13. How we will measure it

These are agreed now and reported monthly:

- **Money:** opex by lever against plan, operating margin, cash and debtor days.
- **Delivery:** utilisation and bench by practice.
- **Clients:** client retention and satisfaction.
- **People:** regretted attrition, an engagement pulse, sickness absence (an early sign of workload), time to fill critical roles, and training hours.

All people measures are reported at team level only.
