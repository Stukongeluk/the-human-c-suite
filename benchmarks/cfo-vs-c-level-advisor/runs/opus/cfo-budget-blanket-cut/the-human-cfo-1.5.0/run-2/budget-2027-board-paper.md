# Budget 2027: reaching the opex target with a ranked plan instead of 8% per cost centre

Board meeting, [date] · Author: CFO · Draft v0.1, 21 November 2026

All amounts in € million. **Every figure in this draft is an estimate,** scaled from three facts: 600 employees, payroll at 68% of opex, and a thin but positive margin. They will be replaced with ledger, payroll and billing data in week 1. The structure and the comparison will hold; the amounts will move. Assumptions are listed in section 14.

## Summary

- We recommend closing the 2027 target with five measures, ranked by how little harm each does to clients and staff, rather than an 8% cut in every cost centre.
- On our illustrative base, the plan cuts 2027 opex by **€4.14m (6.0% of a do-nothing 2027)**, adds €0.54m of net margin, and lifts operating profit from €1.47m (do nothing) to **€6.15m (8.7%)**. By the end of 2027 opex is running 7.2% below do-nothing, with no compulsory redundancies.
- An 8% cut per cost centre books €5.48m in the budget but delivers **€2.50m (3.7%) of lower reported opex and €2.57m of profit in 2027**. Roughly 48 roles, mostly in client delivery, take months of Dutch process to leave, cost severance before they save anything, and take sold work with them.
- The mid case falls €1.34m short of 8% on opex and €0.80m short on margin. We propose to close that gap through pricing and a July checkpoint with triggers agreed in advance, not by building redundancies into the budget now.

## Decisions requested

1. **Confirm what the 8% protects** (a margin percentage, an EBITDA amount, cash or a covenant) **and its base** (a do-nothing 2027, or 2026 actual). See section 1.
2. **Approve the ranked plan as the basis for the 2027 budget,** with a target for both the year and the run-rate, an owner for each measure, and a July checkpoint.
3. **Approve the leadership measures:** the leadership bonus budgeted at half and paid only against a balanced scorecard, and a reduction in MT base pay for 2027.
4. **Note** that management will brief the works council on the approach now, before the budget is final.

Doing nothing doesn't keep costs flat. Collective wage increases and inflation add about €2.3m to opex, plus the higher Aof premium in 2027 (rate to be taken from payroll). So "8% lower" means two very different things:

| Reading of "8% lower" | 2027 opex | Reduction vs do-nothing | Profit at target (do-nothing revenue) |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) 8% below a do-nothing 2027 | 62.98 | 5.48 (8.0%) | 6.95 (9.9%) |
| (b) 8% below 2026 actual | 60.88 | 7.58 (11.1%) | 9.05 (12.9%) |

If what the board needs is a margin, an EBITDA amount or covenant headroom, we propose stating that number as the target. Pricing then counts alongside cost.

**The case for A.** It is simple to explain, it feels fair, it gives every manager ownership, and nobody can lobby for an exemption. It also cuts more opex in year 2 than B does.

**Why it falls short in a services business:**
- With payroll at 68% of opex, 8% per cost centre comes to about 48 roles, and most of them sit in client delivery, whose hours we sell.
- Works council advice, the UWV or settlement route and notice periods of one to four months mean that only €1.87m of the €3.74m payroll saving lands in 2027. Severance, outplacement, counsel and handover (€0.90m) are paid first.
- If half of the delivery roles cut were carrying sold work, revenue falls by €1.40m in 2027 and €2.80m a year after that. Each delivery FTE brings in about €155k of revenue and costs about €78k.
- Downsizing raises voluntary attrition among the people who stay. A 1% reduction in headcount was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). We assumed a far smaller effect: 14 extra leavers.

**A fair test.** Row A+ gives the blanket approach every non-payroll, pricing and leadership measure in B as well. The gap that remains (€1.24m of profit in 2027, €1.05m in year 2) comes purely from how the payroll part is reached.

If a quarter of the cut delivery roles carried sold work, it nets +0.06 in 2027 and +1.75 in year 2. If three-quarters did, it nets −1.34 and −1.05.

## 4. What we protect, and why

- **Delivery capacity with sold work.** A delivery FTE brings in about €155k against a cost of about €78k, so cutting one who carries sold work lowers profit.
- **Training (€1.02m), in full.** Cutting it 8% would save €0.08m, at a lasting cost to a business that sells skills.
- **The graduate and junior intake.** Without juniors today there are no seniors in five years.
- **Information security, compliance** and the systems client delivery runs on.
- **CAO pay increases,** which are contractual.

## 5. What stops or waits

Each MT member completes this list in week 2. Candidates:
- internal projects without a business owner or a measurable result;
- duplicate tools, and reports nobody uses;
- events and sponsorships with no evidence of pipeline;
- client work below a minimum contribution margin, re-priced or exited at renewal;
- office space beyond measured occupancy, released at lease breaks.

**Rules for refilling.** A weekly panel (COO, HR director, CFO) refills a role when one of these holds:
1. it carries sold or highly probable work;
2. it is needed for security, compliance or a regulatory duty;
3. it is a single point of failure;
4. it is in the junior intake.

Everything else is filled internally or not at all. We track overtime, absence and regretted attrition team by team, and loosen the freeze where they rise. No compulsory redundancies are planned.

## 7. Leaders first

- **Leadership bonus pool** (about €0.66m at target): budgeted at 50% and paid only if the margin target is met on a balanced scorecard. The scorecard covers margin, honestly defined (restructuring costs stay in), cash conversion, client retention and satisfaction, regretted attrition, and quality. Part of the bonus is deferred, and no part rewards a reduction in headcount. Saving: €0.33m.
- **MT base pay** cut by 10% for 2027, offered by the MT (8 people, CEO and CFO included). Saving: €0.18m.
- Total: €0.51m. Next to payroll it saves little. What it buys is credibility when we ask 600 people to carry a hiring freeze.

## 8. The gap that remains, and how we would close it

| | Target (a) | Plan B, mid | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opex reduction | 5.48 | 4.14 | 1.34 |
| Effect on profit (opex + net pricing) | 5.48 | 4.68 | 0.80 |

- **Pricing.** Passing the full CAO increase through where contracts allow (3.8% instead of the 2.5% assumed) is worth about €0.89m, enough to close the margin gap on its own. Many contracts need indexation notice before 1 January, so this step can't wait for the board.
- **The freeze.** Extend it where utilisation data shows unsold capacity.
- **July checkpoint.** If the run-rate is more than €0.5m behind plan, step two consists of voluntary measures designed with the works council: voluntary reductions in hours, unpaid leave, and a voluntary leaving scheme with decent terms. Compulsory redundancies come only after that, once, with a social plan.

**Target (b),** €7.58m below do-nothing, is out of reach in 2027 for every option, A included (A delivers €2.50m). If (b) is what the board means, the honest choices are a margin target or a two-year path.

Cost programmes rarely stick. One line of research cited by McKinsey found that only 10% showed sustained results three years later. So every measure has an owner and a date, and we track it monthly against the run-rate, not only against the budget.

## 10. What would change this budget

- **July checkpoint:** run-rate more than €0.5m behind plan → step two (section 8).
- **Revenue more than 5% below plan for two consecutive months** → the problem is revenue, not cost; review pipeline and pricing. If the drop is temporary, reduce hours rather than people.
- **Regretted attrition above 1.5 × last year's level, or overtime and absence rising** in a team → loosen the freeze there.
- **Client retention or satisfaction falling** → review the measures behind it before going further.

## 11. Employees, works council and the law (Netherlands)

- **Works council.** The OR is entitled to the budget and the financial outlook (art. 31a WOR). It has an advisory right on reorganisations and major reductions of activities (art. 25 WOR), and that advice must be requested in time to influence the decision, with appeal to the Enterprise Chamber. For plan B we brief the OR in week 2 and share the real numbers. A freeze and vendor savings usually need no formal advice; counsel to confirm.
- **Option A would also need:**
  - art. 25 advice before the decision;
  - a WMCO notification to UWV and the unions if 20 or more dismissals fall in one UWV work area within three months (employer-initiated settlement agreements count), made before decisions are final;
  - on the UWV route, selection by the afspiegelingsbeginsel (by age group within interchangeable roles), so managers can't choose who goes;
  - the statutory transition payment (one-third of a month's salary per year of service);
  - notice of one to four months, minus the time the UWV procedure took;
  - a check of the applicable CAO for social plan obligations.
- **Accounting.** Any restructuring cost stays in reported opex and in the bonus measure, and is not labelled one-off. Whether a provision could be booked in 2026 depends on a detailed formal plan announced before year-end; that is for the auditor to confirm.
- This paper is not legal or tax advice. Employment counsel and the auditor review it before the budget is final.

## 12. How we will measure it

Reviewed monthly by the MT and quarterly by the board:
- **Money:** opex against plan for each measure and owner, for the year and the run-rate; margin; cash and DSO; price realisation.
- **People:** regretted attrition, time to fill critical roles, overtime and absence, engagement pulse, internal moves.
- **Clients:** retention, satisfaction, and utilisation and bench by team.
