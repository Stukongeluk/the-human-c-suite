**Why 8% everywhere backfires in a services firm**

With payroll at 68% of opex, 8% everywhere comes to about 48 roles, mostly in delivery, and delivery hours are what you sell.
- Works council advice, the UWV or settlement route and one to four months' notice mean only €1.9m of the €3.7m payroll saving lands in 2027.
- About €0.9m of severance, outplacement, counsel and handover goes out first.
- If half the delivery roles cut carried sold work, revenue falls by about €1.4m in 2027 and €2.8m a year after. The cuts pay for themselves in 2027 only if fewer than 1 in 5 of all the roles cut carried sold work.
- Training gets cut 8% too, and attrition rises: a 1% downsizing was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year ([Trevor & Nyberg, 2008](https://clipsheet.strategiccommunication.wisc.edu/?p=218008)).

So the blanket books 8% and delivers about 3.7% lower reported opex in 2027.

| Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 1.5 | 2.1% | 0 |

The third row is the fair test. Even with every other measure added, cutting 48 roles instead of freezing 22 still costs about €1.2m of profit in 2027 and €1m in 2028.

**The plan**

1. **Zero-base third-party spend:** external hires, software and cloud, consultants, travel and events, office and lease cars. About €2.9m (range €1.6–4.3m). Training is protected.
2. **Pricing and billing:** index contracts for the wage rise, chase unbilled hours and unapproved discounts, and re-price the weakest contracts at renewal. About €0.7m of margin. Many contracts need indexation notice before 1 January, so this can't wait for the board.
3. **Leaders first:** budget the leadership bonus at half, paid only on a balanced scorecard (margin, cash, client retention, regretted attrition). The MT, CEO and you included, offers 10% of base pay for 2027. About €0.5m: small, but it makes the freeze credible.
4. **Stop low-value work,** named by each MT member, so the freeze doesn't become overwork.
5. **Hiring freeze with exceptions, internal moves first:** refill only roles carrying sold work, security roles, single points of failure and the junior intake. That means about 22 fewer roles through normal attrition (600 → ~578): about €0.8m in 2027 and €1.7m a year after.

**The gap that remains**

The mid case is about €1.3m short of 8% on opex and €0.8m short on margin. Passing the full wage rise into prices where contracts allow is worth about €0.9m on its own. I'd add a July checkpoint with triggers agreed in advance:
- if the run-rate is more than €0.5m behind, step two is voluntary measures designed with the works council (reduced hours, a voluntary leaving scheme);
- compulsory redundancies come only after that, and only once.

**Two questions that change the numbers**

1. **What is the 8% protecting, and against what base?** 8% below a do-nothing 2027 is €5.5m. 8% below this year's actual is €7.6m, because wages and inflation add about €2.3m before anyone acts: CPB forecasts 3.8% CAO wage growth for 2027 ([AWVN](https://www.awvn.nl/nieuws/mev-2027-wat-werkgevers-ervan-moeten-weten/)). No option reaches the second figure in 2027, the blanket included. If the real target is a margin, EBITDA or a covenant, put that on the slide instead.
2. **How many of the 600 are billable, and what are utilisation and bench by team?** That sets how far the freeze can safely go, and what any headcount cut would cost in revenue.

- probably a WMCO notification;
- the afspiegelingsbeginsel for selection;
- transition payments.

Check this with employment counsel; it isn't legal advice. Any restructuring cost stays in reported opex, not labelled "one-off".
