# CFO view: €300m buyback vs. €120m investment

## Bottom line

Recommend a split rather than picking a side. Approve the COO's €120m programme in gated tranches. Authorise a buyback of up to €150m, paced over about 12 months under a published cash-return policy. Take this year's few-cent EPS miss openly rather than buying shares to cover it.

**Confidence:** medium-high on the direction. Low on the €150m figure for now: most of the inputs below are my assumptions, not your data, and three numbers (listed at the end) could change the size.

Tags used: [GIVEN] = from your message, [VERIFIED] = checked against a published source, [ASSUMED] = my placeholder until you replace it.

## What

- **Both asks together are more than you have.** €300m + €120m = €420m against €400m net cash [GIVEN]. That's before your seasonal working-capital swing, and before IFRS 16 lease liabilities, which for a store network are probably larger than the cash [ASSUMED]. So the real question is how to size each one.
- **Whether the buyback saves EPS depends on how much of the year is left.** Take an illustrative company with a €2bn market cap. Bought evenly over the last 6 months, €300m adds about 5 cents to this year's EPS. Over the last ~5 weeks it adds about 1 cent. In that window, the EU buyback safe harbour also caps daily purchases at 25% of average daily volume, which would probably stop you buying €300m at all [VERIFIED rule; ASSUMED company numbers]. An accelerated buyback through a bank solves the timing problem but not the cash problem.
- **The investment will cost more EPS than the miss does.** Training is expensed, and under the IFRIC's 2021 agenda decision cloud configuration work usually is too. If ~40% of the €120m hits the P&L over two years, that's ~22 cents a year on the same illustrative numbers [ASSUMED split]. You'll be having the guidance conversation either way. Buying this year's few cents just moves the miss to next year, with €300m less cash.
- **This is the textbook pattern.** Firms that would miss EPS forecasts by a few cents are much more likely to buy back shares, and they then cut employment, capex and R&D over the next four quarters (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, *JFE* 2016) [VERIFIED]. To be fair to the activist, the same study finds that on average the market doesn't punish these buybacks. The damage shows up in later operating performance at the firms that cut investment to pay for them.
- **Both sides have a fair point.** Idle cash earning ~1.5% after tax against a ~7% earnings yield destroys value, so a real return belongs in the plan [ASSUMED rates]. And €120m needs gates: on average, large IT projects run 45% over budget and deliver 56% less value than predicted (McKinsey with the University of Oxford, 5,400+ projects) [VERIFIED].

## Why this matters

The board is choosing between an EPS number this quarter and whether the stores and the website can compete on service in three years. The split gives shareholders real cash and keeps the business that produces it. It also saves you from explaining an engineered beat this year followed by a bigger miss next year.

## How to act

1. **Set the liquidity floor.** Use weekly net cash for the last three years, the seasonal low, a stress case for a bad peak season, and lease-adjusted net debt. My placeholder floor is €130m, and that's what makes €150m work. The buyback size adjusts to the floor, not the other way round. Owner: CFO / Treasury. Deadline: before the board pre-read.
2. **Ask for a business case, not just a budget.** It should cover KPIs (online conversion, services attach rate, store productivity, staff retention), payback and return vs. cost of capital, the opex/capex split by year, three stage gates, and a contingency. Owner: COO, with CTO and CHRO. Deadline: before the board pre-read.
3. **Check disclosure and execution.** Is the expected miss inside information that must be disclosed now under Art. 17 of the Market Abuse Regulation? How much headroom is left in the AGM buyback authorisation? Can a broker-led programme keep buying through closed periods? Owner: General Counsel and brokers. Deadline: within one week.
4. **Engage before deciding.** The Chair should meet the activist to walk through the framework and the floor logic, and sound out your top ten holders. Owner: Chair, with CEO and CFO. Deadline: before the board vote.
5. **Make one announcement, then start buying.** Publish the trading update, the investment programme, the buyback and the cash-return policy together. No purchases before it's out. Owner: CEO / CFO / IR. Deadline: on board approval.

## Risks

- **The activist escalates** (public letter, AGM resolution, demand for a board seat) if €150m reads as a token. Mitigation: a published policy that returns cash above the floor at each full-year result. If trading and the gates allow, that could reach their €300m over two years.
- **The floor has to be higher than €130m.** Then the buyback shrinks, which looks like backsliding if you've already named a number. Don't name one externally until step 1 is done.
- **The platform overruns or the service pilot doesn't move the KPIs.** No rollout tranche without pilot results. Decide now that overruns need fresh board approval and don't come out of the floor.
- **The shares aren't actually cheap.** If the price is above your own view of intrinsic value, return the cash as a special dividend instead. EPS accretion is not the same as creating value.

## Your decision

- **Option A, the activist plan:** €300m buyback this year, investment shrunk or deferred. Best EPS next year (~+24 cents run-rate, illustrative). It meets guidance only if enough of the year is left, leaves ~€100m net cash before seasonality, and shelves the service and online transformation.
- **Option B, the split (recommended):** €120m gated, a buyback of up to €150m over 12 months, a published policy and an honest guidance update. You take the miss, and next year's EPS is lower than under A while the investment runs. The activist gets cash now and a policy for more.
- **Option C, invest and hold:** spend €120m and keep ~€280m. This is the safest balance sheet, but it hands the activist a fair argument about idle cash.

**Recommendation: B.** It's the only option that funds the business, returns real cash and still holds up against next year's numbers.

## Three numbers that would change this

1. **Net cash at your seasonal low, and the date the €400m was measured.** If it's a post-peak-season figure, the real headroom may be much smaller.
2. **Months left in the fiscal year, and your average daily trading volume.** Together they decide whether the activist's EPS claim is even true.
3. **The COO programme's return and its opex share.** If it can't show a return above your cost of capital, the answer moves towards a smaller investment and a bigger buyback.
