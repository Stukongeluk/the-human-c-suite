**CFO: €300M buyback vs €120M investment, recommendation to the board**

**BOTTOM LINE**
Don't pick one or the other. Fund the €120M programme in stage-gated phases. Return the cash that is actually surplus through a buyback, starting with a €100M first tranche now. Reset EPS guidance instead of buying shares to hit it.
Confidence: high on the structure, low on the exact buyback size. Most of the sizing inputs below are my assumptions until you put in your own cash low point.

**WHAT**
- **You can't do both in full.** €300M + €120M = €420M against €400M of net cash. Paying both out of today's balance leaves you €20M net debt before any seasonal swing. The activist's ask on its own is 75% of your cash. *[Verified: your numbers. High]*
- **The number that matters is your cash low point during the year, not the €400M.** An electronics retailer's cash moves with the inventory build and with supplier payment timing. On my base case the distributable surplus is about €110M, and depending on how deep your low point is the range runs from €10M to €160M (table below). *[Assumed: €100M seasonal swing, €50M headroom, 15% contingency, zero free cash flow. Low until you replace them]*
- **The buyback hardly moves this year's EPS unless you buy fast.** Illustrative case: 100M shares at €20, forecast EPS €1.45 against €1.50 guidance. €300M bought evenly through H2 adds about 4.5c. Started in Q4, it adds about 2c. To buy €300M in H2 inside the MAR safe harbour (no more than 25% of average daily volume) your stock would need about €9.6M of trading a day. €300M would also be 15% of the shares, which is above the ~10% standing buyback authority many companies hold. *[Assumed: illustrative share data. Medium]*
- **The investment widens the EPS miss whatever you decide on the buyback.** Training costs are expensed under IAS 38. Configuring or customising a cloud (SaaS) platform is usually expensed too, under the 2021 IFRIC agenda decision. At 25% tax and 100M shares, every €10M of spend expensed this year costs about 7.5c of EPS. A buyback can't cover that. Approving the programme means resetting guidance, unless the programme gets quietly cut back to save a few cents, which is the worse outcome. *[Accounting: High. Size: Medium]*
- **The €120M has to earn its place too.** At a 9% hurdle rate it needs about €24M a year of extra after-tax cash flow for 7 years, or about €31M a year if the benefit only lasts 5. The COO's case must show that against a do-nothing case in which the old platform keeps losing customers, not against flat earnings. *[Assumed: 9% hurdle. Medium]*

How the surplus moves with the seasonal swing: a €50M swing gives €160M, €150M gives €60M and €200M gives €10M. Each €50M of free cash flow after the dividend adds €50M. The €100M first tranche is covered as long as your swing is no more than about €110M (with zero free cash flow) or about €160M (with €50M).

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
A €300M buyback is permanent. A few cents of EPS matter for one reporting period. In a business that runs on supplier credit, the expensive way to miss is this: you hit the number, then reach the cash low point with a thinner balance sheet, and the credit insurers behind your suppliers react. If they cut limits, suppliers shorten their terms, and that drains cash faster than the buyback added EPS.

**HOW TO ACT**
1. Calculate the real surplus: the cash low point over the last 24 months, the minimum headroom, programme phasing and lease obligations. **Owner:** CFO and treasury. **Deadline:** before the board papers go out.
2. Get the COO's business case into board shape: phasing by quarter, capex/opex split, KPIs, a do-nothing case, and three stage gates with kill criteria. **Owner:** COO with CFO. **Deadline:** before the board papers go out.
3. Ask the board to approve the package:
   - the programme, with gate 1 funded
   - a €100M first buyback tranche
   - a published capital-allocation policy: a minimum liquidity floor, with surplus above it returned every year

   **Owner:** board. **Deadline:** next board meeting.
4. Before announcing, check with the GC whether the expected EPS miss is already inside information. If it is, it may have to be disclosed first. Under the safe harbour you can't buy back while you are delaying disclosure of inside information, unless the programme is time-scheduled or run independently by a bank. **Owner:** CFO and GC. **Deadline:** before the board meeting.
5. Meet the activist with the framework and the numbers before anything is announced. Then reset guidance at the next trading update: show the programme costs separately, and show EPS both with and without the buyback. Ask the remuneration committee to take the buyback effect out of incentive EPS. If management pay (yours included) vests on EPS, the board will ask about it, so deal with it before they do. **Owner:** CEO, CFO, chair and remco chair. **Deadline:** before announcement / next trading update.

**RISKS**
- **The activist escalates** (public letter, AGM resolution) if this reads as a no. It shouldn't: they get real money now, a policy in writing, and if free cash flow holds, a credible path to their €300M over about two years instead of one.
- **The programme overruns or under-delivers.** Funding is released gate by gate, and gate 2 money is released only when gate 1 KPIs are met. Pilot stores are measured against control stores.
- **Trained staff leave.** Store turnover erodes the value of the training. Pair the new service model with pay and progression for the people who are trained, or the money walks out of the door.
- **Weakest point of this recommendation:** if your seasonal swing turns out small and your free cash flow strong, I'm holding back cash the activist is right to want. Step 1 settles that, which is why it comes first.

**YOUR DECISION**
- **Option A: €300M buyback, defer the programme.** It probably hits guidance and wins over the activist this year. The price is a weaker platform and a thin balance sheet at the cash low point.
- **Option B: both in full.** €20M net debt before seasonality. This option is the most exposed to the working-capital low point and to credit insurers.
- **Option C: programme + buyback of verified surplus (€100M now, more at the half-year if the surplus is confirmed) + guidance reset.** It protects liquidity and future earnings and still returns real money. The cost is a guidance miss and a harder conversation with the activist.
- **Option D: programme, no buyback.** Cash sits earning about 2%, and the activist wins the argument.

**Recommendation: C.** It returns the cash that is actually spare and funds the business your share price depends on. A few cents of EPS shouldn't decide a permanent €300M move.
