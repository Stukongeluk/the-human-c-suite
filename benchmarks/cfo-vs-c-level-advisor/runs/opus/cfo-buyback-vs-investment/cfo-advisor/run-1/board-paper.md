# Board paper (draft): Capital allocation, transformation programme and share buyback

**From:** CFO
**Status:** Draft. Figures marked [ASSUMED] are placeholders to replace with company data before circulation. Figures marked [●] still need filling in.

---

## 1. Decision requested

The board is asked to approve:

1. The €120M e-commerce and store-service transformation programme, funded in three stage gates. Gate 1 is released now and the full envelope is ring-fenced in cash.
2. A first share buyback tranche of €100M, for capital reduction, to start after the disclosure checks in section 6.
3. A published capital allocation policy that returns surplus cash above a stated liquidity floor every year.
4. A reset of current-year EPS guidance at the next trading update, with programme costs shown separately.

## 2. Summary recommendation

The activist's request (€300M buyback this year) and the COO's request (€120M programme) together come to €420M, which is more than our €400M net cash. If both were paid out of today's balance we would hold €20M of net debt before seasonal working-capital swings.

The recommendation is to fund the programme first, then return the cash that is genuinely surplus. On the base case that surplus is about €110M, which supports a €100M first tranche now and further tranches as surplus is confirmed. We should not use a buyback to close a few cents of EPS gap. The programme's own in-year costs will widen that gap by more than any buyback can close it.

## 3. Options considered

| Option | What it does | Main benefit | Main cost / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. €300M buyback, programme deferred | Returns 75% of net cash this year | Probably meets EPS guidance and satisfies the activist this year | Thin balance sheet at the seasonal low point. Platform and service model fall further behind. |
| B. Both in full | €420M out against €400M net cash | Everyone gets what they asked for | €20M net debt before seasonality. The option most exposed to the cash low point and to credit insurers' limits on supplier credit. |
| **C. Programme + surplus buyback + guidance reset (recommended)** | €120M ring-fenced and gated. €100M buyback now, more from verified surplus. | Protects liquidity and future earnings, and still returns real capital | Guidance miss this year. A harder conversation with the activist. |
| D. Programme, no buyback | Holds surplus cash | Maximum liquidity | Idle cash earning about 2% against a cost of equity near 9-10%. Gives the activist a fair argument. |

## 4. How much cash is actually surplus

Reported net cash is a point-in-time figure. For an electronics retailer it is shaped by the inventory build and supplier payment timing. The figure that limits what we can return is net cash at the **intra-year low point**, after keeping minimum headroom.

| Item | €M | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Reported net cash | 400 | Reported |
| Seasonal working-capital swing to the intra-year low point | (100) | [ASSUMED]: replace with the actual low point over the last 24 months |
| Minimum liquidity headroom at the low point | (50) | [ASSUMED]: covers store cash, payment-provider float and a buffer |
| Transformation programme, ring-fenced | (120) | COO request |
| Programme contingency (~15%) | (20) | [ASSUMED] |
| Free cash flow over the next 12 months after the ordinary dividend | 0 | [ASSUMED]: deliberately conservative; replace with forecast |
| **Distributable surplus** | **110** | |

**Sensitivity of the surplus to the seasonal swing (€M, zero free cash flow):**

| Seasonal swing | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distributable surplus | 160 | 110 | 60 | 10 |

Each €50M of free cash flow after the dividend adds €50M of surplus. The €100M first tranche is covered if the swing is no more than €110M with zero free cash flow, or no more than €160M with €50M of free cash flow.

**Not counted on purpose:**
- Undrawn credit facilities. They are backup liquidity, not buyback funding. Using them indirectly to fund a buyback would mean levering a low-margin retailer to retire equity.

**To check before circulation:**
- Lease liabilities. "Net cash" normally excludes IFRS 16 leases, but lease-adjusted leverage is what rating agencies and supplier credit insurers look at. [●] Lease-adjusted net debt / EBITDA before and after each option.
- Credit insurer sensitivity. If insurers cut limits on our key suppliers, those suppliers will shorten payment terms. That would drain working capital at exactly the point where the balance sheet is thinnest.

## 5. The transformation programme: what it must prove

**Hurdle.** €120M at a 9% hurdle rate [ASSUMED] requires about €24M a year of incremental after-tax cash flow over 7 years, or about €31M a year over 5 years. (Annuity: 120 × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n).) The business case must measure this against a **do-nothing case**, not against flat earnings. The question is what happens to online share, services revenue and store traffic if the current platform and service model carry on as they are.

**Comparison with the buyback.** At the illustrative price below, the earnings yield is 7.25% (€1.45 / €20). If the shares are fairly valued, a buyback returns roughly the cost of equity. The programme has to beat that after allowing for execution risk. If the board believes the shares are materially undervalued, the case for the buyback tranche gets stronger, but the case for the liquidity floor does not change.

**Stage gates.** The amounts are placeholders for the COO to confirm.

| Gate | Scope | Funding | Released when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Platform design, vendor selection, foundation build. Service-model training pilot in about 10% of stores, compared against matched control stores. | €30M [ASSUMED] | Now |
| 2 | Platform migration. Training rollout to about 50% of stores. | €50M [ASSUMED] | Pilot stores beat control stores on services attach rate and NPS; platform spend within ±10% of budget |
| 3 | Full rollout. Old platform decommissioned. | €40M [ASSUMED] | Gate 2 KPIs met; online conversion and cost-to-serve on target |
| Contingency | Held centrally | €20M | Board approval only |

**KPIs:**
- Online conversion rate
- Platform availability and cost-to-serve
- Services attach rate (installation, setup, repair, protection plans) in pilot stores against control stores
- Customer NPS
- Staff retention in trained stores
- Spend against budget

**Kill criteria** (agree them now, not in the middle of the programme):
- No measurable services uplift in the pilot against control after [●] months
- Gate 1 cost overrun above 20%

**People.** The training only pays off if the trained staff stay. Store turnover will erode the investment unless the service model comes with a pay and progression path for the colleagues who take it on. The budget should include that cost explicitly.

**Accounting and P&L phasing.**
- Staff training is expensed as incurred (IAS 38.69(b)).
- Configuration and customisation of a cloud (SaaS) platform is usually expensed as well, unless it creates code we control (IFRIC agenda decision, 2021).
- [●] The COO and finance to agree the capex/opex split and its phasing with the auditors.
- At a 25% tax rate and 100M shares (illustrative), every €10M expensed costs about 7.5c of EPS.

## 6. The buyback: size, execution and constraints

**Size.** €100M first tranche now. Further tranches are paid from verified surplus under the policy in section 7, reviewed at half-year and full-year results.

**Purpose and form.** Capital reduction, with the shares cancelled. This is one of the purposes that qualifies for the MAR safe harbour.

**Execution constraints to confirm with the GC:**
- **Volume.** Under the safe harbour we can buy no more than 25% of average daily volume per day (Delegated Regulation 2016/1052, Art. 3).
  - Illustrative: €300M in roughly 125 H2 trading days needs about €9.6M a day of trading in our stock. €100M needs about €3.2M a day.
  - [●] Our actual average daily volume.
- **Authority.** At the illustrative market cap of €2.0bn, €300M would be 15% of the shares. That is above the ~10% standing authority many issuers hold, so it would need an EGM.
  - [●] Our remaining authority under the current AGM resolution.
- **Inside information and closed periods.** Under the safe harbour we cannot trade during closed periods or while we are delaying disclosure of inside information, unless the programme is time-scheduled or run independently by a bank (Art. 4).
  - The GC should confirm whether the expected EPS miss is already inside information that needs prompt disclosure. If it is, it has to be disclosed before the buyback starts.
  - Recommendation: a non-discretionary bank mandate.

**Why a buyback rather than a special dividend:** a buyback can be paused at the cash low point or if trading weakens. A special dividend cannot be paused, and it sets expectations.

**Ownership effect.** A buyback increases the percentage held by shareholders who don't sell, including the activist. For example, a 5% holder becomes 5.9% after a 15% buyback. [●] Check the effect on disclosure thresholds.

## 7. Proposed capital allocation policy (for publication)

1. **Liquidity floor.** Net cash at the intra-year low point stays at or above €[●]M, plus lease-adjusted net debt / EBITDA at or below [●]x.
2. **Ordinary dividend:** [●]% payout, unchanged.
3. **Organic investment** that clears the hurdle rate, funded through stage gates.
4. **Surplus above the floor** returned to shareholders through buybacks, assessed at half-year and full-year results.

If free cash flow holds, this policy gives the activist a credible route to their €300M over roughly two years rather than one. That is the message to take to them.

## 8. EPS guidance

**Illustrative only.** Assumptions:
- 100M shares at €20 (market cap €2.0bn)
- Forecast EPS €1.45 against guidance of €1.50
- 25% tax rate
- 2% pre-tax yield on cash
- No price impact from our own buying (this flatters the buyback)

| Scenario | Shares retired | In-year EPS | Against €1.50 guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| No buyback | 0 | €1.450 | −5.0c |
| €100M, bought evenly through H2 | 5.0M | €1.465 | −3.5c |
| €300M, bought evenly through H2 | 15.0M | €1.495 | −0.5c |
| €300M, Q4 only | 15.0M | €1.472 | −2.8c |
| €300M, bought evenly over the full year | 15.0M | €1.543 | +4.3c (only possible if buying started at the beginning of the year) |

Method: in-year EPS = (net income − after-tax interest lost on the average cash deployed) / weighted-average shares. Shares bought evenly from point s to point e of the year reduce weighted-average shares by N × (e − s) / 2.

**What this shows:**
- A buyback's EPS lift is real, but most of it lands in the following year. On a run-rate basis, €300M adds about 14% to EPS and €100M about 4%.
- As a tool for this year's guidance it is weak. It depends on buying early and fast, and liquidity may not allow that.
- Expensing programme costs this year cuts EPS by about 7.5c per €10M. Approving the programme therefore means resetting guidance whatever is decided on the buyback.

**Recommendation:**
- Reset guidance at the next trading update, on a reported basis. Show programme costs separately so investors can see the underlying trend, and show EPS both with and without the buyback.
- Ask the remuneration committee to take the buyback effect out of incentive EPS for management (including the CFO). That removes any suggestion that the buyback was sized to meet pay targets.

## 9. Activist engagement

The chair, CEO and CFO meet the activist before any announcement. We show them:
- the surplus calculation
- the liquidity floor and the reasons for it
- the €100M first tranche
- the policy and the expected path for future returns

We negotiate on the **timing** of returns, not on the **floor**. The board's duty is to the company and all its shareholders. The policy gives every shareholder the same transparent rule.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Trigger | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Activist escalates (public letter, AGM resolution) | The package reads as a refusal | Real money now, a written policy, published numbers, and a path to €300M if free cash flow holds |
| Liquidity squeeze at the cash low point | Seasonal swing deeper than modelled, or credit insurers cut limits | Size the buyback against the low point, not the reported figure. Tranches can be paused. Facilities held back. |
| Programme overrun or under-delivery | Gate KPIs missed | Funding released gate by gate. Kill criteria. Contingency released only by the board. |
| Training value lost to staff turnover | Attrition in trained stores | Pay and progression tied to the service model. Retention tracked as a gate KPI. |
| Market reads the guidance reset as weakness | Miss announced without context | Announce the reset together with the investment case and the capital return in one update |
| **Weakest point of this recommendation** | The swing turns out small and free cash flow strong | Then the surplus is larger and the second tranche comes sooner. The surplus calculation (section 4) decides this, not negotiation. |
