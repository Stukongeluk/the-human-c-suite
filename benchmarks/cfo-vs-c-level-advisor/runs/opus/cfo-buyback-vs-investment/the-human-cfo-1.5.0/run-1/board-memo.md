# Use of €400m net cash: invest €120m in stages, return the cash above a liquidity floor, and report EPS as it is

Board paper: draft for discussion · From: CFO · November 2026
All figures are in € millions. (E) marks an estimate or placeholder that finance will replace with actuals before the meeting.

## 1. Recommendation

1. Approve the COO's €120m programme (e-commerce platform and store service model) in **two gated phases**, provided the business case clears our hurdle rate.
2. Adopt a **capital allocation framework**, applied in this order: (a) a liquidity floor at our seasonal cash low; (b) investment that clears the hurdle rate; (c) the ordinary dividend policy, unchanged; (d) all cash above that goes back to shareholders.
3. Authorise a **time-scheduled or broker-led buyback** sized to the cash above the floor. On central estimates that is **about €100m (E)**, within a range of €0–170m depending on the seasonal low.
4. **Do not size or time the buyback to meet this year's EPS guidance.** Update the market on the expected shortfall of a few cents, on a timetable counsel advises.

## 2. Decisions requested

| # | Decision | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Liquidity floor at the intra-year low | €80m (E) |
| 2 | Release of phase 1 of the €120m | €40–50m (E); phase 2 decided at a gate review |
| 3 | Buyback programme | Up to about €100m (E): time-scheduled or broker-led, within the AGM authorisation |

## 3. Commitments first: how much of the €400m is genuinely excess

Reported net cash in consumer electronics retail is usually flattered by supplier payables from peak season. What counts is net cash at the intra-year low, after keeping enough to reassure suppliers and their credit insurers and to absorb a bad quarter. If we drain cash, credit insurers may cut cover on our suppliers, and our payment terms would then tighten exactly when we can least afford it.

**Genuine excess = €400m − €120m committed investment − seasonal gap − floor**

| Seasonal gap (reported vs intra-year low) | Floor €60m | Floor €80m | Floor €100m |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 170 | 150 | 130 |
| 100 | 120 | **100** | 80 |
| 150 | 70 | 50 | 30 |
| 200 | 20 | 0 | −20 (no buyback) |

Assumptions: the €400m excludes IFRS 16 lease liabilities, which lenders and rating agencies may count. Finance will replace the seasonal gap and the floor with the 13-week and full-year cash profile.

## 4. The €120m: what it must prove

| Phase | Amount | Scope | Gate measures | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 40–50 (E) | Platform foundations; service-model pilot in a set of stores, with comparable control stores; training in paid work time | Delivery against milestones and budget; online conversion; service attach rate and service margin; customer scores; staff retention and regretted attrition in pilot stores vs control | Board gate review after about 6–9 months (E) |
| 2 | 70–80 (E) | Roll-out to remaining stores and the full platform | Phase 1 measures met; business case refreshed on pilot results | Released on evidence; anything not released goes back to shareholders |

- **Hurdle rate:** our WACC plus a premium for delivery risk, to be confirmed. The COO's case should show return and payback by phase, with a downside case.
- **Delivery risk:** across 1,471 IT projects the average cost overrun was 27%, and about one in six overran by around 200% on average (Flyvbjerg & Budzier). For illustration, if €80m (E) of the €120m is platform spend, the average overrun would take it to about €102m, and a tail outcome to about €240m. **Stop rule:** if phase 1 is more than 20% (E) over budget or misses its measures, phase 2 is re-planned or stopped.
- **Accounting:** training is expensed, and configuration and customisation of cloud software is often expensed too (IFRS Interpretations Committee agenda decision, April 2021). The auditors should confirm how much of the €120m hits this year's P&L. We show it openly rather than adjust it away.
- **People:** store managers and staff help design the service model in the pilot stores. Training happens in paid work time, with stable schedules during the roll-out. The works council is involved early wherever it has a say in how store work is organised.

## 5. Returning the excess

- **Programme:** time-scheduled or lead-managed by a broker who decides timing independently. That lets it run lawfully through closed periods (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/1052, Art. 4(2)). It is announced with a maximum amount and duration, stays within the AGM authorisation, and buys no more than 25% of average daily volume a day (Art. 3(3)). The terms let the board stop it.
- **Valuation test:** buy back only while the board judges the shares to trade below intrinsic value. Otherwise, return the cash as a special dividend.
- **Top-up commitment:** after each year-end, return cash above the floor plus committed investment, including any phase 2 money not released.
- **Cost:** €100m returned gives up about €1.9m a year of after-tax interest (E: 2.50% ECB deposit rate, 25% tax).

## 6. What we won't do

- Size or time a buyback to meet an EPS target.
- Cut training, store staffing or maintenance to fund a payout.
- Borrow to fund a payout.
- Label recurring costs as one-off. Every adjusted figure is reconciled to the reported one.

## 7. Options compared

Illustrative, assuming a seasonal gap of €100m (E).

| Option | Returned | Invested (2–3 yrs) | Net cash after | At intra-year low | Interest given up on cash returned (after tax, per year) | EPS this year | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. €300m buyback, investment deferred | 300 | 0 | 100 | 0 | 5.6 | Meets guidance if bought early enough | No |
| B. Both in full | 300 | 120 | −20 | −120 | 5.6 | Meets guidance, less costs expensed | No |
| C. Invest only | 0 | 120 | 280 | 180 | 0 | Misses by a few cents | Partly (gates) |
| **D. Recommended** | **~100** | **120, gated** | **180** | **80** | **1.9** | **Misses by a few cents, explained** | **Mostly (gates, programme can stop)** |
| E. Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 400 | 300 | 0 | Misses | Yes |

A and B leave no buffer at the seasonal low, and B goes into net debt. C and E leave idle cash and an unresolved activist. D is the only option that both builds the business and returns the cash we don't need.

## 8. EPS and guidance

**A buyback moves this year's EPS much less than its full-year effect.** EPS uses the weighted-average share count.

| When shares are bought | Share of the full-year effect counted this year |
|---|---|
| Evenly over the last 2 months | 8% |
| Evenly over the last quarter | 12.5% |
| In one block at the start of the last quarter | 25% |
| Evenly over the last 6 months | 25% |
| Evenly over the whole year | 50% |

**Volume limits how fast we can buy.** At 25% of average daily volume, buying €300m takes 240 trading days at €5m a day of trading, 120 days at €10m, 60 days at €20m and 30 days at €40m. Finance will run the activist's claim against our actual calendar, share price and trading volume.

**Draft message to the market** (to be agreed with counsel):
> "We expect full-year EPS of about €[x], slightly below our guidance of €[y]. We are investing €120m over [2–3] years in our online platform and a new in-store service model, in stages with published milestones. Under our capital allocation framework, we are returning €[z]m now and will return all cash above our liquidity needs and committed investment each year."

**Incentives:** where executive bonuses pay on EPS, the buyback's effect is taken out of the calculation, and the shortfall falls on executive bonuses, the CFO's included, not on the training budget.

## 9. The shareholders' view

- **The activist's strongest points:** retailers have overspent on IT; cash earning under 2% after tax costs shareholders when equity costs far more; and missing guidance costs credibility. Our answers are the gates, the stop rule and the top-up commitment. A small miss explained by a clear plan costs less credibility than a beat that analysts can trace to a lower share count.
- **The pattern to avoid:** firms that would just have missed analysts' EPS forecasts were much more likely to buy back shares, and those buybacks were associated with cuts in employment and investment (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, 2016).
- **The long-term case:** companies classified as long-term grew revenue 47% more and earnings 36% more than the rest (McKinsey Global Institute, 2017; US companies, 2001–2015). In our sector, Best Buy's turnaround improved store staff training, pay and benefits and treated cutting people as the last resort. Its share price went from a 2012 low of $11 to $110 by 2020. That is one company with many factors at work.

## 10. Sensitivities and what would change this recommendation

- **The seasonal low and the floor** are the biggest variables. A shallow low with strong free cash flow supports a larger buyback, up to about €170m. A deep low means none this year.
- **If phase 1 fails its gate,** we stop or re-plan phase 2 and return the money.
- **If the shares trade above our view of intrinsic value,** we pay a special dividend instead of buying back.
- **If trading turns down,** we pause the programme and protect the floor before anything else.

## 11. Legal and governance (counsel and auditors to confirm; this paper is not legal, tax or investment advice)

- **Inside information:** an expected guidance shortfall may be inside information that must be disclosed promptly (MAR Art. 17). Discretionary buying is barred while disclosure is delayed. Settle this before any purchase.
- **Engaging the activist:** share no inside information selectively. All shareholders get the same information at the same time.
- **Authorisation:** confirm that the AGM buyback authorisation covers the size, price range and duration.
- **Directors' duties:** the board acts in the interest of the company as a whole, not of one shareholder. The minutes record the reasoning.
- **Works council:** consult early on the service model wherever it has a say.
- **Auditors:** confirm what is capitalised and what is expensed in the €120m.

## 12. Indicators, reviewed quarterly by the board

- **Cash:** net cash at the low against the floor.
- **Programme delivery:** spend and milestones.
- **Customers:** online conversion and online share of sales; service attach rate and margin; customer scores.
- **People:** staff retention, regretted attrition and paid training hours in pilot stores.
- **Returns:** ROIC.
- **Buyback:** amount bought back and the average price against our view of intrinsic value.
