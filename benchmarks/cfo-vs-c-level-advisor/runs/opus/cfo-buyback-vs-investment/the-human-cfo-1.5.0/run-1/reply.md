**My recommendation: fund the COO's €120m in gated stages and set a minimum cash level for your seasonal low. Then return everything above that level through a scheduled buyback. On my illustrative numbers that's about €100m now, not €300m. And tell the market the truth about the EPS miss rather than buying your way to guidance.**

**Why not the activist's version**
- **The cash isn't there for both.** €300m plus €120m is €420m against €400m. In consumer electronics, reported net cash is usually flattered by supplier payables from peak season. If your intra-year low is €100m below the reported figure (an estimate), the buyback alone takes you to zero at that low, and doing both puts you €120m into net debt. Thin cash also makes trade-credit insurers cut cover on your suppliers, and your payment terms tighten.
- **It's the pattern the research warns about.** Firms that would just have missed EPS forecasts were much more likely to buy back shares, and those buybacks went with cuts in jobs and investment ([Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v119y2016i1p168-185.html)). Here, the investment that gets squeezed is the platform and the training for your store staff.
- **Check the activist's arithmetic.** EPS uses the weighted-average share count, so shares bought evenly over the last quarter count for only about 12% of their full-year effect this year. EU rules cap purchases at 25% of average daily volume ([Delegated Regulation 2016/1052, Art. 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/1052/chapter/II/data.htm?view=plain)), so at €10m of trading a day, €300m takes about 120 trading days. And at today's rates ([ECB deposit rate 2.50%](https://ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp261029~9a493adf17.en.html)), €300m of cash earns about €5.6m a year after tax.

**What I'd put to the board**
1. **Set a liquidity floor first.** This is the minimum net cash at the seasonal low, covering supplier and credit-insurer comfort and a bad quarter (placeholder: €80m). Anything above the floor and the committed investment is genuine excess.
2. **Fund the €120m in two stages,** if the business case clears your hurdle rate. Phase 1 (estimate €40–50m) covers the platform foundations plus a service-model pilot in a set of stores, with training in paid work time. Phase 2 goes ahead only on evidence: online conversion, how often customers add a service and its margin, customer scores, and staff retention in the pilot stores. Across 1,471 IT projects the average overrun was 27%, and one in six overran by about 200% ([Flyvbjerg & Budzier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0265)). The gates answer the activist's fear of a blank cheque.
3. **Return the excess on a schedule, not to hit a reported number.** Run a time-scheduled or broker-led programme of about €100m. Commit that any of the €120m a gate stops, and any cash above the floor next year, goes back too. Buy back only if the shares trade below intrinsic value; otherwise pay a special dividend.
4. **Give honest guidance.** Report the miss, explain the investment, and show the part of the €120m expensed this year next to reported EPS. No new "one-off" line to make the gap disappear.
5. **Leaders go first.** If any executive bonus, yours and mine included, pays on EPS, take the buyback's effect out of the calculation. The miss should land on our bonuses, not on the training budget.

**The activist's best points**
- *Retailers overspend on IT.* True. Hence the gates, the published milestones, and unspent money going back.
- *Idle cash earns under 2% after tax; equity costs far more.* Agreed. That's why D returns the excess.
- *A miss costs credibility.* A few cents with a clear plan costs less than a beat everyone can see came from a lower share count. Best Buy's turnaround improved store staff training and pay ([CTA](https://www.cta.tech/articles/how-hubert-joly-broke-the-mold-and-saved-best-buy-1/)) and cut people last ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2015/10/29/best-buy-ceo-turnaround-tips/)), and its shares rose from $11 in 2012 to $110 by 2020 ([HBS](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/best-buy-how-human-connection-saved-a-failing-retailer)). One company, many factors, but your sector.

**Legal flags** (get counsel; this isn't legal advice)
- An expected miss may be inside information that must be disclosed promptly, and the EU safe harbour bars discretionary buying while disclosure is delayed. Settle this first.
- Check the AGM buyback authorisation (size, price, duration). The board's duty is to the company as a whole, not to one shareholder, so minute the reasoning.
- A new service model changes store work, so involve the works council early wherever it has a say.

2. What does the COO's case return by phase, and how much of the €120m hits this year's P&L?
