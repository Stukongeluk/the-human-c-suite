# Use of €400m net cash: invest €120m first, return the spare cash, guide honestly

*Draft for board discussion. Prepared by the CFO. Figures marked (est.) are estimates. Before circulation they should be replaced with the monthly cash curve, the COO's business case and our own share data. Counsel and the auditors should review the sections flagged in part 9. This paper is not legal, tax or investment advice.*

## 1. Decisions requested

1. **Adopt a capital allocation order.** First our commitments and a liquidity floor, then investment that clears our hurdle rate, then return of the cash that is genuinely spare.
2. **Approve the €120m customer and service programme in principle** (e-commerce platform rebuild and store staff training for the new service model). The money is ring-fenced and released in tranches against gates. The first tranche is released once the full business case is approved.
3. **Authorise a share buyback of up to €130m (est.).** The final size is €400m minus €120m minus the agreed floor. It is executed over 12 months, through a programme that runs on a fixed timetable or is lead-managed independently by a broker, within the AGM authorisation.
4. **Approve a market update** on EPS guidance, published together with the capital allocation framework. The timing follows counsel's view on disclosure.
5. **Ask the remuneration committee** to exclude buyback effects from any EPS used in incentive plans.

## 2. Recommendation

We should not do a €300m buyback this year. The two asks add up to €420m against €400m of net cash. Funding both means borrowing to fund a payout. Funding only the buyback gives up an investment that is central to how we compete, in order to reach an EPS figure the buyback creates only arithmetically. We fund the investment first, hold a floor sized on our seasonal cash low, and return the rest: €80–180m (est.) depending on the floor, €130m at the central estimate. We tell the market early that EPS will come in a few cents below guidance, together with what we are investing in and how shareholders can hold us to it.

## 3. Commitments first: how much cash is really spare

Year-end net cash for an electronics retailer is flattered by Christmas payables. Stock bought for the peak is paid for in the following months, so the cash low comes later in the year. The floor we need is:

**Floor = minimum operating cash + the seasonal working-capital low + a downside cushion (a weak season, tighter supplier credit) + any covenant or rating minimum**

The board should also see our IFRS 16 lease liabilities next to "net cash", because the net cash figure usually excludes them.

| € m | Floor €100m (est.) | Floor €150m (est.) | Floor €200m (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net cash | 400 | 400 | 400 |
| Less: €120m programme (ring-fenced) | −120 | −120 | −120 |
| Less: floor | −100 | −150 | −200 |
| **Spare cash to return** | **180** | **130** | **80** |
| For comparison: net cash after a €300m buyback | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| Net cash after a €300m buyback, against the floor | 0 | −50 | −100 |

This table leaves out next year's free cash flow, which is a conservative choice. Free cash flow above the floor is returned under the policy in part 5.

## 4. The €120m: conditions and gates

**The business case we need from the COO before tranche 1:**
- the split between platform and training;
- cash and P&L by quarter;
- the capex/opex split, agreed with the auditors (configuration and customisation costs in cloud software arrangements are often expensed, not capitalised);
- benefits sized against a baseline: online conversion, services attach rate, gross margin, store NPS, staff retention;
- a return measured against our hurdle rate, with contingency for overruns.

| Tranche | Scope | Share of €120m | Gate to release the next tranche | Measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Platform foundation and first release; new service model piloted in a set of stores, with matched control stores | about one third (est.) | About 6 months (est.): platform milestones on time and on budget; pilot stores ahead of control stores | Online conversion, services attach rate, store NPS, staff turnover in pilot stores against control stores |
| 2 | Platform rollout; training rolled out across stores | about two thirds (est.) | Quarterly review against the business case | The same measures, plus margin and ROIC |

- **Money a gate does not release is returned to shareholders** under the policy in part 5.
- **People:** training happens in paid time and is scheduled outside the Q4 peak. Employee representatives are involved early, because the new service model changes store roles and schedules. Where the new skills justify it, they are linked to pay progression. If the programme involves no job reductions, we say so plainly to staff.

## 5. What is left, and how we return it

- **The proposed policy, for publication:** "We hold net cash of at least €[floor]m through our seasonal low. Cash beyond that and our committed investments is returned to shareholders each year. We use buybacks when the shares trade below our estimate of their value, and a special dividend otherwise. Investment funds not released at a gate are returned in the same way."
- **This year:** a buyback of up to €130m (est.), spread over 12 months. Any existing ordinary dividend policy stays unchanged.
- **Buyback or special dividend:** a buyback creates value for long-term holders only when the shares are cheap. A dividend treats every holder the same way. The choice depends on our valuation work and our shareholders' tax position, so we need advice from our tax advisers and brokers.

All options are costed on the same basis, using the central floor of €150m (est.).

| Option | Cash out | Net cash after | Against the €150m floor | EPS this year | Long term | Reversible? | Who carries the cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The recommendation also carries its own costs: a likely share price reaction to the guidance update, the risk that the activist campaigns publicly, delivery risk on the platform, and the management time and advisers needed to run the gates.

## 7. Guidance and the EPS arithmetic

**Why a buyback lifts EPS.** The earnings yield on the shares we buy is higher than the after-tax interest the cash earns. At a fair price, the value per remaining share is unchanged, so the EPS lift is arithmetic, not value. EPS uses the weighted-average share count, so purchases made late in the year barely move this year's figure.

*Illustration only, not our data. It assumes a market value of €2.0bn, a P/E of 12, 3% pre-tax interest on cash, a 25% tax rate and a constant share price. On those assumptions, €300m buys back 15% of the shares.*

| €300m bought evenly over the last... | 12 months | 6 months | 3 months | 1 month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lift to this year's EPS (illustrative) | 5.9% | 2.8% | 1.4% | 0.5% |

Once the purchases are complete, the full-year run-rate lift in this illustration is 12.9%. Before anyone relies on "the buyback gets us to guidance", the figures should be rerun with our own share price, share count, cash yield, tax rate and purchase timing. Our broker's view of daily volume is needed too, because buying is capped at 25% of average daily volume.

**What we tell the market.** We show underlying EPS against guidance and the effect of the investment as separate figures, both reconciled to reported EPS. If we show an adjusted EPS that excludes programme costs, we label them as investment costs over the programme's life, not as "one-off". We publish the capital allocation policy, the size of the return, the programme's milestones and the measures in part 11 at the same time.

**The evidence the board should weigh:**
- Firms that would just have missed analysts' EPS forecasts were sharply more likely to buy back shares, and those buybacks were associated with cuts in employment and investment (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, 2016).
- Investors discount the part of an EPS beat that comes from a buyback, but they punish a miss more (Hribar, Jenkins & Johnson, 2006; US data, 1988–2001). That is why an early, explained update matters more than the few cents themselves.
- Best Buy's turnaround from 2012 did not start with layoffs or store closures. It matched Amazon's prices, shipped from stores and changed how it trained store staff, and its share price went from $11 in 2012 to $110 by 2020 (HBS Working Knowledge). That is one case, with many other factors, but it is in our sector.

## 8. What we will not do

- Size a buyback to close an EPS gap.
- Borrow to fund a payout in order to make this year's number.
- Defer training or platform investment to pay for a buyback.
- Call recurring programme costs "one-off".

If the board wants a more leveraged balance sheet as a long-term policy, that is a separate and deliberate decision, with rating and covenant analysis. It is not something to decide this quarter in order to hit EPS.

## 9. Governance, legal and incentive flags (counsel and auditors to confirm)

- **Disclosure.** If we are EU-listed, counsel should confirm whether the expected miss is inside information that must be disclosed as soon as possible (MAR Article 17).
- **Buyback safe harbour.** A buyback run while disclosure is delayed falls outside the safe harbour unless the programme runs on a fixed timetable or a broker that decides the timing independently lead-manages it (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/1052, Article 4).
- **Shareholder engagement.** Any conversation with the activist or other holders that involves non-public information follows the market sounding rules.
- **Authorisation and volume.** We check that the size fits the current AGM buyback authorisation and the 25% average daily volume limit.
- **Directors' duties.** The board decides in the interest of the company as a whole, not of one shareholder; in many European jurisdictions, including the Netherlands, that is a legal duty. Shareholders may ask and advise, and the decision rests on evidence.
- **Employee representatives.** The new service model changes how store staff work, so information and consultation rights may apply. We involve the representatives while the design can still change.
- **Leaders first.** If any bonus or LTIP vests on this year's EPS, the remuneration committee excludes the buyback effect. Management's bonuses, the CFO's included, then reflect the underlying miss, and the paper says so before the vote. Anyone with a personal interest in the outcome declares it.

## 10. The activist's case, and our answer

| Their point | Fair? | Our answer |
|---|---|---|
| Idle cash earns little and weakens returns | Yes | We return the spare cash now, and every year under a published policy |
| Retail platform projects overrun and destroy value | Often, yes | The money is released in tranches against gates; money a gate doesn't release goes back to shareholders |
| Missing guidance will hit the share price | Yes, in the short term | An early, explained update, published with the investment plan and the return, instead of a surprise miss |
| The shares are undervalued | Possibly | If our valuation work agrees, the spare cash goes back as a buyback; that test, not the EPS gap, decides the size |

**Process:** the chair, CEO and CFO meet the activist privately before the announcement, within the market sounding rules, to explain the framework. We also test it with our largest long-term holders.

## 11. Risks, sensitivities and what would change this recommendation

- **The floor.** A shallower seasonal low raises the return. A deeper low, or a weak consumer outlook, lowers it.
- **The business case.** If the programme doesn't clear the hurdle rate, or gate 1 fails, we shrink or stop it and return more cash.
- **Valuation.** Shares well below intrinsic value favour a buyback, and possibly a faster one for the spare portion. Shares at or above value favour a special dividend.
- **Delivery.** A platform overrun is absorbed within the ring-fenced amount or comes back to the board, and the floor stays untouched.

**Reported to the board every quarter:** cash against the floor; programme milestones and spend against each tranche; online conversion; services attach rate; store NPS; store staff turnover and regretted attrition; training completion; gross margin; ROIC.
