Return the cash that is genuinely spare (my estimate €80–180m, central €130m) through a buyback spread over 12 months, under a standing policy. And tell the market now that EPS will land a few cents below guidance, and why.** Don't do €300m this year. **Why not €300m**

- **The two asks don't fit in the cash.** €300m + €120m = €420m, against €400m. Doing both means borrowing to fund a payout. Doing only the buyback leaves €100m and no investment.
- **€400m isn't €400m of spare cash.** You pay for the Christmas stock in the new year, so your cash swings. Spare cash is net cash, minus the investment, minus a floor (operating cash, the seasonal low and a downside cushion). Without your monthly cash curve, these floors are estimates:

| Floor (estimate) | €100m | €150m | €200m |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left to return after the €120m | €180m | €130m | €80m |
| Net cash after a €300m buyback, against the floor | €0m | −€50m | −€100m |

- **The EPS lift is arithmetic, not value.** The earnings you buy back yield more than the interest the cash earns, so EPS rises; at a fair price, each remaining share is worth the same. Check the "gets us to guidance" maths too: EPS uses weighted-average shares, so late purchases barely count (in the paper's illustration, €300m bought over the last three months adds about 1.4% to EPS), and the lost interest income must come off.
- **There's evidence on exactly this trade.** Firms that would just have missed analysts' EPS forecasts were sharply more likely to buy back shares, and those buybacks were associated with cuts in employment and investment ([Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, *JFE* 2016](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v119y2016i1p168-185.html)). Deferring the platform and the training to pay for a buyback is that pattern.
- **The investment is where you compete.** Best Buy's turnaround from 2012 didn't start with layoffs or store closures. It matched Amazon's prices, shipped from stores and changed how it trained store staff, and the shares went from a low of $11 in 2012 to $110 by 2020 ([HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/best-buy-how-human-connection-saved-a-failing-retailer)). One case, but in your sector.

**Conditions on the €120m (not a blank cheque either)**
- A business case that clears our hurdle rate: platform and training split, cash and P&L by quarter, capex versus opex, and sized benefits (online conversion, services attach rate, margin, staff retention).
- Tranches: pilot the service model in some stores against matched control stores, and release the rollout on results and platform milestones, not on money spent.
- Money a gate doesn't release goes back to shareholders. That answers "management will waste it".
- Training in paid time, outside the Christmas peak, with employee representatives involved early: the new model changes how store staff work.

**The activist's case, fairly put**
They're right that idle cash has a cost, that retail platform projects often overrun, and that a miss hurts: investors discount buyback-driven EPS beats, but punish misses more ([Hribar, Jenkins & Johnson, *JAE* 2006](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jaecon/v41y2006i1-2p3-27.html); [summary](https://source.washu.edu/2005/12/repurchasing-stock-wont-fool-the-market/)). So give them what's legitimate: the spare cash back now, a published policy that returns cash above the floor and committed investment every year, milestones they can hold us to, and an early, explained guidance update instead of a surprise miss.

**Flags for counsel and the board**
- If you're EU-listed, check now whether the expected miss is inside information to disclose promptly (MAR Article 17). Buying back while disclosure is delayed falls outside the safe harbour unless the programme runs on a fixed timetable or a broker sets the timing independently ([Delegated Regulation 2016/1052, Art. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/1052/article/4)). Check the AGM authorisation and the 25%-of-daily-volume limit too.
- The board decides for the company as a whole, not for one shareholder.
- If any bonus or LTIP vests on this year's EPS, the remuneration committee strips the buyback out, and management's bonus, mine included, reflects the underlying miss.
- Not legal, tax or investment advice; buyback versus special dividend depends on valuation and your shareholders' tax position.
