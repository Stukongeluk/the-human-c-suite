## 3. Why not the €300m buyback

- **It doesn't fit.** €300m plus €120m exceeds net cash before any buffer, so either the investment is dropped or we go below our liquidity floor.
- **The reason is wrong.** A buyback adds to EPS at almost any price, because our cash earns about 2% after tax. It creates value only if we buy below intrinsic value. Our valuation estimate is €[ ] per share against a current price of €[ ] [complete].
- **Legal and execution risk.** Management expects to miss guidance, which may be inside information. Buybacks must not be made while inside information is undisclosed, except under an independent or pre-scheduled programme. The daily volume cap (25% of average daily volume) means €300m needs about [ ] trading days. Shares bought late in the year have little effect on this year's weighted-average EPS.
- **Evidence.** Buybacks made to meet EPS forecasts tend to come with cuts to investment and employment, and the results are weaker where investment is cut (Almeida, Fos & Kronlund, JFE 2016).
- **Incentives.** Executive pay linked to EPS: [state the position]. The remuneration committee is asked to exclude buyback effects when assessing EPS targets.
