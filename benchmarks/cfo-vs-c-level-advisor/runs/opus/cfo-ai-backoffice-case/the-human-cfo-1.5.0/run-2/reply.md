**My recommendation: don't take 27 redundancies to the board for 2027. Take a gated AI programme instead.** On my estimates, AI frees about 15 FTE of net capacity by the end of 2028 (range 6 to 25), not 27 next year. The cheapest way to bank that is a hiring freeze and natural attrition: SEK +4.8m in 2028 and about SEK 8.4m a year from 2029. The 27-person version costs SEK 25m net in 2027 and about SEK 14m more over 2027–28, and it ends at the same headcount.

I've built the case and costed it next to the alternatives: a board paper (`board-paper-ai-finance-backoffice.md`) and a model with live formulas and a scenario switch (`ai-backoffice-business-case-model.xlsx`). I haven't seen your data, so every input is a labelled estimate scaled to your 90 people at Swedish market rates. Swap in payroll, ERP volumes and vendor quotes, and the tables update.

**Base case, SEK m (estimates)**

| Option | 2027 | 2028 | 2027–28 | Per year from 2029 | People dismissed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| B. 27 redundancies decided Q1 2027 | −25.0 | 2.0 | −23.0 | 8.4 | 27 |
| C. Gated AI, hiring freeze, attrition | −13.9 | 4.8 | −9.1 | 8.4 | 0 |

Three things drive these numbers.

1. **No version saves money in 2027.** The AI programme costs about SEK 14.5m in 2027 whichever route you take. Option B adds SEK 12m of packages, legal fees and outplacement, plus about SEK 9m of contractors for work the AI isn't doing yet. At the end of 2027, B has 27 fewer people but only about 5 FTE of work taken over by AI.
2. **AI frees tasks, not 27 people.** Sized task by task, it frees about 35% of AP hours, 30% of reconciliation hours, 12% of reporting hours (Solvency II reporting, the SFCR and the annual report still need people to review and sign them) and 8% elsewhere. After about 3 FTE of new work (reviewing AI output, handling exceptions, controls), that leaves roughly 15 FTE, or 17%. 30% sits above even my high case. B only matches C if AI frees about 34 FTE, roughly twice my estimate.
3. **Swedish rules move the dates and pick the people.** MBL negotiations come before the decision. Arbetsförmedlingen needs four months' notice for 26 to 100 dismissals, and notice periods run one to six months. LAS selects by length of service, not by task, so the people who leave would mostly be your newest hires rather than those whose work was automated. A different order can be negotiated with the unions, but that usually costs packages. Anyone dismissed also has nine months' priority if you need to rehire.

Both routes end at about 75 FTE, because the work decides the headcount, not the percentage. B just pays more to get there, and it puts the 2027 close and your Solvency II deadlines at risk while the AI catches up. C beats B in every sensitivity I ran.

**What I'd ask the board to approve**

- **A 2027 programme budget of SEK 14.5m, released in stages.** Only about SEK 3.5m is at risk before Gate 1 at the end of April, when a pilot on AP and reconciliations is measured against a baseline.
- **A hiring freeze with exceptions from January.** Leavers are covered with fixed-term staff until the gates prove the capacity is free; then their roles stay empty.
- **Gate 2 at the end of Q3 2027.** This is where the range becomes a 2028 commitment, including voluntary options if freed capacity outruns attrition.
- **No redundancies because of this programme in 2027, and no executive bonus tied to headcount.** Score it on cost per invoice, close days, errors, deadlines met and regretted attrition.

**Human impact in brief.** In option C nobody is dismissed, and about 15 roles go unfilled by the end of 2028. Everyone's work changes, with about 40 hours of paid learning each in 2027 and about three FTE moving into AI oversight and controls. Bring the unions (in insurance, typically Forena and the Saco unions) into the baseline and pilot design now, under MBL, while they can still shape it. Measure teams and processes, never individuals.

**For counsel, compliance and your auditor** (this isn't legal or accounting advice): DORA third-party rules for the AI vendors; whether anything counts as outsourcing an important function that Finansinspektionen must hear about in advance; segregation of duties and a named approver for every payment; GDPR for invoice data; and the IFRS treatment of implementation costs, mostly expensed for cloud software.

**The trap, in one sentence:** a case that starts from 27 people rather than from the hours AI really removes makes you pay for the cut first and discover the real capacity later, in the quarter-end close.

**Two questions that would change the numbers most:**

1. What is the board actually solving for? An expense-ratio gap in SEK by a date, a peer comparison, or an owner's AI story? If it's a cost gap, 30% of finance is worth about SEK 23m a year gross, and the bigger cost pools may sit outside finance.
2. Where do the 90 people's hours go today, and how much is already automated (e-invoice share, auto-match rate)? If AP is already largely touchless, its share shrinks.
