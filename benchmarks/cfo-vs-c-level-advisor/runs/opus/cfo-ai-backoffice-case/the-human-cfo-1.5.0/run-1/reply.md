My recommendation: take the board a gated case for freeing capacity with AI, not a case for replacing 27 people in 2027. On my estimates AI can take on about 11% of the team's hours by the end of 2027 (range 6–16%) and about 25% at maturity in 2029 (13–36%). Cutting 27 roles next year would cost about SEK 36m in 2027. The capacity route costs about SEK 13.5m in 2027, pays back in 2030 and takes headcount from 90 to about 75 by end-2029 with no redundancies.

I've drafted the board paper and the model behind it:
- `board-paper-ai-finance-back-office.md`: recommendation, task sizing, four options costed by quarter, gates, people, regulation, and an assumptions register.
- `business_case_model.py`: every table comes from it, and it checks that each one adds up. Swap in your own figures and rerun it.

All figures are labelled estimates scaled to 90 FTE (SEK 0.86m per FTE-year: an assumed SEK 48k monthly salary plus SCB's finance-and-insurance on-costs). Replace them with payroll aggregates, activity sampling and vendor quotes before this goes out.

**Base case, SEK m against today's cost base**

| Option | 2027 | 5 years | Headcount end-2029 | Redundancies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 90 | 0 |
| B. 27 redundancies in 2027 | −36.3 | −10.7 | 71 (8 rehired) | 27 |
| **C. Capacity first: vacancies, internal moves (recommended)** | **−13.5** | **+13.6** | **75** | **0** |
| D. C plus a 2028 round, if the autumn gate justifies it | −13.9 | +10.4 | 75 | 2 (10 in the high case) |

The slide version of B is 27 × SEK 0.86m = SEK 23m saved. In practice:
- **Swedish process moves the savings.** MBL negotiations come first, notifying 26–100 dismissals to Arbetsförmedlingen takes four months, and LAS notice runs one to six months. Exits land June–October; severance (about SEK 10m at an assumed six months' pay) comes first.
- **The AI isn't doing the work yet.** It covers about 10 FTE by Q4 2027. The gap of about 17 FTE costs about SEK 18m in contractors, peaking at the year-end close and the annual Solvency II reporting.
- **You can't choose who leaves.** LAS selection order goes by length of service, not by whose tasks were automated.
- **The AI never frees 27 FTE in the base case.** It frees 22, 3 are needed to run it, and about 8 roles get refilled. The people you dismissed have re-employment priority for nine months.

In none of nine cases (low, base and high capacity for B, C and D) does a redundancy round beat the capacity route over five years. B only catches up if AI could take on about 48% of the team's hours.

**The trap, in one sentence:** a case built to prove a number the board has already picked will get approved, miss in 2027, and the miss will land on finance's credibility and on 27 people.

**What the paper asks the board to approve**
- SEK 13.5m for 2027, released in stages. The first SEK 6m buys the pilot, and that is the most we lose if it disappoints.
- Gates with criteria fixed before any results come in:
  - Gate 1 (June 2027): AP and reconciliation pilots.
  - Gate 2 (October 2027, before the 2028 budget): measured capacity sets the 2028 headcount plan. That is where a number becomes a commitment.
- Freed capacity used in a set order: quality and a faster close, then a three-person automation and controls team, then vacancies left unfilled, then voluntary moves into other roles.
- Controls: a person approves anything that moves money or posts to the ledger; audit trails and segregation of duties; DORA due diligence on vendors; a compliance check on whether any arrangement is outsourcing that Finansinspektionen must be told about; EIOPA's 2025 AI governance opinion as the standard.
- No management target for headcount removed. Targets are capacity freed, close days, error rates and regretted attrition.

**People:** everyone's work changes; nobody is dismissed because of AI in 2027. Bring the unions in under MBL now, while they can still shape the design (the OECD finds AI adoption goes better for workers where their representatives are consulted). Measure processes and teams, never individuals. Fund paid learning time, and keep "30%" from circulating: the people with the most options leave first.

**The board's side:** they're right that AI in invoices and reconciliations is real, peers are moving, and without a target nothing happens. Keep the ambition as a measured capacity target, and commit in October once we know what the tools do.

**What would change my mind:**
- Pilot results at the high case with attrition well below 6%. Then a small, voluntary-first round in 2028 is worth costing at Gate 2.
- Much lower vendor quotes. That improves the case without changing the ranking.
- A hard 2027 SEK target. AI can't deliver that under any option; non-payroll spend, claims leakage and pricing would have to.

Before the paper goes out, I'd walk the chair and the CEO through it one-on-one.

Two questions would change the numbers most:
1. What is the 30% meant to achieve: a SEK figure in the 2027 plan, an expense-ratio target, or a signal to owners? And is 2027 a hard date?
2. What are the team's actual attrition, tenure and age profile, and how many open roles elsewhere could people move into?

Key assumptions: 9% attrition a year; AI programme SEK 12m in 2027, falling to SEK 3.7m a year by 2029; a quarter of automated hours come back as review; six months' severance; contractors at SEK 850 an hour. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
