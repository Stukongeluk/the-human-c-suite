## 1. What the board is asked to do

1. **Note** that 120 × €75k = €9.0m is the most the programme could save in a full year. It is not a 2027 saving. It assumes all 120 leave on 1 January 2027, cost exactly the average, are never backfilled and cost nothing to exit.
2. **Decide** which 2027 savings figure the programme is held to: in-year saving, cash, or run-rate.
3. **Decide** whether to go ahead with 120 compulsory redundancies or the alternative path in section 5, before anything is announced.

*Downside:* consultation is contested (conciliation board in Germany, negative works-council advice in the Netherlands), the social plan is generous and leakage is 25%. *Base:* the social plan is negotiated without a major dispute and leakage is 15%. *Upside:* agreement comes fast, a voluntary leaver option speeds up exits and leakage is 10%.

**No scenario reaches €9m in 2027, and none reaches it at run-rate either.** At base assumptions, a €9m run-rate needs about 149 redundancies. A €9m saving inside 2027 needs about 396, more than a quarter of the group, and a programme that size would take longer to consult on. A bigger cut does not close the 2027 gap.

## 5. Alternative path: same 120 positions, fewer compulsory dismissals (Base, € millions)

| | A: 120 compulsory redundancies | B: hiring freeze + attrition + targeted redundancies |
|---|---:|---:|
| Positions removed by not replacing leavers | 0 | 56 |
| Compulsory redundancies | 120 | 64 |
| 2027 underlying saving | 2.73 | 3.01 |
| One-off costs | (5.38) | (3.36) |
| **2027 net cash effect** | **(2.65)** | **(0.35)** |
| Run-rate saving from 2028 | 7.27 | 7.27 |

B assumes 8% voluntary attrition and that half of leavers are not replaced. Both are placeholders until we have 24 months of actual leaver data. Releasing agency staff and contractors first, and moving at-risk employees into those roles, would cut compulsory redundancies further. In the Netherlands this is generally required anyway for interchangeable roles (art. 17 Ontslagregeling). B depends on redeploying staff between sites and roles. The 2027 budget must not already count these vacancy savings.

## 7. Risks

- **Pressure to shortcut consultation.** In Germany, carrying out the change before a reconciliation of interests has been attempted exposes us to compensation claims (§ 113 BetrVG). These run up to 12 months' pay per person, about €3.4m on 60 leavers. In the Netherlands, the OR's advice must be sought while it can still influence the decision, and a decision that departs from the advice is suspended for one month (art. 26 WOR). Any announcement in December should present a proposal and open consultation. It should not announce 120 redundancies as decided.
- **Losing the wrong people.** 1,400 employees face 6–10 months of uncertainty. Scarce technicians with outside options tend to leave first, which costs revenue and agency premiums.
- **Billable roles.** If any of the 120 are billable and the work remains, each cut costs revenue. The model has a line for this. It stays at zero until operations sizes it.
- **European Works Council.** With 1,400 employees across two countries, the group may be in scope (1,000+ in the EU and 150+ in each of two countries). If an EWC exists, it must be informed and consulted on a cross-border programme.
- **A second round.** If 2027 is booked at €9m, the shortfall surfaces mid-year. A second round costs more and does more damage than sizing the first one correctly.
