**CFO: 2027 redundancy savings model**

**BOTTOM LINE**
The €9m (120 × €75k) is a full-year run-rate that assumes all 120 people leave on 1 January at no cost. Once you allow for German and Dutch consultation, notice periods and severance, the base case for 2027 is €2.73m of savings and €5.38m of one-off costs, so 2027 cash is −€2.65m. From 2028 the run-rate is €7.27m. That profile belongs in the board pack, not €9m for 2027.
Confidence: medium. The arithmetic is checked. The timing and severance inputs are estimates until HR and counsel confirm them.

- **Downside:** consultation is contested and leakage is 25%. Leakage means the work comes back as overtime, agency staff or contractors.
- **Base:** the social plan is negotiated without a major fight and leakage is 15%.
- **Upside:** there is a quick agreement plus a voluntary leaver option, and leakage is 10%.

**WHAT**
- **€9m has no headroom.** It holds only if all 120 cost exactly €75k, leave on 1 January, are never backfilled and cost nothing to exit. None of those holds. At base assumptions, a €9m run-rate needs about 149 redundancies. Getting €9m inside 2027 would need about 396. No headcount number fixes 2027. *[verified: arithmetic on your inputs]*
- **Timing accounts for €5.34m of the €6.27m 2027 gap.**
  - *Germany:* the works council must be consulted on an Interessenausgleich and Sozialplan (reconciliation of interests and social plan) before notices go out. Notice is then 1–7 months depending on tenure. Base exit: end of September.
  - *Netherlands:* you need works-council (OR) advice, a collective-dismissal notification (WMCO), and UWV permits or settlement agreements. Notice is then 1–4 months. Base exit: end of June.
  *[medium: statute checked, durations are estimates]*
- **One-off costs are about €44.8k per leaver.**
  - *Germany:* about 9 months' gross pay, which is social-plan practice.
  - *Netherlands:* about 4.5 months, which is 1.5× the statutory transition payment of 1/3 month per year of service.
  - *Plus:* outplacement, advisers and retention.
  *[medium]*
- **A hiring freeze plus targeted redundancies removes the same 120 positions.** Not replacing leavers covers 56 of them, so only 64 compulsory redundancies are needed. 2027 cash comes out about €2.3m better (−€0.35m instead of −€2.65m), with the same €7.27m run-rate. *[assumed: 8% voluntary attrition, half of leavers not replaced. Needs your data]*
- **Revenue is not in the numbers yet.** If any of the 120 are billable technicians whose work doesn't go away, cutting them loses revenue. The model has a line for this. It stays at zero until ops sizes it. *[not sized]*

**WHY THIS MATTERS**
If the pack books €9m for 2027, the board will find a gap of more than €6m by mid-year. The usual response is a second round, which is slower, costs more and does more damage than sizing it right once. If €9m in 2027 is a hard budget need, redundancies cannot close it. The gap would have to come from levers that act within weeks: agency and subcontractor hours, overtime and non-people spend.

**RISKS**
- **Pressure to hit 2027 invites shortcuts in consultation.**
  - *Germany:* carrying out the cuts before a reconciliation of interests has been attempted triggers compensation claims of up to 12 months' pay per person (§ 113 BetrVG). That is about €3.4m on 60 leavers.
  - *Netherlands:* OR advice has to be sought while it can still change the decision.
  - *What to do:* announce a proposal and the start of consultation, not "120 redundancies" as a done deal.
- **Six to ten months of uncertainty for 1,400 people.** Scarce technicians with other options leave first. The retention budget (€0.25m in the base case) may be too small.
- **The charge and the cash can fall in different years.** Under IAS 37, announcing a detailed plan in December can pull most of the one-off cost, especially severance, into FY2026. The cash still goes out in 2027.
- **A European Works Council may also need consulting.** You are probably in scope at 1,400 staff across two countries, if each country has at least 150 employees. If an EWC exists, it must be consulted on a cross-border programme.
