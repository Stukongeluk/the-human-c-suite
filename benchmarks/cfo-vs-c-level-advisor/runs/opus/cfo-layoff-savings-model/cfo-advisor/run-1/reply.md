**CFO: savings model for the 120-redundancy proposal (board pack)**

---

**BOTTOM LINE**

On realistic German and Dutch timelines, 120 redundancies save about **€2.9m in 2027, not €9m**. They also cost about **€5.1m one-off**, so if those costs land in 2027, the year ends about **€2.3m worse off**. The saving reaches about **€7.7m a year from 2028**. I would not put €9m for 2027 in the board pack, because no scenario supports it. Confidence: medium. The legal steps behind the timing are well established; the exact euros will move once we have the actual list of roles.

---

**WHAT**

- **The €9m is 120 × €75k.** That assumes a full year of savings from 1 January with no costs, but nobody can leave on 1 January. In the base case, German notices go out at the end of May 2027, after works-council negotiations, and people leave June-December. Dutch exits fall in May-August. Only 37% of a full year's saving lands in 2027. *[Legal steps: VERIFIED. Exact months: ASSUMED]*
- **Bridge (base case):** €9.0m → people who leave cost less than average −€0.45m → leakage (overtime, agency, re-hires) −€0.86m → **run-rate €7.7m** → timing −€4.8m → **2027 saving €2.9m** → one-off costs −€5.1m → **2027 net −€2.3m**. *[Arithmetic: VERIFIED in the model. Inputs: ASSUMED]*
- **Scenarios** (table below). Even the upside gives €3.9m in 2027 and €8.55m a year at run-rate. Even if every notice went out in January, which consultation rules make impossible, 120 exits would save at most about €5.2m in 2027. Reaching €9m in 2027 would take about 377 redundancies; reaching €9m a year at run-rate would take about 140. *[MEDIUM]*
- **The figure of 120 is €9m ÷ €75k.** It was sized from the target, not from the work. Size a reduction right once: a second round does more damage than the first. *[VERIFIED: the arithmetic]*
- **Announcing "120 redundancies" as a decision next month is the biggest risk in the plan.**
  - *Germany:* the works council must be consulted on how the cuts happen (Interessenausgleich) and on compensation (Sozialplan). Errors in the collective-dismissal procedure can make the dismissals invalid.
  - *Netherlands:* the works council (OR) must be asked for advice while it can still change the decision. UWV refuses dismissal permits if that procedure is incomplete, and the OR can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer).
  - So announce a proposal with a consultation timetable. *[VERIFIED: general sources; counsel to confirm]*

---

**WHY THIS MATTERS**

Suppose the board approves on €9m and 2027 delivers about €2.9m before costs. The gap shows up mid-year, when the only quick fix left is a second round of cuts. That is the worst outcome for staff, for relations with the works councils, and for the board's trust in finance's numbers. The cheapest moment to correct the target is before anything is announced.

---

**HOW TO ACT**

1. **Get the role-by-role list behind the 120** (site, country, salary, tenure, revenue-earning or not, protected status) and enter it in the Inputs tab. → HR director + FP&A → before the board pack closes.
2. **Confirm the timeline, likely social-plan cost and protected groups.** These include works-council members, severely disabled staff, pregnancy and parental leave, long-serving staff protected by collective agreements, and Dutch staff on sick leave. → German and Dutch employment counsel → this week.
3. **Agree with the auditors which year carries the restructuring provision.** A December announcement of a detailed plan may put it in FY2026. → Group controller → before the pack closes.
4. **Build the bridge from €2.9m to €9m with levers that start saving in January:** a hiring freeze with exemptions, agency, subcontractor and overtime spend, procurement, and a voluntary leaver scheme. → You + COO → for the same board meeting.
5. **Rewrite the announcement as a proposal with a consultation timetable.** Inform the works councils first, and the European Works Council if you have one, before staff and the market hear it. → CEO + HR director + counsel → before anything is said publicly.

---

**RISKS**

- **Consultation runs long.** If the German talks go to conciliation or the Dutch works council advises against, most German exits slip to late 2027 or 2028. Each month of German delay costs about €0.35m of 2027 savings; each Dutch month costs about €0.26m.
- **Revenue-earning roles.** If technicians are cut while demand holds, revenue leaves with them, at about €25k of lost contribution each per year (placeholder).
- **Leakage.** Every 5 points of leakage costs about €0.43m a year of run-rate. Every 5 points lower cost of the people who leave costs about €0.41m.
- **Not in the numbers:** people you want to keep resign once a number is public, and productivity drops through 5-10 months of consultation and notice.

*Challenge before sending:*
- *Weakest point:* every euro rests on "€75k × 120". We don't yet know who the 120 are.
- *Missing perspective:* the COO's view on whether the work actually disappears.
- *If wrong, the cost:* the downside is −€7.4m in 2027 against a promised +€9m.

---

**YOUR DECISION** (what goes in the pack)

- **Option A: €9m in 2027, as requested.** No scenario supports it. I would not put finance's name to it.
- **Option B: the model as built.** About €2.9m in 2027 before €5.1m of one-off costs, €7.7m a year from 2028, with downside and upside cases.
- **Option C: Option B plus a costed alternative path.** Start a hiring freeze with exemptions now. In the base case that gives about €1.5m net in 2027 with no severance, about 112 fewer roles by the end of 2028, and €6.6m cumulative over 2027-28, against €5.4m for the redundancy route. Then run a voluntary scheme, and use compulsory redundancies only for what is left, sized role by role during consultation. The paths are not additive: each role that goes through the freeze or the voluntary scheme is one fewer severance.

**Recommendation: Option C.** It gives the board the true 2027 number and a real choice, and it avoids a programme sized from the target divided by the average cost.
