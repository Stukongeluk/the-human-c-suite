**The staged plan:**

- A hiring freeze with exceptions from January. Vacancies in roles you still need are filled by redeploying people from roles where the work is falling.
- Up to 40 voluntary exits after consultation, offered only where the work is reduced.
- A board gate in July 2027, based on actual results. If the gap isn't closing, or work has ended at particular sites, the board decides on targeted compulsory reductions then, in one round.

It wins mainly because it pays no severance for about 80 of the 120 roles and starts saving in January rather than after consultation. Its weak spot is turnover. I've assumed 7%; at 4% it reaches 120 roles only in mid-2029, though it still stays ahead of the proposal through 2028.

**Next month, change what is announced rather than the date.** Open consultation with both works councils on the problem and on the options, including a reduction of up to 120 roles.

- **Legal risk.** Announcing 120 redundancies as a decision invites challenges that push the savings out further. In the Netherlands that means the Enterprise Chamber. In Germany, acting before a reconciliation of interests has been attempted brings compensation claims (§113 BetrVG).
- **Who leaves.** Social selection in Germany and the reflection principle in the Netherlands decide who goes, not management.
- **Timing.** Avoid telling staff in the weeks before Christmas.
