# 2027 savings: the redundancy proposal, net and by quarter, and a staged alternative

**Board paper, draft for discussion** · Prepared by Finance · November 2026
**Status:** draft. Figures marked *(est.)* are estimates; each is listed in section 11 with where the real number will come from. All figures come from the attached workbook, `savings-model-2027.xlsx` (inputs on its Assumptions and Timing sheets).

## 1. Recommendation

**The proposal to announce 120 redundancies in December does not deliver €9.0m of savings in 2027.** Net of its costs it is a net cash cost of about **€2.7m in 2027**. It saves about €5.0m in 2028 and about €6.1m a year from 2029. The €9.0m is a gross run-rate (120 roles × €75,000) that assumes every person leaves on 1 January 2027 and that leaving costs nothing.

We recommend a staged plan instead:

1. **December 2026:** open consultation with the Dutch and German works councils on the cost problem and on options, including a reduction of up to 120 roles. Do not announce redundancies as a decision.
2. **From 1 January 2027:**
   - a hiring freeze with exceptions, where vacancies in roles we still need are filled first by people from roles where the work is falling;
   - renegotiation of third-party spend and work on pricing and revenue leakage;
   - leaders first: executive base pay down 10% for 2027 and the 2027 leadership bonus pool suspended. The executive team should say plainly, when consultation opens, that the shortfall is its responsibility.
3. **Q2 2027, after consultation:** a voluntary exit scheme of up to 40 places, offered only where the work is reduced.
4. **July 2027 board gate, on first-half actuals:** if the gap is not closing, or work has ended at particular sites, the board decides on targeted compulsory reductions for what remains. They would be made in one round, with the same consultation steps.

On the same basis, and with the same non-payroll levers counted in both, the staged plan does better than the proposal in every year: **+€1.8m vs −€1.0m in 2027 cash, €9.4m vs €8.4m in 2028, and €9.25m vs €8.95m a year from 2029.** It does this without compulsory dismissals unless the July gate decides they are needed.

**No route delivers €9m in 2027.** If €9m has to show in 2027 because of a covenant test, a cash need or a commitment to the owners, that calls for a conversation with the bank or the owners now. We will model it on that basis once the board confirms what the number protects.

## 4. The staged plan

**How it works**

- **Freeze with exceptions from 1 January.** At an estimated 7% voluntary turnover, about 98 people leave each year. Not replacing 60% of them *(est.)* removes about 4.9 roles a month. Safety-critical, customer-critical and scarce-skill roles are exceptions. Vacancies in roles we still need are filled first by redeploying people from roles where the work is falling, with a retraining budget of €0.15m *(est.)*. The freeze stops when the total reaches 120 roles, in May 2028 in the base case.
- **Voluntary exits.** Up to 40 places are offered after consultation, only where the work is reduced, and approval protects scarce skills. Packages are 25% above the compulsory package *(est.)*. Exits fall in July to September 2027.
- **July 2027 gate.** The board reviews first-half actuals: savings against this model, overtime and agency hours, attrition, safety and customer measures. Any targeted compulsory reductions are decided there, in one round. They are not in the figures below; per role they would cost what they cost in section 3.

**Why it does better:** it pays no severance for about 80 of the 120 roles. It starts saving in January instead of after consultation. It brings fewer contested dismissals and, on our estimate, less attrition among the people who stay.

**What it costs:**

- It reaches the full run-rate three months later (May 2028 rather than February 2028).
- Freeze vacancies fall where people resign, not where work is lower. We assume 20% of that saving has to be bought back as overtime and agency work, against 15% for targeted exits.
- It depends on a turnover rate we have not yet confirmed (section 6).

**Break-even for the proposal**

- About **€6.2m of extra lost revenue** over 2027–28, around 3.5% of a year's estimated revenue, wipes out its cumulative net saving to end-2028. About €17.5m a year, around 10%, wipes out the recurring saving.
- **75 extra regretted leavers** at half a salary each, or **38** at a full salary, wipe out the cumulative net saving to end-2028.

## 8. Accounting, reporting and the bank

- **When the provision lands.** In the base case the exit costs are provided for in March 2027, when the plan is final after consultation. If the plan is announced as decided in December, about €4.5m may have to be provided for in the 2026 accounts. The 2027 P&L would then show about +€1.1m for the proposal while 2027 cash is still −€2.7m. It is the same money in a different year, and the board should look at both. The recognition criteria differ between IFRS (IAS 37), Dutch GAAP and HGB; the auditors should confirm the treatment before the pack is issued.
- **Adjusted figures.** If restructuring costs are shown as an adjustment, reconcile every adjusted figure to the reported one. Do not describe them as one-off if the group has restructured in the last two years or is likely to again.
- **The bank.** Check whether the facility agreement caps restructuring add-backs in covenant EBITDA, and what each option's 2027 profile does to headroom. If headroom is tight, talk to the bank before anything is announced.

## 9. People

- **Who is affected.** Under the proposal, 120 people, 8.6% of the workforce. In Germany, social selection protects longer service and dependants, so compulsory exits fall mostly on shorter-service staff: often younger technicians, the skilled-trades pipeline for the next decade. In the Netherlands, exits are spread across age groups.
- **What the evidence says.**
  - A 1% downsizing was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, *Academy of Management Journal*, 2008). We applied that uplift once and did not scale it to an 8.6% reduction, so this is a conservative reading.
  - Replacing an employee costs one-half to two times annual salary (Gallup). We used the low end.
  - In a study of US manufacturing firms, downsizing was associated with lower subsequent profitability, most strongly in industries with high R&D intensity, growth and low capital intensity (Guthrie & Datta, *Organization Science*, 2008).
- **Support, in either plan.** Outplacement and coaching from the day of notice (€5,000 per person in the model), paid notice, priority for rehiring, written references, and extra care for people close to retirement or on leave.
- **How people hear it.**
  - The works councils come first.
  - Affected people hear it from their own manager, in person, before any wider announcement.
  - Everyone else is told the same day, with what is decided and what is still open.
  - Staff are not told in the weeks before Christmas.
- **The people who stay.** Decide which work stops rather than spreading the same work over fewer people. Hold 1:1s within two weeks, and watch absence, regretted exits and safety for the following year.

**Measures, reported monthly to the board from January 2027**

- **Money:** net savings against this model by quarter (cash and P&L); overtime, agency and subcontractor spend.
- **People:** headcount and FTE by site; voluntary and regretted attrition; absence; a short engagement pulse.
- **Operations and customers:** safety (lost-time injury rate), SLA performance, complaints, revenue on key accounts.

## 10. The case for the proposal, and what would change this recommendation

**The case for the proposal, put fairly:**

- One decisive round ends the uncertainty sooner.
- It targets the roles where the work has gone, not wherever people happen to resign.
- It reaches the full run-rate about three months earlier.

Those advantages are real. On these estimates they cost about €3.9m more in net cash to the end of 2028 (€11.2m against €7.3m), plus 120 compulsory dismissals. Natural turnover and volunteers can largely deliver the same reduction.

**What would change the recommendation:**

- **Concentrated roles.** If the 120 roles are in work that has ended, such as a lost contract or a site closing, a group-wide freeze will not remove them. Targeted reductions there, after voluntary exits and redeployment, would be right sooner.
- **Low turnover.** If voluntary turnover is well below 7%, the freeze is too slow, and the July gate should bring forward voluntary or targeted exits.
- **A covenant or cash need.** If the 2027 number is about covenant headroom or cash, the first moves are the bank and working capital (receivables, inventory, capex timing), not either headcount route.
- **A temporary dip.** If the German volume drop is temporary, short-time work there beats both routes.

**Questions for the board:**

1. What does €9m in 2027 protect: a covenant test, cash, or a budget commitment to the owners?
2. Which work stops with the 120 roles?
