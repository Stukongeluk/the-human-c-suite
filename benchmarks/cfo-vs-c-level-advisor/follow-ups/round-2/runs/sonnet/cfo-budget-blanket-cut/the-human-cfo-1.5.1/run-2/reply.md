On my illustrative numbers that route reaches about 6.9% run-rate (EUR 5.2m) with no forced exits. The blanket cut, with 48 people leaving, nets about EUR 0.2m in 2027.

**Why the blanket cut doesn't work here**

The CEO's case is fair: it is simple, quick and avoids a fight between cost centres. But with payroll at 68%, it becomes about 48 redundancies. In the Netherlands that means works council advice, UWV and union notification (20 or more in one work area within three months), notice periods and transition payments. Exits land around mid-year, so the saving mostly misses the year it is meant to fix.

| EUR k, estimates | Gross saving | One-offs | Lost revenue, rehiring, extra leavers | Net 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blanket 8% | 3,576 | -1,167 | -2,211 | about 200 |
| Recommended | 3,248 | -550 | -162 | 2,535 (3,405 with leaders' temporary measures) |

The gross EUR 3.6m looks fine on a slide; the net does not. The blanket cut also falls on billable capacity, which is our revenue, and research links downsizing to more voluntary turnover the following year.

**What I recommend instead**

1. **Non-payroll spend, zero-based:** vendors, licences and cloud (EUR 1.2m), contractors and consultants (0.6m), travel (0.3m), office space (0.5m, from Q3). Together EUR 2.6m run-rate. Renegotiate before cancelling.
2. **Hiring flows:** at 12% attrition we lose about 72 people a year. Not refilling 30 of those roles, non-billable first, is EUR 2.55m run-rate and EUR 1.3m in 2027.
3. **Leaders first:** a 10% fixed-pay cut for the top team, half the leadership bonus pool and perks, about EUR 0.87m. It is one-year only, so I keep it out of the run-rate.
4. **Voluntary exits in reserve:** about 10 roles from 1 July would take the run-rate to exactly 8.0%. Decide it at the end of Q2 against triggers, with counsel and the works council.
5. **Protected:** training and learning time, security and quality controls, top-customer delivery capacity.

**The honest part:** no route puts 8% into the 2027 P&L. The recommended plan gives EUR 3.4m (4.5%) in-year and 6.9% at the Q4 run-rate, so the board should adopt the run-rate definition explicitly. If the aim is margin, price renewals and billing leakage (about EUR 0.8m for 1% of price, plus 0.4-0.8m from unbilled hours) are worth sizing alongside, though they are not opex.

**One thing I'd raise with the CEO before the meeting.** A EUR 6.0m cut on a margin of about EUR 3m lifts margin from about 4% to 12%. What is the board actually solving for: a covenant, cash, margin or a valuation? The answer changes the plan.

**Questions that would change the numbers**

- What are the covenant headroom and the loan terms?
- Do you have a works council and a collective agreement or social plan?
