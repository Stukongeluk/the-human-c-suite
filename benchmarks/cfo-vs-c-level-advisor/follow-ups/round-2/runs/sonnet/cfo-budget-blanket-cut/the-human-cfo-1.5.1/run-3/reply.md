I would not take a flat 8% per cost centre to the board. I'd take a targeted plan that delivers about EUR 4.7m (6.8% of opex) in the year and an exit run-rate of about EUR 5.1m (7.4%), with named reserve levers for the rest and no forced exits in the base plan. A flat cut lands mostly on payroll and, on my estimates, nets only about EUR 0.8m in 2027, because the exit costs come first and the exits come late.

All figures are my estimates, scaled from what you told me. I assumed opex of about EUR 69m (payroll about EUR 47m, EUR 78k per FTE loaded). Replace them with ledger and payroll actuals in week one. The full draft board paper is in board-budget-approach.md.

**Why a flat 8% fails here**
- 68% of opex is payroll, so a flat cut means about 48 FTE (about EUR 3.75m), or non-payroll cuts far beyond 8%.
- In a services firm most cost centres are delivery. If 70% of those 48 are billable and their work is sold, you lose about EUR 4.0m of revenue to save EUR 2.6m. At thin margin that makes things worse.
- Dutch process takes time: works council advice before the decision (art. 25 WOR), WMCO notification and consultation if 20 or more in one UWV region, UWV approval, notice, transition payments. Exits land in Q3, so about EUR 0.9m is saved in the year against about EUR 1.9m of one-off costs. Please confirm with employment counsel.
- Cuts of this kind also push up voluntary attrition (1% downsizing was associated with 31% more turnover, Trevor & Nyberg), and replacing a leaver costs 0.5x to 2x salary (Gallup).

The reserve levers are not counted above, and I have not counted any I could not size: pricing and utilisation (each point of utilisation is worth about EUR 0.5m of revenue, though that is not opex), working capital and bank talks if the real aim is cash or a covenant, an explicit stop-doing list, voluntary exits for about 10 people (about EUR 0.8m a year, EUR 0.4m one-off), and only last, involuntary exits after works council consultation.

**For the three weeks**
- Week 1: actuals by cost centre, attrition, utilisation, contract end dates; agree the baseline with the CEO; brief the works council chair early.
- Week 2: budget-holder sessions, the stop list, owners and dates per lever.
- Week 3: CEO review, then a board paper with a flat cut, the targeted plan and do nothing side by side.

**For the CEO conversation:** a flat cut is simple to say, but it is a decision to cut about 48 people and the revenue they bring in, said as a percentage. The targeted plan is just as clear: one number per lever, one owner, one date. Offer him that.

**Two questions that change the numbers**
1. 8% of what: this year's actual, or next year's baseline including wage indexation (about EUR 1.6m on its own)?
2. What is the 8% for: margin, a covenant, valuation? The answer decides whether pricing, cash or financing belong in the mix.
