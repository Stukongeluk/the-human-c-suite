**Why the blanket cut fails**

Payroll is 68% of opex, so "every cost centre cuts 8%" turns into about 48 FTE leaving. In the Netherlands that is slow and expensive:
- Your works council has to give advice before the decision is final (art. 25 WOR).
- 20 or more dismissals in one UWV region within three months trigger the collective-dismissal route (WMCO).
- Then UWV procedure, notice periods and transition payments follow.

Exits land around mid-2027 and the payments come when people leave. My estimate:

| EUR M, 2027 | Blanket 8% | Recommended plan |
|---|---|---|
| Gross saving | 2.7 | 3.9 |
| One-off costs | -1.6 | -0.25 |
| Extra attrition cost | -0.6 | 0 |
| **Net effect in 2027** | **0.6** | **3.65** |
| Jobs lost involuntarily | about 48 | 0 |

By Q4 the blanket cut's run-rate is similar, about EUR 5.8M against 5.6M. Revenue loss of about 2.4% (EUR 1.9M) would wipe out its 2027 saving. A cost cut also arrives after the deadline it is meant to meet.

**What I'd propose instead**

1. **Zero-base the EUR 24M non-payroll spend**: subcontractors, licences, travel, facilities. I sized about 12%, EUR 2.85M run-rate (EUR 1.96M in 2027).
2. **Hiring freeze with exceptions**: leave 25 non-billable or bench roles open as people leave. Expected attrition is 72 leavers at 12%. That is EUR 2.1M run-rate (EUR 1.06M in 2027).
3. **Stop two low-value internal programmes**: EUR 0.6M run-rate.
4. **Leaders first**: the leadership team takes the first and largest cut, bonus pool and a temporary pay reduction, about EUR 0.5M (temporary, so not in the run-rate).
5. **Revenue**: re-price the contracts renewing in 2027 (+3% on 40% of revenue is about EUR 0.9M margin). This helps margin, not opex, so tell the board it counts differently.
6. **Gate at end of Q2**: if delivered savings are below 70% of plan, open voluntary exits and redeployment first, with the works council already involved.

This leaves EUR 0.4M run-rate open. I won't count it until a lever is sized for it.

**Three questions that change the numbers**
- Is the 8% measured against 2026 actuals or the 2027 baseline after wage indexation? The second adds about EUR 1.5-2.0M to the gap.
- Is it an in-year or a Q4 run-rate target? No option reaches a full 8% in calendar 2027 without large job losses, and even those don't get there.
- What is it for: margin, a covenant, a valuation? If it is margin, price and utilisation count, and the 8% opex number may be the wrong target. If a covenant is involved, we need a 13-week cash forecast and an early talk with the bank.
