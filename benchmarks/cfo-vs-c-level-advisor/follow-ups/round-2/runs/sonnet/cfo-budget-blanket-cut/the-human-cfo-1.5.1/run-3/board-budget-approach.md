# Budget 2027: reach 8% lower opex through targeted levers, not a flat cut

Draft for the board meeting. Status: all figures are labelled ESTIMATES scaled from what we know (600 people, payroll 68% of opex, thin positive margin). Replace them with ledger and payroll actuals before the paper goes out.

## 1. The target and why

- Board ask: opex 8% lower next year. CEO proposal: every cost centre cuts 8%.
- Working base (estimate): opex EUR 69.0m, of which payroll EUR 46.9m (68%) and non-payroll EUR 22.1m (32%). Average loaded cost per FTE about EUR 78k. 8% = EUR 5.5m.
- To confirm before the meeting: (a) 8% of what? This year's actual, or next year's baseline including wage indexation (about 3.5% of payroll, roughly EUR 1.6m, is a hidden extra gap if the baseline is this year's cost); (b) what the 8% is for: margin, a covenant, a valuation or a bank conversation. The purpose decides the right mix; (c) what share of staff is billable, and the order book.
- Revenue and pricing are not opex, so they do not count towards the 8%, but they can meet the board's margin goal. I show them separately.

## 2. Why not a flat 8% per cost centre

1. It lands on payroll. With 68% of opex in payroll, a flat cut means about EUR 3.75m of payroll, around 48 FTE (estimate), unless payroll is exempted, in which case non-payroll must fall by far more than 8%.
2. In a services firm, most cost centres are delivery. If 70% of the 48 are billable and their work is sold, EUR 4.0m of annual revenue (at an estimated EUR 120k per billable FTE) goes against EUR 2.6m of cost saved: margin falls by about EUR 1.4m a year. At thin margin that is a worse result than doing nothing.
3. It is late. In the Netherlands the works council (advisory right on reorganisation, art. 25 WOR) must be consulted before the decision, collective dismissal of 20 or more in one UWV region triggers notification and consultation with the unions and UWV (WMCO), and dismissals for business reasons need UWV approval, followed by notice periods and statutory transition payments. Realistic exits: third quarter. Check timing and obligations with employment counsel.
4. It costs money first. Estimated one-off costs (transition payments, notice, advice, outplacement) of about half a year's loaded cost per person come to about EUR 1.9m, against about EUR 0.9m saved in the year. Net in-year: about minus EUR 0.9m on payroll.
5. It does not stick, and it causes more leavers. Downsizing of 1% was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008). An extra 3 points of attrition is 18 leavers; at 0.5x annual cost each to replace (Gallup gives a range of 0.5x to 2x) that is about EUR 0.7m.
6. It treats the efficient and the bloated alike, and non-payroll contracts (leases, licences) do not shrink 8% on request.

Reserve levers, not counted above and only to be used when the earlier ones fall short, decided by quarter:
- Revenue and pricing (not opex, helps margin): rate indexation on contracts that allow it, unbilled work and unapproved discounts, utilisation. Each 1 point of utilisation on 420 billable people is worth about EUR 0.5m of revenue (estimate).
- Working capital and financing (if the aim is cash or a covenant, not margin): faster invoicing and collection, early talk with the bank. Do not stretch small suppliers.
- Focus: an explicit list of low-value work and internal programmes to stop.
- Voluntary exits for about 10 people, partly overlapping with attrition: about EUR 0.8m a year at exit, EUR 0.4m one-off (estimate). Offered, never pressed.
- Hours: if the dip is temporary, reduced hours led by leaders. Short-time work in the Netherlands currently covers only calamities outside normal business risk; check whether the replacement crisis scheme has passed.
- Involuntary exits only if all of the above fall short, once, with a fair package and support from the day of notice, after consultation with the works council.

## 5. What we protect, and why

- Billable and customer-facing capacity for sold work: each billable FTE brings about EUR 120k of revenue for about EUR 78k of cost (estimates).
- Training, security, quality and maintenance: cut only with the future cost written next to the saving. None is cut in this plan.
- Retention of our best people: replacing a leaver costs 0.5x to 2x annual salary (Gallup).

## 6. What stops or waits

To be filled with budget holders in the next two weeks: an explicit list of low-value programmes, reports, tools, trips and vendors. A budget that names nothing that stops is a flat cut under another name.

## 7. Headcount (flows, not cuts)

Opening 600. Expected leavers about 66. Refilled about 36 (sold-work capacity, critical roles). Not refilled about 30. Exit headcount about 570 through attrition. Every refill above the freeze needs a business case signed by the COO.

## 8. Leaders first

Management bonus pool and perks cut first and deepest, board and directors included. Bonus for 2027 on a balanced scorecard (profit honestly defined, customer retention, regretted attrition, quality), with part deferred. No bonus for headcount reduction.

## 9. Risks and sensitivities

- Non-payroll: every point missed on lever 1 is EUR 0.22m.
- Attrition: if leavers fall to 8% (48), and we still refill 36, only about 12 positions go unfilled and lever 2 shrinks to about EUR 0.9m run-rate. If leavers rise, protect sold capacity first.
- Pay review delay: it may raise regretted attrition. Break-even: it pays only if it causes fewer than about 20 extra leavers (EUR 0.8m / (0.5 x EUR 78k)). Watch it quarterly.
- Revenue: any billable capacity left unfilled while work is sold costs EUR 120k per FTE against EUR 78k saved.

## 10. What would change this budget

- Quarterly check on opex run-rate, regretted attrition, utilisation, customer retention and margin. If the run-rate is below plan at the end of Q2 by more than EUR 0.5m, the reserve levers open in order. Involuntary measures only after works council consultation.
- If the real aim is a covenant or cash, we add a 13-week cash forecast with base and downside cases and take it to the bank early.

## 11. People and legal process

- Works council (mandatory from 50 employees) engaged at the problem stage, with the real numbers, before the paper is final. Consultation with employee representatives on pay review changes and any reorganisation.
- Counsel to confirm WMCO, UWV and transition-payment treatment if any involuntary measure comes into play. This paper is not legal or tax advice.
