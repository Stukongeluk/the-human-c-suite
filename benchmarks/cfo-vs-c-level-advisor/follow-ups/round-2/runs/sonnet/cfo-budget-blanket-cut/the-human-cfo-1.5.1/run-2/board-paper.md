# Budget 2027: reach an 8% lower opex run-rate by Q4 through spend, hiring flows and leadership pay first, with people exits held in reserve

Draft for the board meeting in three weeks. Every figure below is an illustrative estimate built from the few facts we have (600 people, payroll 68% of opex, thin positive margin). Replace the inputs with ledger and payroll actuals before the paper goes out; the structure and the logic will hold.

## 1. The target and why

**Assumed baseline (estimate, to be replaced with actuals):**

| Item | Amount | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Fully loaded cost per FTE | EUR 85k | assumption, Dutch B2B services |
| Payroll | EUR 51.0m | 600 x 85k |
| Total opex | EUR 75.0m | payroll / 68% |
| Non-payroll opex | EUR 24.0m | the remainder |
| Revenue | about EUR 78m | assumption: 130k per FTE |
| Margin | about EUR 3m (about 4%) | "thin but positive" |
| 8% target | EUR 6.0m | 8% x 75.0m |

**The question the board must answer first:** what is the 8% for? A EUR 6.0m cut on a margin of about EUR 3m would lift margin from about 4% to about 12%. If the need is a margin level, a covenant, a valuation or cash, say which number and by when, because cost, price and working capital all move margin and they carry very different human and customer costs. If it is a pure 8% on the opex line, we also need to know whether the baseline is this year's run-rate and whether the planned wage indexation (about EUR 1.8m at 3.5%, estimate) is inside or outside the 8%.

**Timing reality:** an 8% cut that starts in January cannot be 8% of next year's P&L. It can be an 8% run-rate by Q4. The paper asks the board to adopt that definition explicitly.

## 2. Why not 8% on every cost centre

Steelman first: it is simple, looks fair, needs no debate between cost centre owners and can start immediately. Those are real advantages.

What it does here (estimates, EUR k, same basis as the alternatives below):

| Line | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Year 1 | Assumption |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross saving: payroll (48 FTE, exits by 30 June) | 0 | 0 | 1,020 | 1,020 | 2,040 | 8% x 51.0m = 48 FTE; works council advice, collective-dismissal notification and notice periods push exits to mid-year |
| Gross saving: non-payroll (8% of 24.0m, ramping) | 192 | 384 | 480 | 480 | 1,536 | ramp 40/80/100/100% |
| Severance, transition payments, notice, outplacement, legal | 0 | -467 | -700 | 0 | -1,167 | about 24k per head: statutory transition payment about 8k, social plan top-up about 6k, paid notice about 5k, outplacement and legal about 5k |
| Revenue at risk | 0 | 0 | -468 | -468 | -936 | 60% of cut roles billable; half their revenue not re-absorbed |
| Rehiring and ramp-up | 0 | 0 | 0 | -255 | -255 | a quarter of roles refilled late in the year |
| Extra voluntary attrition | 0 | -204 | -306 | -510 | -1,020 | 24 extra leavers at half a year's cost to replace; conservative, since research links even a 1% downsizing to 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor and Nyberg) |
| **Net effect** | **192** | **-287** | **26** | **267** | **199** | about 0.3% of opex |

Read it this way: 48 colleagues leave and the year-1 saving is about EUR 0.2m against a EUR 6.0m target. The gross EUR 3.6m is real on the slide and mostly gone in the net. Run-rate in year 2 is about EUR 6.0m gross but about EUR 3.1m after lost revenue and rehiring. Totals differ by rounding.

Other problems: it taxes the efficient and the bloated alike, it lands on payroll because that is 68% of the base, it hits billable capacity (our revenue), and cost programmes rarely stick: one line of research found only 10% of cost reduction programmes showing sustained results three years later (cited by McKinsey).

Work the levers in order of least harm to people and customers. Each has an owner, a date and a measure.

**Run-rate at Q4 exit:** about EUR 5.15m gross (6.9% of opex), about EUR 4.8m after revenue risk. **In-year:** EUR 3.4m (4.5%) including the temporary leadership measures, EUR 2.5m (3.4%) without them.

**Leaders first (temporary, one year, not counted in run-rate):** the executive team and directors (about 15 people, estimate) take a 10% reduction in fixed pay (about EUR 270k), 50% of the leadership bonus pool is not paid (about EUR 500k on an assumed EUR 1.0m pool) and perks are cut (about EUR 100k). Total about EUR 870k. They say plainly that the result is theirs, and the bonus scheme states in advance that leadership pay moves before frontline jobs. Fixed-pay cuts need each person's agreement or a basis in their contracts; check with counsel.

**The gap, stated honestly:** EUR 6.0m target less EUR 3.4m in-year = about EUR 2.6m in year 1. Three ways to treat it, none of them hidden:

1. **Redefine the target as a Q4 run-rate** (recommended). The permanent measures reach 6.9%.
2. **Close the last 1.1% through a voluntary exit scheme held in reserve** (see below), decided at the end of Q2 against triggers, not now. This brings the run-rate to EUR 6.0m (8.0%).
3. **If the real aim is margin, use price and revenue leakage**, which do not count as opex but do count as profit: 1% net price realisation on renewals is about EUR 0.8m, and recovering 0.5-1.0% of unbilled hours or unapproved discounts is about EUR 0.4-0.8m (both estimates; CRM and billing data will size them). Cash and working capital (invoicing on time, disputed invoices, payment terms) protect cash but are not opex.

## 5. What we protect, and what stops or waits

**Protected, with the cost of cutting them:** training and learning time, because our product is the people and a junior pipeline that stops today means a shortage of seniors in five years; security and quality controls; account management and delivery capacity on our top customers; pay for people in the lowest bands (indexation is a separate decision, see questions).

**Stops or waits:** discretionary consultants without a named deliverable; tools with low usage (licences reconciled against logins); non-billable projects without a sponsor and a measure; floor space not used on at least three days a week; events and travel without a customer reason; backfilling non-billable roles by default.

## 6. Headcount through flows

Expected leavers at 12% attrition: about 72 in the year (estimate; replace with our actuals). Rule: every vacancy is reviewed by the director and the CFO; billable roles are refilled when utilisation supports it, non-billable roles by exception. Roughly 30 of the 72 are not refilled, 42 are. Internal moves fill critical roles first. Fixed-term contracts are handled case by case, with counsel on the chain rules.

## 7. Dutch legal and process points (to verify with employment counsel; checked November 2026)

- We have more than 50 employees, so a works council is mandatory. Reorganisations carry an advisory right (article 25 WOR), and the council can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber. Share the problem and the numbers early, while the plan can still change. Systems that monitor attendance, behaviour or performance need its consent (article 27 WOR).
- Collective dismissal of 20 or more people in one UWV work area within three months requires notifying UWV and the unions and consulting them (WMCO). Employer-initiated settlement agreements count towards the 20, and the notification comes before the decision is final. This is why the reserve scheme is sized below the threshold and decided with counsel.
- Dismissed employees generally get a statutory transition payment, and employer notice runs one to four months by service, minus the time a UWV procedure took. Together with the advisory procedure these set the earliest realistic exit date at around mid-year, which is why the blanket option's saving arrives in Q3 and Q4.
- Check our collective agreement and any existing social plan.

## 8. Risks, sensitivities and what would change this budget

Two numbers matter most: **the non-payroll yield** (each 10% shortfall on the EUR 2.6m non-payroll run-rate is about EUR 0.26m) and **the attrition rate** (each 1 point is 6 people, about EUR 0.5m run-rate if they are not refilled; if attrition falls to 8%, the flow lever shrinks to about 20 roles). Break-even for the blanket option: it already nets to about zero in year 1, and a further EUR 0.8m of lost revenue (1% of revenue) turns it negative.

**Triggers and the action each sets off:**

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Non-payroll run-rate below EUR 1.5m at end of Q2 | Escalate to the CEO; extend zero-base to the remaining lines; look at price and leakage |
| Attrition below 9% at end of Q2 | Add voluntary exit reserve, after works council consultation |
| Regretted attrition above 8%, or utilisation below target for two months | Pause the freeze on affected teams |
| Top-20 customer loss or satisfaction drop | Reverse freeze in the affected delivery team |

**Measures reviewed monthly:** cash, margin, opex run-rate against plan, utilisation, regretted attrition, time to fill, employee engagement, customer retention and satisfaction.

- **Week 1:** pull the ledger by vendor and cost centre, payroll in aggregate by team, attrition and utilisation history, contract and lease renewal dates, the loan agreement (covenants), CRM renewals. Replace every estimate here.
