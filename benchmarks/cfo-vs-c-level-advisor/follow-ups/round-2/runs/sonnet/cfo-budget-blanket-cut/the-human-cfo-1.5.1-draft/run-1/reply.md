**Why the blanket cut fails.** Payroll is 68% of the base, so 8% there is about 48 roles. Under Dutch rules that means works council advice first, then the collective dismissal notification (20 or more in a UWV work area within three months), notice and transition payments. Exits land mid-year at best, so I estimate:

| 2027, € m | Blanket 8% | Recommended plan |
|---|---|---|
| Gross saving | 2.6 | 3.8 |
| One-offs, rehiring, extra attrition | -2.4 | -0.2 |
| Net | **0.3** | **3.6** |
| Exit run-rate | 5.3 | 5.4 |

In a services firm most of the 48 would be billable. If 60% are, removing them lowers margin by about €1.1m a year. The research is similar: a 1% downsizing was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year (Trevor & Nyberg).

**The plan (run-rate, € m):**
- Zero-base vendors, licences, cloud, consultants, facilities and travel: 2.2
- Stop list of low-value internal projects and events: 0.5
- Hiring freeze with attrition: not refilling 25 overhead roles out of about 80 expected leavers: 1.9
- Leaders first: leadership variable pay and perks at 50%, management team fixed pay at -10%: 0.8

Training, security, quality and customer-facing capacity are protected, because cutting them costs more later than it saves now.

**Closing the in-year gap, in order:** (1) agree the 8% means exit run-rate; (2) pricing and revenue leakage, about €0.7m per 1% of rates (margin, not opex, so the board must say whether it counts); (3) move bench staff into billable work; (4) a checkpoint at end of Q1, then a voluntary exit scheme for named roles if still needed; (5) forced reductions only after that, once, with full consultation. Short-time work is not available in the Netherlands for ordinary demand dips.

**For the CEO:** keep the simplicity. The rule becomes "no cost centre gets a percentage, every one gets a challenge, and leadership goes first." That is as easy to say as 8%, and it will survive contact with the numbers.
