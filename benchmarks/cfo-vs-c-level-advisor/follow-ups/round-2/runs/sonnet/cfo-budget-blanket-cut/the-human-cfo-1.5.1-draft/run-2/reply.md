I'd take a different budget to the board than the flat 8%: a targeted plan that reaches about 7.2-7.7% lower opex by the end of 2027 with no redundancies planned, and a decision point in Q2 on whether anything more is needed. Neither route delivers 6.0m inside 2027. I've drafted the full board paper with the numbers (board-budget-paper.md).

**Why not 8% everywhere.** Payroll is 68% of opex, so an equal percentage lands on people: about 48 jobs. In the Netherlands those exits arrive late. The works council must give advice first (mandatory from 50 employees), a dismissal of 20 or more people triggers the collective dismissal procedure with UWV and the unions, and notice runs one to four months. I assumed exits at the end of Q2, so the cost lands before the saving.

The figures below are my estimates (revenue 78m, opex 75m, 8% = 6.0m, about 85k per head), so please replace them with your ledger.

| m euro | Blanket 8% | Targeted plan | Do nothing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net saving 2027 | 1.3 | 3.4 (4.2 with pricing) | 0 |
| Net saving run-rate 2028 | about 3.4 | about 5.4 | 0 |
| One-off cost | about 1.0 | 0.15 | 0 |
| Redundancies | about 48 | none planned | none |

In a services firm the blanket cut has a second problem. A billable consultant brings in about 130k and costs about 85k, so any delivery role billed above roughly 77% loses more margin than it saves. Spread evenly, the cut will hit billable people.

**The targeted plan**
1. Zero-base every non-payroll line (24m): subcontractor rates, licences, cloud, travel, premises, advisers. Target 10%, which is 2.4m run-rate and 1.8m in 2027.
2. Hiring freeze with exceptions. Of about 72 expected leavers, leave about 30 unfilled, chosen from work we explicitly stop. That is 2.55m run-rate and 1.3m in 2027, less about 15% for cover.
3. Leaders first. Cut the leadership bonus pool 50% and ask the management team for a voluntary 10% pay reduction: about 0.7m in 2027. The bonus and pay rules may need works council consent.
4. Protected: billable delivery capacity, customer success, training, security and quality.
5. Reserve for Q2, only if we are off track: voluntary exits for about 12 people (about 1.0m run-rate, 0.5m one-off).

Pricing is outside "opex" but is the cheapest margin lever. Two percent from Q2 is about 0.8m in 2027 (estimate).

**The CEO's case.** One rule is simple, looks fair and is fast to launch. I'd keep what's good in it: every cost centre gets a target and an owner, set from its actual spend instead of an equal percentage, and there is one review date.

**Two questions that change the numbers**
1. Is the 8% a 2027 in-year saving or a run-rate by year-end, and off which baseline? If 2027 already carries about 1.8m of pay indexation, the gap is nearer 7.8m.
2. What is the 8% for: margin, a covenant, an owner's return? If it is margin, pricing and revenue count, and the answer changes.

Before the meeting, I'd confirm the works council chair is briefed, since the board can approve the approach but not individual measures. This is not legal advice, so have counsel check the timeline.
