**Why the cuts don't work for this meeting**
- Dutch process takes time: the works council (mandatory at your size) must be asked for advice before the decision, and 20 or more dismissals within three months in one UWV region means notifying UWV and the unions. Exits fall in Q3 at the earliest. Announcing first also invites an Enterprise Chamber challenge.
- Costs come first, savings later. I estimate €0.49m one-off (transition payments, outplacement, advisers) against €1.6m gross per year, or about €1.2m after leakage. Payback is about five months after the last exit. That is a decent later business case, but it is not a fix for the test date.
- At the test, leverage is 3.61x with the provision added back and about 3.94x without. Doing nothing is 3.60x. If sales come in 5% under forecast, doing nothing gives 3.78x and the cuts 3.8x to 4.1x.
- An announcement can also read to the bank, your credit insurers and your best craftspeople as distress, not control. The bank will look for a credible forecast and for what the owners are putting in. A headcount percentage shows neither.

**What shows the bank you're serious**
1. A 13-week cash forecast with base and downside cases, updated weekly.
2. Covenant headroom by scenario, calculated the way the agreement defines it.
3. Named owners, sizes and dates for each lever, and what each scenario triggers.
4. The owner and leadership contributing first.
5. A request for a reset or waiver covering the next two tests, as insurance. Ask while the breach is still a forecast. Also check whether your agreement requires early notice of an expected breach, and what the auditor needs for going concern.

**The owner's side, fairly put.** A surprise breach is worse than a visible plan, and if demand has fallen for good, you will need a smaller cost base eventually. I agree with both.
