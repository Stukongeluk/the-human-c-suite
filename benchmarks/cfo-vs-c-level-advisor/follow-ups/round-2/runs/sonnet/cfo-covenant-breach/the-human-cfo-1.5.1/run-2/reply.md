**Why the cut doesn't fix the covenant**

- **Size of the gap.** To reach 3.5x you need about €0.5m less net debt, or €0.14m more EBITDA. To have a real buffer (3.25x) you need about €1.75m less net debt.
- **Timing.** With 50+ employees you have a works council. It must be asked for advice before the decision is made (art. 25 WOR). If you announce first, you invite an Enterprise Chamber challenge. Twenty or more dismissals in one UWV region within three months also triggers the collective-dismissal notification and consultation with the unions. Then come notice periods of one to four months. Realistically people leave around April to June, so Q2's LTM EBITDA gets almost none of the saving.
- **Cost.** I assumed 26 people at about €62k loaded each, so gross saving is about €1.6m a year. One-off costs (transition payments, legal, outplacement) are about €0.4m, paid around Q2 and Q3.
- **Effect on the ratio at 30 June.** Net debt goes up by the one-offs. If your facility doesn't let you add them back to EBITDA, you test at about 4.0x. If it does, about 3.7x. Either way you are further from compliance than today.
- **Beyond Q2.** The first-year net saving is roughly €0.95m, after one-offs and an assumed 15% of roles refilled. Furniture skills such as upholsterers, CNC operators and finishers take years to rebuild. A 1% downsizing has been associated with 31% more voluntary quits the following year (Trevor & Nyberg).
- **How the bank reads it.** Cuts announced three weeks before a review can look like panic, or like a demand problem you haven't mentioned.
