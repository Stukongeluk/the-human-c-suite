Don't announce the 10% cuts before the bank meeting. Go in with a plan that closes the covenant gap without job cuts, ask the bank for a safety net, and keep a prepared restructuring as a defined fallback. The cuts would make the June test worse, not better.

**Why the cuts don't fix the covenant**

The gap is small. At 3.6x against 3.5x, you need about EUR 0.09m more EBITDA or EUR 0.30m less net debt. I don't have your real figures, so I assumed about EUR 40m revenue, EUR 3.0m EBITDA and EUR 10.8m net debt, with 26 jobs at about EUR 58k loaded. These are estimates to replace with yours.

- Savings arrive late. In the Netherlands, 26 jobs is above the 20-in-three-months threshold for notifying UWV and the unions. The works council must be asked for advice while the decision can still change. Then come the UWV procedure or settlements and one to four months' notice. Exits before 30 June are optimistic.
- Costs arrive early. Transition payments, a social-plan top-up, outplacement and advisers come to roughly EUR 0.8m, paid when people leave. That is cash out before the test date.
- Result at 30 June: about 3.7x in the fast case, 3.9x if exits slip to the summer. If the bank doesn't add back restructuring costs, it is 4.9x or worse. Payback is about six months after exit, and leverage would be near 2.2x by mid-2028. It helps next year and hurts this test.
- Announcing first is also a legal risk: the works council's advisory right (art. 25 WOR) only works if the decision is still open. And under the Dutch selection rules (afspiegelingsbeginsel) the owner can't choose who goes. Selection runs by age band and then shortest service, so you may lose your youngest craftspeople.

**What I'd do instead (all labelled estimates)**

| Lever | Estimate, EUR m | Hits |
|---|---|---|
| Collect receivables 4 days faster, stock down 6% | 0.3 to 0.9 | Net debt |
| Defer capex that isn't safety or maintenance | 0.2 to 0.4 | Net debt |
| Pause the owner's dividend or drawings | 0.15 to 0.5 | Net debt |
| Pause leadership bonuses, management pay -10% for six months | 0.1 to 0.2 | EBITDA |
| Purchasing, energy and waste, price rise of about 1.5%, hiring freeze (part-year) | 0.13 to 0.37 | EBITDA |

If only half of this arrives, leverage at 30 June is about 3.15x. If everything arrives, about 2.7x. In a downside with EBITDA 5% lower and half delivered, about 3.35x. The worst combination (lowest estimates, downside, half delivered) is 3.56x, a narrow miss. That is why you also ask the bank for a headroom safety net, such as a reset to 4.0x for two test dates or an equity cure right. I'd budget EUR 30k to 60k for that, far less than a restructuring. The measures cost about EUR 0.05m up front (advisers and bank fees).

Two cautions. Working-capital cash must be real, not payments pushed onto small suppliers or moved around the test date. And don't cut maintenance, safety or training to make June.

**Taking it to the bank**

Bring a 13-week cash forecast with base and downside cases, the headroom for each, the actions each case triggers, and what the owner is putting in: the dividend pause first, leaders' pay next. My illustrative forecast shows the downside leaving about EUR 0.5m of cash at week 13 without action and about EUR 1.0m with the measures. Also ask whether restructuring costs are added back in the covenant EBITDA, so you know the true cost of cuts if you ever need them.

**Meeting the owner's real point**

The owner wants to show seriousness. That is fair: banks reward decisive action. But the bank's question is whether leverage is heading below 3.5x with margin, and a sudden cut announcement can read as panic, and it may also be a signal that the forecast is weaker than stated. Better proof of seriousness: the owner stops the dividend, takes a pay cut first, and signs up to defined triggers. For example, if order intake is 8% below plan for two months, or forecast leverage is above 3.4x at end of March, or working-capital measures deliver under half by March, then the prepared restructuring starts. It would be done once, properly, with the works council, counsel and a fair package. Prepare it now with counsel, but don't announce it.

**Questions that would change my numbers**

1. What do the loan documents say about EBITDA add-backs, shareholder loans and an equity cure?
2. Is the gap really about EUR 0.1m of EBITDA, or is demand weakening? If the true shortfall is above EUR 1m, the plan changes.
3. How large is the owner's dividend or drawings, and will the family contribute equity if needed?
4. Is there an elected works council, and which collective agreement applies? Short-time work (werktijdverkorting) currently covers only calamities, so I wouldn't count on it.
