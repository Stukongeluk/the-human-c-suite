**Don't announce the cuts. Go to the bank with a covenant plan that closes the gap without them, and ask for a pre-agreed amendment as insurance.** The gap is small: 3.6x against 3.5x is roughly 3% more EBITDA or 3% less net debt, and cash levers can cover that. A 10% cut (26 jobs) would not help the Q2 test and would probably make it worse.

**Why the announcement backfires**

- **It arrives too late.** You need a works council advice procedure first (it is mandatory above 50 employees), then, for 20 or more dismissals in one UWV region within three months, a collective-dismissal notification and a UWV procedure, then notice periods. Exits realistically fall in June to August. The Q2 test gets almost no saving.
- **The cost lands first.** I estimate transition payments, legal and outplacement at about €0.46m (26 people, about 10 years' service, about €3,800 gross a month). The saving is about €1.5m a year (26 × about €58k loaded), but it starts after the test date.
- **The leverage effect.** Net debt rises about €0.46m, so leverage goes to about 3.7x even if the bank allows the charge as an add-back. If your definition doesn't allow it, it is about 4.1x. Check your covenant definitions.
- **Announcing before the works council is heard** invites a challenge at the Enterprise Chamber and loses you the people you need. In a tight labour market the skilled workers leave first.
- **Banks look at cash, headroom and a credible plan, not headcount.** An announcement without a plan reads as panic. Have employment counsel confirm the procedure and timing.

**Options, same basis (illustrative, €m, 31 Dec 2026 to 30 Jun 2027 test)**

| | Leverage at Q2 | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | 3.6x (3.8x if EBITDA is 5% lower) | Breach; the bank sets the terms |
| Announce 10% cuts | about 3.7x | About €0.1m saving by Q2, €0.46m cost; €1.5m a year from H2 |
| Cash and margin package, low case | 3.3x | Net debt -€0.9m, EBITDA +€0.2m |
| Cash and margin package, mid case | 3.1x | Net debt -€1.5m, EBITDA +€0.4m |
| Mid case with EBITDA 5% lower | 3.2x | Still inside the limit |

The package is made of these levers, each a labelled estimate (low to high):

- **Working capital:** inventory and receivables, €0.4–1.0m. Do not stretch small suppliers to get there.
- **Capex timing:** defer growth capex only, €0.2–0.5m. Keep safety and maintenance spending.
- **Owner:** hold the dividend and drawings, and consider a subordinated shareholder loan, €0.3–0.7m. Check whether your agreement counts it as equity.
- **Spend:** energy, materials and freight renegotiation, €0.1–0.3m EBITDA.
- **Pricing and discount leakage:** €0.1–0.4m EBITDA.

Two limits. The low case fails if EBITDA drops 10% (about 3.6x), so the bank should also hear about the amendment. And these are unsized until your data confirms them; none of them counts as closing the gap until it is sized.

**Also do**

- **Leaders first.** Management and owner pay cut visibly for six months. I haven't counted it as a saving. Its value is as a signal to the bank, which is what the owner wants to show.
- **Hiring freeze with exceptions.** Natural attrition in manufacturing is often 6–8% a year, so a freeze removes 15 to 20 roles within a year without a dismissal. Keep scarce skills and apprentices.
- **Ask the bank now,** not after the breach: a covenant reset to about 4.0x for two to three quarters, or an equity cure right, agreed in advance. It costs a fee, but it is cheaper than a default or a repricing.
- **Bring the works council in early,** as a partner on the plan with real numbers, not at the end.
