**Don't announce the 10% cut. Go to the bank with a funded plan that closes the 0.1x gap without job losses, and tell the owner why that shows more seriousness than a headcount number.**

The gap is small. At 3.6x against 3.5x you need roughly 2.9% more EBITDA or 2.8% less net debt. I don't have your absolute figures, so here is an illustration: on EBITDA of about €3.5m and net debt of about €12.6m (my estimates for a 260-person manufacturer), you need about €0.35m less debt or €0.1m more EBITDA. Please replace these with your own numbers. Everything below scales from them.

**Why the cuts would not fix Q2, and could make it worse**

A 10% cut is about 26 people, roughly €1.7m a year gross at an assumed €65k loaded cost. That sounds like it solves the problem, but the timing does not work:
- **Process:** the works council must be asked for advice while the decision can still change (art. 25 WOR). 26 dismissals in three months in one UWV region also trigger the WMCO notification and consultation of the unions. Then come the UWV procedure and notice periods of one to four months. Realistically the first exits land around the end of Q2, so the savings arrive after the test date.
- **Cost:** transition payments, paid notice and advisers come first. I estimate about €0.55m in cash (about €22k a person, a rough guess).
- **Effect on the test:** with no savings inside the period, the ratio goes from 3.6x to about 3.76x if the facility lets you add back restructuring costs, and to about 4.5x if it does not. With two months of savings it is still 3.68x to 4.0x. Check how your facility defines EBITDA and one-off items.
- **Other risks:** announcing a decision before the works council has advised invites a challenge at the Enterprise Chamber. The bank may also read a hurried headcount announcement as a sign of panic. The people you most want to keep, with the most options elsewhere, tend to leave first.

**What I'd do instead**

These are estimates in €m, to be validated against your ledger. Nothing is counted twice.

| Lever | Effect on net debt / EBITDA | Lands | Cost in jobs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inventory and WIP down 3–5% (assume about €6m held) | 0.18–0.30 | Q1–Q2 | none |
| Receivables: 2 days faster (assume €44m revenue) | about 0.24 | Q1–Q2 | none |
| Capex timing (defer, don't cancel maintenance or safety) | 0.2–0.4 | Q1–Q2 | none |
| No dividend or owner's drawings until leverage is back under 3.0x | 0.2–0.5 | immediate | none |
| Vendor, energy and spend renegotiation (about 1% of an assumed €30m addressable) | 0.15–0.30 | Q1–Q2 | none |
| Hiring freeze with exceptions (about 8 leavers not replaced) | 0.1–0.25 | gradual | none |
| **Total** | **about 1.1–2.0** | | |

Even the low end is about three times the €0.35m gap. Pricing and material surcharges are further upside I have not sized. Two honesty notes. Inventory and receivables only count if you do not squeeze small suppliers to get there. And these are my ranges, so the first job is to replace them with your own.

Add a leadership step. The owner and management waive bonuses this year and the owner takes the first and largest sacrifice. Dividends and drawings stop first. That is the visible seriousness the owner wants, and it is more credible to the bank than a cut announced in the same week.

**The bank meeting**

Ask for a conversation, not a verdict. Bring:
1. A 13-week cash forecast, base and downside, with weekly headroom against the covenant and the minimum cash.
2. The path of the ratio at each quarter end through Q2, with and without the levers, and the one or two inputs that move it most (volume, material prices).
3. The actions each scenario triggers, with owners and dates. State the trigger: if headroom falls below an agreed level by a set date, which further step happens, including a works-council-led discussion of hours before any exits.
4. What the owner contributes: dividend stop, bonus waiver, and a willingness to discuss a subordinated shareholder loan or equity cure if the facility has that right.
5. A request, only if needed, for a covenant holiday, reset or waiver for the Q2 test. Early and prepared is usually cheaper than a breach discovered later.

**If the downside case still breaks the covenant**

Job cuts then become the last step, not the first. First consider reduced hours, noting that the Dutch short-time scheme covers only calamities and may not apply, and then voluntary exits. If you do reach involuntary cuts, do it once, with the works council involved from the start, and follow the selection rules (afspiegelingsbeginsel). I'd cost that option properly before the bank meeting so you can say what it would save, when, and what it would cost.

**What to tell the owner**

For a family firm, the name in the region, the next generation and customer trust are the assets. A cut announced to impress a lender destroys some of that to fix a 0.1x gap that cash and working capital can close. Offer him the bank pack and the leadership sacrifice as his proof of seriousness.

**Assumptions, and what would change my mind**
- The illustrative figures above (EBITDA, net debt, revenue, inventory, cost per person) are estimates.
- Whether restructuring costs count in covenant EBITDA, and whether your quarter ends make Q2 six or seven months away.
- If sales are falling structurally, not just dipping, or the downside case shows a much larger breach, my view on cuts changes. In that case they may be unavoidable.
- This is not legal advice. Check the facility terms with your lawyer, and the works council, WMCO and UWV steps with employment counsel.
