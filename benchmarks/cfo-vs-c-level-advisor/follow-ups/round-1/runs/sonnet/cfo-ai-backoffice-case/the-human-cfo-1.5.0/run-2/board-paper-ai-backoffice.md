# Board paper: AI in finance and back-office (90 FTE) - what the evidence supports

Draft for the CFO. All SEK figures are in millions. Every number marked (E) is an estimate with its basis stated; none comes from our ledger or payroll yet. Replace them with actuals before the paper goes to the board. Not legal, tax or investment advice: Swedish employment law, the collective agreement and Finansinspektionen requirements need counsel and, where relevant, the auditors.

## 1. Recommendation

Ask the board to approve a gated programme that targets a 15-30% reduction in back-office FTE, with 22% (about 20 FTE) as the base case by end of 2029, and to commit to the full 30% only if the pilot gates show the hours are really freed. Do not approve 27 redundancies in 2027.

The numbers behind this (base case, E):

- Cutting 27 of 90 roles in 2027 and hoping the AI catches up: gross saving of only 5.6 in 2027 (against a 23.0 annual run-rate the board imagines), net **-17.4** in 2027 and **-11.5** cumulative over 2027-2029. It turns positive quarter by quarter only from Q4 2028 and cumulatively not before 2030.
- Staged plan (freeze backfills, attrition, voluntary exits, redeploy; headcount follows proven capacity): net **-6.6** in 2027, +1.2 in 2028, +11.4 in 2029; **+5.9** cumulative, cumulative break-even in Q3 2029, steady-state net saving about 13.5 a year from 2030 (17.0 gross for 20 FTE less 3.5 AI run cost).
- 30% by end of 2029 is reachable only in the high case (+14.6 cumulative). In the low case the staged plan roughly breaks even (-3.4) and we have learned cheaply that the tasks are not automatable at our quality bar.

## 2. The problem the board is actually solving

"Replace 30%" is a solution, not a problem. Before the paper is final we need the target behind it: an expense-ratio or combined-ratio point, a SEK amount, a date, and whether it is a cost problem or a growth/volume problem the team will have to absorb. The case below works for any of these, but the timing in section 5 shows 2027 cannot deliver a 30% saving whichever target it is.

## 3. Where the hours are (task sizing, E)

Team split is an assumption (get the real one from HR in aggregate): AP 25, reconciliations 25, reporting and analysis 25, management, controls and other 15.

| Area | FTE | Share of tasks plausibly automated at steady state (E) | FTE-equivalent freed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accounts payable (invoice capture, coding, matching, payment runs) | 25 | 40% | 10.0 |
| Reconciliations (bank, intercompany, claims and premium sub-ledgers) | 25 | 30% | 7.5 |
| Reporting and analysis (close, management reports, IFRS 17 / Solvency II inputs) | 25 | 12% | 3.0 |
| Management, controls, other | 15 | 5% | 0.8 |
| Gross freed | 90 | | 21.3 |
| Less review, exception handling and monitoring of the AI itself | | | -1.3 |
| **Net freed, base case** | | | **20 (22%)** |

Range: low 13 (14%), high 27 (30%). The board's 30% is the top of the range, not the middle. Basis: AP and matching are the most mature use cases; judgement-heavy reporting is the least. These shares are to be replaced by measured results from the pilot, against a baseline from our own volumes and error rates. Some of the "AI" saving is plain process work (e-invoicing, matching rules already in the ERP, fewer approval steps) and should be done first because it is cheaper and carries less risk.

Base rates to keep in view: an MIT NANDA study (2025) reported that about 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact (small sample, criticised method: a direction, not a rate); Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end-2027 on cost, unclear value or weak risk controls.

## 4. Options, costed on the same basis

All options carry the AI programme cost (non-staff): 6.0 in 2027, 4.0 in 2028, 3.5 a year after (E: licences and usage, ERP integration, data work, controls, monitoring, audit trail). Including it in every option is deliberate; the staged plan is not "free".

| Option | What it is | 2027 net | 2028 net | 2029 net | 3-year net | FTE out by end 2029 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Do nothing | Keep 90 FTE, no AI programme | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| A. Board ask | 27 redundancies in 2027, AI follows | -17.4 | -3.5 | +9.4 | -11.5 | 27 |
| B. Staged plan (recommended) | Gates; freeze backfills, attrition, 3 voluntary exits, redeploy | -6.6 | +1.2 | +11.4 | +5.9 | 20 |

Sensitivity to how much the AI really frees (low / base / high):

| Option | Low (13 FTE) | Base (20 FTE) | High (27 FTE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Board ask, 3-year net | -26.7 | -11.5 | +12.5 |
| B. Staged plan, 3-year net | -3.4 | +5.9 | +14.6 |

Option A only beats B if the AI delivers the high case, and it is the only option that is deeply negative if it does not. B loses at most about 3 in the low case and keeps the ability to stop. Do nothing has no incremental cost but leaves the cost base to wage inflation and volume growth; it is the benchmark, not a recommendation.

Why the "work still to be done" line is so large: in the base case the AI has freed only 5 FTE-equivalents by end 2027 and 15 by end 2028, yet 27 roles are gone. The remaining work does not disappear; it is done by contractors, overtime or rehires, or it is done late. That is how the evidence says such cuts usually end: a 2026 Careerminds survey of 600 HR leaders found two in three companies that cut for AI were already rehiring, and for 30.9% rehiring costs exceeded the savings (vendor survey: a strong signal, not a precise rate). The 1.3x premium and the 20% rehire rate are estimates; the shape holds across reasonable values.

Also not in the table (not priced): service and control quality in AP, reconciliations and reporting at a regulated insurer; and the Swedish selection rule below.

A headcount saving is booked only after the gate shows the hours are freed. Until then the benefit is reported as capacity: hours freed, and where they go (backlog, controls, IFRS 17 and DORA work, better service).

## 7. What goes first, before any job

1. **Process fixes that need no AI:** e-invoicing and supplier consolidation, auto-matching rules in the existing ERP, fewer manual approvals. Size from AP and reconciliation volumes (E to be replaced).
2. **Non-payroll spend of the function:** vendors, licences, consultants, tools. If non-payroll is about 20% of the 76.5 payroll (E), a 5-10% saving is 0.8-1.5 a year. Small, and not counted in the tables above; it does not close the gap.
3. **Hiring freeze with exceptions, then attrition, then redeployment and reskilling**, which is Option B.
4. **Leaders first.** If the programme underdelivers, the executive team's bonus is reduced before any frontline role is touched, and no bonus is paid on headcount reduction as such. Scorecard to accompany the programme: cost per processed invoice and per reconciliation, close time, audit findings, customer-facing service levels, regretted attrition, engagement.
5. **Involuntary reductions only after the above, once, with proper support** (section 8), and only for the part of the gap the evidence shows cannot be reached otherwise.

## 8. Sweden: process and law (verify with counsel and HR; checked November 2026)

- **Co-determination (MBL):** negotiate with the unions before a major change is decided. Bring them in at the problem stage with these numbers; that is both the law and the best risk review.
- **Selection (LAS):** if people are made redundant, selection follows length of service within each operational unit and collective-agreement area, unless a collective agreement or union-agreed list says otherwise, with an exemption for three employees. The roles AI makes redundant are therefore not necessarily the people who leave; newer and often younger staff go first, long-serving staff stay. Management cannot choose. This is a cost and a risk to Option A that the table does not capture.
- **Notice and notification:** one to six months' notice by length of service. Dismissing 26-100 people must be notified to the Public Employment Service at least four months ahead. People dismissed for lack of work generally have priority for re-employment for nine months.
- **Transition support:** job security councils funded through collective agreements step in from notice; check which one covers our collective agreement.
- **Regulatory:** Finansinspektionen expectations on internal control, outsourcing and ICT/operational resilience (including DORA) apply to automated finance processes and any AI vendor. The EU AI Act obligations on informing workers about high-risk workplace AI start 2 December 2027 per the 2026 Digital Omnibus (they do not apply yet); AI that evaluates or allocates work among staff would fall in scope, so keep the finance AI on documents and transactions, not on ranking people. GDPR applies to the data used.
- **Controls:** segregation of duties, audit trail on every AI-touched posting, and a named human who signs off anything that moves money or affects a person. No AI posting or payment without approval.

## 9. Risks, sensitivities and what would change this paper

Biggest sensitivities: (1) share of tasks really automated (13 / 20 / 27 FTE; the table in section 4); (2) cost of covering the gap in Option A (1.3x premium; at 1.0x Option A improves by about 8 over three years but is still negative in the base case, about -3); (3) loaded cost per FTE (0.85, range 0.75-0.95; a 10% change moves gross savings by about 10%).

What would change my recommendation: a pilot that shows 25%+ touchless on AP and a 30% reconciliation match rate by Q2 2027 with controls intact (accelerate); our actual attrition being much lower than 10% (add voluntary exits earlier, with packages); a hard external constraint such as a regulatory capital or covenant event that needs cash sooner (revisit the ladder, not just payroll).

Pre-mortem to run with the team: "It is December 2027 and this failed. Why?" Likely answers: data quality in the ERP, close deadlines missed, auditors reject the audit trail, the best people left, the savings were spent on contractors.
