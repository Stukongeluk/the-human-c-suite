I'd not put a 30% cut (27 people) in front of the board as a 2027 commitment. I built the case that the numbers support instead: a gated programme with a planning range of 8 to 18 FTE-equivalents of real capacity by end-2028 (9% to 20% of 90, base case 13). That is worth about SEK 2.9m / 6.9m / 10.9m a year net of AI running costs. The full draft board paper is in board-paper.md, and the model is in model.py. Every figure is my estimate (loaded cost SEK 0.8m per FTE, AI one-off 5.0 and run cost 3.5 a year), so please replace them with your ledger, payroll and vendor-quote data.

**Why not 30%**
- 30% of 90 is 27 FTE, about SEK 21.6m a year. For AI to carry that, it must do roughly 30% of all the work at audit quality, plus 4 to 7 FTE-equivalents just to pay for itself. My task sizing for AP, reconciliations and reporting gives a technical ceiling of about 28 to 45% of effort. After review, exceptions, month-end peaks and fragmented hours, that comes down to the 8 to 18 above.
- The base rates are poor. About 95% of enterprise GenAI pilots showed no P&L impact in the MIT NANDA study (method disputed, so direction only). In vendor surveys of firms that cut jobs for AI, about two in three were already rehiring and only 8.4% would repeat the restructure unchanged.
- Swedish rules slow the saving. Under MBL you negotiate first. Notifying Arbetsförmedlingen takes four months for 26 to 100 people. LAS puts selection by length of service, so you can't choose who goes, and the cut lands on your newest people. Exits are realistically around 1 July 2027, before any pilot has proven the capacity.

**Two options, same basis, SEK m (base case)**

| | 2027 | 2028 | 2027+28 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Board ask: 27 exits by redundancy | -11.7 | +2.3 | -9.4 |
| B. Gated: fix processes, pilot, freeze, attrition, redeploy | -7.7 | +3.7 | -4.0 |
| Do nothing | baseline | baseline | baseline |

In A, severance and legal come to about 7.6 and covering the work AI hasn't taken over costs about 17 (contractors and overtime). Rehiring and extra attrition come to about 5.8, and about 6 to 10 FTE-equivalents of work is simply left undone, which is a close and control risk for an insurer. B is better in every capacity case I ran: low -13.3 vs -8.0, high -5.7 vs -3.9 over 2027+28. A only wins later, and only if AI really does about 27 FTE of work. Both options lose money in 2027 because AI costs come first, so neither fixes a 2027 earnings number. Break-even is about 4.4 FTE-equivalents freed to cover run costs alone, or 6.5 with the one-off included.

**What I'd put to the board**
1. First, the target. What is the real goal (expense ratio, a SEK amount, a date)? "30%" is a solution to it.
2. Work the non-people levers: vendor and outsourcing spend, stopping low-value reports and duplicate reconciliations (my estimate is 3 to 6 FTE-equivalents of effort, overlapping with AI, not additive), and fixing supplier data and approval rules before automating them.
3. Pilot AI on AP and reconciliations against a baseline taken beforehand. Gate 1 at end Q2 2027 sets the commitment from measured hours. Gate 2 at end Q4 decides scale or stop.
4. In the meantime, freeze hiring and let attrition (about 9 a year, my estimate) and redeployment into controls, claims and finance data carry the change, with paid reskilling time.
5. Only if a gap remains after the gate: voluntary exits, then involuntary once, with MBL, TRR or your agreement's job security council, and re-hire priority.

The board's concern is fair: AI will change this work and waiting has a price. The answer is a fixed calendar and an agreed trigger to move faster if the pilot beats the base case. A hard 2027 cash or regulatory deadline would change my advice.

Please check the Swedish employment-law points, Finansinspektionen/Solvency II/IFRS 17 and DORA/AI Act implications, and the auditor's view with counsel before this goes anywhere. Nothing here is legal advice.

Three things would sharpen the numbers most: the board's actual target and date, your headcount split across AP, reconciliations, reporting and other, and your attrition and contractor spend. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
