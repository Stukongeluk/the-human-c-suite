| Option | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 3-year net | Roles out by end 2029 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Both options carry the same AI programme cost (about 6.0 in 2027, then 4.0 and 3.5 a year), so B isn't flattered.

**Why 2027 can't deliver 30%**

- **Timing.** Under Swedish law you negotiate with the unions first (MBL). Dismissing 26-100 people must be notified to the Public Employment Service four months ahead. Notice runs one to six months. Exits land in Q3 and Q4, so the gross saving in 2027 is about 5.6, not the 23 a year it becomes later. Severance, outplacement and legal costs of about 11 fall in the same two quarters.
- **The work doesn't leave with the people.** In my base case the AI has freed about 5 FTE-equivalents by end 2027 and 15 by end 2028. With 27 roles gone, the rest is covered by contractors, overtime or rehires. That alone costs about 20 in 2028 and almost cancels the saving. The surveys of companies that cut for AI point the same way (two in three were already rehiring; vendor surveys, so a signal, not a rate).
- **Sweden's selection rule.** In a redundancy, selection follows length of service within each unit, not "whose work AI replaced". The newest people go first and the long-serving stay. You can't choose, and that risk isn't in my table.
- **How much the AI can free is a range.** On my task sizing (AP about 40% automatable, reconciliations about 30%, reporting about 12%) it is 13 / 20 / 27 FTE (low / base / high). 30% is the top of the range. Option A beats B only in the high case and loses about SEK 27 million in the low case. B loses about SEK 3 million in the low case and can be stopped at any gate.

**What I'd ask the board to approve**

1. A pilot on AP and bank reconciliation with a baseline, finished by end Q2 2027, including whether Risk and the auditors accept the audit trail.
2. Headcount that follows proven capacity: freeze backfills once a gate is passed, then attrition, then voluntary exits and redeployment into backlog, IFRS 17 and DORA work. Book no payroll saving until the hours are shown to be freed. Until then, report it as capacity.
3. Before any of that, the fixes that need no AI: e-invoicing, supplier consolidation, matching rules already in your ERP, and non-payroll spend. They are small (I estimate SEK 1-1.5m a year) and don't close the gap, but they're cheaper and carry less risk.
4. A decision on the headcount number at mid-2028, from 12 months of production data, not by decree.
5. Leaders first. If the programme underdelivers, executive bonuses are cut before any frontline role, and no bonus is paid on headcount reduction as such.
6. Employee representatives involved now, while the numbers can still change. That's what MBL requires anyway, and their objections are a free risk review.

If involuntary reductions are still needed after that, I'd do them once, decently and last, funded from the day of notice, for the part of the gap the evidence shows can't be reached otherwise.

**What this doesn't cover.** As an insurer you have Finansinspektionen and DORA expectations on controls and outsourcing, and the EU AI Act's worker-information duties start in December 2027 (after the Digital Omnibus delay). That affects the design, not whether to proceed. Keep the AI on transactions and documents, not on ranking people. This isn't legal advice: counsel, HR and your collective agreement need to confirm the Swedish steps.
