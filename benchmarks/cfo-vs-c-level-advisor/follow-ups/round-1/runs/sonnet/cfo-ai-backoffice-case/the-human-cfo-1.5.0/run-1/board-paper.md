# Board paper (DRAFT): AI in finance and back-office, what we can commit to and when

**Recommendation.** Do not commit to a 30% (27 FTE) reduction in 2027. Approve a gated programme: fix and simplify the processes, pilot AI on accounts payable and reconciliations, freeze hiring and use natural attrition and redeployment to bank the capacity that is proven. Commit to a number only at a gate in mid-2027. Planning range: 8 to 18 FTE-equivalents (9% to 20% of 90) of real capacity by end-2028, base case 13 (14%). Payroll saving at that point, net of AI running cost: SEK 2.9m / 6.9m / 10.9m a year (low / base / high).

All figures are SEK millions and are ESTIMATES built on stated assumptions, to be replaced with ledger, payroll-aggregate and pilot data before this goes to the board. Not legal, tax or investment advice; Swedish employment law, MBL consultation, Finansinspektionen and audit points need counsel and the auditors.

## 1. What problem are we solving?

"30% of the team" is a solution. The board's real target needs stating: an expense-ratio goal, a SEK amount, a date, or a response to a competitor? The 30% equals 27 FTE, about SEK 21.6m of fully loaded cost a year (27 x 0.80, estimate). Questions for the board: is it the amount or the percentage? Is it recurring? What is the cost of not meeting it?

## 2. What AI can honestly do here (hypotheses until the pilot measures them)

| Area (assumed FTE, replace with actuals) | Tasks AI can plausibly take | Share of effort, est. range |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts payable (30) | invoice capture, coding, matching, exceptions triage | 40-60% |
| Reconciliations (25) | matching, break classification, proposed entries | 30-50% |
| Reporting (20) | data assembly, commentary drafts, variance flags | 20-35% |
| Other back-office (15) | queries, accruals support, controls evidence | 10-20% |

Weighted, that is roughly 28-45% of task effort at the technical limit. After review, exceptions, month-end peaks, controls and the fact that freed hours come in fragments across people, the headcount-convertible share in the first two years is lower: the 8 to 18 FTE-equivalent range above. Reaching 27 would need AI to do about 30% of all the work to the required quality, plus another 4 to 7 FTE-equivalents just to pay for itself. The only way to know is a pilot against a baseline.

Base rates to keep in mind: about 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact in MIT NANDA's 2025 study (method criticised; direction only); Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by end-2027; and in vendor surveys of companies that cut jobs for AI, about two in three were already rehiring and only 8.4% said the restructure delivered and they would repeat it unchanged (Careerminds, 2026; vendor survey, strong signal not a precise rate).

## 4. Options on the same basis (base capacity case)

Capacity freed by AI, average FTE-equivalent per period: 0 / 0 / 3 / 6 / 11 (Q1-27 to 2028).

Both options are negative in 2027 and cumulatively through 2028 in the base case: AI costs money before it saves any. Neither fixes a 2027 earnings number.

**Sensitivity (2027+2028 net):** low capacity: A -13.3, B -8.0. High capacity: A -5.7, B -3.9. A is worse in every case. A beats B only after 2028, and only if AI really does about 27 FTE-equivalents of work, which is above the high end of the range. Run-rate end-2028 for B (annual): 2.9 / 6.9 / 10.9. In B the 2028 reductions are limited by attrition and redeployment capacity (about 10 FTE average in the base case), so a high capacity case needs more internal moves.

**Break-even:** AI run cost alone needs 4.4 FTE-equivalents of freed capacity; including the 5.0 one-off over three years, 6.5. Below that, the programme loses money.

**What is not in the numbers (upside, unbooked):** faster close, fewer errors and duplicate payments, a smaller backlog, better service to claims and customers, capacity redeployed to controls, data and analysis.

## 5. Other levers worked first (not additive to the AI capacity above; sizing is an estimate)

1. Spend: vendor, licence, outsourcing and consultant lines in the back-office: zero-base each; amount unsized until the ledger is pulled.
2. Stop low-value work: reports nobody reads, duplicate reconciliations, manual approvals below a threshold. Estimate 4-7% of effort (3 to 6 FTE-equivalents), overlapping with the AI range, so counted once.
3. Fix the process before automating it: supplier master data, e-invoicing, approval rules. Automating a broken process buys faster errors.
4. Hiring freeze and attrition (about 9 a year) and redeployment into claims, controls and finance data roles, with paid reskilling time.
5. Only if the board accepts that a saving beyond this is still needed after the gate: voluntary exits, then involuntary reductions once, decently, via MBL, with LAS order of selection, transition support from the first day, and re-hire priority.

## 6. Swedish and regulatory points (counsel to confirm; rules move)

- MBL: negotiate with the unions before the decision, while the plan can still change.
- LAS: selection by length of service within each unit and agreement area, with three exemptions per employer; we cannot choose who stays. A 27-person cut will therefore tend to fall on the shortest-serving staff, the people with the newest skills.
- Notification to the Public Employment Service: two months ahead for 5 to 25 people, four months for 26 to 100. Re-hire priority for nine months for those with enough service.
- Job security council: TRR for private white-collar staff; check what our collective agreement provides.
- Insurer-specific: reporting deadlines (Solvency II, IFRS 17, Finansinspektionen), internal control and segregation of duties, DORA for ICT third parties, GDPR, and the EU AI Act duties on tools used at work. A control failure in the close costs more than the saving.

## 7. Governance, gates and measures

- Gate 1 (end Q2 2027): pilot results on AP and reconciliations against a baseline taken before the pilot. Measures: touchless rate, error and rework rate, hours freed, cycle time, audit findings. Commitment is set here, from measured hours.
- Gate 2 (end Q4 2027): scale or stop. Quarterly review after that on real results.
- People measures next to the money: attrition and regretted attrition, absence, engagement pulse, time to fill, skills moves.
- Humans sign off on anything touching money or people; audit trail on every AI-posted entry.
- Leaders: if results disappoint, leadership bonuses are reviewed before any job is.
- Pre-mortem: "it is Dec 2027 and this failed." Top reasons to test: pilot quality below target, hidden exceptions, key people leave, auditor objection.

## 8. Steelman: the board's view

The board is right that AI will change this work, that competitors will lower their costs, and that waiting has a price. A gated programme can be wrong if it is run slowly. The answer is a fixed calendar and an agreed trigger: if the Gate 1 result supports more than the base case, we move faster and say so.

## 9. Key assumptions and what would change this

Loaded cost 0.80 per FTE; AI run 3.5 and one-off 5.0; capacity ranges above; 60% gap cover. What would change the recommendation: a pilot showing 30% or more of effort freed at the required quality; a vendor quote far below our run-cost estimate; a hard cash or regulatory deadline that makes a 2027 saving necessary. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?

Reference points: Gallup (replacement cost 0.5-2x salary); Trevor & Nyberg (downsizing and later voluntary turnover); MIT NANDA (2025); Gartner (June 2025); Careerminds survey (2026); OECD, Back to Work: Sweden (2015).
