I'd take the board a gated plan that releases about 20% of finance capacity by the end of year 2 and 27-30% by the end of year 3. I would not take it a promise of 30% next year. On my estimates, cutting 27 roles in year 1 saves nothing in that year: the net is about SEK 25m negative if we cut immediately and about SEK 15m negative under the gated plan, because the AI programme and exit costs land before the savings do.

I've written the full board paper, with the options costed by quarter, in `board_paper.md`. The model is in `model.py`. The inputs are my estimates, not your data, so please replace them (loaded cost per FTE, attrition, task shares) before anyone quotes a number.

**Why 30% next year doesn't hold**

- **Timing.** In Sweden a redundancy round of this size means MBL negotiation with the unions first. Notification to Arbetsförmedlingen follows, at least four months ahead for 26-100 people, and then notice periods of one to six months. First exits land around the end of Q2 and full effect in Q4. Counsel should confirm the exact path.
- **Selection.** LAS sets the order by length of service, with room to exempt three people. The shortest-serving go, not the people we'd choose to keep.
- **Evidence.** No pilot has yet shown the work is covered once the people are gone. My task sizing puts the hours AI could take at 19 to 41 FTE-equivalents, base case about 30, and that is at maturity after 18-24 months. The low case is below the board's number. MIT NANDA reported that about 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact (the method is contested). In an Orgvue survey, 55% of leaders who cut roles for AI said they got it wrong.

**Options, same basis (SEK m, estimates)**

| | Year 1 net | Year 2 net | Year 3 net | 3-year total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0, but rising manual load |
| A: 27 redundancies in year 1 | -24.9 | +0.7 | +10.7 | about -13.5 |
| B: gated (freeze, attrition, 5 voluntary exits, redeployment, AI staged) | -15.4 | -2.2 | +8.8 | about -9 |

Both options carry the AI programme at about SEK 12-17m in year 1. I assumed in A that AI covers 30% of the removed work at first, with contractors buying back the rest, which is why A's year 2 is thin. Revenue at risk is excluded and would hurt A more.

In B, the finance team goes from 90 to about 79 at the end of year 1, 72 at the end of year 2 and 66 at the end of year 3, plus about 6 people redeployed into growing work (IFRS 17 and Solvency II reporting, DORA and controls, reviewing the AI's output). Three gates decide how fast: end Q2 (pilot hours freed against baseline, error rate no worse, internal audit satisfied on controls), end Q4, and mid year 2. If the AI delivers but attrition is too slow, a single involuntary round can be considered at the last gate, through the union process, with its costs priced in.

**What I'd tell the board plainly**

- On headcount alone this pays back slowly (year 4 in B). The AI run cost is the swing factor: 30% lower and B roughly breaks even over three years, 30% higher and it is about SEK 18m negative. The stronger reasons are a faster close and absorbing regulatory workload without hiring in proportion, which I haven't counted.
- Separately, about SEK 2-5m a year (estimate, not counted) is available in every option without touching jobs: payment-run timing and early-payment discounts without squeezing small suppliers, interim and consultant spend, licence overlap, and reports nobody uses.
- No bonus for headcount reduction. Finance leadership variable pay should sit on a balanced scorecard over more than one year, and if the gates are missed, leaders' pay is cut before any role.
- Flags for counsel, the auditors and compliance: MBL, LAS, the Arbetsförmedlingen notification, segregation of duties, DORA and outsourcing rules for external AI, Solvency II data-quality expectations. This is not legal advice.
