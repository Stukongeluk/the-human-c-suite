- **The AI programme itself costs about SEK 12m in 2027.** I estimate SEK 18.4m over two years, plus or minus 40%. That covers integration, licences, controls and model validation, and paid reskilling time. Both routes carry it. It turns cash-positive in Q2 2028, and both routes converge at about SEK 10m a year by 2029.
- **Dismissing in 2027 saves less than it looks like.** Swedish process (union negotiation under MBL, notification to the Public Employment Service at least four months ahead for 26 to 100 people, then notice) puts exits around mid-August. That gives about SEK 7.6m of gross saving in 2027, against 5.1m of transition packages, contractor cover for work the AI can't yet do and replacement of extra leavers.
- **LAS decides who goes, not you.** Selection follows length of service, so the 27 would be your newest people, who are often the best placed to work with the new tools. You have three exemptions.
- **The staged plan.** Freeze back-fills, let attrition and redeployment reduce headcount (to about 72 by end 2028), and book no payroll saving until the gates show the hours are really freed. Gate 1 is at the end of Q2 2027: straight-through rate, error rate against the human baseline, and a clean audit-trail test.
- **Controls matter more for an insurer.** Finansinspektionen supervision, the auditor, segregation of duties and DORA third-party rules for the AI vendor all apply. Cutting control capacity before the controls exist in the system is the failure to avoid.

Outside evidence says to be careful about the date. MIT NANDA's 2025 study found about 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots with no measurable P&L impact (the method is criticised, so read it as a direction). Gartner expects over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by end 2027. In an Orgvue survey, 55% of leaders who cut jobs for AI said they'd made the wrong decisions. The last two are vendor or analyst surveys, so treat them as signals, not rates.
