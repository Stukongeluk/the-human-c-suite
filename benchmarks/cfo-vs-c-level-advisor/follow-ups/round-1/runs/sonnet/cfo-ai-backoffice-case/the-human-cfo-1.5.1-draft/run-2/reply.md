My recommendation: don't take a "30% of 90 roles replaced in 2027" case to the board, because I can't build it on evidence. What I can build, and have drafted as a board paper (business-case.md, with the model in model.py), is a staged programme that gets to about 15 roles (17%) by end of 2028 without involuntary dismissals, with a path towards 30% only if the pilots land in the high case.

**The numbers behind it** (million SEK, estimates from your headcount; replace with payroll and ledger data):

- 30% of 90 is 27 roles, a gross run-rate of about 24 mSEK a year (0.9 mSEK fully loaded per FTE, est.).
- On task sizing across AP, reconciliations and reporting, AI frees about 6 (low), 14 (base) or 24 (high) FTE of net capacity at steady state, after 25% of freed hours go to review and exceptions, and it ramps through 2028, not 2027.
- Cutting 27 in 2027 nets about -18 mSEK in 2027 and about -0.5 in 2028 in the base case (cumulative -30 low, -18 base, -3 high). Savings arrive late: Swedish union negotiation, a four-month notification to Arbetsförmedlingen (27 people is in the 26 to 100 band) and notice periods mean most exits land in Q3, while the work still has to be done, by contractors, rehires and extra leavers.
- The staged path nets about -6 in 2027 and +5 in 2028 in the base case, with an exit run-rate of about 7 mSEK a year. It costs far less if the pilots disappoint, and it keeps the option open if they don't.

**What the staged plan is:** pilots in AP and reconciliations in Q1 and Q2, measured against a baseline; a hiring freeze, with natural attrition (about 7 roles a year, est.) absorbing freed capacity; stopping low-value reports and approvals; retraining and redeploying people into backlog, controls and reporting quality; a board gate at the end of Q2 where measured hours, error rates and review time decide what becomes a payroll commitment; and voluntary exits from 2028 only against proven capacity. Roles out never exceed capacity actually released.

**Why I'd resist the 30%:** a percentage is not a plan. The evidence on AI-driven cuts is poor: in an Orgvue survey, 55% of leaders who made people redundant because of AI said they had decided wrongly, and two-thirds of companies that cut for AI were rehiring. A 1% downsizing has been associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year. In Sweden, selection is by length of service (LAS), so you can't choose who goes, and the newest, often most tool-literate colleagues leave first. The unions must be negotiated with before a decision (MBL).

**Also check with counsel, the unions and your auditor:** Finansinspektionen's outsourcing expectations, DORA for AI vendors, Solvency II governance, GDPR for policy and claims data, and controls over anything AI posts. A human signs off anything that moves money or affects people. Any tool that ranks or monitors individuals is a separate AI Act and co-determination matter; report on processes, not people.

**Humane note for the board:** if results disappoint, leadership bonuses move before frontline jobs, and no bonus should pay for headcount reduction as such.
