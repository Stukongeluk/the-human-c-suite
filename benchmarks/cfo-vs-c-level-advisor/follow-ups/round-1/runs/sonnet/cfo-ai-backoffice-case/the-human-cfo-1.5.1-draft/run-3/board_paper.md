# Board paper: AI in finance and back-office operations

**Draft for the CFO. Every figure marked (est.) is a labelled estimate to be replaced with payroll aggregates, ledger volumes and pilot data. Amounts in SEK million. This is not legal, tax or investment advice; Swedish employment counsel, our auditors and the compliance function should review before this goes further.**

## 1. Recommendation

Commit to a gated programme that releases about 20% of finance capacity by the end of year 2 and 27-30% by the end of year 3 (base case, range 18-40%), mostly through a hiring freeze, natural attrition, voluntary exits and redeployment. Do not commit to removing 30% of the 90 roles next year. On our estimates, removing 27 roles in year 1 does not save money in that year under any option: the net result is a cost of about SEK 15m (gated plan) to SEK 25m (immediate cut), because the AI programme and one-off costs arrive before the savings do.

## 2. What the board is trying to fix

The request is "30% fewer people". The underlying aim is not stated. Before the board votes, we need it in money terms: a SEK expense target, an expense-ratio or combined-ratio effect, or capacity for growth and regulatory load, and the date it is needed. Everything below is built so it can be re-cut once that target is known.

## 4. Options, costed on the same basis

Common assumptions (est.): fully loaded cost SEK 0.85m per FTE per year; in a Swedish redundancy the people who leave are the shortest-serving, so we assume 10% below average cost; AI programme cost in year 1 of SEK 12-18m (licences and usage, integration, data work, controls, monitoring, human review) and SEK 8-12m a year after that.

**Do nothing.** No programme cost, no saving. Manual volume keeps growing with premium volume and reporting load, and the team stays exposed to attrition and rework. This is the baseline against which the others are measured.

**Option A: the board's ask. 27 involuntary redundancies in year 1.**
- Timeline: negotiation with the unions before the decision (MBL), notification to the Public Employment Service at least four months ahead (26-100 people), then notice periods of one to six months by length of service. First exits fall around the end of Q2; full effect from Q4. All this needs counsel to confirm.
- Selection follows length of service (LAS) within each unit and collective agreement area, with room to exempt three people. We cannot choose to keep the most skilled; those who go may be the people who know the reconciliations best.
- No pilot has shown that the work is covered when the people have gone. We assume AI covers 30% of the removed work in Q3 rising to 85% by the end of year 2; the shortfall is bought back through contractors and overtime.

**Option B (recommended): gated programme.**
- Immediately: hiring freeze with exceptions for control roles (we assume 80% of leavers are not backfilled; attrition 9% a year, est.).
- Q1-Q2: baseline the task times, run pilots in accounts payable and reconciliations.
- Gate 1 (end Q2), Gate 2 (end Q4), Gate 3 (mid year 2): scale only what the measured results support.
- Voluntary exits (5 people, packages of about 6 months' pay plus job-security-council support) only after Gate 2.
- Redeploy about 6 people into growing work: IFRS 17 and Solvency II reporting, DORA and internal-control work, data quality, and the reviewer roles the AI itself needs. Redeployment is not booked as a saving.

**Contingent step.** If Gate 3 shows the AI is delivering but attrition alone is too slow for the agreed target, an involuntary round can be considered once, through the union process, with the exit and transition costs from Option A priced in. We do not plan for it today and we do not book it.

## 5. What this says about the AI case

- The AI programme is the swing factor. At 30% lower AI cost and the base attrition, Option B is about break-even over three years; at 30% higher cost it is about SEK 18m negative. At 6% attrition instead of 9% the three-year result is about SEK 8m worse; at 12% it is about SEK 8m better.
- On headcount alone the case pays back slowly. The stronger reasons are faster close, fewer errors and absorbing IFRS 17, Solvency II and DORA workload without proportional hiring. Those benefits are not sized here and are not counted.
- Evidence to keep in view: MIT NANDA (2025) reported about 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots with no measurable P&L impact (method criticised, so a direction rather than a rate); Gartner expects over 40% of agentic-AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027; in an Orgvue survey (2025), 55% of leaders who made people redundant because of AI said they had made the wrong decisions.

## 6. Levers that cost no jobs (not counted, available under every option)

Sized as ranges to be validated; none is counted toward the gap. Together an estimate of SEK 2-5m a year.
1. Cash and working capital in accounts payable: payment-run timing and early-payment discounts where they pay, without stretching terms for small suppliers.
2. Spend: interim and consultant cost in reporting and close, tool and licence overlap.
3. Stop low-value work: reports nobody reads, duplicate reconciliations, manual reports that regulators do not require.
4. Revenue and recoveries: reinsurance and premium receivable follow-up, a reconciliation exception backlog that hides leakage.

## 7. Controls, regulation and law (for counsel and the compliance function)

- Segregation of duties, audit trail and a human sign-off on payments and journals; internal audit and the external auditor involved before Gate 1.
- DORA and outsourcing rules for any external AI provider; Solvency II governance and data-quality requirements; consult Finansinspektionen's expectations before relying on AI in regulated reporting.
- Employee data in the pilots: only aggregated task data, GDPR basis documented, union informed.
- Swedish employment: MBL negotiation before decision, LAS selection order, notification to Arbetsförmedlingen, collective-agreement transition support (job-security council). Priority for re-employment for nine months for those dismissed for lack of work.

## 8. People plan

- Involve the unions at the start, while the plan can still change, and share the real numbers.
- Offer redeployment and paid training first. Everyone hears their own news from their own manager.
- Track engagement, regretted attrition, absence and error rates next to the savings, quarterly.
- Pre-mortem before launch: "It is twelve months from now and this failed. Why?"

## 9. Leaders first and incentives

- No bonus for headcount reduction as such. Finance leadership variable pay uses a balanced scorecard: cost, close quality and control findings, customer and supplier measures, retention of regretted leavers, over more than one year.
- If the programme misses its gates, leaders' variable pay is cut before any role is.

## 10. Decisions asked of the board

1. Approve the gated programme and Year 1 budget of SEK 12.5m (est.), with Gates 1-3 as the only way further commitments are released.
2. Approve the hiring freeze and the MBL information process.
3. Agree the target in SEK and the date, so the options can be re-cut.
4. Agree the indicators for money and people and a quarterly review.

## 11. Key assumptions and what would change this

Loaded cost SEK 0.85m per FTE; 9% attrition; 80% of leavers not backfilled; the task shares in section 3; AI run cost. Change any of these and the numbers move, as shown in section 5. What am I missing, and what would make this wrong?
