# Finance and back-office: what AI can honestly deliver in 2027

Draft board paper from the CFO. All figures in million SEK (mSEK) unless stated. Every number marked (est.) is my estimate from the figures given (90 FTE; accounts payable, reconciliations, reporting) and must be replaced with ledger, payroll and pilot data before the board relies on it. Not legal, tax or investment advice; employment-law points need Swedish counsel and the unions' input.

## 1. Recommendation

Do not commit the board to removing 27 of 90 roles in 2027. On a realistic task sizing, AI frees about 6 to 24 FTE of capacity at steady state (base 14, or 15%), and it reaches that during 2028 rather than 2027. A 27-role cut in 2027 loses money in both years in the low and base cases (cumulative about -30 and -18 mSEK over 2027-28), because the work does not disappear when the roles do.

Commit instead to a staged programme with a hard gate: pilot in AP and reconciliations, freeze hiring and let natural attrition (about 7 roles a year, est.) absorb the freed capacity, redeploy the rest, and turn capacity into a payroll commitment only where a pilot has proven the hours. Base case: about 15 roles (17%) out by the end of 2028 without involuntary dismissals, a 2027 net cost of about 6 mSEK, a 2028 net benefit of about 5 mSEK and an exit run-rate of about 7 mSEK a year. The high case approaches 30% in 2028-29. The board should hear that range, with the gate dates, rather than a single 30% figure.

## 2. What problem is the board solving?

"30%" is a solution. I have not been given the problem behind it: a cost-to-income or expense-ratio target, a Solvency II or capital reason, a close-speed or error problem, or a general efficiency ambition. The levers differ a lot (see section 6). The 30% on 90 people is a gross run-rate of 24.3 mSEK a year (27 x 0.9 mSEK fully loaded, est.), against a whole team costing 81 mSEK. If the real gap is, say, 10 mSEK, we should say so, and the options below close it with fewer people affected.

## 3. How much work can AI take over? (task sizing, est.)

Assumed split of the 90 FTE: AP 25, reconciliations 20, reporting 25, other (controls, treasury, tax, management) 20. The "other" group is kept at 0% because it is judgement and sign-off work.

| Scenario | Share of work done by AI at required quality: AP / Recon / Reporting | Gross FTE-equivalent | After 25% of freed hours go to review and exceptions | Plus work stopped or simplified | Net steady-state capacity | % of 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 15% / 10% / 4% | 6.8 | 5.1 | 1 | 6.1 | 7% |
| Base | 30% / 22% / 10% | 14.4 | 10.8 | 3 | 13.8 | 15% |
| High | 50% / 40% / 20% | 25.5 | 19.1 | 5 | 24.1 | 27% |

Reasoning: standard invoices, matching and bank and ledger reconciliations are where today's tools work best; breaks, disputes, judgement, regulatory reporting and sign-off stay human. These are priors, not findings. The pilot below replaces them with measured numbers on our own invoices and reconciliations. Capacity ramps: 0% in Q1 2027, 15% Q2, 40% Q3, 70% Q4, then 85% to 100% through 2028.

Base rates to keep in mind: an MIT NANDA study reported that about 95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact (the method is disputed, so read it as direction); Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027; and in an Orgvue survey, 55% of leaders who had made people redundant because of AI said they had made the wrong decisions. Two-thirds of companies that cut jobs for AI were rehiring, many within six months.

## 4. The options, costed on the same basis

Cash, mSEK, quarterly, 2027 and 2028 (positive = benefit). Each option carries its own AI costs, controls, review and exceptions. A saving is counted in one option only; in the board option the 27 includes 4 natural leavers and 23 redundancies.

### A. Do nothing
No AI programme, no cut. Net 0 against today's baseline, with the board's efficiency question unanswered, wage inflation continuing and manual control risk unchanged. Included as the reference point.

### B. The board's ask: 27 roles out in 2027
Timing (est.): negotiation with the unions, notification to the Public Employment Service at least four months ahead (27 people falls in the 26-100 band), and notice periods of one to six months mean the first exits land in Q2 and most by the end of Q3. Average roles out per quarter: 0, 3, 20, 27, then 27.

Low case net: -20.8 in 2027 and -9.1 in 2028. High case: -14.3 and +11.0. Exit run-rate (Q4 2028 x 4): about -7 (low), +2 (base), +14 (high) a year. Even in the high case it has not paid back by the end of 2028 (cumulative -3.3). Two points the table understates: there is a control risk in Q3-Q4 2027, when roles are gone before the tools are proven (payment errors, late close, missed regulatory reporting deadlines), and the Swedish selection rule is last-in-first-out, so the newest and often most tool-literate colleagues would go first.

### C. Staged: pilot, gate, freeze, attrition, redeploy, then voluntary exits
Steps: pilots in AP and reconciliations in Q1-Q2 against a baseline; hiring freeze with named exceptions; stop or simplify low-value reports and approvals; a gate at end of Q2 (see section 5); scale in Q3-Q4 only where passed; retrain and move freed capacity to backlog, controls and reporting quality; voluntary packages (up to 3, est.) only from 2028 and only against proven capacity. Roles out are capped at the capacity actually released.

## 5. The gate: when capacity becomes a payroll commitment

At the end of Q2 2027 the board sees measured results, not estimates:
- AP: touchless-processing rate on standard invoices, error and duplicate-payment rate, exception volume, against the pre-pilot baseline.
- Reconciliations: share auto-matched, break ageing, hours per reconciliation.
- Reporting: hours saved on data prep and commentary, with accuracy checked by the people who sign off.
- Review burden: hours spent checking AI output (we assumed 25% of freed hours).
- Controls: segregation of duties, audit trail, a human signing off anything that moves money, and the auditor's view.
Roles become part of the plan only for work whose hours the pilot proved freed, and only through attrition, redeployment and voluntary exits first. Review quarterly; stop or scale on evidence.

## 6. Other levers that cost no jobs (to size once we know the gap)

Sized only as labelled estimates until we see the ledger: vendor, licence and consulting spend in finance (size from the ledger); simplifying the close, approvals and reporting calendar (included above as "work stopped", 1-5 FTE-equivalent); invoice terms and early-payment discount capture; and for the insurer, whether outsourcing or shared-service arrangements already in place are duplicated work. Any hour is counted once: stopping a report and automating it are not both savings.

## 7. People, law and governance (to check with counsel and the unions)

- Co-determination Act (MBL): negotiate with the unions before a decision on major change, while the plan can still change.
- Employment Protection Act (LAS): selection by length of service within the operational unit and collective agreement area; employers may exempt three people; notice one to six months; nine-month priority for re-employment. Managers cannot pick who leaves.
- Notification to the Public Employment Service at least four months ahead for 26 to 100 redundancies, two months for 5 to 25.
- Job security council (TRR for private-sector white-collar staff) support starts at notice. Sweden's rules and support are real but not a substitute for our own package; in an OECD study over 85% of displaced Swedish workers found work within a year, with earnings still down a few percent.
- Insurer-specific checks: Finansinspektionen expectations on outsourcing and operational resilience, DORA (ICT third-party risk for AI vendors), Solvency II governance and the role of key function holders, data protection (GDPR) for claims and policy data in AI tools, and the auditor's view on controls over automated postings.
- EU AI Act: back-office AP and reconciliation tools are mostly not high-risk, but any tool that monitors, ranks or evaluates staff is. Do not use AI to rank individuals; report on processes and teams.
- Controls: no postings, payments or journal entries by AI without explicit human approval.

## 8. Leaders first, and no bonus on headcount

Executive bonus should not pay for headcount reduction as such. If the board wants the programme to be self-funding, link part of leadership variable pay to the gate results, error and close-quality measures and retention of regretted leavers, over more than one year. If results disappoint, leadership bonuses move before frontline jobs.

## 9. Risks and what would change this recommendation

- Pilot results far above the base case move us towards the high case sooner; we would bring the gate forward and offer voluntary exits earlier.
- Attrition lower than 8% slows the no-dismissal path; higher attrition speeds it.
- A hard external deadline (a covenant, a capital or expense-ratio commitment with a date) would change the order of levers; I'd then bring the cash forecast and lender or supervisor timing into the paper.
- Pre-mortem for the board: "It is December 2027 and this failed. Why?" Likely answers: pilots measured on easy invoices only; review time higher than 25%; best people left after the announcement; payment error or late regulatory report; AI vendor outage. Each needs an owner and an early-warning measure.

## 10. Assumptions to replace with data

Fully loaded cost 0.9 mSEK per FTE; split of the 90 FTE by area; automation shares by area; review share 25%; attrition 8% a year; AI build and run costs (7 mSEK build and 4 mSEK a year run in the staged plan; 10 and 5 in the rushed plan, est. from vendor ranges I have not yet seen quotes for); package 0.3 mSEK per leaver; contractor cost 1.3x; rehiring 25% of cut roles at 0.5 mSEK each (replacement cost one-half to two times salary, Gallup).
