**Don't announce the cuts before the meeting. Go to the bank with a plan that closes a gap of roughly 3% without job losses, and a pre-agreed trigger for what happens if it doesn't.** A 10% reduction (about 26 people) would most likely make the Q2 test worse, not better, and announcing it before the works council has been asked for advice puts you in legal trouble.

**The gap is small.** 3.6x against 3.5x means net debt about 2.8% too high, or covenant EBITDA about 2.9% too low. I don't have your figures, so as an illustration I assume EBITDA of €4.0M and net debt of €14.4M. Then you need roughly €0.4M less net debt, or €0.11M more EBITDA, by the test date. The percentages hold at any scale. I also assume the test is on 30 June 2027, so you have about seven months, which is time for levers that cost no jobs.

**Why the cuts don't fix Q2 (estimates, my assumptions)**

- *Timing.* With 260 employees you have a mandatory works council, and a reorganisation needs its advice before the decision (art. 25 WOR). Announcing it as decided first invites an Enterprise Chamber challenge. Twenty-six dismissals also pass the 20-in-three-months threshold, so UWV and the unions must be notified and consulted (WMCO). Consultation, a UWV procedure and notice periods of one to four months mean most exits land around the test date. Savings counted in the trailing twelve months by 30 June are close to zero.
- *Cost comes first.* At about €60k loaded cost per person, the gross saving is about €1.56M a year. Transition payments, a decent package, notice and advice cost an estimated €20–30k per person, about €0.65M. That is cash out before the test.
- *Effect on the ratio.* Net debt rises to about €15.05M. If your facility agreement adds back restructuring costs, leverage is about 3.76x. If it doesn't, EBITDA falls to €3.35M and leverage is about 4.5x. Either way it is worse than 3.6x at the test, and the real saving only shows from the second half of 2027, net of rehiring and lost know-how.
- *Banks.* A bank cares about whether the covenant plan is credible and the owners are standing behind it. A hasty announcement without a cash forecast can read as panic.

**Options, same basis (illustrative, sized as ranges, not additive until validated)**

| Lever | Effect by 30 June | Jobs affected |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory (wood, components, finished goods) and receivables, customer deposits | €0.3–0.8M less net debt | none |
| Defer non-maintenance capex | €0.2–0.4M | none |
| Owner: stop dividend and drawings; shareholder loan or equity if the definition allows | €0.2–0.5M | none |
| Procurement, energy, freight | €0.1–0.2M EBITDA | none |
| Price increases on new orders | €0.05–0.15M EBITDA | none |
| Hiring freeze, not replacing about 10 leavers | €0.1–0.2M | none, by attrition |
| 10% redundancies | about -€0.65M cash, about 0 saving | 26 |
| Do nothing | breach at 3.6x | none now |

The low ends alone come to about €0.95M against a €0.4M gap, but I haven't validated any of them. Don't count a lever until someone owns it and the number is checked. EBITDA levers are worth less than they look, because covenant EBITDA is usually a trailing twelve months, so only the months already saved count.

**For the bank meeting in three weeks**

1. A 13-week cash forecast and a monthly covenant bridge to June, base and downside.
2. The actions behind each scenario, each with an owner and a date.
3. What the family contributes, and first: dividend off, owner pay and management bonuses cut before anyone else's work is touched.
4. A request, before any breach: a covenant holiday or reset, or an equity cure right. Expect a fee (an estimate is 0.1–0.25% of the facility) and possibly tighter reporting. Check whether your facility obliges you to notify an expected breach.
5. The trigger: if by a set date (say end of March) the levers are more than a set amount short, you start works council consultation then, with the bank informed. That shows seriousness without a premature announcement.

**To the owner.** His instinct is fair: banks want to see decisive action, and I'd steelman it. But the most visible proof of seriousness is the family going first, and a cut that arrives after the test date and costs cash before it doesn't earn credit for it. In a family firm, the name in the region and scarce craft skills that take years to rebuild are assets the bank also values.

**What would change my mind:** if demand is structurally lower, not temporarily, or the levers above can't be validated. Then reduction becomes honest, done once, with the works council from the start. Reduced hours for a temporary dip can bridge. Dutch short-time work currently covers only calamities, so check whether the new crisis-scheme bill has passed.

**Two questions that change the numbers:** (1) How does the facility define EBITDA and net debt (add-backs, trailing twelve months, whether a shareholder loan counts), and is the test date really 30 June? (2) Is the breach driven by weak EBITDA or by debt, for example a stock build?
