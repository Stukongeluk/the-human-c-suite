**Don't announce the 10% before the bank meeting. Go to the bank now, ahead of the meeting, with a plan that closes a gap that is probably small, and keep job cuts as a prepared last step rather than a headline.** The numbers say layoffs would not fix the Q2 test and could make it worse.

**Why the announcement is the wrong move**

- **The gap is small.** Going from 3.6x to 3.5x takes about 2.8% more EBITDA or about 2.8% less net debt. I don't have your figures, so here is an illustration: EBITDA €5.0m and net debt €18.0m. You would need about €0.5m less debt, or about €0.14m more EBITDA. Substitute your own numbers.
- **Cuts arrive after the test date.** 10% of 260 is 26 people. The works council has an advisory right on the plan (WOR art. 25), and the advice has to be requested while the decision can still change. 26 dismissals within three months also pass the 20-person threshold for the collective dismissal notification to UWV and the unions (WMCO). Then come the UWV procedure and notice periods. Announcing a finished decision first invites a works council challenge at the Enterprise Chamber. It also hands your best people, who can leave easily, a reason to go first.
- **Cash goes out before savings come in.** Illustration: 26 people at about €65k loaded is about €1.7m a year, or about €0.14m a month at full run rate. One-offs of €12-25k a head (transition payment, legal, outplacement, my estimate) are €0.3-0.65m. If exits fall after Q2, you pay the one-offs first and net debt rises. If your covenant EBITDA doesn't add back restructuring costs, EBITDA drops too. Cuts would then deepen the breach at the test date.
- **Banks look at the plan, not the headline.** A sudden 10% announcement may suggest things are worse than you told them. I would expect them to prefer early notice, a 13-week cash forecast, headroom scenarios and visible owner commitment. Please check this with your relationship manager.

**What to do instead, in this order** (all figures are labelled estimates scaled to the illustration, in debt-equivalent €m, EBITDA levers valued at about 3.5x)

| Lever | Range (€m) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Receivables and inventory/WIP release | 0.3-0.5 | Don't squeeze small suppliers |
| Capex timing | 0.2-0.4 | Not maintenance or safety |
| Owner dividend/drawings paused | 0.2-0.5 | The owner's own signal |
| Purchasing, energy, freight | 0.1-0.3 | Cash by Q2 only if started now |
| Price increases where value supports them | 0.1-0.2 | Lag in order book |
| Hiring freeze and natural attrition | 0.05-0.1 | A few leavers by Q2 |
| Leadership pay cut, largest at the top | 0.05-0.1 | Small, but it's the signal |
| **Total, with overlap not netted** | **1.0-2.1** | **Gap: about 0.5** |

These are unvalidated ranges, so size each from your ledger before showing the bank. Financing sits beside them: a waiver, covenant reset or equity cure from the owner (a €0.5m shareholder contribution would close this gap on its own, subject to the loan terms). The same is true of the amendment fee, which is usually cheaper than cuts. For a temporary demand dip, Dutch short-time work (werktijdverkorting) currently only covers calamities, so don't count on it. A crisis scheme bill was before parliament in 2026, so check its status.

**Showing the owner "we're serious".** Seriousness is credible when the owner goes first: a paused dividend, an equity commitment and a visible leadership pay cut, together with a dated plan and named owners per lever. Steelmanning the owner: if the weakness is structural overcapacity, cuts may still be needed. So prepare that option now. Brief the works council in confidence, set a trigger (for example, if H1 cash levers deliver less than agreed by a set date), and tell the bank the trigger exists. Involuntary exits then remain a last, once-only step, decided with the works council while the plan can still change, and not a signal sent to decorate a meeting.

**Two questions that would change the numbers**

1. What exactly does the covenant measure: LTM EBITDA, net or gross debt, treatment of restructuring costs and one-offs, cure rights, and the test date? And what are the real EBITDA and net debt figures?
2. Is the Q2 weakness a temporary demand dip or a structural cost problem? That decides whether cuts belong in the plan at all.

This is not legal or financing advice. Involve employment counsel and your auditor before anything is announced, and the works council as the plan takes shape.
