Don't announce the cuts. Take the bank a dated plan that closes the gap without jobs, with the owner's own contribution at the front, and ask for a covenant safety margin as insurance. A 10% cut cannot fix a 3.6x against 3.5x at the end of Q2, and announcing it first would put your works council process at risk.

I don't have your ledger, so the figures below are labelled estimates scaled to the one fact you gave me (I assumed revenue of about €45M, EBITDA of about €4.5M and net debt of about €16.2M at 30 June). Please replace them with real numbers.

**The gap is small.** Getting from 3.6x to 3.5x needs about €450k less net debt, or about €130k more EBITDA, roughly 3% of EBITDA. Ten percent of 260 is 26 people, which is a response sized for a much bigger problem.

**Why the cuts don't solve it by Q2 (€ thousand, same basis for each option):**

| Option | Leverage at 30 June | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Do nothing | 3.60x | Breach |
| 10% cut, statutory UWV route | 3.60x | Works council advice, UWV notification (26 is above the 20 that triggers it), permit and notice put exits around July. The roughly €780k one-off cost comes after the test |
| 10% cut, settlement agreements, exits 31 March | about 3.50x, or 4.6x if the loan agreement doesn't allow restructuring costs as an add-back | About €1.2M paid out against about €360k of savings in the test period. Still no headroom, before overtime, lost output and extra attrition |
| Plan without job cuts | 3.3x to 2.8x | See below, including its own costs of €80-160k |

In 2027 itself, either cut costs more than it saves. The saving of about €1.4M a year only shows up from 2028, if it sticks. Research on downsizing is not kind here: a 1% cut was associated with 31% more voluntary turnover the following year.

**The plan without job cuts (estimates, low to high):**
- Stock down 5-10% and receivables 2-4 days faster: €550-1,140k off net debt. Real and sustained, not timed for 30 June; the bank will look at the weeks around the quarter-end.
- Defer non-safety capex: €200-400k.
- Stop or postpone any dividend or drawings planned before the test: up to €300-600k, if one was planned.
- Purchasing, a targeted price rise, not backfilling half of normal attrition, and a 10-15% cut in leadership pay: €200-520k of EBITDA.

Together that gives roughly €1.0-2.1M off net debt and €0.2-0.5M of EBITDA, against a need of €450k. If the forecast is 10% too optimistic, the low case still breaches at 3.7x. So ask the bank for a reset or waiver for the 30 June and 30 September tests as insurance. Expect a fee; I put 30-60k in the plan costs.

**What I'd take to the bank:** the plan with owners and dates, a 13-week cash forecast with a downside case, monthly reporting of net debt, stock and receivables, and what the owner and leadership are putting in. Talking early is worth more than a headcount number. I've drafted all of this, including the 13-week base and downside tables, in `bank-review-plan-draft.md`.

**The owner's instinct deserves a fair hearing.** Banks do want to see seriousness, and a family firm doesn't want to look passive. The strongest signal available is the owner going first: no dividend, drawings or subordinated loan if needed, and a visible pay cut for leaders. A job announcement signals panic and still leaves the Q2 number where it is. If demand is structurally lower, a reduction may become necessary, but then it is decided once, in the right order, with a trigger (for example, end-February forecast EBITDA more than about 8% below base, or the levers delivering below the low case). It would help the September and December tests, not June.

**Before anyone announces anything (check with employment counsel):**
- The works council (mandatory at your size) must be asked for advice before the decision is taken, with time to change the plan. Announcing a decided 10% first invites an Enterprise Chamber challenge and loses the council's goodwill. Tell it about the situation in confidence now.
- The afspiegelingsbeginsel decides who goes, by age band, and the shortest-serving go first in each. Management doesn't choose, and you could lose the newest people with the skills you can't easily rebuild.
- Read the facility agreement for any duty to notify the bank of an expected breach.

**How I'd put it to the owner, privately and first:** "Announcing 26 redundancies before the bank meeting costs about €1M in cash and saves nothing by June. If we show the bank a plan we can deliver, and we go first, we keep the covenant and the people."
