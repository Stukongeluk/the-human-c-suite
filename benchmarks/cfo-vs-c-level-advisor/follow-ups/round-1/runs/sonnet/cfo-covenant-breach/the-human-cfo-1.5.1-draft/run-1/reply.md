**Why the 10% doesn't work for this test**

I don't have your figures, so I've used illustrative ones that fit your 3.6x: EBITDA €5.0m, net debt €18.0m, loaded cost €60k per FTE, calendar Q2 2027. Swap in yours. The gap is EBITDA +€0.14m, or net debt -€0.5m.

- **Timing.** In the Netherlands, 26 dismissals in one UWV region within three months is a collective dismissal. You must notify the unions and UWV and consult them. The works council must get a chance to advise (art. 25 WOR) before the decision is taken. Announcing first invites an Enterprise Chamber challenge and delay. Even if exits start 1 May, which is optimistic, you save about €0.26m gross by 30 June.
- **Cost.** Transition payments (assuming nine years' average service) come to about €0.30m, plus about €0.15m for legal and support. That is cash going out, and it raises net debt.
- **Result at the June test (best case):** 3.51x if your agreement lets you add back restructuring costs (still a breach), and 3.85x if it doesn't. Savings only arrive from Q3: about €1.3m a year net from year two. Break-even is a revenue loss of about 8% of sales. Beyond that you risk lost deliveries, and a firm that cuts 10% tends to lose more people voluntarily afterwards.

Do-nothing is the worst case (3.6x, and 4.0x if EBITDA slips 10%). The cuts are a poor answer to this particular test.
