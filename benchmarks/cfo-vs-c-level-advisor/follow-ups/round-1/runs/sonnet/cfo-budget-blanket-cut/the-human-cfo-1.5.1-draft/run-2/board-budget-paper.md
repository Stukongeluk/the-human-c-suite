# Budget 2027: reach an 8% lower cost base by the end of 2027 without a blanket cut, and decide in Q2 whether anything more is needed

Draft for the board meeting. Every figure in euros is an illustrative estimate built from the three facts we have (600 people, payroll 68% of opex, thin positive margin). The assumptions in the table below are placeholders for ledger and payroll figures; each is labelled as an estimate and must be replaced before the paper is final.

## 1. What the board is asking, and what we need to settle first

The ask: next year's opex 8% lower. The CEO's proposal: every cost centre cuts 8%.

Three things decide whether that number is reachable and what it costs, and both need an answer from the board:

1. **Is the 8% a 2027 in-year saving, or a run-rate by the end of 2027?** The two differ by more than 2x (section 3).
2. **8% off which baseline?** If it is measured against 2026 actuals and 2027 already carries pay indexation (about 3.5% of payroll, roughly 1.8m, estimate), the real gap is about 7.8m, not 6.0m.
3. **What is it for?** A margin target, a covenant, an owner's return? Revenue and pricing moves help margin but are not "opex"; if margin is the goal they should count.

## 3. Options, costed on the same basis (m euro, estimates)

### Option A: every cost centre cuts 8% (the CEO's proposal)

Payroll share: 8% of 51.0m = 4.08m, or about 48 people. Non-payroll: 8% of 24.0m = 1.92m, of which about 80% is realisable in 2027 because contracts and fixed lines cannot all move (estimate).

Timing for the people cut in the Netherlands: the works council must be asked for advice before the decision is final (works council mandatory from 50 employees; advisory right on reorganisations, article 25 WOR). A dismissal of 20 or more people in one UWV region within three months also requires notifying UWV and the unions and consulting them (WMCO); settlement agreements the employer initiates count towards the 20. Employer notice runs one to four months by service. We assume exits land at the end of Q2. Counsel to confirm.

Run-rate in 2028: gross about 5.6m, net about 3.4m after cover, lost margin and replacement. Q2 is negative: the cost lands before the saving.

Sensitivity: a delivery role billed above about 77% costs more margin than it saves (130k revenue x 85% margin = 110k, against 85k cost). If the cut falls on busy teams, Option A destroys margin. Studies of downsizing link it to lower later profitability and to more voluntary turnover (see sources).

### Option C: do nothing

No saving; the target is missed. Pay indexation of about 1.8m would take operating profit from about 3.0m to about 1.2m, so margin falls to about 1.6%.

Neither option delivers 6.0m inside 2027. Option A does not either, because notice, consultation and payments push its saving into the second half, and its Q2 is negative. The honest choice for the board is between reaching about 7.2-7.7% by the end of 2027 without redundancies, and a larger involuntary programme that costs more upfront and puts revenue at risk.

## 4. What we protect, and why

- Billable delivery capacity, account management and customer success: this is where revenue is made; each role is worth about 130k of revenue against about 85k of cost.
- Training and the learning budget, security and quality work: cut only with the future cost written next to the saving. Rehiring and replacement cost one-half to two times annual salary (Gallup).
- Anyone's bonus is not cut to fund anything else, and nothing here funds a dividend.

## 5. What stops or waits

A list to be agreed with the management team in the next three weeks: internal projects without a customer or revenue case, duplicate reporting, low-value subscriptions and tools, discretionary events and travel, non-critical premises costs. Every item carries an owner, a date and a measure. This list is what makes the non-refilled roles safe.

## 6. Headcount

Flows, not lists: freeze with exceptions for billable and critical roles, an expected 72 leavers, about 42 roles refilled and 30 not, internal moves first. We report refilled and unfilled roles every month. No individual names or pay in this model; aggregated data only.

## 7. Leaders first

The management team takes the first and largest share: the bonus pool is cut before anyone else's pay is touched, and the executive team's voluntary pay reduction is announced before anything else. If results disappoint, leadership variable pay is reduced first, in advance, in writing.

## 8. Works council and law (counsel to confirm)

At 600 employees there is a mandatory works council. It must be involved before the decision is final, with the real numbers. Changes to pay and bonus rules can need its consent (article 27 WOR), and a reorganisation needs its advice. The board approves the approach and the envelope; individual measures follow the proper procedures. This is not legal advice.

## 9. Risks and sensitivities (the three numbers that matter)

1. Share of removed delivery capacity that was being sold: at 25% Option A loses about 0.9m revenue a year; every 10 points more adds about 0.3m of lost margin. Option B avoids this by design.
2. Non-payroll realisation: at 5% instead of 10% (1.2m instead of 2.4m run-rate) Option B's 2027 effect falls by about 0.9m; at 15% it rises by about 0.9m.
3. Attrition: if voluntary attrition is 8% not 12%, only about 20 roles can be left unfilled and lever 2 falls by about a third.

## 10. What would change this budget

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| End of Q1: net effect below 0.2m, or lever 1 below 60% of plan | Open the voluntary exit scheme from Q2 |
| Regretted attrition above 10% annualised | Pause lever 2, review workload |
| Utilisation of delivery staff above 85% | Release refills in those teams |
| Revenue more than 3% below plan | Bring forward the Q2 review and discuss financing with the bank |
| Q2: run-rate below 4.5m | Board discusses the next steps with the works council, before any involuntary measure |

## 11. Indicators, reviewed monthly

Money: net savings against plan by lever, margin, cash. People: regretted attrition, time to fill, utilisation, engagement. Customers: retention and complaints.

## 12. Three-week plan to the board meeting

Week 1: confirm baseline, definition of opex and the purpose of the 8% with the CEO and the chair; pull the ledger by vendor and payroll in aggregate; confirm whether a works council exists and brief its chair. Week 2: zero-base sessions with each cost centre owner; size each lever with real data; counsel checks the timeline. Week 3: pre-mortem with the management team ("it is December 2027 and this plan failed; why?"), final paper.

## 13. The other side, argued fairly

The CEO's case: one rule is simple to explain, looks fair, is fast to launch and avoids a political fight between cost centres. The board's case: a clear number and discipline. Both are right about discipline. This plan keeps what is good: every cost centre gets a target and an owner, set from its spend instead of an equal percentage, and there is a fixed review date.
