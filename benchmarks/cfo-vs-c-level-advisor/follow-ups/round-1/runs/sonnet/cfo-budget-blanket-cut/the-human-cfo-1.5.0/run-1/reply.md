**Every figure is an estimate.** I had only three facts, so I scaled from them: 600 people at about €85k loaded cost, which puts payroll at €51m and opex at about €75m. The 8% target is then about €6.0m. Please swap in ledger and payroll aggregates before anything goes to the board.

**Why the blanket cut doesn't work for you**
- Payroll is 68% of opex, so about €4.1m of a flat cut is people, roughly 48 FTE. The other €1.9m is cut without asking which spend is worth keeping.
- In a services company payroll is the product. Cutting delivery capacity by 8% cuts revenue as well as cost. With my assumptions, year 1 saves nothing if about 65% of lost billable capacity turns into lost revenue.
- It lands late. A reorganisation needs works council advice before the decision is final (art. 25 WOR). Twenty or more dismissals in a UWV area within three months bring in WMCO, and settlement agreements count towards the 20. Add notice periods and transition payments. The board can approve an intention in three weeks, but not savings in Q1. My model has the payroll saving at €1.9m in year 1, with €1.0m of one-off costs coming first.
- Cost programmes rarely stick. Only about 10% showed sustained results after three years (McKinsey).

The CEO's case has real merit: it is simple, looks fair and signals seriousness. The plan keeps the simplicity by using one number, with every centre owning a plan against the same tests. It drops the flat split.

**The recommended plan (year 1 / run-rate, €k, estimates)**
1. Zero-base non-payroll lines (vendors, licences, subcontractors, travel, office): 12.5% of the €24m, which is 1,875 net of fees in year 1 and 3,000 run-rate.
2. Do not backfill 35 of the roughly 72 people expected to leave (12% attrition, estimated), non-billable roles first: 1,488 in year 1 and 2,975 run-rate, less revenue at risk.
3. A named stop list of low-value work, about 1% of opex: 412 and 750. Without names, this lever is zero.
4. Leaders first: all variable pay for about 15 leaders suspended, plus 10% off fixed pay from Q2 by consent: 858 in year 1. This is temporary, so it is not counted in the run-rate.

Year 1 nets about €4.3m, which is 71% of the target. The Q4 exit run-rate is about €6.3m, so the 8% is reached as a run-rate. That leaves an in-year gap of about €1.7m. I would ask the board to accept the target as a Q4 run-rate. Pricing is the honest further lever: each 1% net rate increase is worth about €0.8m, and it counts towards margin, not opex. Involuntary exits would not close the gap in-year either, since the blanket option nets only €1.0m. I've kept them out of the base plan, with triggers in the paper for what happens if Q1 savings run below 60% of plan or covenant headroom shrinks.

**Two things to settle before the meeting**
- **What is the 8% for?** On my numbers it is twice today's operating profit and would lift margin from about 4% to about 11%. If it is a covenant, a cash target or a valuation, the plan should be built for that.
- **Is the baseline this year's actual or the draft 2027 budget?** If it is this year's actual, wage indexation (my estimate is €2.4m) widens the gap to about €8.4m, and even this plan leaves about €2.1m open.

**Human impact:** no one is made redundant in the base plan. The risk is workload on the people who stay, which is why backfill exceptions follow signed demand and why regretted attrition and utilisation are tracked quarterly. The works council should see the plan early, while it can still change it, and counsel should confirm the procedural steps.
