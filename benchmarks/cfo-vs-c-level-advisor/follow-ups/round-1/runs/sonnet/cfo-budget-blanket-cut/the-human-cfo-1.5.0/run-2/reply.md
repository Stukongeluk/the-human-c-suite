My recommendation is not to run 8% per cost centre. Commit the board to 8% lower opex as an exit run-rate by Q4, reached through a zero-based review of non-payroll spend, a hiring freeze with exceptions (natural attrition), and leaders' pay first. Set a gate at the end of Q2 for anything harder. On illustrative numbers, this delivers about 5% in the budget year and about 8% by the exit run-rate, with no redundancies in the base plan. The blanket cut delivers about 2%.

The full board paper is in `board-budget-paper.md`, with options by quarter, the gate and a three-week plan. I only had four facts, so the baseline (opex about EUR 66m, payroll EUR 44.9m, 8% = EUR 5.3m, loaded cost about EUR 75k per FTE, revenue about EUR 69m) is an estimate. Replace it with ledger and payroll data before it goes out.

**Why the blanket cut fails here**

With payroll at 68% of opex, 8% in every cost centre means about 48 of your 600 jobs go (about EUR 3.6m a year). Otherwise non-payroll spend would have to fall about 25%, and it won't. In the Netherlands the timing is the problem:

- The works council has an advisory right (art. 25 WOR).
- A collective dismissal of 20 or more people in one UWV region needs WMCO notification before the decision is final.
- Notice periods and the transition payment apply.
- Selection follows the afspiegelingsbeginsel, not performance.

Counsel should confirm all of this. First exits land around the end of Q2. The saving arrives in half the year while the one-off costs arrive in full.

A is negative in the first half-year. B is positive from Q1.

**What B is**
- Zero-based non-payroll (about EUR 21m): about 10% (range 8-12%). That is EUR 1.7m in-year and EUR 2.1m at run-rate.
- Hiring freeze: about 47 of roughly 78 expected leavers not replaced (13% attrition is my estimate). That is EUR 1.8m in-year and EUR 3.5m at run-rate. Exceptions cover roles with signed demand and regulated roles.
- Leaders first: 10% pay reduction for about 12 leaders and the profit-linked bonus suspended, EUR 0.5m. Leaders go first, and the bonus scheme should move to profit, customers, people and quality with part deferred.
- One-offs and cover costs are included: EUR 0.3m one-off, 15% overtime and contractor cover, and a little extra attrition.

**The honest gap:** B leaves about EUR 2.0m (3 points) short in the budget year. I would put that in front of the board rather than hide it. At the end of Q2, if run-rate savings are more than EUR 1m behind, the next step is a voluntary exit scheme with works council advice. Involuntary reductions come last, and only once. Separately, working capital (5 days of DSO is about EUR 0.9m cash), rate indexation (about EUR 0.8m) and utilisation (about EUR 0.5m) help margin and cash but do not count towards the opex number.

**The trap, in one sentence:** "8% everywhere" is a solution, not a diagnosis. It usually lands on payroll and delivery, which is where the revenue is.

**Two questions that would change the numbers**
1. Is the board's real aim a margin, a cash or covenant figure, or opex as such? Does the 8% include restructuring costs? If the aim is margin, revenue levers count and cutting billable capacity can backfire.
2. What are actual attrition, utilisation and the non-payroll split (subcontractors, licences, travel, office)? These size the freeze and the zero-based review.

**For the CEO's version:** it is simple and forces every manager to act. B keeps that discipline through named owners, a freeze, a gate date and a number each owner signs. It does not make delivery and compliance shrink as fast as the places with real waste.
