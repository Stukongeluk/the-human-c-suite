# Part 1: Implementation plan

## 1. What is decided and what is still open

- **Proposal:** reduce the company from 180 to 158 people (22 roles, 12.2%), mostly in operations and customer support.
- **Status:** until the works council has given its advice, this is a *proposed decision* (voorgenomen besluit). Use those words everywhere, including in the staff announcement. A reorganisation presented as final before the works council has advised can be challenged at the Enterprise Chamber, which can order it undone (articles 25 and 26 WOR).
- **Still open**, to be settled with the works council and the unions: the exact number per function group, the social plan and the timetable. The plan has to leave real room for them to improve it.
- **One round.** The 22 must cover the whole gap. Tell staff "this is the only reduction we plan" only if the numbers support it.

## 3. Legal track (Netherlands, confirm with counsel)

| Step | What it involves | When |
|---|---|---|
| Works council advice (art. 25 WOR) | A written request giving the reasons, the expected consequences for staff and the measures proposed for them (art. 25(3)). Include the alternatives put to the board and why they were not enough: the works council will ask, and the Enterprise Chamber looks at it if it comes to that. Offer to pay for an adviser the works council chooses (art. 16 and 22 WOR). Agree the timetable at the first meeting | Day 1, before the all-hands |
| If there is no works council | With 180 employees the company is required to have one (from 50). Raise this with counsel now; the steps below still apply | Now |
| Collective dismissal notification (WMCO) | 22 dismissals within three months in one UWV work area (Noord-Holland) is above the threshold of 20. Notify UWV via the employer portal and every union with members at the company, and invite the unions to consult. Settlements count toward the 20. The notification states the reasons; numbers by function, age and gender; usual headcount; end dates; selection method; severance arrangements; how contracts will end; and when the works council is asked for advice | Day 1 |
| WMCO waiting period | No contract may end, by notice, court or signed settlement, until one month after UWV confirms the notification is complete, unless the unions declare in writing that they were consulted and agree | No signatures before [date] |
| Flexible workers in the same roles | In the same interchangeable functions, agency workers, freelancers, on-call workers and staff past state pension age generally go before permanent employees (Dismissal Regulation, art. 10; counsel to confirm the list). They lose income too: same notice, same honesty | Weeks 1 to 4 |
| Selection | Per category of interchangeable functions, the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel): employees are split into age groups (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55+), the reduction is spread proportionally, and within each age group the shortest service goes first. Done and documented by HR and counsel, never by manager preference, performance ratings, leave status or who has raised concerns | Draft before Day 1, final after the advice |
| Dismissal protections | Check each selected person against the protections for illness, pregnancy and maternity leave, works council membership and any others counsel lists | Before individual conversations |
| Redeployment | Check every vacancy for a suitable role, with reasonable training. Freeze external hiring for roles that affected people could do. UWV tests this | From Day 1 until the end of notice |
| How contracts end | Offer a settlement agreement with the social plan. Keep the employer's initiative and the full notice period so unemployment benefit (WW) is not put at risk. Employees have a 14-day cooling-off period (art. 7:670b BW). Those who don't sign go through the UWV permit procedure | After the final decision |
| Notice | Statutory employer notice is 1 to 4 months depending on length of service (art. 7:672 BW), or longer where contracts say so | |
| Regulated work | If the company holds a DNB or AFM licence: compliance confirms that customer due diligence, transaction monitoring and complaint deadlines still hold with the new staffing (payment service providers must answer complaints within 15 business days, art. 101 PSD2), and whether any change must be reported | Before Day 1 |
| Residence permits | Highly skilled migrants have three months after their employment ends to find a new sponsoring employer, and the company reports the end of employment to the IND. Consider longer paid notice or garden leave for them, and check the effect on the 30% ruling | Before individual conversations |

## 4. Social plan proposal (to negotiate with the unions and the works council)

| Element | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Severance | Statutory transition payment plus [top-up, e.g. 50%], with a minimum of [two months' pay] for people with short service. The statutory payment is one third of monthly pay (including 8% holiday allowance and fixed pay components) per year of service, capped at €102,000 in 2026 or one year's pay if that is higher |
| Notice | Full notice paid. Option to stop working straight away on full pay |
| Transition budget | €[4,000] per person for coaching, training or outplacement, available from the day the person hears and for [12] months, also after they leave |
| Time | Paid time for interviews and training during notice |
| Legal advice | The company pays an independent lawyer of the employee's choice to review the settlement, up to €[1,000] excluding VAT |
| Practical | Keep laptop and phone after company data is wiped. A written reference and an offer of reference calls. CV and LinkedIn review |
| Rehiring | Priority for vacancies for [12] months; an alumni group |
| Support | Confidential psychological and financial counselling for [6] months, for affected people and for those who stay |
| Extra care | Residence permit holders (section 3), people close to state pension age, people on sick leave or parental leave (with counsel and the occupational doctor) |

**Cost model** (fill in from payroll): per person = transition payment + top-up + transition budget + legal fee + notice pay. Worked example: €4,000 gross a month and 3 years' service gives €4,320 a month including holiday allowance, so a statutory transition payment of 3 × ⅓ × €4,320 = €4,320. Show the board both the one-off cost and the annual saving, because the first-year saving is lower than the run-rate.

## 7. The individual conversations

- Own manager plus HR, in person or on a live video call, about 20 minutes. Nobody hears it from a colleague, an email or a login that stops working.
- Say it in the first minute: "I'm sorry to tell you that your role is one of the 22. This is not about your performance." Then the reason in two sentences, how selection worked and what happens next.
- Don't debate the selection or negotiate in the room. Offer a follow-up with HR within two working days to go through the package.
- A written summary the same day: the package, the draft settlement, deadlines, the cooling-off period, contacts and the counselling number.
- Access stays on. Any change happens after the conversation and is agreed with the person, who chooses whether to go home on full pay or stay to finish and say goodbye. Offer a farewell.
- Managers rehearse beforehand and have a debrief with HR the same day. This is hard on them too.

## 8. The people who stay

- The day of the conversations: a message to everyone that the conversations are finished, [only if true: that nobody else is affected in this round,] and what changes for the teams.
- Within a week: each team re-plans its work with its manager using section 5. Stop work rather than spread it.
- 1:1s within two weeks; career conversations for people whose roles change.
- Managers ask the people the company most needs what would keep them, rather than waiting for resignations.

## 9. Indicators, reviewed monthly for 12 months

| Area | Indicator | Act when |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Run-rate saving against plan; one-off costs against budget | Either is more than [10]% off |
| People | Regretted exits, sickness absence, pulse ("leadership is honest with me", workload) | Regretted exits above [n] a quarter; absence or workload worsening two months running |
| Customers | First response and resolution times, complaints, churn | A target missed two weeks running |
| Compliance | KYC and monitoring backlogs, complaint deadlines met | Any regulated deadline missed |
| People who left | Share in new work after 3 and 6 months (aggregated, from the outplacement provider) | Below [n]%: extend the transition budget |

Share the indicators with the works council each quarter. After six months, write an honest review: what was saved, what it cost including hidden costs, what we would do differently, and what in our planning made this necessary.

## 10. Assumptions

- All 22 roles are in Amsterdam and end within three months, so WMCO applies.
- No collective labour agreement (CAO) with its own redundancy rules applies. If one does, it may change the selection and the package.
- The works council has not yet advised on this proposal.
- The company can fund a package above the statutory minimum.

This plan changes if any of these is wrong.
