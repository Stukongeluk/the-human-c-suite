# Plan: reducing 22 roles at [Company]

**Status:** proposed decision, subject to the works council's advice · **Owner:** [CEO] · **Prepared:** [date] · **For:** leadership team, HR, employment counsel, people managers

Legal points were checked against the sources at the end on 20 November 2026. This is not legal advice: employment counsel checks every legal step before Day 0.

## Summary

- The board intends to remove 22 of our 180 roles (12.2%), mostly in operations and support, to lower annual costs by €[x] from [date]. 158 roles remain.
- In the Netherlands this stays a proposed decision until the works council (OR) has advised on it. On Day 0 we ask for that advice, notify UWV and the unions (22 is above the collective dismissal threshold of 20), and tell staff, all on the same day.
- We do this once, stop work along with the roles, and offer a package above the legal minimum, with support starting on the day people hear.
- Example calendar: Day 0 on Tuesday 1 December 2026, final decision on 19 January 2027, individual conversations on 2 February, earliest last working days on 31 March 2027.

## 1. What is fixed and what is open

| Fixed (the board's intention) | Open to the works council's advice |
|---|---|
| The saving needed: €[x] a year by [date] | Whether 22 is the right number, and the split across teams |
| The intention to reduce roles, mostly in operations and support | The selection categories, the timing and the package |
| | The voluntary window, and which work stops |

**Action before Day 0:** ask the board to minute its decision as a proposed decision (voorgenomen besluit), subject to the works council's advice. Agree in advance which changes the leadership team may accept without going back to the board.

Why it matters: the works council must be asked for advice at a moment when its advice can still substantially influence the decision (article 25(2) WOR). If the decision is presented as final, the council can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber (Ondernemingskamer). UWV also treats dismissal applications as premature until the works council procedure is complete.

## 4. Selection

1. **Define the categories of interchangeable positions** and document why. This is the step that gets challenged most often.
2. **Within each category, end external assignments first:** agency workers, seconded staff and freelancers. Their income ends too, so give them proper notice ([at least four weeks, or the contract term if longer]), an honest conversation and a reference.
3. **Then apply the dismissal order:** employees past state pension age, then zero-hours contracts, then fixed-term contracts ending within 26 weeks, then permanent contracts. Within that, apply the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel): five age bands (15–25, 25–35, 35–45, 45–55, 55+), and within each band, the person who joined last goes first.
4. **Check for another suitable role for every affected person,** including a role they could do after training within a reasonable time, and document it. Open vacancies go to affected colleagues first from Day 0.
5. **Check dismissal protections with counsel:** illness, pregnancy and maternity leave, works council members and candidates, and others.
6. **Never select on** performance ratings, leave, protected characteristics, or on who raised concerns.
7. **Voluntary window** (19–29 January in the example calendar). It is open to employees in the affected categories, with the same package. Where a volunteer can take the place of a colleague who would otherwise be selected in the same category, counsel confirms how to document it. We may decline a volunteer whose role is critical, and we say so up front. If the works council agrees, the window can open earlier.

Selection data (age, start dates, contract types) stays with HR and counsel. Managers learn the outcome for their own team when they need it to prepare the conversations.

## 5. Package: our proposal to the works council and unions

| Element | Legal minimum | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Severance | Transition payment: one third of a month's salary (including holiday allowance and fixed components) per year of service. Maximum €102,000 in 2026, or one year's salary if that is higher. The 2027 maximum is indexed on 1 January, so check it | The statutory amount plus [50]%, with a minimum of [two] months' salary |
| Notice | Contract or law: one to four months, by length of service | Paid in full; release from work on request; paid time for job search and interviews |
| Transition support | None | €[x] per person for coaching, outplacement or training, from the day of the conversation, usable until [six] months after the last day |
| Legal advice | None | A contribution of €[x] (excluding VAT) to the employee's own lawyer to review the agreement |
| Equipment | None | Keep the laptop [and phone] after company data is wiped |
| Equity | Per the option plan | Treat everyone as a good leaver, with [x] months to exercise vested options |
| References | None | A written reference and a reference call on request |
| Rehiring | None | Priority for similar roles for [12] months; alumni network |
| Confidential support | None | Employee assistance programme for [six] months, plus one session with a financial adviser (for example on mortgages and unemployment benefit) |
| Colleagues on a highly skilled migrant permit | None | The IND gives a search period of up to three months, or six months for people who lost their job on or after 22 May 2026 and have held the permit for two years or more. It never runs past the permit's end date. We fund a session with an immigration lawyer, help them find a new sponsor, and take the permit into account when setting the last working day |

**Illustration** (examples, not real staff; gross amounts with 8% holiday allowance included):

| Monthly salary | Years of service | Statutory transition payment | Proposal (statutory + 50%, minimum two months) |
|---|---|---|---|
| €4,000 | 1 | €1,440 | €8,640 |
| €4,000 | 2.5 | €3,600 | €8,640 |
| €5,000 | 8 | €14,400 | €21,600 |

Why the minimum: with short average tenure, the statutory amount is often less than one month's pay.

**Cost:** HR calculates the total from payroll as the sum, per person, of the higher of (statutory × 1.5) and (two months' salary), plus 22 × (transition support + legal contribution), plus salary for notice periods with release from work. We compare it with the annual saving and state the payback in months in the advice request.

**Settlement agreements** must keep people's right to unemployment benefit (WW): the initiative is the employer's, there is no urgent cause, and the notice period is respected. Employees have a statutory 14-day cooling-off period after signing (article 7:670b of the Dutch Civil Code, BW).

## 7. Day 0 and the conversation day

**Day 0**
- 08:30 The works council receives the advice request in a meeting, with the full numbers.
- 09:30 Managers phone colleagues in the affected teams who are on leave or off sick, so they don't hear it from the all-hands.
- 10:00 Team sessions for operations and support, each led by the team's own manager with a leadership team member and HR.
- 11:30 All-hands, live, led by the CEO, with open Q&A.
- 12:15 The written announcement and Q&A go out, with an intranet page listing the contacts. Publish in Dutch as well if not everyone works in English.
- Same day: the WMCO notification goes to UWV and letters go to the unions. HR runs drop-in sessions in the afternoon, and the employee assistance line is open.
- Days 1–3: everyone in an affected team is offered a 1:1 with their manager, for questions and support, not selection.

**Conversation day (Tuesday 2 February)**
- Each affected person hears it from their own manager, with HR present, live, in the morning. The message is clear in the first minute; the conversation takes 20 to 30 minutes.
- The same day, they get a written summary: the package, the dates, the draft agreement, the time to consider it, contacts, and an introduction to the outplacement provider.
- No access changes before or during the conversation. Handover and access changes are agreed with the person. Where privileged production or payment access has to end early for security, say so in the conversation and change it afterwards.
- They decide how and when their colleagues hear, and may take paid time off for the rest of the week.
- Colleagues in the affected categories whose roles continue hear it from their manager the same morning.
- 15:00 Team meetings: who covers what, what stops, and that this is the only round [only if that is true].
- At the end of the day, managers debrief with HR. Delivering this is hard on them too.

## 8. Customers, regulators and systems

- **Compliance check before Day 0:** (a) regulated processes such as KYC/AML, transaction monitoring, complaints handling and incident reporting stay staffed at the level our licence requires; (b) whether any change to a key-function holder or an outsourcing arrangement must be reported to DNB or AFM.
- **Customers:** service changes are announced ahead of time; support coverage is planned for the notice period and after.
- **Automation:** if tooling is meant to absorb any of the work, it is live and measured before the last working days, not after.
- **Knowledge:** a handover plan for each leaving role (runbooks, shadowing), done in paid time from week 10.

## 9. The 158 who stay

- Team meetings on the conversation day; the stop list is final and published within one week.
- Priorities re-planned with each team within two weeks; overtime watched, with no expectation of extra unpaid hours.
- 1:1s within two weeks for everyone in operations and support, plus skip-level conversations.
- Leaders stay visible and answer questions in the weekly update until the last person has left.

## 11. Risks to watch

| Risk | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| The procedure is challenged | The works council asks for more information or time | Give it; move dates, not the process |
| Strong people in operations and support leave during the wait | Resignations or sick leave rise | Keep the wait short, give honest dates, hold 1:1s |
| Service or compliance backlogs | SLA breaches, growing queues | Use the stop list, add temporary help, slow down exits |
| News leaks before Day 0 | Rumours | Keep the circle small; be ready to bring Day 0 forward |
| The saving falls short and a second round looms | Forecast vs actual | Model the saving net of one-off costs and rehiring now; no further reduction without the full process |

## 12. Assumptions

- All 22 roles are in the Netherlands, in the Noord-Holland UWV work area.
- We have an elected works council (mandatory from 50 employees). If we don't, counsel advises at once.
- No collective agreement or existing social plan sets other terms.
- Nobody has been pre-selected.
- This is the only round: [confirm before using that sentence anywhere].

What would change this plan: the works council's advice; a collective agreement; affected staff outside the Netherlands; a change in the size of the financial gap.
