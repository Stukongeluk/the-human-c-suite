Announce it on **Tuesday 8 December as an intended decision, not a final one.** Under Dutch law, 22 roles in Amsterdam within three months is a collective dismissal, so the works council has to advise and UWV and the unions have to be notified first. That puts individual conversations in early February and the first last working days around the end of March 2027.

I haven't gone back over the alternatives. There are two files:

- **plan.md**: for you, HR and counsel. It covers prep through 7 December, the legal track, the timeline, the work that stops, the package, a Day A run of show, the individual conversations, the people who stay, indicators and risks.
- **staff-announcement.md**: your all-staff email, plus an FAQ to publish with it. Send it at 13:00 on Day A, after the works council and the affected teams have heard.

What's built in:

- **Order.** The works council hears first, then the affected teams from their own managers, then everyone, all on the same day. Nobody hears individually before the works council has advised, nothing changes over the holidays, and nobody's access changes before their conversation.
- **Leadership owns it.** The announcement says plainly that this result is leadership's, not that of operations and support. It also has a bracketed line about a leadership pay cut and no leadership bonuses. If that isn't agreed yet, I'd agree it before 8 December. If it can't be agreed, delete the line rather than imply it.
- **The work goes with the roles.** Every position removed is paired with work that stops. Compliance also confirms that operations still meets your licence and Wwft obligations before the advice request goes out.
- **Selection by Dutch rules.** The reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel) applies within each group of interchangeable roles. Contractors in those roles go first. Performance ratings and leave are never criteria.
- **A package above the legal minimum**, to agree in the social plan: 1.5 times the statutory transition payment with a minimum of 3 months' salary (the minimum matters most for support staff with short service), a transition budget from the first day, paid notice, a contribution to legal fees, and help for visa holders.

One trap to avoid is treating the works council round as a formality. Under article 25 WOR, its advice must still be able to change the plan. So keep the number per team, the work redesign, the package and the dates genuinely open, and tell staff what changed because of the advice.

Before anything goes out, fill in:

- the reason, with real numbers
- positions per team
- what has already been saved outside jobs
- the package amounts
- names and contacts

Keep "we're not planning another round" only if the board has committed to it. If 8 December is too soon to prepare properly, use 12 January rather than a date in between.

Two questions that could change the plan:

1. Has the leadership team agreed its own cut?
2. Does the works council know anything yet? If it has already been briefed informally, or if no works council has been elected, the dates move.

This isn't legal advice. Have Dutch employment counsel check the dates, the count and the selection before Day A.
