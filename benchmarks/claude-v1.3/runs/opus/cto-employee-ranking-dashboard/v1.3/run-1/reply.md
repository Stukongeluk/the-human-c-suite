You can do a lot without being the difficult one: don't argue against the VP, help them get what they want a better way. And one fact makes this a shared problem, not your personal objection: at a 300-person Dutch company, a system suited to tracking individual performance needs the works council's consent (article 27(1)(l) WOR). So this ranking probably can't go live as planned anyway.

**Why it fails, in two lines**
- Acceptance rate measures the tool, not the engineer: GitHub describes it as showing [whether suggestions are "relevant and trusted"](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/copilot-usage-metrics/copilot-metrics). Accept-then-delete games it in a day.
- PR counts reward splitting work and punish the people who review, mentor and handle incidents. And a bottom 10% always exists, even when everyone is good.

**What to do, in this order**
1. **Ask about the problem first, privately.** Assume pressure, not malice: a new VP may need to show Copilot pays off, or may think delivery is slow. "What do you want this to tell you?" turns "no" into "how".
2. **Bring an alternative and offer to build it.** Team-level measures: change lead time, review wait time, change fail rate, plus a short developer survey. For Copilot, compare teams before and after, against a group without it. Concerns about one person go through their manager, with evidence from the work. Offering to pilot this makes you the helpful one.
3. **Mention the works council as a fact, not a threat.** "Has this been to the OR yet? It'll need their consent, and HR will want improvement plans based on the work, not a rank." That saves the VP a stalled rollout. (Check details with HR; this isn't legal advice.)
4. **Find allies quietly:** your manager, other seniors and EMs, HR, the privacy officer.
5. **Follow up in writing:** a short, neutral note of what you discussed and proposed.

If it goes ahead unchanged, contacting the works council yourself is legitimate. That's what it's for.

**A line you can use**
"I want us to see where we're actually slow and whether Copilot pays off. I'm worried a per-person ranking will mostly measure who games the numbers, and cost us the people who do the reviews and incidents. Can I set up a team-level version with two teams for six weeks?"

One honest caveat: managers often [rate people who challenge lower](https://instituteforpr.org/risks-rewards-speaking-managerial-responses-employee-voice), even when the challenge is useful. That's why questions and an offer of work beat objections. How far you take this is your call.
