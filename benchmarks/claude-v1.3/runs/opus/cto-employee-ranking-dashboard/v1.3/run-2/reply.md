You can push back without being the difficult one: ask what your VP wants to learn, offer a better way to learn it, and let Dutch rules do the hard part. A 300-person Dutch company must have a works council, and a system suited to monitoring performance needs its consent before it runs (article 27(1)(l) WOR). A weekly ranking that feeds improvement plans is exactly that.

**Why it's worth pushing on**
- Acceptance rate counts clicks on "accept", not good code. Uplevel's 2024 study of about 800 developers found no significant change in cycle time with Copilot, and 41% more bugs.
- PRs merged punishes the people who review, mentor, fix incidents and delete code. In Faros AI's 2025 data (10,000+ developers; Faros sells analytics), heavy AI users merged 98% more PRs while review time rose 91%.
- There is always a bottom 10%, even in a strong team. People will game it within weeks.

**What to do, in this order**
1. **Ask privately what problem it solves.** Proving Copilot is worth the money? Faster delivery? Worry about specific people? Each has a better answer. A new VP is often under pressure to show results; make it easy for him to explain.
2. **Offer an alternative and volunteer to build it.** Per team, against each team's own past: change lead time, review wait time, change fail rate and rework, before and after Copilot. That answers "is AI paying off?" Concerns about one person go through their manager, with evidence from the work.
3. **Mention the works council as timing, not a threat.** "This needs works council consent before it goes live; easier to design it with them now than have it stopped later." Loop in your privacy officer too.
4. **Find one or two allies first:** other seniors, an engineering manager, HR. Three people asking the same question aren't one difficult person.
5. **If it goes ahead anyway,** talk to a works council member yourself, and keep a short factual note of what you raised and when.

**A line you can use**
"I want us to get real value out of Copilot too. Can I show you a way to measure it per team that nobody can game, and that the works council will sign off on?"

Not legal advice; HR or the works council will know how article 27 applies at your company. Being careful about your position is reasonable.
