**Recommendation:** meet the board's real worry, cash runway to 2028, with a runway plan and hard tripwires, not a headcount target. That's measurable discipline that keeps your 2028 teams intact.

**Why the 15% cut is weak** (my estimates; check with your CFO): ~38 people at ~€80k fully loaded saves ~€3m a year, but only ~€1.5m in year one after works council advice, the UWV procedure, notice and transition payments. A quarter of the loss at best. And it risks 2028: a 1% downsizing has been linked to 31% more voluntary quits the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008).

**Thursday's plan**
1. **Own it.** Say plainly the flop is leadership's responsibility, starting with yours. You take 30% less pay, the leadership team 20%, no leadership bonuses until 2028. A signal, not a savings plan: after the 3DS flopped in 2011, Iwata halved his salary, Nintendo used its cash to keep its teams, and they built the Switch.
2. **Aim for the same ~€3m without dismissals:** freeze non-critical hiring and backfills (at ~8% attrition, ~20 roles a year) and cut tools, licences, office, events and outsourcing (with fair notice).
3. **Earn more from what you have:** platform and subscription deals for the launched game, co-development work, a publisher co-funding one 2028 project.
4. **Gate both projects** on playtests and wishlists, after an honest post-mortem of the flop. If one fails, move its people to the other.
5. **Tripwires:** a cash floor (say €20m) and quarterly gate reviews. Miss either and you return with deeper options, voluntary exits first.

Brief the works council now with the real numbers; run the legal steps past counsel.
