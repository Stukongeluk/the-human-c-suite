Don't cut 15% now. If the €12 million loss is mostly cash and doesn't grow, €40 million covers over three years of it, and the 2028 projects are your recovery. The board is right that a flop needs a visible response; give it discipline it can measure.

**Why the cut doesn't fit**
- 15% is about 38 people. At an assumed €75k loaded cost, that saves about €2.85 million a year before severance: under a quarter of the loss.
- It thins the teams building the 2028 games. A 1% downsizing has been linked to 31% more voluntary leavers the next year (Trevor & Nyberg, 2008).
- 20+ dismissals is a collective dismissal (WMCO) needing works council advice (article 25 WOR). Check with counsel.

**The alternative for Thursday**
1. **Leaders first.** You and the exec team take a visible pay cut and no 2026–27 bonuses. After the 3DS flopped in 2011, Satoru Iwata halved his salary and kept Nintendo's teams; they built the Switch. A signal, not the savings plan.
2. **Spend before people.** Marketing, co-development and outsourcing contracts, tools, licences, office.
3. **Focus.** Move the launch team onto the 2028 projects; keep the flopped game on minimal support unless data shows a way back.
4. **Hiring freeze**, backfilling only critical roles. At an assumed 8–10% attrition, that's 20–25 fewer roles in a year, no dismissals.
5. **Fix why it flopped.** Stage gates for both projects, with playtest milestones and kill criteria.
6. **Trigger.** If forecast cash at the 2028 launches falls below six months of costs, or a project misses a gate, you return with deeper options, starting with voluntary exits. Review quarterly.

Bring the works council in now with the real numbers.
