Don't let the platform team lead go. Own the outage yourself, and give the board accountability that makes the next migration safer: an independent, blameless review within two weeks, ending in fixes that each have an owner, a date and a measure. Nine hours down is usually a system failure (the plan, the rollback, the approvals, the timing), and those were yours.

The trap: a dismissal answers "who?" when the board's real question is "will this happen again?", and it teaches every engineer that the safe move is to hide risk. You'd also lose the person who now knows most about how this failed, right after paying €2 million for that knowledge.

The board is right that a €2 million loss needs visible consequences. Give them ones they can check, starting this week:

1. **Say it's yours, first.** To the board, before anyone else's part: "This was my migration; the result is mine."
2. **Talk to the lead today,** privately. Their job isn't on the table, you need them in the review as a witness, not a defendant, and ask how they're doing. Give the people who fought the outage time off.
3. **Commission the review,** led by someone outside the platform team. Was rollback tested on production-sized data? Were there go/no-go criteria, and who could call no-go? Who set the window and the deadline? Include a sourced breakdown of the €2 million.
4. **Report back** with the causes and five to eight actions, such as "no migration without a timed rollback rehearsal", and update the board monthly until they're closed.
5. **If the board wants a cost someone feels, offer your own bonus,** not the lead's job. It's a signal of trust, not a savings plan.

One exception: if the review shows the lead knowingly skipped an agreed safeguard or hid a known risk, that's a conduct question for your normal HR process, with their side heard. In much of Europe, one failed change is rarely lawful grounds for dismissal anyway; check with HR or counsel (this isn't legal advice).

A line for the board: "I'm accountable for this outage, and I won't offer you a name instead of a fix. In two weeks you'll have an independent review with owned, dated actions, reported to you monthly until they're done. Firing the person who knows this failure best makes the next one more likely, not less."
