Don't fire the platform team lead now. Take the accountability yourself, and give the board an independent post-mortem with owned, dated fixes. A 9-hour, roughly €2 million outage is a failure of the system around the migration, and you own that system. Dismissing the person who ran it, before anyone knows why it failed, fixes nothing and teaches every engineer to hide risk next time.

**Why not the dismissal**
- You don't know the cause yet. Did the lead ignore a warning, or raise risks that were overruled? Was there a rehearsed rollback, a go/no-go review, and who approved the weekend window? If the answers are "no rehearsal, schedule set above the team", the cause sits with you and the approvers.
- It may not hold up. In the Netherlands a dismissal needs a valid ground and a fair process, and "the board wanted someone" isn't one. Talk to employment counsel before anything is said. This is not legal advice.
- You'd lose the person who understands the system best, right when you need them for recovery.

**What to do this week**
1. Tell the board first, plainly: "This happened on my watch, and I own it."
2. Commission a blameless post-mortem led by someone independent of the platform team: timeline, decisions and who made them, what rollback existed, which warnings were raised. Aim for two to three weeks.
3. Give the board accountability they can check: every action has an owner, a date and a measure, such as a timed rollback rehearsal, a named go/no-go sign-off, and no migration without a tested fallback. Report monthly.
4. Tell the lead yourself, before they hear it elsewhere, what the board raised and what you're doing. Make sure the team is rested and not left to absorb the blame.
5. Decide on individual consequences after the facts. If someone knowingly bypassed an agreed control, handle it through your normal HR process, proportionately and with the person heard. That is a finding, not a starting point.

**A line for the board**
"I'm accountable for this outage. I've asked for an independent review, reporting back in three weeks, with every fix owned and dated. If it shows individual misconduct, we'll act on it. If it shows our process failed, then the process, and my oversight of it, changes."

**The board's side, steelmanned:** a €2M loss with no personal consequence can look like nothing changed. That's fair, so make your own accountability visible and the actions measurable.
