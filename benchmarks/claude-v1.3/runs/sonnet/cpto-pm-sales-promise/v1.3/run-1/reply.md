Don't say yes and don't say no. Treat the promise as a request that still needs estimating, and put the trade-off in front of the CEO so he decides openly. That protects the relationship better than quietly absorbing it, and better than refusing.

His real goal is almost certainly "keep our biggest customer happy and renewing." Three features by Q1 is his guess at how. You are on the same side of that goal, so say so.

**What to do this week**

1. **Get the facts first.**
   - Find out exactly what was promised, ideally in writing, and which customer problem each feature solves.
   - Ask the engineers for a rough range per feature (S/M/L is fine).
   - Work out your real Q1 free capacity: headcount minus leave, plus normal unplanned work and support.

2. **Show the trade-off in numbers, on one page.** For example: "A is about 6 weeks, B about 4, C about 8. We have about 5 free. To fit all three, X and Y drop, affecting N customers and €Z of revenue."

3. **Bring options, not a refusal.**
   - The smallest version of each feature that solves the customer's actual problem.
   - The most valuable one by end of Q1, dates for the others, and an early preview for the customer.
   - A workaround or configuration for one of them.
   - A swap: he chooses which roadmap items slip. It is his call.
   - Not on the list: overtime or skipped testing. That comes back as incidents and attrition.

4. **Talk to him one-to-one before any group meeting.** No "you shouldn't have promised." Try:

   > "I want [customer] to be happy. The team has sized the three features. Here's what fits in Q1, what it costs elsewhere, and three ways to get them what they need. Which trade-off do you prefer?"

5. **Agree a lightweight habit for next time:** "Can I give you a yes, a not-yet or a smaller version within two days before dates go to customers?"

Also tell your team now what is being discussed, so they don't hear it second-hand, and don't let dates be repeated to the customer until they are estimated.

**Assumptions:** the promise was a date with loose scope, and he will weigh a trade-off if he sees it clearly. If he says "just make it happen" after seeing the numbers, that is a different conversation. Then get finance or another exec to see the cost with you.
