I wouldn't select on tickets resolved plus CSAT, and in Sweden you probably can't. Redundancy selection there generally follows LAS seniority order (last in, first out) per job category at the workplace, unless your collective agreement says otherwise. Up to three people can be exempted. Not legal advice; confirm with employment counsel.

**Selection approach (draft)**

1. **Prove nine fewer roles are needed.** Use real ticket volume, escalation rates and AI resolution quality. Test attrition, a hiring freeze, redeployment (AI quality review, escalations, knowledge base) and voluntary exits first. Leaders take the first cut.
2. **Consult before deciding.** Under MBL you negotiate with the union(s) before the decision, with real numbers, and ask for their alternatives. Then notify Arbetsförmedlingen as required.
3. **Set the order with HR and counsel.** Apply LAS seniority order, plus the three exemptions if used. Write the exemption criteria down before naming anyone, such as a scarce language or skill the remaining work needs.
4. **No performance rankings.** With AI taking the easy tickets, "tickets resolved" mostly reflects who got which queue, and CSAT tracks case difficulty. Both penalise people who mentor or take hard cases.
5. **Support from day one of notice.** Engage the job security council (TRR for covered white-collar staff), with coaching, training time and job-search help. Offer vacancies and re-employment priority.
6. **Do it once.** Decide which work stops, so the 21 who stay aren't handed the same work.

**Two questions that change this:** what does your collective agreement say about selection, and how many escalated tickets do you expect over the next 12 months? Nine is a lot to cut if the remaining work is harder.
