I wouldn't use tickets resolved and CSAT. In Sweden you probably can't pick people by performance at all, and those two metrics would pick badly anyway.

**Why it breaks**
- **Law:** the Employment Protection Act (LAS) sets selection by length of service, unless your collective agreement says otherwise. Every employer can exempt three people. You must also negotiate with the union under the Co-determination Act (MBL) before deciding. Check the details with HR and employment counsel.
- **Metrics:** with AI taking first-line tickets, the remaining volume and difficulty are uneven. Tickets per person rewards easy queues and punishes people who help colleagues or handle hard cases. CSAT swings with the type of customer.

**Draft approach (9 roles, 30 to 21)**
1. **Frame the work first.** Which queues and tasks does AI actually remove? Size the human work that remains, by team, from real volumes. Is 21 the right number?
2. **Reduce without exits.** Natural attrition, hiring freeze, redeployment into AI quality review, escalations, knowledge base and onboarding. Offer voluntary departures.
3. **Open the process to the union** now, with the numbers, while it can still change the plan.
4. **If involuntary cuts remain,** use the legal selection order, with criteria fixed in writing before any names. Use the three exemptions only for skills the new set-up needs, backed by documented, role-based reasons. Keep individual metrics out.
5. **Support from day one of notice:** job security council (TRR or similar), paid training and job-search help. Dismissed staff generally have re-employment priority for nine months.
6. **Do it once.** Say clearly that the round is final, and only if it is true.

One question that changes the answer: is the saving needed now, or can 9 roles go through attrition and redeployment over 6 to 9 months?
