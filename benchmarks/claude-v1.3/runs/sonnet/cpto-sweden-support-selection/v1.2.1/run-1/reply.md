Don't select on tickets resolved and CSAT. In Sweden you probably can't, and it would pick the wrong nine people even where you could.

**Why not**
- Swedish law (LAS) sets the selection order by length of service within each employer-defined unit and job category, unless a collective agreement says otherwise. Since 2022 you may exempt three people. Performance scores don't override this.
- The unions have to be negotiated with under MBL before you decide. Bring them in now, with the numbers.
- AI took the easy first-line tickets, so today's ticket counts mostly reflect who gets hard cases, and CSAT swings with case type and shift. Both reward the wrong behaviour and punish people who mentor and handle escalations.

**Selection approach (draft)**
1. State the gap: AI-handled volume, remaining volume per skill (escalations, languages, enterprise, hours), and the capacity needed. Check that 21 really covers it.
2. Reduce before you cut: pause hiring, redeploy into AI quality review, knowledge-base work and escalation roles, offer voluntary exits and reduced hours, and leaders take the first cut.
3. Define the units and job categories with HR and the unions, then apply LAS order. Use your three exemptions on documented, role-based skills the remaining work truly needs, not on scores.
4. Fund support from the day of notice: job security council (TRR or equivalent), coaching, paid training. Those dismissed get re-employment priority for nine months.
5. Do it once, and have HR and employment counsel confirm the process, notice to the employment agency and timing.

**Two questions that change the answer:** Is there a collective agreement with its own selection rules? And does the work shrink by a third, or has it just changed shape?
