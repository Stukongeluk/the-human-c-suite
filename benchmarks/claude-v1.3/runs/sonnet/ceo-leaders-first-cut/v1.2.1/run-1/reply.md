**Recommendation: don't cut 15% on Thursday. Offer the board a dated discipline plan with hard triggers, and keep layoffs as the last step, not the first.**

The numbers: €40M cash against a €12M loss is about three years at that burn, so you reach the 2028 launches. Cutting 38 people (15% of 250) saves roughly €2.5-3M a year (my estimate, at ~€70-80k loaded cost), before severance and a UWV collective-dismissal process. It would also cut into the teams that build the two projects you are betting on. Games lose their best people first when cuts start.

**What to put in front of the board**

1. **Stop spending first.** Vendors, tools, marketing and the flopped title's live-ops costs. Review every one line by line.
2. **Cut the work.** Stop or pause the weakest prototypes so the two 2028 projects are fully staffed.
3. **Hiring freeze** and natural attrition. A studio this size typically loses 10-15% a year, which does most of the work without a single dismissal.
4. **Leaders first.** Executive and board pay and bonuses are cut before anyone else's job.
5. **Redeploy** people from the flop onto the 2028 projects.
6. **Revenue:** back catalogue, porting, co-development or publisher funding for 2028.

**The credible part:** agree numeric triggers now, for example "if cash falls below €20M, or a 2028 milestone slips by two quarters, we reopen headcount." That is discipline the board can hold you to. Involve the works council early; they have a legal right to advise.

**Before Thursday, find out:**
- Monthly burn today. Is the €12M a one-off write-off or the real run rate?
- Committed spend through 2028, and whether the 2028 projects are funded to ship.

If the burn is much higher than the loss suggests, my answer changes. Then the smallest, once-only reduction, done decently, is on the table.
