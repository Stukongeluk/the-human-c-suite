Don't cut 15%. Bring the board a staged plan that shows discipline, owns the flop at the top, and keeps the people who ship the 2028 titles.

**Why the cut is weak:** it removes about 38 people. At an estimated €80-90k loaded cost each, that saves roughly €3-3.5M a year, about a quarter of the loss. Severance, works council and UWV process, and rebuilding the team later would eat much of it. The loss is mostly a one-off launch result, and €40M covers it. The real risk is cash on the day the 2028 games ship.

**The alternative (my estimates, check with your CFO):**
1. **Leadership first.** Say plainly the launch was leadership's call and result. Executives take the largest pay cut and forgo bonuses. This is a trust signal, not a savings plan.
2. **Non-people spend.** Vendors, licences, marketing commitments, office: about €1M.
3. **Focus.** Pause work not tied to the two 2028 projects.
4. **Hiring freeze and attrition.** Studios your size typically lose 10-15% a year, releasing about €2-3M a year without forced exits.
5. **Redeploy** launch-team people onto the 2028 projects, with paid learning time.
6. **Voluntary exits** only if the numbers still fall short.

That targets about €3-4M run-rate, comparable to the cut, and it is reversible. The cut is not.

**Give the board a safeguard.** Set a cash floor (for example €20M) and a date. If it is breached, you return with the next step. Layoffs stay possible, but you will have shown they aren't needed yet.

"Cut 15%" is a solution, not the problem. The problem is runway to 2028 and the board's confidence in you.

If cuts do come later, Dutch rules apply: works council advice and collective redundancy notification. Take counsel; this is not legal advice.

**Two questions that would change my answer:** What is monthly burn once launch costs fall away? How much cash do the two projects need to reach release?
