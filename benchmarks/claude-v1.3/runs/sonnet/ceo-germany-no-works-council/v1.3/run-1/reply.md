**Short answer:** 15 dismissals by end of March is legally doable without a works council, but expect roughly €0.5–0.8M net saving in 2027 (my estimate), not the full run-rate. Have employment counsel confirm everything below; this isn't legal advice.

**Process (Germany, KSchG applies above 10 employees)**
1. **Document the business reason:** a concrete decision that specific work is ending, and why no other vacancy fits.
2. **Social selection (Sozialauswahl):** within comparable roles, weigh tenure, age, dependants and severe disability. You can't pick by performance ranking.
3. **Check protected people:** pregnancy, parental leave, severe disability (needs Integrationsamt approval), long illness.
4. **Notify the employment agency before dismissing.** Mass-dismissal rules (§17 KSchG) trigger at more than 10% of an establishment this size: 15 of 140 is 10.7%, so you're likely over. Counsel should confirm.
5. **Written notice,** with statutory periods (§622 BGB: one month, rising to seven after 20 years). Many will be settled by agreement.

No works council means no social plan duty, but employees can elect one, and retaliating against organising is unlawful.

**What it saves (estimate, assumes ~€90k fully loaded cost, ~4 years average tenure)**
- Run-rate: 15 × €90k ≈ €1.35M a year.
- Notice periods push many exits past March, so 2027 captures roughly 70%: about €0.95M.
- Settlements (~0.5 monthly salary per year of service) plus legal costs: about €0.2–0.3M.
- Net 2027: about €0.6–0.8M, before attrition from survivors and rehiring.

**Before you commit**
"15 roles" is a solution. What's the gap, in euros, and what closes it first? At 140 people, normal attrition may free 10–15 roles a year, so a hiring freeze, vendor and cloud spend, and pricing deserve numbers next to the cuts. Leadership should take the first and largest pay cut, and say plainly that the result is theirs. If cuts remain unavoidable, do one round, with paid outplacement and a package above the minimum.
