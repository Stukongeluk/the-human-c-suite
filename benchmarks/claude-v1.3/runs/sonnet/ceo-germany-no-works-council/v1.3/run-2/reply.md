**Process (German law; not legal advice, confirm with employment counsel)**
1. **Document the business reason** and which roles disappear. "Revenue is down" alone is thin if challenged.
2. **Social selection (§1 KSchG)** among comparable roles: tenure, age, dependants, disability. Performance rankings are not a valid method.
3. **Mass-dismissal notice (§17 KSchG):** 15 of 140 is just over 10% (14), so notify the Agentur für Arbeit *before* giving notice. Miscounting can void dismissals; employer-initiated exit agreements count too.
4. **No works council** means no social plan duty and no hearing, but protected people (pregnancy, parental leave, severe disability) need special approval.
5. **Notice periods (§622 BGB)** grow with service, up to seven months. Employees have three weeks to sue; most cases settle, commonly around 0.5-1 month's salary per year of service (market practice, not law).

**Savings (estimates)**
- Assumed €90k loaded cost per role: €1.35m a year.
- Notice given in January, exits around end of March or later: about nine months saved, roughly €1.0m.
- Less severance (about €0.28m at ~4 years' average service) and legal/outplacement (about €0.08m): **roughly €0.65m**.
- Not counted: lost knowledge, survivors leaving, rehiring.

**Before you commit**
Fifteen roles is about 11% of staff. What is the actual gap, and is the dip temporary or structural? Cheaper first moves: a hiring freeze, vendor, cloud and licence review, voluntary exits, and executive pay and bonuses cut first. The board and leadership own this result, so say so in the announcement. If a gap remains, do one round, not several, with paid outplacement from the day of notice.
