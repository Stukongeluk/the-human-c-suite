You're not being difficult by raising this: you can do it as a question about how to make the VP's goal succeed, and in the Netherlands the process itself gives you a legitimate, non-personal way in. Your instinct is right on the merits. Acceptance rate measures how often someone presses Tab, not value, and PRs merged rewards small, frequent changes. A weekly bottom-10% cut punishes the people who review, mentor, fix incidents and delete code. It also invites gaming, and AI makes both numbers cheap to inflate.

**What you can actually do, in order**

1. **Ask before you argue.** Talk to the VP one-to-one, or through your own manager. Start with the goal: "What problem are you trying to solve: slow delivery, quality, cost, or specific people you're worried about?" That is what they care about, and rankings are only one way to get at it. Don't open with "this is wrong."

2. **Bring a better option, not just an objection.** Offer team-level measures from your own pipelines: change lead time, review wait time, change fail rate, time to restore, unplanned work, plus a short developer-experience survey. These answer "are we getting faster and safer?" without ranking anyone. If you're worried someone is underperforming, the route is their manager, clear expectations, feedback and a fair chance, with evidence from the actual work and from peers.

3. **Use the Dutch process.** At 300 people you must have a works council (ondernemingsraad). Under article 27(1)(l) WOR, systems suited to monitoring employees' behaviour or performance generally need its consent before introduction. A per-person Copilot and PR dashboard looks like exactly that, and it also raises AVG/GDPR questions about purpose and proportionality. Raising it is a procedural point, not a personal one: "Has this gone through the OR?"  This is not legal advice, so check the specifics with HR or the OR.

4. **Find allies.** Other senior engineers, engineering managers, HR (improvement plans can become a dismissal paper trail, so they have reasons to care), and the OR.

5. **Offer a time-boxed alternative.** "Let's run team-level metrics for a quarter, agree the indicators with the engineers, and review whether they explain delivery better than the ranking would."

**A line you can use:** "I want this to work. My worry is that acceptance rate and PR counts will move for reasons unrelated to real contribution, and people will start gaming them. Could we start with team-level delivery and quality measures, and check with the works council first?"

**Your own risk.** If the plan goes ahead unchanged and people are harmed, you have a documented, polite record that you raised it. You don't have to sacrifice your career over it, and how far you push is your call.
