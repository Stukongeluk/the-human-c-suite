You can push back without being difficult, because the strongest move isn't "this is wrong" but "how do we make sure this measures what you want?" Do that in a way that gives the VP a better option, and bring in the works council (OR), who have a formal say here.

**Why the metrics fail (your one-liner)**
Acceptance rate measures tool usage, not value, and it can rise while quality falls. PRs merged rewards volume and punishes the people who review, mentor, fix incidents and delete code. AI makes volume cheap, so both numbers are easy to game. A *weekly* bottom 10% is also noisy: with 300 people, about 30 names land at the bottom every week by chance and by who happened to work on what.

**What to do, in order**

1. **Ask what he is trying to fix.** Slow delivery, quality, AI return on investment, or a specific performance worry? Each has a better measure. Ask privately and curiously, not in a meeting.
2. **Offer an alternative that serves the same goal.** Team-level delivery and health: DORA metrics (lead time, deployment frequency, change failure rate, recovery time), review wait time, work in progress, unplanned work, plus a developer survey. If the question is whether Copilot pays off, compare teams before and after with a comparison group. METR's 2025 trial found experienced developers 19% slower with AI tools while believing they were faster.
3. **Check who has to agree.** At 300 people you have a works council. A system suited to monitoring individual performance needs its consent under art. 27(1)(l) WOR, and individual tracking raises GDPR questions. Ask your OR representative, or HR, whether it has been consulted. That moves the objection from "you" to "the process", which is also the safest place for it.
4. **Make plans a manager decision, not a dashboard output.** Improvement plans should come from clear expectations, feedback and a fair chance, based on the work and on peers. Ask HR how a plan built on these numbers would stand up.
5. **Write it down, neutrally.** A half-page of facts and a proposal, sent to your manager or the VP. Not "this is wrong" but "I'm concerned this will drive the wrong behaviour; here's what I'd measure instead."

**A line you can use**
"I want the same thing you do: a clear view of delivery and the return on Copilot. I'm worried individual counts will reward volume over quality and trigger works council questions. Could we try team-level measures for a quarter and compare?"

**On risk:** raising this with data and an alternative is normal professional behaviour. Don't gather other people's data to prove a point. If things turn hostile, the vertrouwenspersoon or your OR is next. Not legal advice.
