# Reduction plan: 22 roles (of 180), mainly Operations and Support

Draft for the CEO. Not legal advice: confirm every step with employment counsel and HR before it is relied on. Items in [brackets] are numbers or facts I do not have.

## 1. What is decided and what is not

- Decided by the board: a reduction of 22 roles (12.2% of 180, leaving 158), mainly in Operations and Support.
- Not yet decided, and open to the works council's advice: which work stops, how the 22 are distributed, the selection method, the timeline and the support package.
- In the Netherlands this is a proposed decision (voorgenomen besluit) until the works council has given its advice. It must not be carried out or announced as final before then (Works Councils Act, art. 25 WOR). Counsel to confirm.

## 3. Leaders go first

- The executive team and the board take the first and largest cut: a reduction in executive pay and cancellation or reduction of 2026 and 2027 bonuses [amounts to be decided by the board]. This is a signal of trust, not a savings plan, and it is announced the same day as the reduction.
- The announcement states plainly that the result is leadership's responsibility.

## 5. Selection (counsel to confirm)

- Dutch law generally requires the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel): roles are grouped into exchangeable functions, then people are chosen within each group by age cohort, with length of service as the tiebreaker.
- Never select on performance rankings, on who is on leave or ill, on who raised a concern, or on any protected characteristic. No individual-level activity metrics (tickets closed, handle time) are used for selection.
- Offer open vacancies and redeployment to affected people first; voluntary exits are offered before any involuntary dismissal where the function group allows it.
- The works council receives the function groups and the method before individual names exist.

## 7. Communication

- The all-staff message (announcement.md) goes out on T0, after the works council has the formal request in hand and the managers of affected teams have been briefed.
- Affected people hear it from their own manager, in person or live, with HR present, never by email and never by a disabled login. Written summary the same day. Access changes happen after the conversation.
- Managers get a briefing and script one day before. They also get support: this is hard on them too.
- Remaining staff: hear the same message on T0, and again on the day individual conversations are done, including a statement that the round is over, only if the board has confirmed that it is true.

## 8. Work and people after the round

- Stop work, do not just redistribute it. Each affected team lead and the remaining team agree within two weeks what stops, what slows and what moves, and publish it.
- 1:1 with every remaining colleague in the affected teams within two weeks.
- Track for 12 months, reported to the works council: regretted exits, absence, a short pulse survey, customer wait times, first-contact resolution, complaint volumes, error and incident counts, backlog size. These are team-level indicators; no individual is ranked on them.
- Write an honest review at 6 and 12 months: what was saved, what it cost including hidden costs, what the leadership team would do differently, and what in planning made this necessary.

## 9. Risks to watch

- Customer service and operational quality in a fintech: wait times, payment exceptions and complaints can rise before the work reduction catches up. Set a threshold at which the board pauses further transfers of work.
- Compliance and regulatory duties covered by the departing roles (see section 2).
- Survivors leaving: the best people often have options. Talk to them early.
- Knowledge loss: run handovers during notice, paid and with a named owner for each process.
- Leakage before T0: keep the pre-announcement window short.
