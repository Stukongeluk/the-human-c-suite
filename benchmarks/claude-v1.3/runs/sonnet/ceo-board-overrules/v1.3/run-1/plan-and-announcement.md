# Reduction of 22 roles in Operations and Support: plan and staff announcement

## Part 1. Plan

### 1. Where this stands

- Intended decision: 22 of 180 roles (12.2%) end, mostly in Operations and Support. The board has decided; for the works council and for staff this is an intended decision until the works council has advised.
- At 180 employees you have a mandatory works council. Under article 25 WOR it must be asked for advice at a moment when its advice can still influence the decision. If you announce a finished decision and consult afterwards, the works council can challenge it at the Enterprise Chamber, which can stop or delay implementation. If the final decision departs from its advice, implementation waits one month (art. 25(6) WOR).
- 22 dismissals within three months in one UWV work area is above the 20 threshold of the Collective Dismissal Notification Act (WMCO). That means consulting the unions and notifying UWV. Dismissal for business-economic reasons then needs UWV permission or individual settlement agreements.

### 2. Before anyone is told: decide the work, not just the roles

Complete this in the next few days, because staff will ask on day one.

- Name the work that stops or shrinks in Operations and Support, so survivors are not handed the same workload with fewer colleagues. [List: processes, queues, service levels, products.]
- Ask Compliance and Risk to confirm that the remaining teams can still meet regulatory duties (customer due diligence and transaction monitoring, complaints handling, incident reporting, regulator contact points). In a fintech, an under-staffed operations team is a licence risk as well as a service risk.
- Agree and write down the leadership contribution (see section 6) so it can be stated in the announcement.
- Set the package (section 5), costed. Total one-off cost: [€]. Expected first-year net saving after severance, notice, outplacement and consultation time: [€]. First-year savings are usually well below the annual figure because the costs come first.

Avoid giving individual news in the last days before the Christmas break. If the works council's advice lands later than mid-December, hold the individual conversations in early January and say so openly in the announcement, rather than leaving people to wait.

### 4. Selection

- Use the legally required method for business-economic dismissals in the Netherlands, the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel): roles are grouped into interchangeable functions, and within each group the people to go are chosen proportionally across age brackets, with length of service as the tie-breaker. Counsel confirms the groups and applies it.
- No performance rankings, no velocity or activity metrics, no choices made by individual managers. People on sick leave, pregnancy or parental leave, and works council members have special dismissal protection; counsel must clear every such case before any step is taken.
- Document the criteria and each step so they hold up to review.

### 5. Package (go beyond the statutory minimum where the company can afford it)

Statutory floor: notice period by length of service, and the transition payment (transitievergoeding). On top, proposed:

- Paid notice with freedom to job-hunt during working hours; for those who want it, release from duties on full pay.
- Severance above the statutory transition payment: [formula, e.g. x months' salary per year of service, with a floor of y months]. Treat this as the company's decision to take, not as an offer to haggle over.
- Support from the day of notice, not the last day: outplacement and coaching, CV and LinkedIn review, paid training with a budget of [€] per person, financial and psychological support through a confidential provider.
- Keep the laptop and phone after data is wiped; a written reference; a promise of a positive reference call; priority for rehiring and an alumni network.
- Extra care where job loss weighs most: people on a highly skilled migrant permit (the time limits to find a new sponsor are short, so involve immigration counsel at once), people close to retirement, people with a mortgage and a single income, and anyone on leave.
- Internal vacancies offered first, with a named recruiter helping people apply.
- Notice for contractors or agency staff whose work also ends: [list]. Same honesty and notice, even though the law differs.

### 6. Leadership goes first

- The executive team takes the first and largest cut: [x% salary reduction for y months for the CEO and the executive team; bonuses for this year waived; perks and discretionary spend frozen]. State it in the announcement with the numbers, once it is decided. It is a signal of accountability and does not replace the savings plan.
- The announcement says plainly that leadership is responsible for the situation, not the people leaving.

### 7. The people who stay

- Tell them the same day, honestly: what happened, why, what it means for them, what work stops. Say the round is over only if it is true: [confirm].
- Re-plan workloads and priorities with each team within two weeks. Stop work, do not just redistribute it.
- Hold 1:1s within two weeks. Track the following monthly for 12 months: engagement pulse ("I can raise concerns without fear", "decisions that affect me are explained"), absence, regretted exits, and service levels in Operations and Support (response times, backlog, complaints, error rates).
- Pre-agreed warning signs and what you will do: [e.g. regretted attrition above x% in a quarter, or a service level breach for two consecutive weeks, triggers a review with the works council and a decision on stopping more work].
- At six months, write an honest review: what was saved, what it cost including hidden costs, and what in the company's planning made this necessary.

### 8. Risks to watch

| Risk | Early warning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Works council challenge because consultation looked like a formality | Requests for more time or information go unanswered | Give the time; treat objections as risk analysis; change the plan where it holds up |
| Best people in the remaining teams leave | Resignations in the first 90 days, falling pulse scores | Retention conversations, visible workload relief, career talks |
| Service or compliance failure in Operations and Support | Queues, SLA breaches, incident count | Stop lower-value work first; Compliance sign-off on staffing levels |
| Leaks before the announcement | Rumours | Keep the window between the works council request and the all-staff session to one day |
| Individual cases handled badly | Complaints, legal letters | Counsel clears each case; manager scripts; HR present at every conversation |

## Part 2. Staff announcement

Delivered live at an all-hands (video for remote staff), by the CEO, after managers of affected teams have been briefed. A written version goes out right after, not instead.

**Subject: A hard decision about our organisation, and what happens next**

Hello everyone,

I have difficult news, and you should hear it from me, in full, today.

We intend to reduce the company by 22 roles out of 180, mostly in Operations and Support. I want to explain why, what is decided and what is still open, what we are doing for the people affected, and what leadership is doing first.

**Why.** [Plain explanation with real numbers: what changed, what the gap is, and what it would mean if we did nothing. For example: our costs grew faster than revenue over the last [period]; we have [x] months of runway at the current plan; the board concluded that we cannot reach our targets without reducing the cost base by [€].] The board and the leadership team looked at other routes first. The board decided that this reduction is necessary. I will be straightforward: the responsibility for this situation is mine and the leadership team's, not that of the people in Operations and Support, who have done their jobs well. [Name the specific leadership decision or forecast that did not hold, in one honest sentence.]

**What is decided and what is open.** The decision to reduce roles is the board's intended decision. It is not final until we have received the advice of our works council, which we are asking for today and to which we are giving the real numbers and enough time to examine them. If the works council finds a better route to our goals, we will change the plan, and I will tell you what changed and what did not. What is not open: [the size of the savings we need].

**Who is affected.** The roles come from these function groups: [list]. No individual has been chosen. When the works council has given its advice, we will select according to the Dutch legal method, within function groups, using documented, objective criteria. We will not rank people on performance or activity numbers.

**How you will hear.** If your role is affected, you will hear it from your own manager, with HR present, in person or on a call, never by email and never by a login that stops working. We expect this in [week of / date], and we will tell you at once if that date moves. Everyone else will be told the same day.

**What we are doing for the people leaving.** [Package summary: paid notice, severance above the legal minimum with the formula, outplacement and a training budget from the first day of notice, laptop and phone, references, priority for rehiring, extra support for people for whom this is hardest.] Open internal vacancies are listed here [link], and affected people get first access.

**What leadership is doing first.** [The executive team's salary will be reduced by x% for y months, we are waiving this year's bonuses, and we are freezing [perks]. This does not replace the reduction; it is a statement of whose responsibility this is.]

**What happens to the work.** We are stopping or reducing [list of work], so that the colleagues who stay are not asked to do the same work with fewer people. Each team lead will meet their team within two weeks to re-plan priorities. [If true: We do not plan further reductions. If this changes, you will hear it from me first.]

**Where to ask questions.** Today at [time] there is an open Q&A with me and [CFO/HR lead]; questions can also be sent anonymously to [channel], and answers will be published within [two] working days. Your manager and HR are available for 1:1 conversations; [confidential support provider and contact] is available to everyone, whether or not your role is affected.

I know many of you will be worried, and some of you will be angry. That is fair. Ask me anything, and challenge what does not add up; I will answer with facts or say that I do not yet know.

[Name]
CEO

## Part 3. Manager brief (one page, for the briefing before the announcement)

- What you may say: everything in the announcement. What you may not say: names, guesses about who is affected, or a promise that no further changes will follow, unless the CEO has confirmed it.
- If asked "Am I on the list?": no list exists yet; selection happens only after the works council has advised, using the legal method. You will hear it from your own manager.
- If asked "Why not other options?": the board decided on this route; the works council is now examining the numbers and can propose alternatives, and the plan changes if it finds a better one.
- If asked about pay and package: give the summary figures; individual calculations come from HR in writing.
- If someone is upset: listen, do not argue, do not rush to fix. Offer the support provider and a follow-up within 48 hours.
- Support for you: your own debrief with HR after each conversation; tell us if you are struggling.
