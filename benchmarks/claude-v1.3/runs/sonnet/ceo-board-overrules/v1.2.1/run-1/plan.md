Draft for CEO, HR and employment counsel. Not legal advice; counsel must confirm every legal step and date before use. Items in [brackets] are facts I do not have and must be filled in, not guessed.

## 1. Decision and scope

- Board decision: 22 roles (about 12% of 180 FTE), mainly Operations and Support. Split by team: [Operations x / Support x / other x].
- Reason to state to staff and the works council: [gap in EUR, the date it matters, the covenant/runway/margin it protects]. The same numbers go to everyone.
- Work stops with the roles: before any names are touched, list which processes, SLAs and service promises in Operations and Support will end or change, so the remaining team is not given the same work with fewer people. Owner: COO/Head of Support. Due: before the works council request.

## 2. Legal path (Netherlands) - confirm with counsel

| Step | What it means | Why it matters for timing |
|---|---|---|
| Works council advice (art. 25 WOR) | 180 employees, so a works council exists. A reduction of this size needs its advice, requested while it can still change the decision's execution. | The board has decided before consulting. Present the decision as the board's proposal, ask for advice with the full numbers and let the council propose alternatives. If it is treated as a formality, the council can appeal to the Enterprise Chamber and the process can be frozen. |
| Collective dismissal notice (WMCO) | 20 or more dismissals within three months in one UWV work area: notify UWV and the unions and consult them. 22 roles is over the line if they sit in one work area. | Counsel to confirm the work area count and whether any roles can be solved by redeployment or voluntary exit (those may change the count, but do not engineer the number to avoid the rule). |
| UWV dismissal permit or settlement agreements | Business-economic dismissal generally needs UWV permission, or individual settlement agreements. | Settlement agreements are faster but must be genuinely voluntary and are not a way to pressure people. |
| Selection (afspiegelingsbeginsel) | Selection by exchangeable function group, then by age cohort. | Documented, objective, applied by HR and counsel, never by manager preference. No selection based on leave, illness, pregnancy or having raised concerns. |
| Transition payment | Statutory transitievergoeding applies in general. | Budget it, plus the package below. |

## 3. Sequence and timeline (relative weeks; fix dates with counsel)

| When | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Week 0 | Counsel review. Leadership pay decision (see 4). Brief works council chair and request a meeting. | CEO, HR, counsel |
| Week 0-1 | Formal advice request to the works council with the full numbers, selection method, package and what work stops. Invite alternatives; agree a response timetable. | CEO, HR |
| Week 1-5 | Works council consultation. Answer questions in writing. Keep a log of what changed because of it. Typically a few weeks; do not set the date for them. | CEO, HR |
| Week 1-5 | Offer vacancies and redeployment to affected function groups first. Open voluntary exit and reduced-hours options. Map open roles and skills. | HR, managers |
| After advice | Final decision, informed by the advice. Notify UWV and unions if WMCO applies. | CEO, counsel |
| Notification day | Individual conversations first, then an all-staff message the same day (section 6). | Managers with HR |
| Week after | UWV application or settlement agreements, per counsel. | HR, counsel |
| Notice period | Paid notice, support starts on day one. | HR |
| Week +2 after notification | 1:1s for everyone who stays. Workload re-plan. | Managers |
| +3, +6, +12 months | Review of savings, hidden costs, attrition, absence. | CEO, HR, CFO |

Staff must not hear from rumour. Keep the circle to the people who need to know, and keep the gap between works council briefing and notification day as short as the law allows.

## 4. Leaders first

Before any announcement: decide and publish what the executive team does with bonuses, raises and perks this year. State the size of it. This is a signal of trust, not a savings plan, and staff will ask. If leadership will not do it, say why plainly; do not skip the question. [Decision needed from CEO.]

## 5. Package (proposal; cost to be confirmed by Finance)

Beyond the statutory transition payment, where the company can afford it:

- Severance or paid notice above the statutory minimum: [amount/formula].
- Paid time to job-hunt, outplacement and coaching from the day of notice, with a budget of [EUR per person] for training.
- CV and LinkedIn review; a written reference agreed before the last day; positive reference calls.
- Laptop and phone can be kept after data is wiped, where possible.
- Rehire priority and an alumni network.
- Confidential psychological and financial support.
- Extra care for people on a work visa, close to retirement, on leave or the sole earner. Counsel to check protections before any selection.

## 6. The day itself

1. Everyone affected hears it first from their own manager with HR, in person or on a live call, in scheduled slots on the same morning. No email, no surprise login failures: access changes after the conversation.
2. A written summary goes to the person the same day: what happens next, the package, deadlines, contacts.
3. All-staff message (announcement.md) goes out only after the last individual conversation, the same day.
4. Managers get a script, a Q&A and their own debrief; they carry this too.
5. People may say goodbye to colleagues if they want to.

## 7. People who stay

- Same-day explanation of why, what it means for them and what stops.
- Re-plan Operations and Support: list the work being stopped, the service levels that change, and customer-facing changes. Tell customers where service changes.
- Do not say "this is the last round" unless the board has actually committed to it and the numbers support it.
- Monitor: attrition and regretted exits, absence, engagement pulse, support quality (response time, backlog, complaints) for 12 months. Aggregated, never per person.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Works council advice treated as a formality, leading to legal challenge or delay | Real consultation, a logged response, willingness to change execution |
| Service quality in Support and Operations falls, especially for a regulated fintech (complaint handling, KYC/AML monitoring, payments operations) | Check with Compliance which roles carry regulatory duties before selection; stop work explicitly |
| Best people among those who stay leave | Early honest communication, retention conversations, visible leadership cuts |
| Knowledge walks out in the notice period | Structured handovers with named recipients, paid time for it |
| A second round later | Size this one properly; be honest in the announcement about what is and is not known |
