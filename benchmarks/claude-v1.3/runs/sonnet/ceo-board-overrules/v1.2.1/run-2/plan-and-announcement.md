# Reduction of 22 roles: plan and staff announcement (DRAFT)

Status: draft for the CEO, HR and employment counsel. Nothing here has been sent to anyone. Dutch law points are for counsel to confirm; this is not legal advice.

Basis: 22 of 180 roles (12.2%), mostly operations and support, as decided by the board. Names, salaries, notice dates and package budget are not known to me and are marked [ ].

---

## Part 1. The plan

### 1. Sequence (the order matters legally and for trust)

| When | Step | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Week 0 | Engage employment counsel. Freeze the list of affected roles; no names circulated. Confirm which UWV work area(s) the 22 fall in | CEO, HR |
| Week 0 | Pre-brief the leadership team and every manager who will hold a conversation | CEO |
| Week 1 | Formally request the works council's advice (art. 25 WOR) with the full file: reason, numbers, roles, selection method, package, timeline, and what work stops. The decision is a proposed decision until the council has advised | CEO, HR |
| Week 1 | Same day as the council request: tell all staff the proposal (Part 2, message A), live, then in writing | CEO |
| Week 1 | Notify UWV and the unions and consult them (WMCO applies at 20 or more dismissals within three months in one work area). Counsel to confirm timing and form | HR, counsel |
| Weeks 1-5 | Council advice period: share the real numbers, answer written questions, meet at least weekly. Be ready to change the plan if the council finds a better route | CEO, HR |
| After advice | Final decision, with a written response to the council's advice. Only then individual notices | CEO, board |
| After final decision | Individual conversations in one day or two consecutive days (Part 3), written summary the same day | Managers, HR |
| Then | Dismissal route per person (UWV permission or settlement agreement, counsel to advise), notice periods, transition support running from day one | HR |
| 2 weeks later | 1:1s with every remaining person; workload re-plan done | Managers |
| 3, 6, 12 months | Review: savings vs. cost, absence, regretted exits, service quality | CEO, HR |

Why not announce a final decision now: the works council's advice has to be asked before the decision is taken and early enough to be able to change it. A board decision already presented to staff as final, with notices following, invites a challenge at the Enterprise Chamber and costs more time than the consultation itself. Presenting it as a proposed decision, honestly, is both legal and fair.

### 2. Cut work along with roles

22 fewer people with the same workload means failure within months, especially in support and operations at a fintech. Before the council file is sent, each affected team lead writes down:

- which work stops, shrinks or is automated, by when
- which service levels change (response times, opening hours, channels), and whether any of this touches regulated obligations (complaint handling, customer due diligence, transaction monitoring, incident reporting). Compliance signs off before the file goes out
- who owns the work that moves, and what they stop doing to make room

### 3. Selection

- Follow the reflection principle (afspiegelingsbeginsel): interchangeable roles are grouped, and within each group age categories are represented proportionally. Counsel confirms the groups and exchange rules (e.g. a justified exception for key knowledge).
- Criteria are written down and applied to roles, not to personal impressions. No selection on protected characteristics, on being on leave or ill, on pregnancy, or on having raised concerns. Counsel checks the final list for these before anyone is told.
- Offer open internal vacancies to affected people first, with a real chance, not a formality.
- Contractors and agency staff in the same teams: if any contracts end as part of this, give the same notice and honesty.

### 4. Leaders go first

Proposal for the CEO to decide and announce in the same message: [leadership team bonuses for the year are not paid / executive base pay reduced by X% for 12 months / perks cut]. The amount is for you to set; it should be visible and meaningful, and stated before the council meets, not after. If you decide not to, expect the first question in the all-hands to be why.

### 5. The package (proposal, to be costed by HR and finance)

Above the statutory minimum, where the company can afford it:

- Statutory transition payment (transitievergoeding), plus [an additional amount per year of service]. Counsel confirms the current statutory formula and cap
- Paid notice, with permission to job-hunt in work time
- From the day of notice, not the last day: outplacement, coaching, CV and LinkedIn review, and a training budget of [EUR x] per person
- Keep laptop or phone where possible, after wiping company data
- Written reference and a promise of a positive reference call; priority for rehiring for [12] months
- Confidential psychological and financial support line
- Extra care and individual talks for people on a work visa (a job loss affects the residence permit and has strict deadlines), close to retirement, on sick or parental leave, or pregnant. Counsel checks protections that apply to leave and illness

Costing: HR to produce per-role gross cost of the package, notice and transition payments. Compare against the annual saving before the council file is sent, so the board and the council both see the real net figure and the payback period. I have no salary data, so I have not estimated this.

### 6. The day of the conversations

- Each person hears it from their own manager, with an HR colleague present, in person or on a live video call. Never by mass email; no access is switched off before the conversation
- Written summary handed over the same day: what happens next, the package, deadlines, who to call
- Managers are briefed and rehearsed the day before, and have someone to debrief with. This is hard on them too
- Schedule the conversations early in the week and early in the day, so people can reach HR and family; no Friday afternoons
- People can say goodbye to colleagues if they want to

### 7. The people who stay

- Same-day message (Part 2, message C) saying what is changing in the work, and that the round is complete only if it truly is. Do not say "no further cuts" unless you are prepared to be held to it
- Workloads and priorities re-planned together within two weeks; what stops is announced by name
- 1:1s with every remaining person within two weeks; track absence, engagement pulse and regretted exits monthly for 12 months

### 8. Indicators

- Money: net saving vs. plan, including severance and transition costs, at 3, 6 and 12 months
- People: voluntary attrition among remaining staff, absence, pulse scores, how many affected people have new work after 3 and 6 months
- Customers: response times, complaint volume, and the service levels that were agreed to change

### 9. Risks to watch

- Council asks for more time or more information: give it; an extra two weeks costs less than a dispute
- Leaks before the announcement: keep the circle small, and keep the pre-brief to the shortest time possible
- Strong performers leave among the "survivors": 1:1s and visible workload relief matter more than a speech
- Service level or compliance failures in support and operations: set early-warning thresholds and a named owner

## Part 2. Staff announcement

Delivery: all-hands live (video plus the room), same day as the works council request, with the written version sent immediately after, not before. Managers are briefed an hour earlier. People in affected teams hear it from their own manager first where possible.

### Message A. All staff (spoken, then sent in writing)

Subject: A difficult decision about the size of our company

Hi everyone,

I want you to hear this from me, directly, and not through the grapevine.

The board has decided on a proposal to reduce the company by 22 roles, out of 180. Most of these are in operations and support. I want to be plain about what that means: 22 colleagues, people you work with, will lose their job if this goes ahead.

Why. [One short, honest paragraph with the real reason and figures: for example, the gap between revenue and costs, the date by which it needs to close, and what happens if it doesn't. Use the figures finance has confirmed. Don't call it "optimisation".] We looked at other ways to close that gap, including [two or three of the things that were considered]. The board concluded they were not enough or not fast enough, and I accept the decision.

What this is, and is not. This is a proposal that has now gone to our works council for its advice, and I will not take the final decision until I have read and answered that advice. The council can and will challenge it, and if they find a better route I will change the plan. I will also be consulting the unions and the UWV as required.

What it means for you.
- Nobody will find out through an email or a locked account. If your role is affected, you will hear it from your own manager, in person or live, with HR present. This will happen on [date], after the works council process. Until then no names have been decided and no one has been told.
- If you are not affected you will also be told, on the same day.
- Selection follows the law and written criteria, applied to roles and not to individuals' popularity. [HR will explain how.]

What we are doing for the people who leave. [Package in two or three lines: more than the statutory minimum, paid support from the first day of notice, training budget, outplacement, references, priority for rehiring.]

What we are doing at the top. [Leadership pay/bonus decision. One line, with the figure.]

What happens to the work. We are not just asking fewer people to do the same work. Each team will decide with their manager what we stop, slow down or automate, and I will share that list by [date].

What happens next, with dates.
- [Date]: Q&A sessions with HR and me, open to everyone; anonymous question form open from today
- [Date]: works council advice expected
- [Date]: my decision, announced to everyone the same day
- [Date]: individual conversations, if the plan goes ahead

I know this is hard to hear, and I will not pretend it isn't. If you are worried, speak to your manager, HR, or the confidential support line, [details]. You can also come to me directly. I will be at [place and time] on [day] and I'll stay until the questions run out.

[Name]

### Message B. Manager briefing note (an hour before, plus the day before the conversations)

- You will hear the news first so you can be ready. Do not share before the all-hands
- What you can say: the points in Message A. What you cannot say: names, numbers beyond those in the announcement, guesses about who is affected, promises that no further cuts will follow
- If asked something you can't answer, say "I don't know yet, I'll find out and come back by [day]", and then do it
- Escalate straight away to HR: anyone distressed, anyone on leave or sick, anyone on a work visa, anyone who says they were selected for the wrong reason
- You are allowed to be upset. You are not allowed to hide behind process

### Message C. To those who stay (same day as the conversations, after they end)

Subject: Where we are, and what happens now

Hi everyone,

Today we said goodbye to [22] colleagues. [One or two sentences of honest acknowledgement, and thanks to what they contributed.]

What this means for you: [the work that stops or changes, by team]. I know many of you will now carry more uncertainty, and some of you more work. We will sit down within the next two weeks, team by team, to decide what we are stopping so that this isn't just the same work with fewer people.

[If true: This round is complete. We will not run a second one.] [If it can't be promised: I can't promise that nothing will ever change, but I will tell you as soon as I know something, and before it becomes a rumour.]

Your manager will schedule a 1:1 with you in the next two weeks. If you want to talk before that, my door is open, and so is the support line: [details].

I will report on how we are doing, including how the colleagues who left are doing and how we are holding up, at the all-hands on [date].

[Name]

## Part 3. Script for the individual conversation (managers, with HR present)

Open with the news in the first sentence, no small talk.

1. "I have difficult news. Your role is one of those being made redundant, and the decision is final." Pause. (Only use "final" if the council process is complete.)
2. "This is about the role and not about your performance." Say it only if it is true.
3. Let them react. Don't fill the silence, don't argue, don't console with "it's for the best".
4. Hand over the written summary: what happens now, dates, the package, who to call.
5. Explain what they can do today: take time, go home, speak to HR, or return to their desk if they prefer.
6. Close: "I am sorry. Thank you for what you have done here." Agree when you will speak next, within two working days.

Do not say: "I know how you feel", "it's nothing personal", "at least you will get a package", or anything about who else is affected.

## Assumptions and what would change this plan

- The company has a works council (mandatory from 50 employees). If one somehow does not exist, the plan changes, and counsel needs to know immediately.
- All 22 are in one UWV work area; if they are spread across more, the WMCO threshold may be met differently. Counsel to confirm.
- The reason given is the real reason; if it is a financial gap, the figures in Message A come from finance and are dated.
- Timeline assumes a 4-5 week council advice period. If the council asks for more, the dates move.
