# Roadmaps that fit the people who build them

Read this when building a roadmap or quarterly plan, when commitments exceed capacity, or when someone asks the teams to "just do more".

## Contents

- 1. Build a capacity model first
- 2. Shape the roadmap
- 3. When demand exceeds capacity
- 4. Communicating the roadmap

## 1. Build a capacity model first

Start from the people, not the wish list:

| Per team | How to estimate |
|---|---|
| Engineers and their availability | Headcount minus planned leave, onboarding, holidays and part-time contracts |
| Unplanned work | The team's recent share of unplanned work and incidents (Jira labels, incident tool) |
| On-call and support | Time on rotation and interrupt work |
| Tech debt and reliability | An agreed share, or the specific items already committed |
| Meetings, reviews, mentoring | A realistic allowance; juniors need senior time |
| Left for roadmap work | What remains, as a range, not a single number |

Use the team's own past throughput for the same kind of work, not estimates made under pressure. Name the uncertainty.

## 2. Shape the roadmap

- Organise by outcomes and time horizons (now, next, later), with confidence levels. Firm dates only for what the team has estimated and committed to.
- Put debt, reliability and platform health in the plan as visible items with owners, not as "if there's time".
- Leave slack. Teams planned at 100% have no room for incidents, learning or the surprise that always comes.
- Sequence work so one team isn't the bottleneck for everything, and check that the most loaded team isn't also the least healthy one.

## 3. When demand exceeds capacity

In order:

1. **Cut scope:** smallest version that delivers the outcome; drop or defer low-evidence items.
2. **Sequence:** move items to the next quarter with a date for a decision.
3. **Remove waste:** stop low-value work in flight, fix the top sources of unplanned work.
4. **Shift capacity:** temporarily move people between teams, with their agreement and support.
5. **Hire or contract,** if the demand is lasting and funded, with the onboarding cost counted.
6. **Never** close the gap with overtime, cutting testing or skipping security review. That debt arrives as incidents, attrition and slower delivery later.

## 4. Communicating the roadmap

- Show what's in, what's out and why, with the trade-offs in plain numbers.
- Give sales and customer-facing teams honest language: what they can promise, what they can't, and how to escalate a deal-critical request.
- Review the roadmap on a fixed rhythm with the teams and with leadership, and update it openly when evidence changes.
